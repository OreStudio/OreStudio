* Summary

The [[id:B5214F3A-A72E-421E-82BC-79338B2E95CB][Cuts]] structure note fixes
what a cut is, what it holds and why. It settles no state, no act, no
authorisation, no identity, and nothing about what selects a cut. This page
settles them. A cut records the body it fixed and the acts that made it, and
nothing else; its state is derived from those facts and stored nowhere; a cut
kind declares whether a cut of its occasion needs an authorisation, and where it
does the authorisation is an approval request of
[[id:0FE6BDD9-EB24-4B07-8B0C-D816AAF1BEBF][the inbox]]. A report carries a cut
*selection* as a property — a kind, or a specific cut — and the run resolves the
selection to a concrete cut at the moment it starts, records that cut and its
timepoint, and prices from its body. One selection kind, LIVE, has no timepoint
of its own: the run supplies it, and the run mints the cut.

The authorisation is not a mechanism this cluster invents, and neither is the
selection a second market association. One record already holds everything that
waits on a person, and one object now names the body a run prices from, which is
what the market bindings were doing in the absence of a cut.

Everything on this page is our working convention. No public source states how a
firm should model the authorisation of a cut or the selection a report carries,
and the [[id:B5214F3A-A72E-421E-82BC-79338B2E95CB][Cuts]] note records which
claims in the cluster rest on a source and which do not.

* The facts a cut records

A cut records six groups of facts. The parts are the body the
[[id:B5214F3A-A72E-421E-82BC-79338B2E95CB][Cuts]] note argues for; the other
five groups name the occasion, the scope, what the assembly could not fill and
who acted.

| Fact | What it holds | Why the cut records it |
|------+---------------+----------------------------|
| The kind | The occasion, from the closed set [[id:33E879B7-496A-406D-B3F2-72637379AD02][Kinds of cut]] classifies, and the instant a process names where no kind applies | The kind fixes the instant, the recurrence and the centre, and it declares whether a cut of the occasion needs an authorisation |
| The timepoint | The instant, with its timezone, and the cut time it came from where a named cut time supplied it | A body fixed at an instant is not fixed until the instant is stated with its clock |
| The scope | The books whose deals the valuation values, and the rates centre whose market data they revalue against | A body that names no population values nothing, and a market data half with no centre denotes whichever centre answered first |
| The parts | One reference per part of the body: the fixed market data set, the deal population, the versions of the reference data and the settings, and the engine and code versions | The four inputs are what the reproducibility argument requires, and a reference is what a rerun replays |
| The gaps | Every part the assembly could not fill, with the item, the part and the reason | A body that could not be assembled is a fact a person acts on, and silence makes the cut look smaller rather than incomplete |
| The acts | Who took the cut and when, and the authorisation: the request the take raised, the decision it reached, the decider and the instant, or the refusal and its reason — or, where the kind requires none, the fact that none was required | The taking identity and the authorisation are not inputs to the valuation: an unsigned body is a candidate, and an occasion that needs no signature still has to say so rather than look unsigned |

Two of these facts are ours alone and no prior art supplies them. The gap list
follows from the definition: a cut missing a part is incomplete rather than
smaller, so the absence is recorded where the body is recorded rather than left
to a run to discover. The act of taking follows from the same sentence:
something fixes the body, and the something is a person acting at an instant — or,
for the one kind whose consumer supplies the instant, the run acting for that
person.

** What is derived, and why nothing is stored

The state, the consumability verdict and every word the screens print about a cut
are functions of the facts above. None is stored.

The precedent is the published set.
[[id:9BC6FEDE-01E5-43B8-8A04-ACF0A5AB12B6][ores.marketdata.curve_publish]]
states that the state a reader sees is a function of the stored facts and is
stored nowhere: withdrawn when an instant holds, superseded when an instant
holds, published when an instant holds, and a candidate otherwise. A cut has the
same shape and the same reason. A stored state follows a write that may fail, may
be repeated or may be missed, so the row and its state can disagree; a derived
state cannot disagree with the facts it is derived from.

** What the kind declares

A cut kind is a row, and it carries five facts beyond its code and name.

| Declaration | What it decides | EOD | SOD | IPV | FLASH | LIVE |
|-------------+-----------------+-----+-----+-----+-------+------|
| What fixes the instant | Where the timepoint comes from | The centre's close | The centre's open | The close of the cut it is built on | An instant the process names | The instant the run starts |
| =requires_authorisation= | Whether a cut of the kind raises an approval request | Yes | No | Yes | No | No |
| =output_is_indicative= | Whether a result priced from a cut of the kind carries the indicative mark | No | No | No | Yes | Yes |
| =approval_kind_code= | Which inbox kind authorises it, where it requires one | =reporting.cut_authorisation= | none | =reporting.cut_authorisation= | none | none |
| =display_order= | The order the take offers the kinds | 10 | 20 | 30 | 40 | 50 |

The declarations are independent, and the kinds show it. A start of day cut needs
no authorisation and is not indicative, because the body was approved when it was
first fixed. A flash cut needs none and is indicative, because its occasion is
before the day settles. A LIVE cut needs none and is indicative, and it differs
from both in where its instant comes from: no person chooses it and no centre
publishes it, so [[*The LIVE kind][the LIVE kind]] takes it from the run.

* The states and what derives them

A cut is in one of seven states. Each is stated by the facts that hold, and the
order of the tests is the order a reader needs them.

| State | The facts that hold | What a run may do | The mark |
|-------+---------------------+-------------------+----------|
| Draft | No taking instant holds, and no gap stands; the assembled body is saved before the take | Nothing. It is not a cut yet | Grey, the mark of an inactive record |
| Incomplete | No taking instant holds, and one or more gaps stand; the attempt is saved as a draft | Nothing. The gaps are the answer | Red, because the body failed its own standard |
| Taken | The body is fixed, the kind requires an authorisation, and no approval is recorded | Nothing. It is the body the decider reads | Blue, the mark of a pending item |
| In use | The body is fixed, and the kind requires no authorisation | Price from it | Green, the colour of a record that is active and in order |
| Authorised | The body is fixed and an approval is recorded | Price from it | Green |
| Refused | The authorisation was refused, with the reason | Nothing. A refusal is terminal for that body | Red |
| Superseded | The cut was authorised or in use, and a later cut of the same kind and scope holds its place | Price from it, and say a later cut is in use | Grey, as the published lists draw an ended set |

The table is a derivation and not a lifecycle: a state is one of the seven and
never a mixture of two. Two facts decide which of the three usable states applies,
and neither is a matter of taste: whether the kind requires an authorisation, and
whether one was recorded.

Six consequences follow, and each is a decision the screens draw.

**The gate follows the kind, not a single rule about cuts.** A run prices from a
body that is fixed and signed where a signature was required. A cut of a kind that
requires none is usable the moment it is taken, and a cut of a kind that requires
one is not usable until the approval is recorded. A gate that demanded an
authorisation of every cut would refuse the occasions that need none, and a gate
that accepted any taken body would price from an unsigned one.

**A draft is a record, not a scratchpad, and a gap is what separates it from an
incomplete body.** The gaps a body cannot close name a person to chase — a series
with no value at or before the instant, a deal at no version, a curve nobody has
approved, an engine version nothing recorded. A take that threw the attempt away
would make the person remember the list. The draft therefore holds the attempt,
each gap with its reason, and the cut's own page is where the list is read.

**An incomplete body is refused before it is signed.** The take raises no request
while a gap stands, because a decider would be asked to sign a body that does not
meet the definition. The refusal is at the take and not at the authorisation, and
the screen says what would close each gap.

**A held request leaves the cut where it was.** The inbox's hold state parks the
request and not the body, so the cut stays *Taken*, its card reads that the
request is held, and the waiting time is the request's to report.

**Supersession does not withdraw authorisation.** A superseded cut is still a
record of an instant, and a run that priced from it still reproduces from it. The
published-set rule that an ended set is refused unless its as-of is pinned does not
carry over, because a curve is a value in use and a cut is a record of what was
used. The one word the screen must say is that a later cut replaced it: a person
who reads a result must not take it for the day's cut.

**The indicative mark is the kind's, and it is not the state's.** A result priced
from a flash cut or a LIVE cut is indicative because the kind says so, and the mark
is drawn on the cut, on the selection's badge and on every result it prices. A cut
of a kind that requires no authorisation is not therefore indicative, and the start
of day kind is the case that shows it.

A LIVE cut is never a draft and never taken by a person: the run mints it with its
body already fixed, so its first state is *In use*. A selection whose cut does not
exist yet is not a state of a cut at all — there is no row to carry one — and
[[*The resolution][the resolution]] refuses such a run and says which cut it looked
for.

* The authorisation is the inbox's

A cut that requires an authorisation raises an approval request of a kind the
reporting component registers. Nothing about the wait for a person is the cut's
own: the record, its lifecycle, its rules, its queue, its notifications and its
history are
[[id:0FE6BDD9-EB24-4B07-8B0C-D816AAF1BEBF][the approval request's]], and the
cut adds a detail table and a descriptor.

| What the cut needs | Where it lives | The precedent |
|--------------------+----------------+---------------|
| The record of the ask, the decision and the wait | An =ores.inbox= approval request: its kind, state, asker, reason, decision and expiry | [[id:0FE6BDD9-EB24-4B07-8B0C-D816AAF1BEBF][Approval requests]] |
| What the request is about | A detail table in the reporting component, keyed by =request_id=: the cut and the counts of the body's parts | [[id:9BC6FEDE-01E5-43B8-8A04-ACF0A5AB12B6][ores.marketdata.curve_publish]] |
| Whether a cut needs one at all | The cut kind's =requires_authorisation=, and the take raises nothing where it says no | [[*What the kind declares][What the kind declares]] |
| Who may decide | The inbox kind's =decide_permission_code= | [[id:A85B6C4E-0F93-4410-BF2E-7167722849ED][Decide a publish]] |
| How many approvals close it | The inbox kind's =approvals_required=; one for a cut | [[id:A85B6C4E-0F93-4410-BF2E-7167722849ED][Decide a publish]] |
| Four eyes | A rule of the schema: a decision by the asker is refused, and one person counts once | [[id:0FE6BDD9-EB24-4B07-8B0C-D816AAF1BEBF][Approval requests]] |
| The queue | The shared Approvals read, filtered to the kinds the caller may decide | [[id:0FE6BDD9-EB24-4B07-8B0C-D816AAF1BEBF][Approval requests]] |
| One open request per subject | The detail table's natural key on the cut, which is what the inbox means by the same subject | [[id:0FE6BDD9-EB24-4B07-8B0C-D816AAF1BEBF][Approval requests]] |
| The screen the decider reads | The request page, which draws the kind's detail as its own section | the inbox's =RequestPage= and its =kinds.tsx= descriptor |
| Notifications | The inbox's: the deciders are told a request waits, the asker is told the decision | [[id:0FE6BDD9-EB24-4B07-8B0C-D816AAF1BEBF][Approval requests]] |
| Applying the decision | The reporting service stamps the cut's authorisation facts when the request reaches approved | the shape IAM uses for a role grant |

**The take raises the request, in one act.** The body is frozen when the request
is raised, so what a decider reads is what gets authorised. The detail row is
written with the body and keyed by the request, as the curve publish detail is
keyed by its request. A take that wrote a body and then asked for a signature
would leave an interval in which the body may move.

**The taker cannot authorise, and the rule is not the cut's.** The inbox refuses a
decision by the person who raised the request, and a unique key on the request and
the decider keeps one person from counting twice. A cut adds nothing to that rule
and depends on it, which is why the take and the decision are two permissions and
not one. A firm with one person who holds both cannot authorise a cut at all: the
screen draws the refusal and names what is missing, which is a second person, and
it does not draw the button.

**A refusal is terminal for that body.** The body stays readable, the reason is on
the request and copied to the cut's page, and the inbox allows no second decision
on a refused request. The remedy is a new take, which is a new cut and a new
request, because an approval is a decision on one body and a decided body does not
become another.

**The apply is a reconciliation, not a second screen.** When a request reaches
approved, the reporting service writes the cut's authorisation facts and marks the
detail row applied. It does so from the request's change event and from the state
the request holds, so a missed event, a repeated one, or an approval made while the
service was down all converge on one outcome, as a role grant does.

**The cut's page links the request and never the reverse.** The request is the
record of the wait and the cut is the record of the body; the request page draws
the body through the kind's detail, and the cut's page names the request. Neither
copies the other's facts, because two copies of one act drift.

* The permissions

Three permissions decide what a person sees and does. Two are the cut's own, and
the third is the inbox's.

| Permission | What it governs | Who holds it, in the fixture |
|------------+-----------------+------------------------------|
| =reporting::cuts:read= | The list, the record, the report's selection, and the run's card that names a cut | Market risk, finance, a reader |
| =reporting::cuts:take= | Assembling and taking a cut by hand, which raises its authorisation where the kind requires one | Finance, for the end of day kind |
| =inbox::approvals:decide_finance= | The decision that authorises an end of day cut: the inbox kind's own decide permission | Finance, and a different person from the taker |

The first two names are proposals and follow the platform's namespace for a
component's own record. The third is not new: it is a permission the inbox has
already seeded, and the cut's kind names it rather than inventing one.

**A LIVE cut is taken by the run, and the run's permission is the reporter's.**
Nobody presses *Take a cut* for a LIVE selection, so =reporting::cuts:take= is not
the permission that guards it: the person who may run the report is the person who
may cause a LIVE cut, and that is =reporting::report_instances:write=. The run
writes the taking identity as itself acting for the run's account, so the cut's
page names a system account and the run that minted it.

**One permission decides every cut that needs a signature.** A cut body is signed
by one function whatever the occasion that produced it, so the inbox kind carries
one decide permission and every authorising cut kind names that same kind.

* The identity

The cut's key is a generated identifier. The label a person quotes is derived from
the three facts that name the occasion, and it is never typed:

#+begin_example
  <kind code>.<scope code>.<timepoint>[.<take ordinal>]
  EOD.ACME-LONDON.2026-10-06
  EOD.ACME-LONDON.2026-10-06.2
  LIVE.ACME-LONDON.2026-10-07T07:40:00Z
#+end_example

The derivation is the rule the
[[id:FB8F701C-E56A-49A9-AA90-1B6BD0B02092][report binding decision]] applies to
a market configuration name: the screen draws what the record will say and no
field asks for it. Three reasons carry the rule.

A typed label is a name, and a name is what the binding decision rejects: it says
nothing about which body it means, and two bodies can carry one. A derived label
states the kind, the scope and the instant, and no second cut can carry it.

The take ordinal is the hub's numbered sequence made visible. A retake of the same
day, kind and scope is the second cut of that day, and the ordinal says so in the
one place a person looks. A LIVE cut carries its instant instead, to the second,
because two LIVE runs in one morning are two bodies and the date alone cannot tell
them apart.

The label is not the authorisation. A person reading =EOD.ACME-LONDON.2026-10-06=
knows which day's body is meant and not whether anybody signed it, so every screen
that shows the label shows the state beside it, and a cut of a kind that requires
no authorisation shows that basis in the same place.

* The report carries the cut

A report definition holds a *cut selection*, and the selection is one of two
things: a kind of cut, or a specific cut. The pair is one fact — the kind code with
no cut named, or the kind code and a cut — because a specific cut is a kind of cut
plus the identity of the one body meant.

**The selection sits on the definition, and not on the run settings
configuration.** Four reasons carry the choice.

A report is the thing a person reads a badge on, and two reports bound to one
configuration may serve different cycles: a board pack of the close and an intraday
monitor share an engine but not a body. A selection on the shared configuration
would give both the same badge and the same body, which is false for one of them.

The selection states what the report *is*, beside its type, its scope and its
regulatory book type. The configuration states how the engine runs and which
documents it reads. The two are different kinds of fact, and the badge belongs to
the first.

The definition is versioned, so a change to the selection is a version with an
account and a reason, and the runs already produced keep the cut they recorded. A
change on a shared configuration would reach every report bound to it without any
of their pages saying so.

A report is a version of an intent, and the intent is *value this scope against the
close of its day*. The configuration cannot express that sentence for one report
and not for another.

**The badge is the selection's kind, drawn on the list and the record.** A person
who reads a list of thirty-one reports sees which of them price from the close,
which from the open and which from the run itself, without opening one. The badge
is a chip carrying the kind's code, and under it the fact that qualifies it: *no
cut selected* where the report was created without one, *pinned* where the
selection names a specific cut, and *indicative* where the kind's values are. LIVE
is the one kind whose badge carries a note rather than a value, because its body
does not exist until a run gives it a timepoint.

The badge is not a status: it is a classification of the report, so it borrows no
green, amber or red. The health of the last run stands elsewhere on the row, where
the reporting journeys already put it.

**Create and update ask for the selection and nothing else.** The selection is one
field on the create flow and one field on the report's *Definition* tab, and the
dialog offers the kinds the party may select plus the cuts already taken, with a
preview of what the current choice would resolve to right now: *EOD would resolve
to EOD.ACME-LONDON.2026-10-06, authorised by a.reyes*, or *LIVE will fix a body at
the instant the run starts, and every result it prices is indicative*. The preview
is the resolution the run will make, run against the same rules and the same store,
so a person sees a stale choice before a run discovers it.

* The resolution

The selection is a rule; the resolution executes it. The run resolves the selection
to a concrete cut before it gathers anything, and the rules are six.

| Selection | Resolves to | Its timepoint | What the resolution refuses |
|-----------+-------------+---------------+-----------------------------|
| =EOD= | The end of day cut of the date the report covers, for the report's scope and centre | The centre's close of that date | No such cut, or one whose authorisation is not recorded |
| =SOD= | The start of day cut of that date, for the same scope | The centre's open of that date | No such cut |
| =IPV= | The independent verification cut of that date | The close of the cut it is built on | No such cut, or one whose authorisation is not recorded |
| A process-named kind | The cut of that kind for the date, at the instant its process named | The instant the process named | No such cut |
| =LIVE= | A new cut, minted at the run's instant | The instant the run starts | A body that cannot be assembled |
| A specific cut | Itself | The cut's own instant | A cut that is not consumable, or one of another party or scope |

**The date the report covers is not the cut.** The report's covered date is a
setting of the run settings, and the resolution reads it together with the report's
scope and its centre. A cut states its own instant; the date says which cut to look
for. That is why a weekly report can price from the daily cut of the day it covers,
and why the same report next week prices from a different body.

**The scope is not chosen at the resolution.** The report's scope names books, and
[[id:D18DF500-2C6C-42FF-BBAE-D5A46D410910][Book groups and rates centres]] states
that the books that revalue together share one centre's market data. The resolution
reads the centres of the report's books, and a scope spanning two is refused before
anything is gathered: no cut of one centre can value books that read another.

**A refusal is one sentence, spoken before the engine.** A selection that resolves
to nothing is refused with the kind it looked for, the date, the scope and — where
there is a cut but its authorisation is not recorded — the request and the person it
waits on. The preview's sentence, the report page's sentence and the run's output
message are one sentence, so a scheduled run nobody watched still says why it did
not price.

**The run records what it resolved.** The run keeps the cut, its label, its kind and
its timepoint, and the report's *Market* tab shows the selection beside the last
run's resolved cut, because the two are different facts: the selection may have
moved since the run. The run's own card names the body, read through the cut.

**The resolution is one operation and it is previewable.** One operation answers the
cut a selection resolves to, its state and the sentence that would refuse it, for a
preview or for a run. One operation serves both, because a preview computed by a
second code path is a preview of that path and not of the run.

* The LIVE kind

LIVE is the kind whose timepoint the consumer supplies. It differs from the four
occasions of [[id:33E879B7-496A-406D-B3F2-72637379AD02][Kinds of cut]] in that
nothing outside the run fixes it: no centre publishes its instant, no person chooses
it, and no process names it. A report that selects LIVE says *value this scope at
whatever time you start*.

**Its body is the four inputs at the instant the run starts.** The population is
what the trade store holds then, the market data is the snapshot the profile
resolves then, the reference data and the settings are the versions in force then,
and the engine and code are what the run will execute. It is the whole body and not
a smaller one, because a LIVE result that could not be reproduced would be a number
with a claim it cannot meet. If a part cannot be assembled the run is refused with
the gaps, and no partial body is minted.

**Its values are indicative, and that is our convention.** No authorisation precedes
a LIVE cut, and the values it fixes are the values in the store at the instant the
run began, which the day's official data will replace. A result priced from a LIVE
cut therefore carries the indicative mark, as a flash result does, and the mark is
read from the kind.

**It requires no authorisation, and the reason is structural.** The run both mints
the cut and consumes it in one act, so an authorisation would hold the intake until
a person answered, and the report it serves would have no result by the time it was
read. Four eyes is not weakened by this: nothing is signed, so there is no signature
to protect. A firm that wanted LIVE signed does not want LIVE; it wants a flash cut,
which a person takes at an instant they name, and which is signed or not by its own
kind.

**A LIVE cut is reproducible only by naming it.** A rerun of the same LIVE report
tomorrow resolves to a new instant, so it is a different body and a different
result. The first result is reproduced by naming its cut: a selection of that
specific cut, or a rerun against the resolved cut, which the run's page offers
because the run knows what it priced from. The card states the difference in the
words the reproduction verdict already uses: two runs of one cut are one result
computed twice, two LIVE runs are two results of two bodies and comparable only as
a movement.

**Supersession applies as it does to any cut.** A later LIVE cut of the same kind and
scope becomes the kind's cut in use, and the earlier one stays in use as a record.
Because LIVE cuts accumulate at the rate of runs, the cuts list's kind filter and its
date order are what make them readable, and a LIVE cut's label carries its instant
to the second for the same reason.

* The role bindings retire

The [[id:FB8F701C-E56A-49A9-AA90-1B6BD0B02092][report binding decision]] made a run
settings configuration name, per market role, the published set the run prices from,
with its as-of, a pin for a historical rerun, and a derived ORE name. That row was
the report's market association because nothing else named the market a run read. A
cut now names it, and two records that name the same curves drift.

**The finding.** A binding row and a cut both name the curves a run reads. The
binding names one published set per role at an as-of; the cut names the fixed
snapshot that resolved every role at its instant. Where both stand, a report can
price from a binding that disagrees with its cut — and the pin makes the
disagreement look deliberate. The precedence is not a matter of taste: the cut is
the body, the binding is a rule of the profile the body was built from, and a rule
does not travel with the result. A result that named a rule instead of the body would
be reproducible only while the rule and the store held still.

**What the selection replaces.** The configuration's market bindings, the *Market
bindings* tab, the pinned as-of, and the binding row as an input to a run. The
profile the bindings were — one published set per market role — becomes a fact of
the market data side, resolved when a cut is assembled, which is where the
[[id:DD59B04C-DAD2-458C-B35E-3F6B0B3D6DC6][Market data cut]] page already puts the
profile.

**What carries over, and is not lost.** The published-state refusal still holds, one
layer down: a candidate set cannot be part of a body, so the assembly reports it as
a gap and the cut is incomplete rather than wrong. The ORE market configuration name
is still derived from the curve and the party, and the cut's market data half records
it. The export still builds the XML from the published set's points, in the ORE
vocabulary of the boundary
([[id:D1A25B9E-B1A0-4E7C-AB5C-1C7C3EF07F90][ORE import and export]]). The run's
market card survives and is read through the cut. The role vocabulary problem
survives with it, because the roles are still a free string in the profile.

**What retires with the row.** The pin, because naming a specific cut is the same
intent and is stronger: a pinned as-of reproduced one curve, and a named cut
reproduces the whole body. And the picker over published sets on the run settings,
because a person who wants a particular curve publishes it and takes a cut; the
report then names the cut.

**The supersession is marked where the decision is cited.** The earlier page carries
a dated status note saying which parts stand and which retire, and the pages that
cite it say *superseded in part* where they name its row, its pin or its tab. Its
argument — that a name is not provenance — stands, and it is the reason the cut, and
not a name, is what a run now records.

* How it meets the report binding

The earlier decision asked for a binding that names the published set rather than a
free name, and recorded that it left the deal population open: *a report that must
state both needs a second binding, to a cut.* This page supplies the cut, and the
selection is where the report states it. The second binding is not a second row
beside the first: it is the object the first row was approximating, so the row
retires and the cut carries both halves.

**The chain, in the words the screens use.** A report names a cut selection; the run
resolves it to a cut; the cut holds the body; the body holds the deal population, the
fixed snapshot with its publish records, the reference data and the engine. A result
names one object at the head of that chain, and every input is reachable from it.

**What the deal population becomes.** Today the gathering step reads the trade store
by valuation date and the run records the date it priced at. After this page, the
gathering step reads the cut's deal population, which is a copy of what the store
held at the cut instant and not a query re-run against it. The
[[id:19630B5D-A163-42B5-B9C4-61A87BAE8F5F][Trade cut]] page states why: the interval
a versioned row states is rewritten when a correction arrives, so two executions of
the same query at different moments can return different bodies. The cut's population
is a fact of the cut, and a rerun replays it.

* What this page does not settle

**The entity.** No cut component exists and no table holds one, and the report
definition holds no selection. This page settles what a cut must record, which states
follow, who may act, what a report selects and how a run resolves it; it does not
design the columns, the generated types or the subjects. That is the next work, and
it starts from this page and from
[[id:B5214F3A-A72E-421E-82BC-79338B2E95CB][Cuts]].

**One inbox kind, or one for each occasion.** This page takes one inbox kind,
=reporting.cut_authorisation=, whose detail row names the cut and therefore the
occasion. The alternative is a kind per occasion, which buys a decide permission per
occasion and a queue grouped by occasion, at the price of a seeded kind for every
kind of cut.

**The apply.** The decision is applied by reconciliation, as a role grant is. The
alternative is an apply workflow, which the inbox already offers a kind through
=apply_workflow_type= and which buys a visible run and a retry where this page buys
one idempotent row.

**The scope of a cut.** The convention taken here is that a cut's scope is the set of
books that revalue against one centre's market data. The hub's narrower convention,
one cut per book, is the smallest such scope and not the only one. Whether a group
report prices from one cut of the group or from a composition of its books' cuts is
open, and a report whose books span two centres is refused rather than composed.

**The resolution of a selection the party took no cut for.** A report may select EOD
and find no EOD cut of its date, because nobody took one. The run is refused and says
which cut it looked for. Whether the report should also draw a warning before the run
— and whether a scheduled run should raise a request for somebody to take the cut —
is open, and belongs with the schedule's journey.

**A LIVE cut's volume.** One LIVE cut per run accumulates fast, and the cuts list must
stay readable: the kind filter, the date order and the scope are what carry it. Whether
LIVE cuts are archived after a period is open.

**The reference data half, by version or by value.** The part holds the versions of the
conventions, the calendars, the settings and the curve configurations the run reads.
Whether the cut stores those versions or the identifiers of the documents is open, and
a LIVE cut makes the question sharper, because the versions in force move between runs.

**Which occasions need no authorisation.** The classification gives the start of day,
flash and LIVE kinds, and the fixtures draw them. Whether a process-named instant needs
one is the process owner's answer, and the kind declares it rather than the design
assuming it.

**Who takes a cut where a party has one person.** The four-eyes rule makes an
authorisation impossible, and this page leaves that as a refusal to draw rather than a
rule to bend. A kind that requires no authorisation sidesteps it, which is a fact about
the occasion and not a route past the rule.

**The relation to the run's settings.** A cut holds the settings in force. Whether the
cut names the engine run settings configuration and its version, or the individual
settings, is open, and it belongs to
[[id:EDAE3C87-7733-4D0C-881F-5AA65DD832AC][Pricing Configuration]] rather than to this
page.

* What the sources establish

No claim on this page rests on a public source. The definitions of the states, the
kind's declarations, the permissions, the selection and its resolution, the LIVE kind,
the derivation of the label and the retirement of the binding row are ours, and the
pages they rest on are cited above. The rules of the authorisation are not ours: they
are the inbox's, stated on
[[id:0FE6BDD9-EB24-4B07-8B0C-D816AAF1BEBF][Approval requests]] and enforced by its
schema. The [[id:B5214F3A-A72E-421E-82BC-79338B2E95CB][Cuts]] note records which
claims of the cluster do rest on a public source, and this page adds none to that list.
