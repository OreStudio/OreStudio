This page documents a [[id:F44766FA-1E9C-416F-849A-E131101B2ECA][user journey]]: A tenant administrator grants and revokes a colleague's roles.

A user journey describes what one person is trying to do, before anything is
built. It is a design document that outlives any sprint, so it names the
screens, the entity fields each screen shows, and the operations and messages
each step needs.

* Goal

"I need to give a colleague the roles their job needs, and take away the ones
it does not." The tenant administrator sets the role list on a colleague's
account. The journey is done when the colleague's access reads correctly, and
the record of the change names the administrator, the time and the reason.

* Actor and trigger

The tenant administrator: an account inside one tenant that holds
=iam::roles:assign= and =iam::roles:revoke=. The trigger is a joiner, a move
between desks, or a leaver. It starts when the administrator opens a
colleague's account from *Accounts*, and ends when the role list is right and
the change is recorded. This is an administrator acting on someone else's
record inside their tenant.

This is the write half of the access topic. The colleague's own read-only view
is [[id:3CA48F40-B601-497B-BA78-9822DBEBA8E5][Know what I may do]].

* Steps

1. *Find the colleague.* The administrator opens *Accounts* and picks the
   person. The system lists the accounts in the tenant, so tenant isolation
   decides who is visible.
2. *Read what they hold.* The system reads the roles assigned to the account
   and shows them. This list is the starting point.
3. *Choose a role to grant.* The administrator opens the role picker. The
   system lists the roles defined in the tenant, with their descriptions.
4. *Grant the role.* The administrator selects a role and confirms. The system
   writes an =account_role= row. The row records =assigned_by= (the
   administrator), =assigned_at= (the time), the change reason and the
   commentary. This step is what makes the grant auditable.
5. *Revoke a role.* The administrator selects a held role, removes it, and
   gives a reason. The system closes the assignment: it sets =valid_to= on the
   open row instead of deleting it, so the grant stays in the history.
6. *Confirm.* The system re-reads the role list. The administrator checks the
   result. The change takes effect on the colleague's next sign-in.
7. *Leave.* The administrator closes the screen.

The assignment row carries =assigned_by=, =assigned_at=, =change_reason_code=
and =change_commentary=, so a granted role is auditable in a way a plain join
table would not be. A revoke closes the row and a later re-grant opens a new
one, so the whole history of a person's access stays readable.

* Screens and wireframes

One screen: the colleague's access. The identity header, the roles they hold
with the assignment audit, the role picker that adds a role, and the change
reason and commentary. The administrator does all of this in one place, so the
panels belong together.

* Entities composed

#+ATTR_HTML: :class hug-leading
| Entity | What it contributes | Model |
|--------+---------------------+-------|
| =account= | The colleague whose access changes: username, full name, account type | [[id:81D4D50F-A8D4-47F8-9500-89F6AC460469][ores.iam.account]] |
| =role= | The roles held and the roles on offer: name and description | [[id:6C0F5B1A-9A3E-4D2C-8B7E-2F4A1C6D9E30][ores.iam.role]] |
| =account_role= | The assignment: =assigned_by=, =assigned_at=, =change_reason_code= and =change_commentary= | no entity model — junction table |
| =permission= | Not shown here. What the roles carry belongs to [[id:3CA48F40-B601-497B-BA78-9822DBEBA8E5][Know what I may do]] | [[id:76B113F4-410A-4DE5-9757-A4E263B7B8E7][ores.iam.permission]] |

The =account_role= row is more than a join. It carries an audit tail, so the
grant itself is a record with an author, a time and a reason. There is no
module entity and no module table in this model: the component segment of a
permission code such as =iam::roles:assign= is what plays the part a module
would play.

This screen shows no credential field. =password_hash=, =password_salt=,
=service_password_hash=, =totp_secret=, cleartext passwords and JWTs must never
reach the browser. The account read currently returns credential material
anyway, and that is a gap named below.

* Operations and messages

#+ATTR_HTML: :class hug-leading
| Step | Operation | Subject | Status |
|------+-----------+---------+--------|
| Find the colleague | List the accounts in the tenant | =iam.v1.accounts.list= | exists |
| Read what they hold | Read the roles on the account | =iam.v1.ops.get_account_roles= | exists |
| Choose a role to grant | List the roles defined in the tenant | =iam.v1.roles.list= | exists |
| Grant the role | Assign a role to the account | =iam.v1.ops.assign_role= | exists |
| Revoke a role | Revoke a role from the account | =iam.v1.ops.revoke_role= | exists |
| Confirm | Read the assignment row: who, when, why | =iam.v1.ops.get_account_roles= | exists |

The role subjects are defined in
[[id:C47A4410-8624-431C-98FE-D05304CEC6D8][ores.iam.authorization_messages]].
Both writes check their own permission before they act:
=iam.v1.ops.assign_role= refuses a caller without =iam::roles:assign=, and
=iam.v1.ops.revoke_role= refuses one without =iam::roles:revoke=. That was not
true when this journey was written — assign checked nothing, and any signed-in
account could grant any role inside its tenant — and it is the first of the two
gaps the [[id:D5A29471-B0E3-4C69-B7CE-3477752C8ED7][access group]]'s survey closed.

* What is missing

The screens run in =ores.web=: *People* lists the tenant's accounts with
their pictures and roles, and a person's page gives a role for a reason and
takes one away. Nobody takes a role away from their own account: the server
refuses it and the screen does not offer it ([[id:D5A29471-B0E3-4C69-B7CE-3477752C8ED7][Implement the access journeys in ores.web]]).


1. *Closed for a grant, open for a revoke.* =assign_role_request= carries
   =change_reason_code= and =change_commentary=, the grant row stores them,
   and an =access= reason category offers new joiner, role change, cover for
   absence and access review. A revoke closes the grant row, which has no
   place for a reason of its own, so the screen asks for none; recording why a
   role was taken away needs a closing-reason column or an event of its own.
2. *No guard on an empty role list.* The account screen warns when an account
   has no party, but stays silent when it has no role (see the user guide
   chapter). The same silence applies when a revoke removes the last role. The
   candidate is a warning before the last role is revoked.

Two more gaps have closed since this journey was written, both by
[[id:D5A29471-B0E3-4C69-B7CE-3477752C8ED7][the access group's]] first build.

The first is the one this journey called its own: no subject returned the
=account_role= row, so =assigned_by=, =assigned_at=, =change_reason_code= and
=change_commentary= could not be shown even though they were stored. The
composed read now carries all four beside each role, for the administrator and
for the member alike, so the * Confirm row above answers from the same subject
as the rest of the screen.

The second is the credential material this journey recorded on the account read.
=password_hash=, =password_salt=, =service_password_hash= and =totp_secret= are
=:no_wire:= in [[id:81D4D50F-A8D4-47F8-9500-89F6AC460469][ores.iam.account]]
now, so no read returns them, and the picker needs nothing else: a username and
a full name come over the wire and the secrets do not. That closes it for every
reader of the entity rather than for this screen alone.

The read is also no longer open to anybody. It requires =iam::roles:read=
before it looks the account up, and refuses with a =denied= result rather than
an empty list, which is what
[[id:804C7048-DBBF-4B39-8737-BFB4949884C4][Authorised reads]] settles for both
access reads.
