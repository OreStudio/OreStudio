* Goal

A bond's static data is stored once per security and referenced by trades,
matching ORE's split between the trade and the reference datum.

The collapse (PR 4, decision D14) left two identities where this component
carried three: the trade and the security. The instrument is now the trade,
so what remains is the security. It is still missing: every bond table
hangs off the trade, so a coupon schedule and a future's contract terms
are repeated once per trade instead of held once per security, and
=bond_issue= -- the one entity already shared -- is missing six fields ORE
has and carries =bond_notional=, which ORE holds on the trade.

This ports the abandoned sprint 25 task =0C525D24= onto the collapsed
model, and carries defect 15 from the owner's ruling of 2026-09-28.

* Acceptance

- The shared bond entities are keyed by the ORE reference datum id, which
  is =SecurityId= for a bond and =ContractName= for a bond future. No
  entity needs the words "reference data" in its name: =bond_issue= is
  already the shape to follow.
- =bond_future= keeps only what ORE keeps on the trade -- =ContractName=,
  =ContractNotional=, =LongShort=, =ApplyConversionFactor= and
  =UseFuturePrice= -- and its thirteen contract terms move to a shared
  entity keyed by the contract name.
- =bond_leg= and its rate, amount and amortization tables hang off the
  security rather than the trade.
- =bond_issue= gains the six fields ORE has and we lack: =CreditGroup=,
  =VolatilityCurveId=, =PriceQuoteMethod=, =PriceQuoteBaseValue=,
  =SubType= and =PriceType=. It loses =bond_notional=, which ORE holds on
  the trade.
- The fields duplicated against the leg tables -- currency, coupon rate,
  coupon frequency and day count -- are held once.
- A trade that states its bond inline rather than by reference still
  round-trips: the inline case populates the security rather than
  bypassing it.
- Defect 15: an underlying is an unqualified name. The underlyings the
  ORE documents name become qualified references, and each place the
  document states only a free name is recorded with its evidence.
- The ORE round trip stays at zero loss across the bond products and the
  trade envelope.
- =check_component_drift.py --component trading-cpp --dry-run= reports
  nothing, the whole-tree build exits 0, and the suites pass on a
  recreated database.

* Plan

ORE is the ground truth. The job is to apply data-oriented
principles to ORE's data model, not to invent one.

** The relationship ORE states

=bondReferenceDatum= is exactly =bondData= minus =SecurityId=,
=BondNotional=, =Payer= and =CreditRisk=. Everything else on a bond is
static data about the security, and the four exceptions are the trade's
own: which security, how much, which side, and whether credit risk
applies.

=bondFutureData= says the same in the other direction: v17 cut it to
=ContractName=, =ContractNotional= and =LongShort= plus the new
=ApplyConversionFactor= and =UseFuturePrice=, and moved the thirteen
contract terms into =bondFutureReferenceDatum=.

=ReferenceData= is a list of =ReferenceDatum=, each with an =id=
attribute, an optional =validFrom=, a =Type= element and one payload from
a closed choice. The key is the datum id, so a bond's is its =SecurityId=
and a bond future's is its =ContractName=.

** Where our model disagrees

- =bond_future= holds twelve contract terms that belong to the contract,
  so ten trades on one Bund future repeat them ten times.
- =bond_leg= and its rate, amount and amortization tables hold the coupon
  schedule, currency and day count, which in ORE are =LegData= inside
  =bondReferenceDatum=, so they belong to the security.
- =bond_issue= is the entity already shared and keyed by =security_id=,
  and it is =bondReferenceDatum= under another name. It is missing the
  six fields above, it carries =bond_notional=, and its =currency=,
  =coupon_rate=, =coupon_frequency_code= and =day_count_code= duplicate
  the leg tables.

** Sequence

Shared entities first, because the per-trade tables point at them. Model,
mapper and tests move together; no commit removes a column before its
replacement exists. The corpus round trip runs before the task closes,
as it did for the collapse.

** Unit 1: the issue fields, inventoried (2026-09-29)

The first slice, measured on the branch cut from =cd00344ddb=. It is the
smallest green unit: =bond_issue= is already shared and keyed by
=security_id=, so the six fields are additive and independent of the datum
keying and of the leg move.

| Field | ORE type | Our shape |
|-------+----------+-----------|
| =CreditGroup= | xs:string optional | text, optional string, nullable |
| =VolatilityCurveId= | xs:string optional | same |
| =PriceQuoteMethod= | xs:string optional | same |
| =PriceQuoteBaseValue= | xs:string optional | same |
| =SubType= | xs:string optional | same |
| =PriceType= | =bondPriceType= {Clean, Dirty} | text code, checked, soft FK to =ores_trading_price_types_tbl=, following =bond_option= |

ORE carries all six on =bondData= (instruments.xsd: 392, 396, 400, 401,
403, 407) and on =bondReferenceDatum= (referencedata.xsd: 101, 104, 108,
109, 111, 112). No =ores.ore= code maps =BondReferenceData=, so only the
=bondData= path matters. Five are =xsd::string=; =PriceType= is the
exception: the reverse needs a local string-to-enum parse, since only
=to_string= is public.

No template needs a new capability: nullable text, a checked code and a
soft FK all have precedents in =bond_option=, and none of the six is a
decimal.

=Decision (D18): the notional's home.= =bond_notional= leaves =bond_issue=
and lands on =bond_instrument=, as an optional decimal. ORE holds
=BondNotional= on =bondData=, which is the trade's own bond data: which
security, how much, which side. The generic trade envelope carries every
product, so a bond-only amount belongs on the bond instrument row, next to
=issue_id=, not on =trade=. =bond_future.contract_notional= is the same
shape for the future.

Consumers to retarget with it, all measured:

| Where | What |
|-------+------|
| =bond_instrument_mapper.cpp=:1088-1089, :1142 | reads =bd.BondNotional= into the issue, writes it back |
| =ore_import_execute_handler.cpp=:965 | copies it into the bond issue request |
| =xml_bond_fact_mapper_roundtrip_tests.cpp=:1714, :1726-1727, :1738-1739 | the only test that pins it |
| =bond_instrument_data.hpp= | the hand-written container that holds the issue |
| =bond_issue.org=:222, :229-233, :246 | the column, its prose and the stale notional Checks prose |

The generated surfacing is mechanical (domain, protocol, entity, mapper,
service, history mapper, create SQL, ER diagram); the drop SQL, notify
trigger, json, table, event, handler, registrar and generator are stable.
The generated TypeScript twins are declared but absent, so nothing to
regenerate there.

** Unit 2: the legs' parents, inventoried and decided (2026-09-29)

The four leg tables -- =bond_leg=, =bond_leg_rate=, =bond_leg_amount=,
=bond_leg_amortization= -- key on =trade_id= today, with =leg_role=
separating the bond's own legs from =trs_funding=, =repo= and
=ascot_swap=.

ORE says the two are different parents. The bond's legs are
=bondData.LegData= inside =bondReferenceDatum=, the security; the funding,
repo and ascot legs are =FundingData=, =RepoData= and
=ReferenceSwapData= inside =trsData=, =repoData= and =ascotData=, the
trade's product. Our own container agrees: it holds those three as one leg
each, not as lists. A blind =trade_id= to =issue_id= swap would key two
BondTRS trades on one ISIN identically and merge their funding terms --
=service_bond_instrument_reader_tests.cpp=:647-691 already builds exactly
that collision.

=Decision (D19): split by parent.= The bond's leg family is keyed by
=issue_id=, the ORE reference datum id, and holds =leg_role= 'bond' only.
The three single product legs stay trade-keyed, with their roles narrowed
to the three they own. Each parent owns its rows and no table alternates
between two parents, which is the shape the collapse removed from the
instrument. The cost is that the leg, rate, amount and amortization shape
exists once per parent rather than once in total.

Two couplings the spec did not name, both measured:

- =instrument_schedule= holds the bond leg's schedules keyed by
  =trade_id= + =owner_role= (=bond_instrument_reader.cpp=:343-356), so the
  bond leg's schedules move with the leg or the round trip loses them.
- =bond_instrument.issue_id= has no foreign-key block, so nothing validates
  it today; the new leg foreign key will be the first to the issue table.
  The generic mechanism already supports a non-=id= target column, so no
  generator capability is needed.

Consumers to retarget: the reader's =read_family_rows= (:100-142) and the
four =read_leg*_by_trade_ids= (=parent_scoped_queries.cpp=:176-250), which
read many parents at once and now read two; the import handler's =save_leg=,
=save_leg_amounts=, rate and amortization writers (:411-605), one trade per
call, with the issue already written first; and
=repository_bond_leg_amount_repository_tests.cpp=, whose parent seed writes
a trade but no instrument or issue.

Early detection, in this order: a reader test holding two BondTRS trades on
one ISIN and asserting each container keeps its own funding leg; the bond
leg's schedules surviving the move; then the corpus round trip.

** Decision (D20): the bond leg's schedules get their own table
   (2026-09-29)

Unit 2's inventory recorded that the bond leg's schedules must move with
the leg or the round trip loses them. The existing =instrument_schedule=
keys on the trade: its primary key is =(tenant_id, trade_id, owner_role,
owner_number, schedule_role, sequence_number, valid_from, valid_to)=, and
a bitemporal primary key admits no null. An either/or parent would
therefore need a surrogate key and a check constraint, which is the shape
D19 rejected for the legs.

So the schedules split the way the legs did. =bond_issue_leg_schedule=
and =bond_issue_leg_schedule_date= key on the issue, drop =owner_role=
(the issue owns the row, so its role is always the bond) and rename
=owner_number= to =leg_number=. =instrument_schedule= keeps the
schedules of the trade's own owners -- the three product legs, the option
block and the return block -- and its =owner_role= check narrows to
=trs_funding=, =repo=, =ascot_swap=, =option= and =trs=.

The cost is the one D19 already accepted: the schedule shape exists once
per parent rather than once in total.

** Unit 2b delivered (2026-09-29)

The move. Every caller now reads and writes the security-keyed family.
The trade-keyed =leg_role= and =owner_role= checks narrow to
=trs_funding=, =repo= and =ascot_swap= (=option= and =trs= for the
schedule), and the bond role is gone from both, so no table alternates
between two parents.

The bond leg's schedules could not stay in =instrument_schedule=: its
primary key leads with =trade_id= and a bitemporal primary key admits no
null, so an either/or parent would need a surrogate key and a check --
the shape D19 rejected. Decision D20 gives them their own issue-keyed
pair, =bond_issue_leg_schedule= and =bond_issue_leg_schedule_date=.

The reader reads the issue's rows once per ISIN and every instrument of
that security copies them, so two BondTRS trades on one ISIN share the
security's legs instead of writing a copy each. One =build_leg= serves
both families, templated on the row set, with =leg_role_of= and
=owner_matches= supplying the two differences: the trade-keyed family
states its role and its owner on every row, the issue-keyed family's
tables already say whose rows these are.

The import handler writes the new family through =save_issue_leg=,
=save_issue_leg_amounts= and =save_issue_schedule=, with the issue
already written first.

Wiring the replacement exposed what unit 2a left unregistered: the new
entities had no NATS handlers, no event mapping and no history provider,
so nothing would have answered a put. All three sites are wired here.

Tests: the collision test holds two BondTRS trades on one ISIN and
asserts each keeps its own funding leg while both read the security's
two; the fixed and floating leg tests pin the schedules surviving the
move; the decimal repository test uses a product role, which is what its
table now holds.
