* Plan

The prototype extends the existing throwaway page, so nothing new has to run:
one file, no build step, no server, no database.

- A fourth variant, =d=, sits beside =a=, =b= and =c=. =b= stays as the
  accepted record of the still mesh; =d= is the argument about turning it.
- =PC.solid= projects the grid through a yawed, pitched camera, sorts the
  quads by their farthest corner and draws them back to front over a dashed
  grid. The projection fits itself to the plot box at every angle, and the axis
  labels ride the near edges, so a turn never loses an axis.
- The second market is EURUSD under SSVI: an ATM total variance per expiry, and
  =φ(θ)= from =η= 0.62 and =γ= 0.42 with a shared =ρ=. Nine absolute strikes,
  six expiries. The strike axis is the one a delta quote hides.
- Five instants move the level and the skew, so the surface twists through the
  day.
- The angle is state, not a reload: =?yaw=&pitch=, written back on release. A
  drag redraws the plot in place, so no turn costs a page load.

* Result

Done on 2026-11-12. Variant =d= draws a candidate surface as a solid the person
turns, with an FX SSVI market in strike space and five instants under it.

What it settled: the strike axis and the day's skew read better as a turned
object than as a still grid. That was the argument the variant was built to
test, and the page now carries it.

What it did not settle: whether the built screen keeps the 3D view. No person
has used it outside this page, and the argument for it is the page itself. The
question a screen has to answer is written in
[[id:C5564FE4-258E-4807-B3FD-DBBAB086EA67][what the prototype does not settle]].

Checked by headless Chrome renders at several angles and all five instants. A
rendered-DOM diff of the committed page against this one shows three changes
only: the fourth *View* button, the CSS the solid needs, and the new script.
Variants =a=, =b= and =c= draw the same surface content as before; the bottom
bar reflows to fit the fourth button, which is the one visible difference.
