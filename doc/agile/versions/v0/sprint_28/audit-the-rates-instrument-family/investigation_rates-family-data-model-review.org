A point-in-time record of a review of the rates instrument family against
[[id:BBD43BF2-9FEE-42B0-A667-9468AF759DC2][Data-Oriented Design in ORE Studio]],
Regime 2, the persisted domain data, and Regime 3, the wire projection.

The family is the third of the instrument families reviewed with this lens.
Bonds were the pilot and were reworked to the method; FX was never reworked and
its review asked whether it had ever been built to the rules. Rates sits
between the two: it was reworked to the instrument table rule across sprint 27,
so this review asks whether the tree the rework produced meets the guidelines,
which is a different question from both earlier reviews.

* Context

The family is one header, =rate_instruments=, nine product fact tables and
fifteen shared children, twenty-five entities over twenty-five tables and 189
business columns. Ten trade-type codes route to the header and each then names
one fact table: =Swap= and =CrossCurrencySwap= to =vanilla_swap_instruments=,
=ForwardRateAgreement= to =fra_instruments=, =CapFloor=, =Swaption=,
=FlexiSwap=, =BalanceGuaranteedSwap=, =CallableSwap=, =KnockOutSwap= and
=InflationSwap= each to their own. The header states the coverage check, the
common dates and the description, and owns the family's delete cascade; the
fact tables carry the product's own fields; the children carry the legs, leg
amounts, leg rates, strikes, schedules, the option block and the three
product-specific lists.

The review was commissioned to establish, with evidence, whether that model
meets the checklists, and to separate a defect from a recorded decision. The
sprint-27 design already recorded twenty findings (R1 to R20) and five
decisions (D1 to D5); this review treats those as context and asks what the
tree does now.

* Methodology

The family is twenty-five entity models under
=projects/ores.trading/modeling/=, their twenty-five tables and insert triggers
under =projects/ores.sql/create/trading/=, and the generated C++ in
=projects/ores.trading/api/= and =projects/ores.trading/core/=. The Regime 2
decision checks were walked per model; the invariant checks were spot-checked
once over the generated output. The ORE import and export seam was read in
=projects/ores.ore/=, because the *One-way numeric conversion* check is decided
there and nowhere else. The Regime 3 checks were walked over the carriers, the
batch and the protocols.

The work was split three ways — the model-to-generated-artefact census, the ORE
conversion seam, and the carrier, service and wire surfaces — and every claim
below was then re-checked in the main thread, and an independent verifier then
re-checked every citation. Thirteen claims and one finding did not survive that:
five claims in the main thread and eight from the verifier, plus the one
withdrawal. All fourteen are recorded in *Corrections made while checking*. No
build, test or database run: every claim is from source artefacts at tree
=3a49daea81=. That tree was the base when the review was written; the base moved
to =b4e641ed35= while it was in review, a nightly format run that touches

* Paths taken

- *The model and its generated artefacts.* For each of the twenty-five models
  the declared column, the generated SQL column, the generated domain member
  and the generated repository entity member were compared. This path found
  nothing: the three artefacts agree on all 189 columns. That is the review's
  first useful result — the codegen chain is not where this family's defects
  are.
- *The ORE boundary.* =swap_instrument_mapper.cpp= was read in full, in both
  directions, then the binding each call reads and the helper it calls. This is
  the path that found the two Criticals and the digit loss. It is also the path
  that changed the review's shape: the defects are in what the mapper does with
  the document, not in the schema that holds the result.
- *The carriers and the wire.* =instrument_batch=, =swap_instrument_data=,
  =trade_instrument=, =rates_fact_variant= and the import handler were read
  together. This path answered the question the review was asked to settle: the
  variant does not cross the wire, and what is wrong with the carrier is not its
  existence but the number of places that must agree about it.
- *Withdrawn: a Regime 3 finding on the variant.* The rule that a message
  carries no =std::variant= field is satisfied. See *What holds*.

* Conclusions

** What holds

| Check | Result |
|-------+--------|
| Identity by key | PASS with a caveat. Every one of the twenty-five tables is keyed on the trade. Twelve hold one row per trade — the header, the nine fact tables, and the two shared children that are single-row, the strikes and the option block — on =primary key (tenant_id, trade_id, valid_from, valid_to)=. The other thirteen add an ordinal and a role: =leg_number=, =sequence_number=, =rate_role=, or =(owner_role, owner_number, schedule_role)=. The relation is the key itself, never an owning-parent link, and there are no pointers anywhere. The caveat is finding 10; the two single-row children are finding 3. |
| Table-shaped children | PASS for the legs, the schedules and the option block. The legs are the headline: =swap_legs= holds the direction, the type, the day count, the business-day convention, the frequency, the index and the currency, and its economics are rows in =swap_leg_amounts= and =swap_leg_rates= keyed by =(trade, leg, role, sequence)=. A leg whose notional amortises or whose rate steps is rows, not a flattened scalar, which is what sprint 27's finding R4 asked for. The exception is finding 3. |
| Explicit variation | PASS. The family states its variation as per-type tables, not as an inheritance web: ten catalogue codes route to nine fact tables, the header's =trade_type_code= check is generated from the single catalogue, and the sets are disjoint. =InflationSwap= sitting on a rates table is a recorded transition (D1), not an oversight: the model's own prose says it is a member until an inflation family is scaffolded. |
| Exact money at rest | PASS with two caveats. All fifteen money columns resolve to =ores::utility::decimal::decimal= in the domain and =numeric(p, s)= in the table, with no case of "model decimal, domain double" and no money column stored as text. The sixteenth numeric-looking column, =base_cpi=, is an index level rather than a money quantity and is =double= in the model, the table and the domain; the three agree, so it is not a disagreement, though a document states such a level exactly and the type cannot keep its digits. The repository entity carries each money value as =std::string= and parses it with a decimal parse, not through a binary float, so no =double= enters the persisted domain. The caveats are the digit loss of finding 7 and the scale of the two notional columns, which are the family's only money columns at =numeric(28, 10)= while the other thirteen are at =numeric(38, 12)= (=trading_flexi_swap_lower_notionals_create.sql:48=, =trading_balance_guaranteed_swap_tranche_notionals_create.sql:44=). That is an inconsistency between siblings, not an exactness failure: the column and its domain type still agree. |
| Core/cold split | PARTIAL. =rate_instrument= and =swap_leg= set both =:domain_identity_group:= and =:domain_audit_group:=, so their scaffolding nests into =identity= and =audit= members. The other twenty-three models set neither. Finding 11. |
| Batched collection access | PASS at the repository and service API. Every rates repository declares the key-vector batch read and each is a single query; =trade_export_service= uses it, bucketing trade ids per routed table and reading each table once per page. The generated protocol handlers and the import path loop per key; that is component-wide (117 of 123 service files) and is recorded in finding 8 as a Regime 3 matter rather than a family one. |
| Value semantics | PASS. Across the twenty-five generated domain headers: 0 =shared_ptr=, 0 =unique_ptr=, 0 =weak_ptr=, 0 raw-pointer members, 0 =virtual=, 0 hand-written destructors and 0 =<memory>= includes. The hand-written carriers in =instrument.hpp= and =instrument_batch.hpp= are equally pointer-free. |
| Codec drift | PASS. Every generated JSON and table codec carries the AUTO-GENERATED banner; the =*_table.hpp= files are a single =convert_to_table= shell that holds no column types. The one stale artefact is a generated diagram, recorded in finding 13 with the family's other stale statements. |
| Flat value messages | PASS. =instrument_batch= is one typed array per concrete entity — 57 arrays, 17 of them rates-related, no variant and no nested aggregate. A fact row's type is static from the array it sits in and the array, not the element, carries the tag. |
| No polymorphic payload fields | PASS. No protocol header, JSON codec or table codec in the tree holds a =std::variant= field. =rates_fact_variant= is reachable only from =swap_instrument_data=, which is an in-process carrier: it is built inside the import handler from the plan and inside =rebuild_instrument= from the batch, and neither it nor =trade_instrument= is serialised. =trade_operations_protocol.hpp:163-169= states the rule and the reason. |
| Feed batching | PASS for the batch, and the batch is the family's wire. The complement — the ingress writes one request per row — is the shared handler's and is recorded as finding 8. |

** The enforcement gap

Five of these findings pass every gate the product has.

- =check_model_sql_types.py= passes the family, correctly: the model and the
  generated table agree on all 189 columns. Findings 1 to 4 and 9 are
  disagreements between the *model* and the *mapper*, and no check reads both.
- =check_money_decimal_columns.py= passes the family, and should: the money that
  reaches a column is exact. Finding 7 never reaches the check, because the loss
  is inside the boundary and the check reads the models.
- Finding 5 is a modelling decision that the store does not constrain, so
  nothing can fail: nine tables accept a row for one trade and no constraint
  objects. Finding 9 is the mirror image — a column that may hold nothing,
  which no constraint refuses either.
- The one check in the neighbourhood, =check_xml_family_coverage.py=, classifies
  whole document families as supported, migrating or unused. It cannot see a
  single uncovered column inside a supported family.
- Four of the criteria the review uses are not on the checklist at all, which is
  why they recur across families: *Field fidelity* asks whether every document
  field has a home and every column a producer, and the checklists test layout,
  identity, typing and batching but never that.

The gap, stated once: *nothing asserts that a model column has a producer and a
consumer at the ORE boundary*. Every defect this review found in the family's
fields is a violation of that sentence, and none can be caught without a check
that reads the mapper against the model.

** Corrections made while checking

- The conversion seam was first recorded as passing. The routed rates leaves are
  =xsd::base<float>= subclasses, whose parser stores the document's text, so
  =exact_decimal= resolves to the lexical overload at fourteen of its fifteen
  call sites, unlike FX where every leaf was a bare float. Reading the
  call sites alone then missed six conversions that never reach the helper, and
  the pass became finding 7 with a count.
- The empty cap/floor fact table was first recorded as a product with no fields
  of its own, which is what the shape suggests. The binding says otherwise: the
  product states a direction and a premium, and the fact table is empty because
  they have no home. That turned a tidiness note into finding 4.
- The variant was first recorded as a Regime 3 violation, on the letter of the
  rule against a polymorphic payload field. It is not: nothing serialises it,
  and the rule is about messages. The finding that survives is about the six
  places that must agree, which is a different defect with a different remedy.
- The recorded decision on the carrier was first read as settling the question.
  The history says otherwise: the variant was renamed, not removed, and the
  next task added an alternative to it.
- A floor becoming a cap was found on a second pass over the reverse mapper,
  after the strike finding was already written. The import half had been
  recorded as a flattening; the export half turns the same missing column into a
  change of product, which is what raised finding 3 from Important to Critical.
  The review's own lesson: a lost column is worth following in both directions
  before it is graded.
- A finding was withdrawn. The leg amount column refuses a negative amount and
  a cashflow leg's amount looked like a signed payment, which would have made the
  check a refusal of legitimate data. The evidence is not there: the mapper
  copies the document's amount verbatim, ORE carries a leg's direction in
  =Payer= rather than in the sign, and no document in the corpus states a
  negative amount. The observation stands as a risk to check if such a document
  ever arrives; it is not a finding.
- Eight claims were wrong after the two re-checks and one finding was
  withdrawn. An independent verifier found all of it by opening every citation
  in this page: the soft-reference count (19, not 13); the leg-direction
  finding's two citations and its claim that the import always supplies a
  direction; three =nats_call= citations that pointed at request construction
  rather than at the call; the task page's copy of the money-column count; the
  batch's array count (57, not 58); the amount column's sequence citation, off
  by eight lines; and the scored regime that contradicted its own checklist row.
  The lesson is the one this page applies to the family: a citation is evidence
  only once someone has opened it.

** Score

Regime 2, *2 of 5*, debt *High*. As with the bonds and FX reviews, the single
number hides a split worth stating. The *structure* is the best of the three
families reviewed: identity is the key throughout, the legs and their economics
are typed rows rather than flattened scalars, the variation is per-type tables
generated from one catalogue, the money is exact at rest, and every generated
invariant holds. The *boundary* is where the debt is: four of the nine fact
tables disagree with the document about what the product contains, one product
cannot be stored, and two are stored wrong — a short forward rate agreement as a
long, and a floor as a cap.

Regime 3, *5 of 5*, debt *Low*. The wire shape is right: one typed array per
concrete type, the tag on the container, no variant in any codec, and the batch
generated from the catalogue. The import's per-row request pattern is real debt,
and it belongs to the component rather than this family — one handler serves
every family — so finding 8 is recorded and does not lower the score. Recorded
findings are context here, as they are in the FX review.

The number follows the nearest anchor rather than the arithmetic. Three Critical
findings would suggest the bottom row, and that row is reserved for identity,
core and cold split, or a hot loop; none of the three is any of those. The row
above it names several Important findings, which the family also has. Two is
therefore the nearer anchor, and the debt follows the page's own band for a score
of 2.

Regime 1 does not apply: the family has no numerical kernel. The mappers are a
boundary translation and the repositories are query wrappers; neither is a hot
loop, and a review that demanded SoA here would have misread the regime.

* Recommendations

1. *Close the three Criticals first.* Two of them corrupt data rather than
   omitting it, and all three are contained: read the FRA's =LongShort= and write
   it back, give the inflation swap's index code a producer — most likely by
   reading it from the CPI leg, which is where the document states it, and
   retiring the column's separate home — and give the strike row the role and the
   ordinal it needs so a floor stays a floor. The first two need no schema change;
   the third needs two columns on the shared strike table.
2. *Decide what a fact table is for, then apply the answer to all nine.* Either
   a fact table carries the product's own fields — in which case
   =cap_floor_instruments= gains the direction and the premium, and its
   reverse mapper stops ignoring it — or it does not exist, in which case the
   two key-only tables are dropped and the header's code is the only tag. Do not
   keep two tables whose only content is a tag the container already states.
3. *Give the strike child a sequence and a role.* It is the one list in the
   family that still flattens, and it also loses which list it came from. The
   fix is the shape its siblings already have: =sequence_number= and a role
   column holding cap or floor, and every strike written rather than the first.
   Until then a floor is a cap after a round trip.
4. *Take the carrier to the credit shape.* Typed arrays with one tuple pair as
   their home, a =static_assert= that the pair lines up, and the append,
   rebuild and stamp paths derived from them. That retires the variant, collapses
   six hand-written lists into two, and lets the rebuild report a rates header
   with no fact row instead of falling through to another family.
5. *Close the boundary's digit loss in one change.* One helper for the sites
   that bypass =exact_decimal=, and the required-element casts first: a required
   element written through a float is the case that reaches a document.
6. *Batch the import writes* — the =put_many_*= requests exist and the handler
   does not use them. This is one handler, so it is also the cheapest fix on the
   list by ratio.
7. *Set the leg's direction for the two products that never do*, and decide
   whether an unset direction should be storable at all once it is (finding 9).
   The export's =value_or(false)= is the part that hides the problem.
8. *Close the gate.* A check that reads the ORE mapper against the model's
   columns would have found findings 1, 2, 3, 4 and 9 and, in the reverse
   direction, the five dead columns. Write it before the next family review, or
   the next family review will find the same class of defect by hand again.
9. *Decide the rest rather than fixing them*: the soft references and the
   doubled vocabularies (10, recorded scope), the flat scaffolding spine (11),
   the two homes for a premium (12), and the three stale statements plus the
   stale diagram (13). Each is a decision with a cost, not a defect to be
   swallowed whole, and the FX review closed with the same item for the same
   reason.
