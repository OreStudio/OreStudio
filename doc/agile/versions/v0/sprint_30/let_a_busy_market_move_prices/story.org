* Goal

A busy market moves prices, not only the clock. When the shared factor rises, a
series' returns grow wider, its tails fatten and its path can gap, and when it
falls the series quietens — so a panic pinned for a demonstration looks like a
panic and not like the same random walk sampled faster.

The factor is the one the arrival work already carries. This story decides what
of it reaches a price law, and settles one question the arrival work left open:
whether a higher tick rate changes how much time the price process covers.

* Acceptance


- A change in the tick rate changes how often a series is observed and not how
  much time its process covers: variance per wall-clock second is invariant under
  the rate factor, measured on a long run.
- A series' volatility follows the shared factor through its own loading, and the
  long-run sigma of a run is the sigma its configuration states unless a state is
  pinned.
- A pinned active market raises the sigma a series realizes, and calm lowers it,
  by the loading the feed carries.
- The factor reaches the tails: the kurtosis of returns rises with activity while
  the mean and the long-run sigma stay calibrated.
- The control plane is the one that exists: the same switch, the same tab, and
  one number per feed beside its sensitivity. No new verb and no new service.
- Each part states the test that proves it, and the note says which of the two
  meanings of volatility a test measures: the process's own sigma, or the sigma a
  client computes from the ticks published.
- On a GARCH feed the market loading and the GARCH multiplier combine by a stated rule. GJR-GARCH is today the only caller of =set_volatility_multiplier= and sets it on each step, so a second writer would be overwritten.

* Notes

The three pieces the note has to scope, in the order they should land:

1. *A rate change changes sampling, not time.* Today a process that takes one
   nominal step per tick gains variance per wall-clock second when the rate
   rises, because more steps fit in the second. A process with a time-scaled law
   does not, because it takes the gap. That inconsistency is a defect, and the
   volatility work would otherwise be built on it.
2. *A volatility loading on the same factor.* The pieces are small and already
   have their shape: a setter on the price process mirroring the arrival
   process's =set_baseline_multiplier=, a =volatility_sensitivity= beside
   =sensitivity= on the link and on both generation-config kinds, and one line in
   each feed. The cost is calibration, not plumbing.
3. *The tails.* An FX feed's law is a Gaussian mixture, so activity can reweight
   the wide components: a panic should fatten tails, not only speed a random
   walk.

Out of scope, and captured rather than scoped: activity and volatility as two
mutually exciting coordinates — a bivariate Hawkes whose one coordinate is
today's heat — with the 2x2 kernel and the spectral-radius stability condition
that replaces the rank-one test.

See [[id:AC70CC11-7819-419A-A317-E543DE837160][Carry a market-wide arrival regime]]
for the factor this builds on.
