* Summary

A =feed_binding= records that one party consumes one producer source. It is
the join between a producer and the consumers of its ticks, and it is the
same join for every asset class. The model is
=projects/ores.marketdata/modeling/ores.marketdata.feed_binding.org=, the
domain type is
=projects/ores.marketdata/api/include/ores.marketdata.api/domain/feed_binding.hpp=,
and the table is =ores_marketdata_feed_bindings_tbl=.

* Detail

** What a row records

The columns are a surrogate =id=, the =tenant_id=, the =party_id= of the
consumer, the =source_name= of the producer, a =producer_kind=, an =enabled=
flag, and the standard audit and version members. The natural key is the
party and the source name, and the table carries one check, that
=source_name= is not empty.

The =source_name= is the producer, unique within a tenant. It is the
=source= every tick that producer publishes carries, and it is the key of
the ingest loop's binding cache.

=producer_kind= references the refdata code table and defaults to =VENDOR=.
The insert trigger validates it with =ores_refdata_validate_producer_kind_fn=.

** The one place a tick finds its owners

[[id:00D63182-C7FF-4AC9-8A83-9A5D984B2876][The ingest loop and the one pipeline]]
reads the bindings through
=feed_binding_repository::read_latest_all_tenants=, which skips the tenant
filter and reads only the rows whose =valid_to= is the maximum timestamp.
It then keeps the enabled rows and groups them by =source_name= into its
=bindings_by_source_= cache.

A tick arrives on the producer subject. The loop looks the tick's =source=
up in that cache, plans the tick against every binding the source has, and
for each binding writes the observation under the binding's tenant and
party and republishes the tick on
=marketdata.v1.ops.market_tick.<tenant_id>.<party_id>.<ore_key>=, where the key is the
datum's canonical ORE key.

A tick whose source has no enabled binding is dropped with the
=unbound_source= reason, and the loop logs the drop once per source rather
than once per tick.

** The enabled flag is a suspend

Setting =enabled = false= removes the source from the loop's cache on the
next refresh and leaves the row in place. Nothing is deleted, so re-enabling
the binding restores ingestion without a new row.

** Rebinding is a source switch

Editing =source_name= points the binding at a different producer. The
producers themselves keep running, because the loop drives them and not the
reverse. The switch takes effect at the next refresh of the binding cache.

The refresh is not on a timer. The marketdata application subscribes to the
=feed_binding_event= stream, and a binding that is created, retargeted or
removed triggers =feed_ingest_loop::refresh()=. That is what makes a source
switch land without a service restart.

** The gap between a binding and the config that authored its source

A binding names only its =source_name=. Nothing on the row references the
=market_data_generation_config= or the feed config that produced that source,
and the collection entity itself is
[[id:49943F4F-D4E4-4A69-9A90-82AA1080818D][The generation config collection and folders]].
No foreign key joins them, and no column carries a config id.

The link back is name similarity. The column comment says the =source_name=
matches the =source_name= of the feed config the binding was created from,
and that sentence is the whole of the relationship.

The consequence is that a binding cannot show which config authored its
source. Two configs that share a source name are indistinguishable on the
binding row, and a config renamed without the matching binding edited leaves
a binding that names a source nothing is publishing. A reader who wants the
config behind a binding has to match names by hand.

This gap is recorded here rather than left for a reader to infer a
relationship the schema does not carry.

** Facts the code shape does not show

- The synthetic side creates its own binding when a bound feed starts, in
  =feed_controller::bind_feed= through =marketdata_binding_store::save_if_absent=.
  That side is [[id:39057B64-8F85-4882-9B66-D2D4FC7468E4][The feed controller]].
  The store writes =producer_kind = "SYNTHETIC"= rather than taking the
  entity default.
- =save_if_absent= reads before it writes, so a feed started, stopped and
  restarted keeps one binding row instead of gaining a second.
- An internal start with no end user session binds nothing, because it has
  no party to bind under. The feed runs and no binding appears.
- The model deliberately binds to no variability profile. The
  =uuid-surrogate-lookup= profile would also enable the shell command facet,
  and the binding model has no shell verb today.
- Bindings are created by provisioning against the system party's config,
  not per office. Each party consumes the shared stream into its own
  per-party series.
