#+title: A feed binding's existence claim is keyed on the ORE key alone
#+description: A feed binding's natural key is its party with its ORE key and its source, and the table's unique index says so. The service's must-not-exist claim reads the row by ORE key alone, so a second party cannot be bound to a source the first party already holds: the store refuses the write and the caller reads a conflict with no message. The key the claim is read by must be the entity's natural key.

This page is a [[id:671F18E4-E09C-4B3B-BD24-D33DF8AE38A6][capture]] in the *inbox* bucket of the product backlog — a pre-sprint idea, not yet pulled into a sprint as a story.

* What

Answer a feed binding's existence claim by the binding's natural key: its
party together with its ORE key and its source. A tenant holds one binding
per party for a given source, and the table's unique index states that
triple, so a claim that means "this party holds no binding for this source"
has to be read against the triple. Today it is read against the ORE key
alone.

* Why

Consistent-world market data gives every party its own bindings onto one
shared stream, so a tenant's parties hold bindings for the same ORE keys and
the same sources by design. A caller that creates them one party at a time
claims that the row it writes does not exist yet, and the second party's
claim is refused although that party holds no such row. The refusal is a
conflict, and the conflict carries no message, so the caller learns neither
which row collided nor which key judged it.

Two consequences follow. Provisioning binds the theme for whichever party is
reached first and fails for every party after it, and a caller that ignores
the response reports a bound tenant that is only partly bound. And every
caller has to compensate with its own read of the tenant's bindings and its
own skip of the party's rows, which leaves the store's guard decorative and
puts the real guard in each caller, where a new caller will omit it.

The fix is the key the claim is read by: the protocol's key for a binding
must carry the party and the source beside the ORE key, and the read that
answers the claim must follow the same triple the unique index states. The
entity model is the authority for the triple. Then a caller can claim
creation and have the store decide it, and the compensation reads disappear.

* Discarded

Out of date. A feed binding no longer has an ORE key: its natural key is the
party and the source, and the unique index states that pair. A write is claimed
by the row's id, so a second party's new row claims nothing that the first
party holds. A read by source keeps the session party's row, because another
party can hold the same source. Read from the code on the branch that made the
republish subject independent of the ORE key; not run against a provisioned tenant.
