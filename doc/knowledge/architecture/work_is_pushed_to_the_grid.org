#+title: Work is pushed to the grid and an idle grid costs nothing
#+description: A wrapper waits for work to reach it; the claim runs only when work exists, and an idle grid runs no queries.
#+type: knowledge
#+level: cross
#+filetags: :architecture:compute:nats:
#+created: 2026-10-26
#+updated: 2026-10-26

* Summary

Work reaches a wrapper; a wrapper does not poll for it. The server publishes a
notice when it adds work for a platform. An idle wrapper waits for the notice
and claims when it arrives, and the claim takes one job under a lease. A wrapper
also claims after a long timeout, measured in minutes, which covers a lost
notice. With no notice and no timeout it sends nothing. An idle grid sends no
claims and runs no queries.

* Detail

** The notice

The notice is a core NATS message on the platform's work subject. It holds no
state; it tells the wrappers of that platform that a claim can succeed. A lost
notice costs at most the long timeout.

** The claim

A claim takes the oldest runnable job of the platform with =FOR UPDATE SKIP
LOCKED=, leases it to the wrapper and mints its token, as
[[id:7D4D8598-555D-4E86-8006-3BC79E926382][How the compute grid queues and claims work]] describes. Several wrappers woken by one notice race; the lock decides
which one takes the job, and the others find none and wait again.

** After a job

A wrapper that finishes a job claims again at once, because work may be
waiting, and waits for a notice only when a claim finds nothing.
