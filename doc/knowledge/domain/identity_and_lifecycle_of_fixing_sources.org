* Summary

A *fixing source* names who produces an observation and how: the
administrator, the method, the time and time zone of publication, the holiday
calendar, the quote convention and the rounding. Market practice identifies a
source by its rate source definition, such as an ISDA settlement rate option
code, and treats the vendor screen that displays it as a mutable attribute.
A source definition can change over time, so the version in force on the
trade date matters. A source can also cease, as LIBOR did; a ceased source stays in
the record, and ORE Studio keeps some as simulated legacy sources for teaching.

* Detail

** What a source holds

| Attribute | Meaning |
|-----------+---------|
| Kind | Published by a feed, keyed by a person, or derived from other sources. See [[id:6A625509-CD9A-4D13-8E16-37B75A4AED83][Derived and composite fixing sources]]. |
| Asset | A currency for an interest rate source, a currency pair for an FX source. |
| Tenor | The tenor of a rate source, such as 3M; none for an FX source. |
| Quote convention | Mid, bid or offer, and whether the publisher quotes the pair inverted. |
| Publication time and time zone | For example 16:00 London for the WM/Reuters fix. |
| Holiday calendar | The days on which the source publishes. See [[id:53DB3AF1-6FA4-4B53-9D8E-A65F739F6F4D][Fixing dates and holiday calendars]]. |
| Rounding | The number of decimal places of the result. |
| Screen reference | The vendor page that displays it, such as a Reuters instrument code. |

A trade's fixing terms repeat what it needs of these: an FX confirmation states
the pair, the quote type, any inversion, the fixing time and zone and the
rounding; a floating swap leg states the source, the time and the zone, and an
FX source as well when a payment is in a non-deliverable currency.

** Identity: the rate source definition, not the screen

Annex A to the 1998 FX and Currency Option Definitions, published by ISDA and
EMTA, lists the /settlement rate options/: each has a name and a code, such as
=KRW KFTC18= or =KRW02= for the Korean won rate reported by Seoul Money
Brokerage Services by about 4pm Seoul time. The 2017 restatement defines each
option by the entity that quotes the rate rather than by the page that
publishes it, to remove the risk of a vendor changing a screen; the screen
references survive only as practitioners' notes. The definitions are deemed to
be incorporated as amended through the trade date unless the parties agree
otherwise. FpML carries the code in =settlementRateOption=. For interest rates
the 2006 ISDA Definitions play the same role through floating rate options.

Two consequences follow for a system. The code is the identity of the source,
and the screen reference is an attribute that may change. And the definition
is versioned, so a trade reads the definition that was in force when it was
done.

** Benchmarks and their administrators

A widely used fixing source is a /financial benchmark/, and its administrator
is subject to the IOSCO Principles for Financial Benchmarks, which require a
transparent methodology, controls on submissions and a record of how each
value was determined. The principles followed the LIBOR and FX benchmark
investigations; Duffie and Stein (2015) analyse why benchmarks based on
submissions invite manipulation and how transaction-based designs reduce it.

** A source can cease

A source has a lifecycle. Its administrator can stop publishing it, or declare
it no longer representative of its market. LIBOR is the leading case: following
the FCA's announcement of 5 March 2021, most LIBOR settings ceased after
31 December 2021 and the remaining US dollar settings after 30 June 2023.
Trades that referenced a ceased setting moved to fallback rates, under the ISDA
IBOR fallbacks for derivatives.

A ceased source remains part of the record. Its past observations still settle
the trades that fixed on them, and a historical valuation still reads them.
ORE Studio also keeps ceased sources such as LIBOR as /legacy/ sources for
teaching: their fixings are simulated, because no administrator publishes them
any more. A legacy source is therefore a source whose producer is simulated and
whose status says it has ceased, not a source that has been removed. See
[[id:2400E0FE-AEF0-4F9A-8392-F7D2E0E2A1E2][A data model for fixings]].

** In ORE Studio

ORE Studio names an FX fixing by its ORE index, =FX-SOURCE-CCY1-CCY2=, so the
source is part of the identity of the fixing series, as
[[id:F4BBEF34-D84C-4AE6-A3AA-DC47C3EE06EE][Public publishers publish daily fixings through a supplier session]]
explains. It does not yet hold the source's other attributes as data; see
[[id:2400E0FE-AEF0-4F9A-8392-F7D2E0E2A1E2][A data model for fixings]].
