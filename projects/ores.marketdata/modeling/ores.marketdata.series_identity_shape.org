#+title: ores.marketdata.series_identity_shape
#+description: The identity fields each series family requires and forbids, stated as data so the writer can refuse a row that misses one.
#+type: ores.codegen.entity
#+component: marketdata
#+filetags: :model:entity:marketdata:
#+entity_singular: series_identity_shape
#+entity_plural: series_identity_shapes
#+entity_title: Series Identity Shape
#+brief: One field of one series family's identity, and whether the family requires it or forbids it.
#+created: 2026-11-15
#+updated: 2026-11-15

The shape of a market series identity, one row per field per family.

A family is the pair of codes the taxonomy already carries -- the asset class
and the series subclass -- narrowed, for an index series, by the index family.
=index_fixing= is not one shape but several: an IBOR fixing states a currency
and an index name, a swap fixing a currency and a tenor, an FX fixing a currency
and the currency it is quoted in. A rule keyed on the subclass alone would be
wrong about all but one of them, so the key carries the index family too.

A row says what the family makes of one field: =required= means a row of this
family that leaves it empty is not an identity, =forbidden= means the field says
nothing about this family and a row that fills it states something untrue. A
field a family does not mention is neither: the family does not use it, and the
writer leaves it as the URI parse left it.

The table is read by the writer that projects an identity row from an oresmd
URI, which refuses a row that misses a required field or carries a forbidden
one, naming the family and the field. It sits beside
=series_classification_rules= because the two are the same kind of statement --
what a series of this identity is -- and because a family the taxonomy gains
gains its shape in the same place rather than in a second mechanism.

A family with no row here is not enforced: the writer projects its series
unguarded, and the backfill counts them as unstated. The catalogue grows family by
family, and refusing every family nobody has stated yet would stop an ingest for a
reason that is the catalogue's.

* Columns

** asset_class
:PROPERTIES:
:type:        text
:cpp_type:    std::string
:primary_key: true
:END:

The family's asset class, as the oresmd authority spells it and as the identity
row carries it: =ir=, =fx=, =credit=. This is the codec's spelling and not
=ores.refdata.asset_class_code='s -- refdata calls the same class
=interest_rates= -- so the column is checked against the oresmd schema rather than
against that catalogue, and a family keyed by one spelling and looked up by the
other would never match.

The same subclass of two classes is two families, so this column is half of the
key rather than a description of it.

#+begin_src cpp :name generator
std::string("ir")
#+end_src

** index_family
:PROPERTIES:
:type:        text
:cpp_type:    std::string
:primary_key: true
:default:     ''
:skip_empty_check: true
:END:

The index family the shape is for, as the identity's =index= column spells it:
=ibor=, =swap=, =fx=. A datum family has no index and holds the empty string.

=ir=/=index_fixing= is two shapes, not one: an IBOR fixing is named by its
index, a swap fixing by its tenor, and a row that stated one of them for both
would be wrong about one of them. So the key carries the family.

#+begin_src cpp :name generator
std::string("ibor")
#+end_src

** field
:PROPERTIES:
:type:        text
:cpp_type:    std::string
:primary_key: true
:END:

The identity column the row speaks about, named as =market_series_identity=
holds it: =ccy=, =unit_ccy=, =index=, =instrument_type=.

A name that is no column of that table is a statement nobody can act on, so
=build/scripts/check_marketdata_identity_columns.py= checks every seeded row
against the table's columns at build time.

std::string("ccy")
#+end_src
