* Goal

The approval chain gave a party and a desk one more field each, and the rule that
keeps C++ types small allows a listed type to shrink but never to grow: =party=
stands at 20 against a recorded 19, =party_entity= at 21 against 20,
=business_unit_entity= at 17 against 16, and =business_unit= crossed fifteen and
may not join the list at all. The flag belongs with the other facts of an
approval, so the fix is to give it a type of its own in the models and let
generation carry it, which returns all four to what their rows state.

* Plan

The plan is an analysis first, because the first two mechanisms this task tried
were both wrong about the codegen.

** What the four counts say

=compass-ng check --codec wide_types= at =0c91ae4d65= reports =party= 20 against
a row of 19, =party_entity= 21 against 20, =business_unit= 16 with no row, and
=business_unit_entity= 17 against 16. All four are exactly one field over, and
all four crossed on the same column: =restricts_product_types=, added to the
party by =01f0864d44= and to the desk by =21981d8ff8=. The four ceilings are
exactly the pre-flag counts, so the flag is the whole of the movement.

** What each layer can lose

| Layer | Its members | How it can shed one |
|-------+-------------+---------------------|
| =party=, =business_unit= (domain, and the wire) | own columns + five audit members | only by losing a member: a group needs either the narrow identity/audit flag or the all-or-nothing table form |
| =party_entity=, =business_unit_entity= (row struct) | one member per column, plus the validity window | only by losing a column: the template has no group support |
| =_codec=, =_protocol=, TS types, shell recipes, screens | generated from the domain | follow the domain, no separate decision |

** What the experiments showed

1. A =** Domain groups= table is all-or-nothing. Declaring it on =business_unit=
   with only the audit group is refused: =column business_centre_code belongs to
   no domain group=. So grouping the audit that way means decomposing the whole
   entity into identity and payload groups — a rename of every =party.= and
   =business_unit.= access in the tree, and a new wire shape for the two most
   used refdata types.
2. =:domain_audit_group:= in the =C++ *= Flags drawer is the narrow form and it
   works: it nests the four audit columns and the recorded instant as one =audit=
   member and leaves the rest flat. Generated from it, =party= is 16 and
   =business_unit= is 12 — both under, with room. This is the answer the
   architecture points at: the audit block is a reused value shape, and
   =convention= and all 48 trading instruments already nest it.
3. That narrow form costs the entity its generic history. Codegen stops emitting
   =party_history_provider_registrar= and =business_unit_history_provider_registrar=
   the moment the flag is on: all 48 models that carry the flag have the history
   field mapper and no provider, and the two files return the moment the flag is
   taken off. An explicit =| ores.cpp.history-provider-registrar | true |= row in
   the model's =* Physical space= table does not bring them back. So the cheap
   route deletes the party's and the desk's =history.v1.get= surface.
4. The row struct has no lever smaller than that flag: keying the template on
   =has_audit_columns= instead would rewrite 550 entity headers and their 550
   mappers across every component and every worktree.

** The options

| Option | What it costs | What it lands | Risk |
|--------+---------------+--------------+------|
| A. Fix the registrar gap in compass-ng, then group the audit on both models | two model lines, a row-struct template change (prototyped, parked in =stash@{0}=), a compass-ng release | all four under with room; no schema, no SQL | the party and desk wire shapes nest their audit, so every consumer learns =audit.*=; blocked on the compass repository |
| B. Move the restriction state off the party and desk rows into an approval row | 2 models, 2 tables, 19 references in 8 SQL files, 4 pgTAP files, the surfaces that carry the flag, the design page's wording | all four exactly on their rows; the party and the desk lose a member | the chain's functions read a row instead of a column; the book keeps its flag, so the chain is mixed until the approval-matrix story moves it too |
| C. Group only the row structs (template keyed on the audit columns) | 550 headers and 550 mappers regenerate repo-wide | the two row structs land; the two domain types stay one over | not sufficient alone, and it makes every other worktree's tree move |
| D. Record the four counts as exceptions in =.compass.org= | four rows with a reason, an approver and an expiry | nothing changes in code | accepts the growth the rule forbids in principle; the house mechanism does allow it, and 13 exceptions are in use |
| E. Amend the rule so a generated row mirror is not counted | a change to =compass/checks/wide_types.org= | 237 of the 596 rows leave the list | a policy change, not a clean-up; the owner has to want it |
| F. Stop this task and capture | a note | nothing | the four rows stay grown |

A and B are the only options that meet the acceptance. By the owner's own
criterion — the common path must not get more expensive for an edge case — B is
the honest one: the flag is the edge case, it is already on the common path
today, and every party read carries a boolean that only the approval resolution
reads. Nesting the audit (A) is the more general fix, but it changes what every
consumer of a party sees, which is a common-path cost of exactly the kind this
task is meant to avoid.

** What the owner decided, and why the plan stops here

The owner took none of A to F on 2026-11-28. The growth is allowed for now, and
the four types are decided with data by a story of their own,
[[id:CDD317B2-65AA-4133-8B0E-E52C4229BBBB][Review the wide types from their data]], because the same question is open for
the other 592 rows of the ceiling table and answering it one type at a time is
how this task came to exist.

The reasoning is worth keeping. Every option that lands the four counts reshapes
a common path to pay for an edge case: A nests the audit block in the party's
wire shape, C regenerates 550 entity headers across every worktree, and B moves
a boolean off the party so that every reader of the approval reads a row instead
of a column. None of them is wrong, but none should be chosen to satisfy a count
before the data says which fields belong together. The task's own premise — that
a party and a desk needed reshaping — turned out to be a question about every
type.

The plan's remaining steps therefore belong to the story: measure with data,
decide, lower the rows, run the approval pgTAP files and the refdata suites, and
record the counts before and after.

* Measurements=. It
shows that the task's premise was wrong in one place and incomplete in another.
Wrong, because a group member cannot bring a row struct back: the entity
template states one member per column by design, and =convention_entity= is
flat although its model declares three groups, so the group reaches the domain
type alone. Incomplete, because the four types were not four different
problems: each was exactly one field over its row, and all four crossed on the
same boolean.

So the saving had to be one the row struct could take, and the audit block is
the only one it holds that the model can state as a group: four of
=party_entity='s 21 members and four of =business_unit_entity='s 17. Making the
row struct group it takes =party= to 16, =party_entity= to 18, =business_unit=
to 12 and =business_unit_entity= to 14 — every one under its row, and
=business_unit= well clear of the fifteen it may not exceed.

The alternative was to move =restricts_product_types= out of both tables into a
table of its own, which also lands all four on their rows. It lost on cost and
on reach: it rewrites the product approval SQL, the junction triggers that
maintain the permitted sets, the publish path that restricts a book, the shell
recipes and both pgTAP files, inside a story another environment is working,
and it leaves the next wide entity exactly as stuck as before.

* Result

Closed on 2026-11-28 without a code change, by the owner's decision.

The task set out to bring four types back under the ceiling the approval chain
pushed them over. It measured them — each exactly one member over, all four
crossing on the same boolean — and then found that every mechanism the codegen
offers costs more than the count it fixes:

- a =** Domain groups= table requires every column of the entity to belong to a
  group, so grouping one block decomposes the whole type and renames every party
  and desk accessor in the tree;
- =:domain_audit_group:= nests only the audit, which lands all four counts with
  room, but the generator then stops emitting the entity's history-provider
  registrar, so the party and the desk lose their =history.v1.get= surface;
- teaching the row struct to group, keyed on anything but that flag, regenerates
  550 entity headers and 550 mappers across every component.

The owner allowed the growth instead, and gave the question to
[[id:CDD317B2-65AA-4133-8B0E-E52C4229BBBB][Review the wide types from their data]], because the same question is open for
every one of the 596 ceiling rows and the answer should come from the data
rather than from the type that happened to trip the check. The four counts are
already over on =origin/main=, so this branch changes nothing: no ceiling row
moves, no generated file changes, and the approval chain behaves as it did.

The analysis, the measurements and the prototype stay in this record. The
prototype is parked as =stash@{0}= so the story can revive it in minutes if the
data favours grouping the audit everywhere.
