This page documents a [[id:F44766FA-1E9C-416F-849A-E131101B2ECA][user journey]]: An auditor reads the trail of a published curve: its blueprint changes, its builds, its manual adjustments, its approvals and its publishes, each with an actor and a time.

A user journey describes what one person is trying to do, before anything is
built. It is a design document that outlives any sprint, so it names the
screens, the entity fields each screen shows, and the operations and messages
each step needs.

* Goal

"I want to be able to say who changed this published curve, what they changed,
and who approved it, so I can answer a challenge and show the trail." The
journey is done when the auditor holds the trail of one curve: every blueprint
change, every build, every manual adjustment and every publish, each with an
actor and a time, and can put one version beside the one before it.

* Actor and trigger

The actor is the auditor, or reviewer: a signed-in person inside their tenant
who holds a read permission on the market data entities and on the approval
records. No dedicated auditor role holds only those reads today; see /What is
missing/. The trigger is an internal or external challenge to a number a run
used, a periodic review of how curves are governed, or a publication the person
must attest. The journey starts at a market series, or from the evolution view,
and ends when the person leaves the trail.

The record is bitemporal: each row carries the transaction time of its versions
beside the financial time of the thing it describes. What the trail can show,
and what it cannot, follows from which entities keep those versions; see /What
is missing/.

* Steps

1. *Open the trail.* The person opens *Market data*, finds the series by its
   =oresmd_uri=, and chooses *Trail*. The system shows the series row: the URI,
   the subclass, the derivation kind, the derivation config and its version,
   and the provenance of the row, which is who modified it, who performed it,
   the change reason code and the change commentary.
2. *Read the blueprint changes.* The system lists every version of the series
   row, newest first, with the field-level difference from the version before:
   when the derivation config or its version changed, and who changed it and
   why.
3. *Read the builds.* The person opens the points. The system lists the series'
   observations by observation time, with each point's =oresmd_uri=, value,
   observation time and source. A build is many points written at one
   observation time under one source.
4. *Read the per-point provenance.* For a derived point the person reads its
   lineage: the derivation config and version, the source as-of, and the source
   series it read. Only a derived point has a lineage row; an observed point has
   none, and that absence is the observed marker.
5. *Read the manual adjustments.* The person asks which points a person wrote
   rather than a feed. The system shows the points whose source names no
   producer channel, beside the change reason on the record. No column marks a
   point as manual; see /What is missing/.
6. *Read the approvals.* The person opens the approval requests and their
   decisions for the object: who asked, when and why, and who decided what and
   when. The generic approval record exists; no market-data approval kind does;
   see /What is missing/.
7. *Read the publishes.* The system shows every publish of the curve: the
   version published, the person who published it, and the time. No publish
   operation exists, so this panel is empty by construction; see /What is
   missing/.
8. *Put one version against the one before.* The person selects a version and
   the system shows it beside the version before, with the field-level
   difference. This exists for the series row; it does not exist for a point;
   see /What is missing/.
9. *Leave.* The person goes back to the series or to the evolution view.

Every read is answered inside the reply. The trail carries no state of its own;
it is a reading of the versions the server already keeps.

* Screens and wireframes

One screen. The object and its provenance sit at the top, the blueprint
versions and their differences sit under it, the builds, the per-point
provenance and the manual adjustments sit in the middle, and the approvals and
publishes sit at the bottom, so the person reads the object, then what was
built, then who signed it off.

The series, the lineage and the binding are versioned, so each has a history
and an actor per version. The observation rows are not: they are the tick-level
table, and they carry no actor. The approval records are versioned and each
decision is a row of its own.

The history request is the generic one, addressed at the owning component's
subject, so the market data one is =marketdata.v1.history.get= with
=entity_type= =ores.marketdata.market_series=,
=ores.marketdata.observation_lineage=, =ores.marketdata.feed_binding= or
=ores.marketdata.series_classification_rule=. Those four register a history
provider. =ores.marketdata.market_observation= does not, because it has no
audit columns and a version must carry an actor as well as a time; see
/What is missing/. The lineage read is keyed by the lineage row's id, not by
the observation, so the screen needs the id before it can ask.

* What is missing

1. *Observations carry no actor.* =market_observation= has no =version=,
   =modified_by=, =performed_by=, =change_reason_code= or =change_commentary=,
   because tick-level volumes made them impractical, so the market data
   registrar withholds a history provider for it. The candidate is a provider
   over the observation rows, or an actor and a change reason carried on the
   write. Until then the screen shows each point's observation time and source,
   and it cannot say who wrote a point or when it changed.
2. *No publish operation and no publish event.* Nothing publishes a curve, so
   the trail holds no publish acts. The bootstrap's republish writes the output
   series' observations directly, and its own protocol says the review and
   approval gate is a separate, later layer. The candidate is a publish
   operation with a published version, and a publish event. Until then the
   publishes panel is empty and the screen says that nothing is published.
3. *No market-data approval kind.* The inbox holds one kind,
   =iam.role_grant=; no kind names a curve publish or a manual adjustment, so
   no approval request can be raised for this object. The candidate is a
   market-data approval kind with its decide permission, its approvals-required
   count and its comment rule, and the operation that applies an approval. Until
   then the approvals panel can show only requests of another kind, and none
   names this object.
4. *No candidate-against-published distinction.* No field on an observation
   says whether it is a candidate or a published value, so the trail cannot
   separate the two. The candidate is a published version or a published flag.
   Until then the screen reads the market's values and does not claim which of
   them were published.
5. *No field diff for a point, and no lineage read by observation.* The history
   read returns versions with their difference for the four entities that
   register a provider. It does not for =market_observation=, and
   =marketdata.v1.observation_lineages.get= takes the lineage row's id, not the
   series, observation time and URI the repository's own lookup accepts. The
   candidate is a lineage read keyed by the observation. Until then the screen
   reads a lineage row by id when it has one, and matches the rest by hand.
6. *The manual adjustments are not marked.* =market_observation= carries a
   free-text =source=, so a point a person wrote cannot be told from a point a
   feed wrote except by that string, and the write's change reason does not
   travel with the row because the entity keeps no version. The candidate is a
   manual marker, or a source convention the column validates. Until then the
   screen treats a source that names no producer channel as manual, and says the
   classification is a convention.
7. *No auditor role.* No role holds only the market data reads and the inbox
   reads this journey needs; a person reaches it through the read permissions
   they already hold. The candidate is an auditor role.
