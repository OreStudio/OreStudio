#+title: Linked cash
#+description: Cash that hangs off a parent trade: linked versus orphan cash, inclusion in the parent's mark-to-market until settlement, and drill-down from a balance to the trades behind it.
#+type: knowledge
#+level: cross
#+filetags: :knowledge:domain:finance:trading:cash:
#+created: 2026-10-02
#+updated: 2026-10-02

* Summary

Some cash explains a trade. A premium, a coupon, a rebate and a
close-out fee all fall due because a trade says they do. *Linked cash*
is the cash a trade explains. The rest — variation margin, CVA, sales
credits — is *orphan cash*: it stands on its own.

The distinction governs reporting. Linked cash is carried with its
parent trade, included in the trade's value until it settles, and
reported against the trade even after the trade has ended. Orphan cash
reports where it arises.

The link must also work in the other direction. A settled balance is
audited through the trades behind it, so a user can drill from a cash
balance down to trade level. A balance that cannot explain itself is an
orphan by accident.

* Detail

** A flow arrives because something created it

[[id:13341B12-0393-4ECF-B392-CD68A4C0CDFB][Cash Flow]] defines the
flow itself: a signed, dated, currency-denominated movement of money.
The first question about any flow is whether a trade explains it. The
answer splits cash into two families.

| Sub-type | Link to a trade |
|----------+-----------------|
| Coupon (payment) | Linked |
| Close-out fee, partial or complete | Linked |
| Rebate | Linked |
| Uncollateralised funding | Open |
| Variation margin | Orphan |
| CVA | Orphan |
| Wrong way risk | Orphan |
| Transfer pricing | Orphan |
| Sales credits | Orphan |
| Default claim | Orphan |
| Compensation (for example, novation payouts) | Orphan |
| Billing fee | Orphan |
| Agency commission | Orphan |

The list is a consequence of the rule, not the rule itself. Coupons,
fees and rebates are linked because a trade explains them; margin, CVA
and sales credits are orphan because no single trade does. A new
sub-type goes on the correct side by answering the same question.

** Carrying an unsettled flow

Until a linked flow settles, it belongs to its parent trade. The
trade's value carries the discounted amount of the flow, so a trade
that owes a coupon is worth less than one that has paid it. For
principal the same rule spans the trade's life: the initial principal
stays with the trade until the final principal settles.

This is where linked cash meets
[[id:886AAFB5-62F3-4AA3-B325-386665AFF926][Positions]]. Position
management adds up cash, discounts it, and gives an accurate view of
settled and future cash. The future half is the linked half: without
the link, an unsettled flow has no home in the valuation.

** After the parent trade ends

A flow can outlive its trade. The close-out fee settles after the
close-out; the final coupon settles after maturity; the rebate settles
after the trigger date. The cash keeps reporting against the parent
trade, and settlement and P&L reports read it there. Tearing the link
at expiry would strand the flow — this is why the link outlives the
trade, not just the booking.

** Drilling down

[[id:886AAFB5-62F3-4AA3-B325-386665AFF926][Positions]] states the
audit principle for positions: a position cannot be audited directly,
the deals beneath it are audited and the aggregation is trusted. The
same principle reaches cash. A settled balance must connect to the
trades that contributed to it, so that a user can move from a balance
to a monthly, then daily, then trade-level breakdown of what produced
it. Without the connective tissue the balance is unexplainable, and
unexplainable balances are a control finding.

The concept of the cash itself — notes and coins, and
currency-equivalent assets — is on
[[id:A281A64C-5A52-4747-BBDD-3CECB7A72F3C][Cash]]; an amount of cash
in a currency is a
[[id:940CB31A-BD50-45D6-98AC-4B0D53973825][Cash Position]].
