* Result

The nine inbox operations are reachable from the browser. The new
=wire-protocol/src/inbox.ts= maps the wire shapes to camelCase views and joins
the names the wire rows do not carry. The new =bff/src/inbox.ts= serves nine
routes; every one is gated on a session and nothing else, because which role
may decide a kind is data and the server re-checks it. The web layer adds the
nine =api= methods, "Your requests" and "Ask for a role" on My access, the
Requests queue and its detail screen, and a notification bell in the header
with the unread count, the list, and a link to what each entry is about.

Two data gaps shaped the screens. A member may not read another person's
request roles, so their own row falls back to the approval kind and the reason.
There is no single-request read, so the detail screen finds its row in the
queue.

=packages/web=: 332 tests pass (22 new), typecheck clean. =packages/bff=: 242
tests pass (19 new), typecheck clean. =packages/wire-protocol=: 140 tests pass.
The bundle builds.

Live, on a fresh database provisioned with ACME
(=evidence/inbox_api_walk.txt=): =ashley.moore=, holding Member and Viewer,
asked for Operations, ReportRun and Sales in turn through the BFF. The tenant
administrator's queue named the requested role on every row. Each decision was
approved and the role was granted. The member read the
=inbox.approval_decided= notifications.

In the browser (=evidence/inbox_member_access.png=,
=evidence/inbox_admin_queue.png=, =evidence/inbox_admin_detail.png=,
=evidence/inbox_after_give.png=, =evidence/inbox_bell.png=): My access drew
Your requests with a Waiting row and its Withdraw, then Given rows after each
answer. The Requests queue drew the requester, the role and the reason. The
detail screen drew the role's permission bundle and the answer panel. Clicking
Give emptied the queue, and the bell listed the decisions in words.
