* Summary

The WM/Reuters (WM/R) 4pm London fix is the most widely used FX benchmark. Its
administrator computes a rate for each currency from market data around 16:00
London time and publishes it shortly after. The Financial Stability Board
described it in 2014 as "by far the dominant benchmark" in FX, used also as an
input to multi-currency equity, bond and credit indices, alongside the
European Central Bank's euro reference rates, published at about 14:15 CET.
ORE Studio fixes its simulated FX cut sources on the WM/R family; see
[[id:5AF9CEEF-A878-443A-8C32-C6E9F6C26FC9][The fixing sources ORE Studio supports, and the cut each one serves]].

* Detail

** Method

Before 2015 the rate for traded currencies was the median of bid, offer and
trade data sampled every second over a one-minute window centred on the fix.
The FSB recommended a wider window, and the window was lengthened to five
minutes in 2015. Rates for currencies with less trading are taken from quotes
rather than trades.

** Why the fix attracts study

Large orders to trade at the fix concentrate in a few minutes, so the fix is
both a liquidity event and a target for manipulation. Melvin and Prins (2015)
show that the rebalancing of equity portfolios drives predictable flows at the
fix. Evans (2018) finds extraordinary volatility and negative serial
correlation in prices around the fix across 21 currencies, contrary to a model
of competitive trading. Evans, O'Neill, Rime and Saakvitne (2018) find that the
five-minute window improved price efficiency and robustness, at the cost of a
larger tracking error for users of the benchmark.

** Administration

WM was a subsidiary of State Street; the fix was later administered by
Refinitiv and then by LSEG. The benchmark is subject to the IOSCO principles
described in [[id:6E566C97-F2A4-4AE8-B509-BDE23568A0B8][A fixing source is identified by its rate source definition]].

** The ECB reference rates are a different index

The ECB euro reference rates are a separate benchmark with their own time and
method, published for information. A fixing of EUR/USD by the ECB and one by
WM/R are two indices with two values; neither may stand in for the other. In
ORE Studio the ECB rates arrive as observed fixings through
[[id:F4BBEF34-D84C-4AE6-A3AA-DC47C3EE06EE][the public sources adapters]].
