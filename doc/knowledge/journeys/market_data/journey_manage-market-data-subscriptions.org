This page documents a [[id:F44766FA-1E9C-416F-849A-E131101B2ECA][user journey]]: A party administrator decides which providers a party takes, with which rights, and which source serves each provider, sees the usage and cost, and reads who changed it.

A user journey describes what one person is trying to do, before anything is
built. It is a design document that outlives any sprint, so it names the
screens, the entity fields each screen shows, and the operations and messages
each step needs.

* Goal

"I want to say which providers of market data my party takes, to choose where
each provider's data comes from, and to see what that costs, without asking
someone to run a script." The journey is done when the person has a subscription
for each provider the party needs, each subscription has a route to a source,
and the person can read the usage and the bill of each.

* Actor and trigger

The actor is the party administrator. Reading needs
=marketdata::subscriptions:read=. Changing a subscription or a route needs
=marketdata::subscriptions:write=. Deleting needs =marketdata::subscriptions:delete=.
The system administrator has the same screens for any party, and the
[[id:986CED57-341F-49DE-8F5D-F186771F10D8][Keep the market data sources running]]
journey covers the system's view of the sources.

The trigger is one of four events. A party is new and has no market data. A desk
needs a provider's data. A development party must move to a live source for
prelive testing. A bill is higher than expected and the person looks for the
cause. The journey starts at *Market data*, then *Subscriptions*, and ends when
the person leaves the screen.

Three facts shape the screens.

- A subscription names a provider and never a source. A route picks the source.
  See [[id:31BCA3F8-31AD-49C2-9B6B-F2312EE7DECB][A party takes a provider's feeds through a subscription]] and [[id:37B27CA8-9F44-4A61-9B02-3EAABD8C3818][A party routes to exactly one source for each provider]].
- A source has a kind: live, replay, test, simulated or derived. The screens show
  it as a badge and a filter and change nothing else. See [[id:F64DB34E-084D-4437-B536-392AA26D8B77][No code path may behave differently because a source is simulated]].
- The party does not own any feed. Every provider's data is system data. The
  party takes it by subscription.

* Steps

The screens are the record screens of the [[id:2BF7F03F-6BF4-45FF-8E26-4D3B4E2D943D][Record screen standard]]: a list, a record page with the
tabs Details, Usage and History, and dialogs for add, edit and delete. This
journey adds no layout of its own.

1. *List the subscriptions.* The person opens *Market data* and then
   *Subscriptions*. The system lists the party's subscriptions: provider first,
   then rights, the routed source with its kind badge, the state of the source,
   whether the subscription is enabled, and when the source last ticked for the
   party.
2. *Search and filter.* The person searches by provider or source and filters by
   source kind. A search that matches nothing says so and offers to clear it.
3. *Open a subscription.* A click on a row opens the record page on its Details
   tab. The system shows the provider, the rights, the period, the route, the
   source's kind and state, and who last changed it, when and why.
4. *Add a subscription.* The person chooses *Add*. The dialog asks for the
   provider, chosen from the catalogue, the rights, and the period. The new
   subscription routes to the provider's default source. The system opens its page.
5. *Change the rights.* The person chooses *Edit*. A right that a composite or a
   demand needs cannot be removed, and the dialog names the user of the right.
6. *Change the source.* The person chooses *Change source* on the route. The
   dialog lists the provider's sources with kind badge and state, asks for an
   effective date and a reason, and says that the series and the consumers stay
   as they are. See [[id:FA280E9B-7E00-4747-8D29-42B3D4258873][A source switch changes one route and nothing else]]. A source that is not ready is not offered.
7. *Read the usage.* The person opens the *Usage* tab. The system shows demand,
   deliveries and the bill by period. For a simulated source the bill is a shadow
   bill, and the tab says so. See [[id:1B8A3DB7-006A-4E14-AC11-57FA3741222D][A rate card on the source turns usage into a bill the platform shares]].
8. *Suspend or delete.* The person clears *Enabled* to suspend delivery, or
   deletes the subscription. Delete names what is lost: delivery stops, and the
   series and the history stay.
9. *See who changed a subscription.* The person opens the *History* tab, the
   shared timeline of the
   [[id:BD492939-118E-4B55-AE5B-300F7A1C3B41][Entity screen specification]]. The
   route changes appear in the same timeline. *Revert* is offered on an older
   version to a person who may write.
10. *Leave.* The person goes back to the *Market data* area.

* Screens and wireframes

The list and the record page of the standard, and dialogs for add, edit and
change source. The wireframe and the prototype are still to be drawn.

* Entities composed

#+ATTR_HTML: :class hug-leading
| Entity | What it contributes |
|--------+---------------------|
| =market_data_subscription= | The party, the provider, the rights and the period |
| =market_data_route= | The source that serves the subscription, with its period |
| =market_data_source= | The source's name, kind, state and rate card |
| =provider= | The provider and its default source |
| =market_data_usage= | Demand, delivery and cost events |
