#+title: Synthetic generation of fixings
#+description: The simulator fixes each simulated source from its own market at the source's time and publishes durable fixing ticks.
#+type: knowledge
#+audience: user
#+level: cross
#+filetags: :architecture:market_data:synthetic:fixing:
#+created: 2026-11-22
#+updated: 2026-11-27

* Summary

A simulated fixing source is served by the simulator exactly as a public one is
served by its adapter. The source's administrator is a publisher in the
catalogue; its fixings are vendor instruments mapped to fixing items; the system
party routes to it, so its fixings are stored once under the system party for
every party to read. A /fixing generator/ fixes each item from the simulator's
own market at the source's time, publishes a durable fixing tick through the
adapter contract, and on start publishes a backfilled history. The simulator
never writes to the store. The fixing history the curves now write directly is
replaced by this path.

* Detail

** Why a publisher, not a side effect

A fixing is a different kind of datum from a quote: it has an administrator, a
time and a calendar, and it is stored durably. Public fixings already arrive
through an adapter as durable fixing ticks, and the ingest stores them in the
fixing series of their index; see
[[id:F4BBEF34-D84C-4AE6-A3AA-DC47C3EE06EE][Public publishers publish daily fixings through a supplier session]].
The simulator is an adapter too, and the rule that it never writes to the store
holds for it; see [[id:54C9E31E-26FF-4530-A868-0850FCE1FE30][The synthetic market data simulator: adapter and authoring tools]].
A simulated fixing source therefore takes the same path, and the only
difference from a public one is inside the adapter, as
[[id:5ABE694A-7F87-46A9-943E-B6734B3B6EF9][A live and a simulated source differ only inside the adapter]]
states for quotes.

The fixing history an IR curve feed now writes straight to the store, through
the synthetic service's fixing writer, breaks that rule and names some indices
differently from their public publishers. It is replaced.

** The catalogue

For each administrator of a simulated source, the system bundle installs:

- a publisher, of kind =reference=, such as =wmr= for LSEG's WM/R rates,
  =emmi= for EURIBOR or =iba= for the legacy LIBOR settings, with its status;
- a simulated source of that publisher, served by =ores.marketdata.synthetic=;
- a vendor instrument for each fixing, in the publisher's notation, and a
  mapping from it to the fixing item, such as
  =oresmd://fx/EUR?type=fixing&index=fx&source=WMR1000NYC&ccy=USD=.

The system party subscribes to each fixing publisher and routes to its source,
as it does for the public publishers, so the demand of the system party names
every fixing item; see [[id:083D4B54-549B-4D56-AE89-430A8A4277FF][Demand drives what an adapter publishes]].

LIBOR is the one publisher whose settings the market retired. The administrator
ceased to publish them between 2021 and 2023, and the teaching material still
uses them, so =iba= publishes them as if the settings continued. A reader of the
store therefore meets LIBOR fixings that no longer exist in the market. That is
the intent of the run and not stale data, and the publisher's own row states it.

** The fixing generator

A fixing generator is a new generator kind beside FX spot, IR curve and FX
volatility surface. Its configuration states:

| Field | Meaning |
|-------+---------|
| Fixing item | The fixing URI it produces |
| Source | The fixing source, with its label, time, zone, calendar and rounding |
| Input | What it observes: an FX spot item, two spot items for a cross, or the index rate of an IR curve config |
| History | How many business days it backfills on start, and the walk it uses |

On start, the generator backfills its history: it reads its input's level from
the run's market and walks back from it, by amount for a rate and by proportion
for a price, over the business days of the source's calendar. It publishes the
history oldest first, each value dated with its fixing date. Then, at each
fixing time on the simulator's clock, it reads its input from the run's market
board, rounds it as the source does, and publishes one fixing. The board is the
run's shared view of spots and curves; see
[[id:675E89FC-B5AA-4250-8B86-78062F0F22BC][Forwards for synthetic volatility surfaces]].

The ingest stores each fixing in the series of its item under the system party.
A value it already holds for that date is skipped, so a restart that backfills
again adds nothing.

** One producer per item

A fixing item has one producer at a time: the source to which the system party
routes the item's publisher, live or simulated. The route decides, as it does
for quotes; see [[id:37B27CA8-9F44-4A61-9B02-3EAABD8C3818][A party routes to exactly one source for each provider]].
An overnight rate with a public adapter is therefore never simulated, and a
cut source such as WM/R, which is licensed, is always simulated.

** A fixing the run computes

Three term indices are stated for the run from its own overnight curves: Term
SOFR at one month, IBR at three months and WIBOR at six. The index belongs to its
administrator — CME Group, Banco de la República and GPW Benchmark respectively —
so the fixing is stated under that administrator, as every other fixing is, and
the administrator's simulated source states it.

That the run computed the value rather than observed it is not a party and is not
recorded as one. It is the series' derivation: =derivation_kind= says
=IR_CURVE_BOOTSTRAP= and the series names the generation config and version that
produced it. The series is seeded with that derivation before any fixing lands,
so a reader never meets it as an observation, and a series the ingest creates
later for a party takes the derivation the item already carries. A screen
therefore says who owns the index and how the value was made, in two fields
rather than in one name.

The rule this replaces had a provider called =derived= state the value, which
said both that the run made it and, by omission, that no party owned the item.
The FX volatility quotes the run infers from stored fixings keep a source of
their own, because no party in the store owns those items; the standing that
would replace it is the subject of the producer beside the publisher.

** Consistency with the run

Because a fixing reads the same board the run's other generators write, the
fixing of EUR/USD at the New York cut equals the run's EUR/USD spot at that
instant, and an interest rate fixing equals the curve's index rate. A trade
that fixes on a generated source therefore settles on the market it was valued
on.

** What changes

| Today | After |
|-------+-------|
| The IR curve feed plans its index history; the synthetic service writes it straight to the store | A fixing generator per index publishes it through the adapter |
| No FX fixing is generated | A fixing generator per cut source and pair publishes it |
| Simulated and public fixings of one index can carry two names | The catalogue names each index once |
| The provider =derived= states the computed term indices | Each administrator states its own index, and the series says the run computed it |
| Generated trades name =TR20H= | Generated trades name a supported source |
