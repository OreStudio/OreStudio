A point-in-time record of a review of the bonds instrument family against
[[id:BBD43BF2-9FEE-42B0-A667-9468AF759DC2][Data-Oriented Design in ORE Studio]],
Regime 2, the persisted domain data. The family is the repository's pilot for
that method, reworked across sprints 25 to 27, so the question is not whether it
was designed this way but whether the tree still holds up.

* Context

Bonds were the pilot: the sprint 25 analysis found twelve defects (B1-B12), the
sprint 26 task finished the pilot, and sprint 27 moved the rates family's shared
children onto the shapes bonds built. The review was commissioned to confirm,
with evidence, that the family's persisted model meets the guidelines, and to
separate what is wrong from what was deliberately decided.

* Method

The family is 27 entity models under =projects/ores.trading/modeling/= and their
27 generated tables under =projects/ores.sql/create/trading/= (54 files counting
notify triggers), plus 25 =field_group= models that carry no table. The Regime 2
checklist was walked per entity model for the decision checks and once for the
invariant checks. Generated C++ was inspected for the invariant surface. No
build, test or database run: every claim is from source artefacts.

The work was split three ways — structural decision checks, the exact-number
census, and the invariant checks plus batched-collection access — and every
critical claim below was then re-checked in the main thread.

* What holds

The shape the pilot set out to build is there, and holds under every check it
was meant to satisfy.

| Check | Result |
|-------+--------|
| Table-shaped children | PASS. Every child is rows in its own keyed table: legs, amounts, rates, amortizations, schedules, schedule dates, call dates, conversion targets, option premiums, fees, prices and payment dates, strikes. No =json=, =jsonb=, array or delimited column in the 27 tables. |
| Explicit variation | PASS at the family level. =bond_instruments= is the header keyed by =trade_id= with the closed ten-code check, and one fact table per product that has facts of its own (=bond_forwards=, =bond_futures=, =bond_options=, =bond_repos=, =bond_trs=), each keyed by =trade_id=. The bond's terms live on =bond_issues=, keyed by =issue_id=, so an amendment to a term touches one row rather than every open instrument. This is finding B11 answered. |
| Core/cold split | PASS for the family. The cold annex is the audit group; =bond_instrument= carries =domain_audit_group=, and the flag sits on the family headers across the whole component (equity, fx, credit, commodity, rates, scripted), so the family follows the component's pattern rather than departing from it. That 24 of the component's 111 entity models set it is a component-wide rollout question, not a bond finding. |
| Exact money at rest | PASS as a type decision. Across all 317 columns of the 27 models there are **0** disagreements between model =type=, model =cpp_type=, generated SQL and generated C++. Money is =numeric(38, 12)= with =ores::utility::decimal::decimal=; **0** money columns are stored as a binary float. The only =double precision= column in the family is =bond_issue_conversion_target.conversion_ratio=, which is correctly continuous. |
| Value semantics | PASS. All 471 members of the 27 generated domain types are values; 0 pointer or reference members, 0 destructor declarations, each type carries its key columns and defaults =operator===. |
| No shared-ownership webs | PASS. =shared_ptr=, =unique_ptr= and =weak_ptr= do not appear in any of the 432 family files across api, core, mappers, repositories and services. |
| Codec drift | PASS. 162 of 162 family codec files carry the generated banner; the JSON codecs are uniformly generated and every marker resolves to a codegen template. No hand-written codec stands in for a generated one. |
| Repository batch access | PASS. 203 vector-returning read signatures across the 27 repository headers, 0 =std::function=, and per-entity batch reads by key vector. |

**Enforcement gap.** =check_money_decimal_columns.py= flags a money column that
is a float in SQL or =numeric= beside a =double= in C++, so it catches none of
findings 1 to 5: money read through a float, money typed =text=, or money at the
wrong scale all pass it. Both of its baselines are a single blank line, so the
gate currently asserts nothing about this family in either direction.

**Documentation.** Four files cite
=doc/knowledge/architecture/trading_bond_relational_model.org=, which does not
exist; =bond_instrument.org= is one of them and cites it as the authority for
the reshape. The sprint 26 pilot task already records this as owed work.
Separately, the sprint 25 design page's *proposed* model has drifted from what
was built — it gives the issue =currency=, =coupon_rate=, =coupon_frequency_code=,
=day_count_code=, =maturity_date= and =description=, none of which the built
table has, because those moved to the leg and its rate and amount children.
That is the implementation improving on the design, and the page should say so.

* Corrections made while checking

- The core/cold split was first recorded as failing in 26 of 27 models. It is
  not a family failure: =domain_audit_group= is an opt-in that the component
  applies to family headers, and every other family's header carries it. The
  bond family complies.
- =bond_issue.face_value= and the six-role =value= column read as defects until
  the models' own prose was found to state them as decisions. They stay as
  findings because nothing enforces the copies, but they are not oversights.
- The tightest missing reference-key case, =issue_id=, turns out to be enforced:
  the generated insert trigger validates it. The 24 unenforced soft FKs are
  reference-data codes, not the structural keys between the family's own tables.

* Score

Regime 2, **2 of 5**, debt **High**, and the single number hides a split worth
stating: the structural half of the checklist is a clean 5, and the debt is
almost entirely in the conversion seam. The shape is right — header, per-product
fact tables, issue-owned terms, table-shaped children, generated invariants
intact. What fails is that money crosses the import boundary through a binary
float in six places, plus one money column typed as text and two at a scale the
corpus exceeds.

* Recommendations

1. *Fix the six conversions first.* They are one root cause in one file and
   they are the only Critical findings. =exact_decimal()= already exists and is
   already used a few lines away; findings 2 and 3 additionally need the ORE
   binding changed from a bare =float=, which is the Large part.
2. *Close the gate.* Teach =check_money_decimal_columns.py= to flag a money
   column typed =text=, and seed the baselines. Until then the family has a
   check that cannot see its own defects, which is how findings 1 to 5 survived
   a completed pilot.
3. *Fix the cheap ones with them:* the =text= premium (4), the two
   =numeric(28,10)= columns (5), the schedule parent/child role mismatch (11),
   and the two nullable-but-non-optional indices (12).
4. *Decide the rest rather than fixing them:* the premium's two homes (9), the
   strike's three-way encoding (10), and the duplicated schedule shape (15).
5. *Take the service N+1 to the component, not the family* (6): it is 27 of 27
   services, so it is a codegen or service-base question.
6. *Replace the missing page reference* with the real home, and correct the
   sprint 25 design page's target model.
