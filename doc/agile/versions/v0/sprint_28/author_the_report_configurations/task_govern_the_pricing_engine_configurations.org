** Why

The first four prototypes treated a pricing engine configuration as something each report author builds, and offered a copy that rebinds one report. In practice a party holds few official configurations, one for each cycle, and model risk signs them off. That model makes a second unofficial configuration in the pricing of an official report the thing to prevent.

** Decisions

- A configuration has a status: approved, in review, draft or retired. The approved one for a cycle is the official one, and the screens mark it.
- An approved version never changes in place. *Edit* is *Propose a change*, and the version in force stays in force until the sign-off completes. A draft changes in place.
- A copy is a draft. It rebinds nothing, and no official report can bind a draft.
- A report needs a price for every product type in its portfolio. The coverage check follows the reports of a cycle, and a configuration that several reports bind must cover the union.
- A configuration made on the authoring screens is a draft, or a candidate for a cycle. A candidate is submitted only when its coverage is complete.
- The sign-off has its own journey and prototype. The proposer cannot sign off, even when they hold a role.
- The roles (quant team, market risk, model validation) are assumed until model risk confirms them.
- Not decided: one official configuration for each cycle as a rule or as a mark; making a proposal official needing a role of its own; the rebinding step of a candidate that replaces the official configuration.
