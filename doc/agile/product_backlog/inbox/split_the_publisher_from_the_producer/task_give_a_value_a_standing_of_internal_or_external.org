* Goal

A value states whether a party can ever read it. An internal value is consumed by
the generators of the synthetic market alone: no series, no entitlement, no bill.
An external value may reach a party, through its subscription or through the set
published for it.

* Acceptance

- A source states a standing, internal or external, and every series records the
  standing of the source that landed it.
- An internal source produces no series, no entitlement and no delivery count. A
  read that asks for one says it is internal rather than that it is missing.
- The latent node of the feed graph is the one internal value, and the graph read
  reports its standing.
- A test proves that an internal source lands no series and meters nothing.

* Plan

1. *Settle where the standing lives.* The recommendation is on the source, as a
   column of the feed, because an internal value has no series to carry it. A
   series repeats the standing of the source at land time, so a read of one needs
   no join.
2. *Add it.* Default external, so no existing source changes meaning. The
   simulator sets it from the node kind the graph already reads: =latent= is
   internal, and =source=, =upstream= and =view= are external.
3. *Teach the readers.* The series read, the feed graph read and the screen that
   shows a series.
4. *Migrate the latent surface.* Its feed becomes internal and keeps publishing
   nothing.
5. *Prove.* A test runs an internal source and asserts no series, no observation
   and no delivery count.

* Notes

- =internal= and =external= are the words the hub now defines. Availability was
  rejected: in an architecture document it already means uptime.
- Delivery was rejected for the act of making a value available, because in this
  platform it means settlement: =delivery_start= and =delivery_end= are series
  identity fields, and the platform speaks of non-deliverable currencies.
- The system party is a party. A feed only the system tenant watches is external,
  with one very privileged consumer, so the pair stays binary.
