This page is a [[id:671F18E4-E09C-4B3B-BD24-D33DF8AE38A6][capture]] in the *inbox* bucket of the product backlog — a pre-sprint idea, not yet pulled into a sprint as a story.

* What

Teach the junction models that service their own shell commands to name the generated handler, registrar and service, and delete the hand-written registrar that duplicates them.

* Why

The assets story found the gap the hard way. Its junction model set =ores.cpp.nats-handler.enabled=, =ores.cpp.nats-sub-registrar.enabled= and =ores.cpp.service.enabled= to nil while setting =ores.cpp.shell-command.enabled= to true. Codegen therefore emitted shell commands for =assets.v1.image_tags.*= and a protocol declaring eight per-operation subjects, but no service to answer them: all nine image_tags recipes failed with "no service is handling subject". Flipping the three flags to true made codegen emit =image_tag_handler.hpp=, =image_tag_registrar.{hpp,cpp}= and =image_tag_service.{hpp,cpp}=, and the service went from 19 to 27 subscriptions. All 29 recipes then answered.

=ores.iam.account_party_junction.org= carries the identical nil flags with its shell command enabled, and it works only because =ores.iam/core/src/messaging/registrar.cpp= hand-writes the handler for the same eight subjects. That hand-written registrar is now a duplicate of what codegen can produce.
