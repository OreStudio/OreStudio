* What

The branch that states each fixing under its administrator deleted the
=simulated-fixings= provider, its source, its subscription and its routes. Three
things of that publisher survive:

- =marketdata_vendor_fixing_instruments_populate.sql= still writes 82 vendor
  instruments in the degenerate =oresmd= notation under the deleted provider,
  a publisher no row holds;
- =tools/vendor_catalogue= still writes that block, so regenerating restores it;
- =simulated_fixing_notation= is still registered, and the instruments and
  mappings of the next task are read from those 82 rows, so the seed of the new
  publishers depends on the retired one.

The ids the new publishers get are also built by regular-expression surgery on
the item address rather than through the notation that is meant to own the
spelling, so nothing fails when the two disagree: the simulator simply offers
fewer fixings.

* Why

A seed that names a publisher the catalogue has retired is a lie a reader of the
catalogue can see. And an id spelling that no test ties to the notation it
claims to follow is a silent failure mode: the fixing stops being stated and no
check says so.

The fix is to write the fixing items as a list of their own, with the
administrator each belongs to, and to delete the =oresmd= block, the notation
and the tool's writer with it. A test then reads every seeded id through the
publisher's notation and fails when one does not read back.
