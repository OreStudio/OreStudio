#+title: The blotter lists trades
#+description: A package is a trade, not a second table. The blotter's row is a trade whose instrument may be a composite, and the legs are its components. This plan retires the structures deviation and gives the blotter a single, indexable row.
#+type: plan
#+filetags: :plan:trading:blotter:structures:
#+created: 2026-11-02
#+updated: 2026-11-02

* Why this exists

The blotter costs 54 seconds and then fails at ten thousand trades, and the
first three attempts to fix it all had the same shape: compute a row that the
model never writes down, then store the computation. A stored projection, a
rebuild job to repair it, and a nightly alarm to notice when it drifted — three
rents paid on a model that could not name its own row.

This plan stops paying them. It states what the domain documentation says a
row is, retires the one table that disagrees, and points the blotter at facts
that already exist.

* What the documents say

[[id:FC31FC9C-7A6E-4EFD-8A62-6DE4137F7160][Trade Structures and Deal Composition]]:

- a structure is one deal assembled from several parts, and each leg *is itself
  a trade with its own identifier*;
- *a structure references its legs by key; no parent owns a child*;
- *the structure's internal version is read from the legs, not stored beside
  them*;
- in this tree, =CompositeTrade= is "our untyped structure today", the blotter
  *"collapses structures to one header row with a disclosure triangle"*, and the
  target model is =composite_instruments= plus =composite_legs= joined by keys.

[[id:2D1B9342-FC33-49DF-8F23-5B2248968156][Composite Trade]]: ORE's =CompositeTrade=
*bundles several component trades into one position* — a container with a result
currency, a notional method and a component list.

* The decision

**A package is a trade.**

A structure is not a second kind of row. It is a trade whose instrument is a
composite, and its components are trades in their own right. So the blotter
lists *trades*, full stop: a standalone trade is a row, a composite trade is a
row, and its component trades are children of that row.

Three consequences follow, and they are the whole point:

1. **The row's columns are facts of a trade.** The book and the date are the
   composite's own booking; the state is its own state. Nothing is derived from
   the legs, so the rules that made the row an expression — "the date is the
   earliest leg's", "the status is the legs' when they agree", "the book is the
   legs' shared book, else the lowest portfolio" — have no work left to do.
2. **The order is an index.** A page is ordered by a column of a table, not by
   =coalesce(column::text, '')= over a union, so the keyset page becomes what it
   claims: a scan from the key, not a sort of the book.
3. **There is no second copy of a fact**, so there is no rebuild to run, nothing
   to drift, and nothing to reconcile.

* The deviation, and its size

Measured on the ACME demonstration, 2026-11-02:

| Table | Rows |
|-------+------|
| =ores_trading_structures_tbl= | 60 |
| =ores_trading_structure_members_tbl= | 115 |
| =ores_trading_composite_instruments_tbl= | *0* |
| =ores_trading_composite_legs_tbl= | *0* |

The target model exists and nothing writes it; the deviation is live. That is
the work: the assembly path writes =structures=, so retiring it is a migration
and not a rename.

* What changes

1. **The blotter reads trades.** =trading.v1.blotter.page= and
   =trading.v1.blotter.rail= — both already on main — are answered from
   =trades= joined to each trade's current booking and state, with the composite's
   components read only when a row is expanded.
2. **The assembly path writes composites.** Grouping legs into a package creates
   a composite trade with its own booking and state, and names its components in
   =composite_legs=. This is the trade event rule: a link that carries an
   economic effect is a trade event, not an annotation.
3. **=structures= becomes what it actually is, or goes.** If the grouping is a
   process run — a compression, an allocation — it is a *trade group* and is
   named and modelled as one. If it is a product, the composite replaces it. It
   does not stay in between.
4. **The summary view stops unioning.** Once every row is a trade, the view is a
   join of a trade to its current booking and state, and the blotter's two reads
   are index scans over it.

* Acceptance

- The blotter's page and rail read a single kind of row: a trade.
- A composite trade appears as one row, with its components listed beneath it,
  and its book, date and status are its own facts.
- No table holds a copy of a fact that lives elsewhere, and there is no rebuild
  job for the blotter.
- The first page of a hundred-thousand-trade book answers in the low tens of
  milliseconds, measured through the service.
- =structures= is either modelled as a trade group with its own words, or gone.

* Checks before the code moves

- **Does the blotter need a =CompositeTrade= to be booked as a trade at
  assembly?** Today the packages are structure rows with no booking; the
  composite model requires the package to have one. That is the migration's
  shape.
- **Which indexes exist for the page?** =ores_trading_trade_bookings_tbl= has
  =trade_bookings_book_idx= and =trade_bookings_id_uniq_idx=; what the order
  needs is a =trade_date= on the path the page scans, which is a small,
  additive change.
- **What does the ORE export read?** It reads legs, not containers — the
  documentation says the ledger and netting need the leaves — so the composite
  must not become the thing that is exported.

* Out of scope

- The projection table, its rebuild function and its nightly alarm: closed with
  [[id:44A58042-462F-46E2-93A5-F6936D38B411][the story]]'s third task.
- The screen's layout. It already draws a structure with a disclosure triangle;
  this plan gives it rows that are already the right shape.
- A general trade-group model, if the compression and allocation work needs one.
  It is named here so it is not smuggled into =structures=.
