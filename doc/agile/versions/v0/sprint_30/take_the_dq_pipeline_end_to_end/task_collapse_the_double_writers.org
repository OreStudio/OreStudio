* Acceptance

- No table is written by both a direct seed and a DQ publish.
- The surviving writer is the one that owns the data, and the reason is stated.
- A check fails when a table gains a second writer.
- The eleven system-only tables are classified, not left undecided.

* Plan

The reader question is settled, in two steps and in this order.

1. Give the world catalogue the shared-reference read: the select policy that
   admits the system tenant's rows, and the repository read that names them.
2. Publish no world bundle into a tenant. The seed profiles name the tenant's
   own bundles, the GLEIF datasets that write a tenant's rows move to
   =lei_hierarchy= and =lei_counterparties=, and no dataset is a member of two
   bundles that differ in level or in scope.
3. Take the fifteen two-writer tables, one at a time, and keep the writer the
   level assigns: the DQ publish for the world catalogue, the populate script
   for a fixed platform vocabulary.
4. Classify the eleven tables that have no tenant route.
5. Add the check that fails when a table gains a second writer.

* Notes

Defect D5 and D14. This is the task that makes the seeding model stop being two models.

The reader question this task was waiting on is settled: the publication-level
page's shared-reference read is in the tree, and the vocabularies are read from
the system tenant. What the review of the tenant bootstrap found is that the
bundle-level half of the same defect remains, and it is now the first thing this
task has to do:

- Every seed profile names the =world= bundle, so the demo tenant publishes the
  world catalogue into itself. A provisioned demo tenant therefore holds 61
  calendars and 170 currencies of its own beside the system tenant's, which is
  the second copy the page forbids. The profiles should name only the bundles
  the tenant owns, and the datasets that write a tenant's rows must move to a
  tenant-level bundle first: the GLEIF counterparties and the counterparty
  aliases write a tenant's parties from a world record, so the page classifies
  them as tenant datasets while the bundle map still lists them under =world=.
- Thirty-two datasets are a member of more than one bundle, and the page states
  that a dataset is a member of one bundle and no other. Twenty-four of them are
  the =crypto= bundle re-listing world members; the rest are worth looking at
  one by one.

Both bullets are closed in the tree. The world catalogue carries the
shared-reference read, so a tenant reads a calendar and a currency where they
lie; the seed profiles name =tenant_defaults= and =lei_counterparties= and no
world bundle; and the crypto bundle keeps only the slice it alone carries.

What the page says about a shared row and what the tree now does agree, with one
correction the work forced. The page said a dataset is a member of one bundle and
no other, which the tree contradicts in three places that are not defects: two
bundles of one level and one scope may carry the same dataset, because the target
and the parameters are the same either way. The rule the page now states is that
a dataset's bundles share its level and its scope, and the instrument conventions
remain the one dataset the map doubles, because the system tenant publishes the
system party's copy and =party_essentials= publishes each other party's.

Two further properties of the world catalogue are worth stating where the next
person will look for them:

- The validator functions that check a code against a catalogue already admitted
  the system tenant for the world datasets the platform published first, and the
  rest now do too: =:tenant_scope: both= rather than =tenant=.
- A tenant may still write a row of its own into a shared catalogue, and a row
  of its own shadows the shared one in every read. The page forbids the write
  path; the permissions do not remove it yet, and the same is true of the
  vocabularies that took the read before this task. It is recorded as the
  capture [[id:34172C9C-FC58-475C-BD18-7AF4782B163F][A tenant can write into a
  shared catalogue]].

See [[id:16920F25-F0EF-4031-9C2D-DF57B5865072][Investigation: publishing pipeline]] for the evidence behind every claim here.

* Result

The first half is in the tree and proven. The world catalogue carries the
shared-reference read, no profile publishes a world bundle into a tenant, and a
check fails when a table gains a second writer.

- 33 entities take the two flags a shared table needs, and the images policy is
  written by hand. The validate functions that check a code against a catalogue
  admit the system tenant's rows.
- A provisioned tenant holds 0 calendars and 0 currencies of its own and reads
  the system tenant's 61 and 170. A pgTAP suite asserts the six catalogues a
  tenant no longer copies, the reads, and that it can neither move nor close a
  shared row.
- Five seed profiles name =tenant_defaults= and =lei_counterparties= and no
  world bundle. =lei_counterparties= is new; the GLEIF counterparties and the
  aliases left the world catalogue for it, and the GLEIF legal entities moved to
  =lei_hierarchy=.
- The crypto bundle keeps only the slice only it carries, and the datasets the
  platform already published left the world catalogue rather than being listed
  twice.
- The eleven tables the investigation left with no tenant route are classified:
  they are platform vocabularies, so every tenant reads them where they lie and
  the ten whose repository filtered them out now find them.
- =check_single_writer.py= states the rule and runs in CI. It finds 18 tables
  written both ways, three of them legitimately, and fifteen as debt.

What the second half needs, found while measuring:

- Eight of the fifteen live in the world catalogue, and deleting their populate
  inserts is not mechanical: =refdata_currency_calendars_seed_populate.sql= and
  =refdata_currency_countries_seed_populate.sql= *derive* their artefact rows
  from the live tables at populate time. With the live inserts gone, the
  derivation reads empty tables and stages nothing. They must derive from the
  artefact tables instead.
- The two copies had already drifted: the live insert names USD -> US.FOMC,
  which the dataset does not carry. Collapsing the writers has to add that row
  to the dataset, or the collapse loses it.
- Three of the eighteen are outside the refdata inventory the investigation
  took: the market data series and their asset classes, and the IAM account
  roles. Both writers reach the system tenant, and the derived-feed work owns
  the collapse.
