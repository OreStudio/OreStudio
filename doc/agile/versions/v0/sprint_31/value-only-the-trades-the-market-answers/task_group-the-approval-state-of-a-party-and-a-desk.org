* Goal

The approval chain gave a party and a desk one more field each, and the rule that
keeps C++ types small allows a listed type to shrink but never to grow. The flag
put four types one member over their recorded ceilings, and this task asked
whether that flag belongs in a type of its own. It measured the four, tested the
mechanisms the codegen offers, and closed on the owner's decision to allow the
growth and decide the wide types from data.

* Acceptance

Re-cut three times, and the third cut is the one that held. The first two assumed
a mechanism the codegen does not offer at a price worth paying; the third records
the owner's decision of 2026-11-28 instead of a code outcome.

- The four counts are measured and recorded, with the command and the commit, and
  the fact that they are already over on =origin/main= is recorded too, so no
  reader mistakes this branch for the cause.
- The mechanisms are analysed and their costs are written down, including the two
  the codegen makes expensive and the defect that makes the cheapest shape
  costly.
- The decision is recorded where the work will happen: the growth is allowed for
  now, and the four types are decided with data by
  [[id:CDD317B2-65AA-4133-8B0E-E52C4229BBBB][Review the wide types from their data]].
- Nothing in the tree moves: no ceiling row rises, no generated file, header or
  SQL statement changes, and the approval chain's behaviour is untouched.

* Plan

The plan is an analysis first, because the mechanisms this task tried in turn
were each wrong about the codegen.

** What the four counts say

=compass-ng check --codec wide_types= at =0c91ae4d65= reports =party= 20 against
a row of 19, =party_entity= 21 against 20, =business_unit= 16 with no row, and
=business_unit_entity= 17 against 16. All four are exactly one field over, and
all four crossed on the same column: =restricts_product_types=, added to the
party by =01f0864d44= and to the desk by =21981d8ff8=. The four ceilings are
exactly the pre-flag counts, so the flag is the whole of the movement.

** What each layer can lose

| Layer | Its members | How it can shed one |
|-------+-------------+---------------------|
| =party=, =business_unit= (domain, and the wire) | own columns + five audit members | only by losing a member: a group needs either the narrow identity/audit flag or the all-or-nothing table form |
| =party_entity=, =business_unit_entity= (row struct) | one member per column, plus the validity window | only by losing a column: the template has no group support |
| =_codec=, =_protocol=, TS types, shell recipes, screens | generated from the domain | follow the domain, no separate decision |

** What the experiments showed

1. A =Domain groups= table is all-or-nothing. Declaring it on =business_unit=
   with only the audit group is refused, and the refusal names the first column
   that belongs to no group: =column business_centre_code belongs to no domain
   group=. So grouping the audit that way means decomposing the whole entity into
   identity and payload groups: a rename of every =party.= and =business_unit.=
   access in the tree, and a new wire shape for the two most used refdata types.
2. =:domain_audit_group:= in the =C++= flags drawer is the narrow form and it
   works: it nests the four audit columns and the recorded instant as one =audit=
   member and leaves the rest flat. Generated from it, =party= is 16 and
   =business_unit= is 12, both under their rows with room. This is the answer the
   architecture points at, because the audit block is a reused value shape:
   =convention= and every trading instrument that carries the flag already nest
   it.
3. That narrow form costs the entity its generic history. Codegen stops emitting
   =party_history_provider_registrar= and
   =business_unit_history_provider_registrar= the moment the flag is on: of the 46
   models that carry it, none has a history-provider registrar, and the two files
   return as soon as the flag is taken off. An explicit
   =ores.cpp.history-provider-registrar= row set to =true= in the model's
   =Physical space= table does not bring them back. The cheap route therefore
   deletes the party's and the desk's =history.v1.get= surface.
4. The row struct has no lever smaller than that flag: keying the template on
   =has_audit_columns= instead would rewrite 597 entity headers and their
   generated mappers across every component and every worktree.

** The options

| Option | What it costs | What it lands | Risk |
|--------+---------------+--------------+------|
| A. Fix the registrar gap in compass-ng, then group the audit on both models | two model lines, a row-struct template change (committed on branch =feature/group-the-change-audit=), a compass-ng release | all four under with room; no schema, no SQL | the party and desk wire shapes nest their audit, so every consumer learns =audit.*=; blocked on the compass repository |
| B. Move the restriction state off the party and desk rows into an approval row | 2 models, 2 tables, 8 references in the approval functions and the two column definitions, the 3 product-type pgTAP files, the surfaces that carry the flag, the design page's wording | all four exactly on their rows; the party and the desk lose a member | the chain's functions read a row instead of a column; the book keeps its flag, so the chain is mixed until the approval-matrix story moves it too |
| C. Group only the row structs (template keyed on the audit columns) | 597 headers and their mappers regenerate repo-wide | the two row structs land; the two domain types stay one over | not sufficient alone, and it makes every other worktree's tree move |
| D. Record the four counts as exceptions in =.compass.org= | four rows with a reason, an approver and an expiry | nothing changes in code | accepts the growth the rule forbids in principle; the house mechanism does allow it, and 13 exceptions are in use |
| E. Amend the rule so a generated row mirror is not counted | a change to =compass/checks/wide_types.org= | 237 of the 596 rows leave the list | a policy change, not a clean-up; the owner has to want it |
| F. Stop this task and capture | a note | nothing | the four rows stay grown |

A and B are the only options that meet the original acceptance. By the owner's
own criterion — the common path must not get more expensive for an edge case — B
is the honest one: the flag is the edge case, it is already on the common path
today, and every party read carries a boolean that only the approval resolution
reads. Nesting the audit (A) is the more general fix, but it changes what every
consumer of a party sees.

** What the owner decided, and why the plan stops here

The owner took none of A to F on 2026-11-28. The growth is allowed for now, and
the four types are decided with data by a story of their own,
[[id:CDD317B2-65AA-4133-8B0E-E52C4229BBBB][Review the wide types from their data]], because the same question is open for
the other 592 rows of the ceiling table and answering it one type at a time is
how this task came to exist.

Every option that lands the four counts reshapes a common path to pay for an edge
case. That is not a reason to reject any one of them, but it is a reason not to
choose one to satisfy a count before the data says which fields belong together.
The task's premise — that a party and a desk needed reshaping — turned out to be a
question about every type, so the plan's remaining steps went with the story:
measure with data, decide, lower the rows, run the approval pgTAP files and the
refdata suites, and record the counts before and after.

* Notes

Two mechanisms this task tried and dropped, recorded so the next reader does not
try them again: a =Domain groups= table requires every column of the entity to
belong to a group, and a =domain_audit_group= flag nests the audit alone but
suppresses the entity's history-provider registrar.

The codegen prototype of option A — =change_audit= in =ores.database=, the entity
template and mapper changes, the flag on the two models — is committed on branch
=feature/group-the-change-audit= rather than left in a stash, so the story can
revive it from any worktree. The four counts it generates are =party= 16,
=party_entity= 18, =business_unit= 12 and =business_unit_entity= 14, read from
the generated headers rather than from the check.

* Result

Closed on 2026-11-28 without a code change, by the owner's decision.

The task set out to bring four types back under the ceiling the approval chain
pushed them over. It measured them, found each exactly one member over and all
four crossing on the same boolean, and then found that every mechanism the
codegen offers costs more than the count it fixes. The costs are in =Plan=.

The owner allowed the growth instead, and gave the question to
[[id:CDD317B2-65AA-4133-8B0E-E52C4229BBBB][Review the wide types from their data]], because the same question is open for
every one of the 596 ceiling rows and the answer should come from the data rather
than from the type that happened to trip the check. The four counts are already
over on =origin/main=, so this branch changes nothing: no ceiling row moves, no
generated file changes, and the approval chain behaves as it did.

The analysis, the measurements and the prototype survive this task: the numbers
are in =Measurements= with the commands that produced them, and the code is on
=feature/group-the-change-audit=.
