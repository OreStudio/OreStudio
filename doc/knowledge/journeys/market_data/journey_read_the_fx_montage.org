This page documents a [[id:F44766FA-1E9C-416F-849A-E131101B2ECA][user journey]]: A trader reads one base currency's crosses at an as-of - each pair as a row, each provider as a column, with the change, the day's high and low, and the pair's day.

A user journey describes what one person is trying to do, before anything is
built. It is a design document that outlives any sprint, so it names the
screens, the entity fields each screen shows, and the operations and messages
each step needs.

* Goal

"I want to see one base currency across its crosses at this as-of: who quotes
what, where they disagree, and how far each pair has moved today." The journey
is done when the person has the wall of quotes and has opened one pair's day.

* Actor and trigger

The actor is the trader, the FX salesperson or the risk manager. The trigger is
a currency that moved, a cross to price, a quote to check against a broker, or a
pair a colleague asked about. The journey starts at *Market data / FX montage*
and ends when the person leaves the page.

The journey reads and changes nothing. It reads spot observations; a forward is
a different read, with its own page and journey.

* Steps

1. *Open the montage.* The person opens *Market data / FX montage*. The system
   opens the base currency the address states, or the last one read.
2. *Choose the base.* The person picks =AUD=. The system answers every cross of
   that base the party can see, including the pairs where AUD is on the quote
   side, because a montage is for the base and not for the pair's spelling.
3. *Read what the numbers mean.* The system states the base, the instrument, the
   quote type, the pair count, the source count, the as-of, and that the
   deployment quotes mid only.
4. *Read a pair.* One row per pair: the pair name, one column per provider that
   quotes it, the consensus, the age and the day's shape. A provider that does
   not quote the pair is a gap, not a zero.
5. *Read the move.* The system states the change, the day's high and the day's
   low, and the instant they are measured from. A client does not decide what
   "today" means: the trading session does, and the server states it.
6. *Open one pair.* The person opens a pair. The system draws the pair's day
   over the stated window and marks the high and the low on it. No new read is
   needed: the row and the chart are one answer.
7. *Meet an empty answer.* No provider quotes a cross of the base. The system
   says so, and does not carry yesterday's price as if it were today's.
8. *Leave.* The address holds the base, the provider set, the as-of and the
   pair that is open.

* Screens and wireframes

One read-only page: the facts row, the controls, the montage table, and the
chosen pair's day below it.

| Step | Shape on the screen | Judgement |

* Operations and messages

#+ATTR_HTML: :class hug-leading
| Step | Operation | Subject | Status |
|------+-----------+---------+--------|
| Choose the base | the instrument catalogue, to find the pairs | =marketdata.v1.ops.list_instruments= | missing |
| Read a pair | the montage: spot per provider, consensus, change, high, low | =marketdata.v1.ops.get_fx_montage= | missing |
| Open one pair | one series' ticks over the window | =marketdata.v1.ops.get_series_ticks= | exists |
| Read a forward | the published forward set of a pair | =marketdata.v1.ops.get_published_fx_forward= | exists |

The montage is not built from =get_series_ticks= per pair: that is one call per
pair per provider, and the change, the high and the low would then be computed
in each client. The operation states them once.

* What is missing

- =marketdata.v1.ops.get_fx_montage= — one answer for a base currency: the pairs,
  the spot each provider quotes, the consensus, the change, the day's high and
  low, and the instant the change is measured from. The prototype shows the
  instant as a fact on the page precisely because a client must not choose it.
- The window. The server states whether the change and the range are measured
  from the trading session's open or from midnight UTC, and states the instant,
  so a person can verify what "today" means.
- The staleness verdict per quote, as a server decision.
