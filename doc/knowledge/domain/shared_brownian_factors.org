#+title: Shared Brownian factors give every synthetic series a signed correlation
#+description: The design of the latent market: each series' Gaussian is the increment of a driver built from shared Brownian factors in simulated time and the series' own noise, so any two series correlate by the inner product of their loadings.
#+type: knowledge
#+level: cross
#+filetags: :market_data:synthetic:correlation:stochastic_processes:knowledge:
#+created: 2026-11-12
#+updated: 2026-11-12

* Summary

Each synthetic series draws the Gaussian that drives its process from a
/latent market/. The latent market holds a small number $K$ of independent
Brownian motions, the /factors/, in the run's simulated time. A series' driver
is a fixed mix of the factors and of its own Brownian motion, with weights whose
squares sum to one. So the driver is itself a standard Brownian motion, and two
drivers correlate by the inner product of their factor weights, signed, over
any interval and whatever the sampling times. The process keeps its law: the
mixture still picks its component, GARCH still sets the variance and the tail
tilt still shapes the shock. Only the Gaussian it scales is shared. This page
states the design and its proofs; the comparison of options that chose it is
recorded in
[[id:8D5466B2-74B5-4C0D-B6DC-528CD466A47F][Investigation: How the synthetic market is generated jointly]].

* Detail

** What the design must meet

| #  | Requirement | Why |
|----+-------------+-----|
| R1 | Any two series correlate by a stated, signed amount | a surface's skew against spot; rates against FX; what Phase 5 prices |
| R2 | Exact under irregular sampling | series tick at their own times, by Poisson or Hawkes, at their own rates |
| R3 | Composes with the regime, the heat, the speed, the volatility channel and the tail tilt | they exist, and a person drives them from the Market regime tab |
| R4 | A series' path does not depend on which other series exist or when they tick | adding a feed, or a burst on another feed, must not move this feed's prices |
| R5 | Cost linear in the series | tens of thousands of feeds on one loop |
| R6 | A feed stays a feed | the latent market emits noise, not prices, so a vendor can replace a publisher |
| R7 | Generic across asset classes | short-rate factors, SSVI and SABR state, equity, commodity, credit, inflation, scenarios |
| R8 | Measurable | a test states a correlation and measures it under irregular sampling |

A joint draw on a common grid meets R1 and fails R2, R4 and R5: the series share
no grid, a burst on one would force a draw for all, and the set of series is the
dimension of the draw. Correlating through the regime alone gives co-movement of
magnitude, not sign, and fails R1. The shared factors below meet all eight,
within the two bounds stated under Determinism.

** From the joint mixture to continuous time

Kienitz fits a joint Gaussian mixture to daily change vectors, one component a
regime with its own mean and covariance
([[id:84E7CEA8-9654-431C-A8D1-C8B97E35B6D5][Paper summary: Gaussian GenAI]]).
The design keeps that structure and moves it into continuous time. The
components become the states of the regime chain $S(t)$; each component's
covariance becomes a low-rank set of loadings $b_{ik}(s)$; and the vector draw
becomes the increments of Brownian factors read at each series' own ticks. Fitting
the mixture becomes calibrating the loadings.

** Time

The scheduler advances one operational time $\tau$, and the run's speed $v$
maps it to simulated market time $t = v\tau$
([[id:F135B9AF-902F-46C5-847C-0820D8C92D28][One scheduler draws every feed's gaps from its arrival law]]).
The factors live in $t$. So every feed, whatever its arrival law, reads one
noise in one time base. That shared time base is what the three options of the
approach note's OD-1 lacked
([[id:BB2B7C4C-FD4E-4DCA-BF1D-00F929622B87][Synthetic market data generation: approach]]).

** The driver of a series

Let $F_1, \dots, F_K$ be independent standard Brownian motions and, for each
series $i$, let $E_i$ be a standard Brownian motion independent of every factor
and of every other $E_j$. Let $S(t)$ be the exogenous regime state of
[[id:7A6E2C55-3F1B-4E8D-9C7A-2B5E8D4F1A63][Market Arrival Regime]]. The driver of
series $i$ is the stochastic integral

\[
W_i(t) = \sum_{k=1}^{K} \int_0^t b_{ik}(S(s))\, dF_k(s) + \int_0^t d_i(S(s))\, dE_i(s),
\qquad
\sum_{k=1}^{K} b_{ik}(s)^2 + d_i(s)^2 = 1 \text{ for every state } s.
\]

The integral form matters when the loadings depend on the regime. The form
$\sum_k b_{ik}(S(t)) F_k(t)$ is not a Brownian motion: at a switch it jumps by
$\sum_k (b_{ik}(s') - b_{ik}(s)) F_k(t)$, a term that grows with the time the
run has lived. With constant loadings the two forms agree.

*Each driver is a standard Brownian motion.* $W_i$ is a continuous local
martingale with quadratic variation
$\langle W_i \rangle_t = \int_0^t (\sum_k b_{ik}^2 + d_i^2)\, ds = t$, so by
Lévy's characterisation it is a standard Brownian motion (Karatzas and Shreve,
1991, Theorem 3.3.16). A process that read a standard normal before reads one
now.

*Two drivers correlate by their loadings.* For $i \ne j$ the cross variation is
$d\langle W_i, W_j \rangle_t = \mathbf{b}_i(S(t)) \cdot \mathbf{b}_j(S(t))\, dt$,
because the idiosyncratic motions are independent. The instantaneous
correlation of the two drivers is $\mathbf{b}_i \cdot \mathbf{b}_j$, signed.

** Increments under irregular sampling

Let series $i$ tick at the ends of the interval $I_i$ and series $j$ at the ends
of $I_j$, with the regime constant on both. The increments are jointly Gaussian
with

\[
\operatorname{Cov}(\Delta W_i, \Delta W_j) = (\mathbf{b}_i \cdot \mathbf{b}_j)\, |I_i \cap I_j|,
\qquad
\operatorname{Corr}(\Delta W_i, \Delta W_j) = (\mathbf{b}_i \cdot \mathbf{b}_j)\,
\frac{|I_i \cap I_j|}{\sqrt{|I_i|\,|I_j|}}.
\]

The covariance is exact for any two intervals. The correlation of the two
increments equals $\mathbf{b}_i \cdot \mathbf{b}_j$ only when the intervals
coincide; otherwise it is smaller in magnitude, by the overlap ratio. This is
the Epps effect, and it is a property of the measurement, not of the noise:
the noise carries the stated correlation exactly, and an estimator that sums
over overlapping intervals recovers it
([[id:92644AAC-BE5F-43B6-96E4-084226CDA094][Tick-to-tick returns understate a correlation that holds exactly]]).

When series $i$ ticks at $T$ after its last tick at $t_i$, the latent market
returns

\[
Z_i = \frac{W_i(T) - W_i(t_i)}{\sqrt{T - t_i}}.
\]

$Z_i$ is standard normal given the tick times, and independent of every earlier
$Z_i$, under one condition: the tick times are independent of the factors and of
$E_i$. That holds while arrival laws read the regime, the heat and their own
draws but no price. An arrival law that reads a price would make a tick time a
stopping time of the noise, and $Z_i$ would no longer be Gaussian in general. So
no arrival law may read a price while it is driven by this noise.

With the volatility channel's subordination
([[id:9D778F72-8FF6-4F01-AF4B-696DC7BA855A][The market's volatility channel]]), the
noise runs in business time $\beta(t) = \int_0^t \lambda(u)\, du$ instead of
$t$, and $T - t_i$ in the denominator becomes $\beta(T) - \beta(t_i)$.

** Regime changes

With per-regime loadings, an increment over an interval that spans a switch is
the sum of the increments of its pieces, each under the loadings of its regime.
The switch times are those of the regime chain, which the scheduler holds and
which is exogenous, so the sum is exact. The latent market records the factor
values at each switch, which is all the pieces need.

** Hierarchy and blocks

The factors are of two kinds. /Global/ factors are shared across asset classes.
/Block/ factors are scoped to a group of series that must correlate beyond what
the global factors carry, such as a currency pair's spot and its volatility
surface. The model stays low rank: $K$ is the number of global factors plus the
block factors.

A block member loads on its anchor's /whole/ driver. A surface state variable
with correlation $\rho$ to spot has the driver
$\rho\, W_{\text{spot}} + \sqrt{1 - \rho^2}\, G$, where $G$ is built from the
block's own factors. Its correlation with spot is then exactly $\rho$, whatever
spot's global loadings are. In factor terms the anchor's idiosyncratic motion is
promoted to a block factor. The block's internal correlations, such as
neighbouring forward variances, come from the lower Cholesky factor of the
block's correlation matrix with the anchor first. That matrix is the one the
synthetic FX surface engine already builds from its three stated correlations
([[id:BB2B7C4C-FD4E-4DCA-BF1D-00F929622B87][Synthetic market data generation: approach]],
"Correlation with spot"). A set of numbers that is not a correlation matrix is
refused, never projected, because a projected value is not the one a person
stated.

** Determinism

A factor's value at a time is a pure function of the run seed, the factor and
the time; it does not depend on which series asked or when
([[id:14C79EA2-4E36-4EF2-A0DB-0EB932655029][A keyed Brownian path is a pure function of seed and time]]).
So a series' path is invariant to the presence, absence and tick times of any
other series. A series' own $E_i$ is drawn from the series' own price stream, so
it depends only on its own ticks, as its noise does today.

Two things bound that invariance. The keyed path is exact to the resolution of
its bridge, not below it. And the heat of the arrival regime is endogenous:
every event of every series feeds it. A volatility loading that reads the heat
makes the noise depend on all events, so a run with the heat in the loading is
reproducible by seed but not invariant to adding a feed. That is contagion doing
what it is for; a test states which of the two properties it checks.

** A series with no loadings

A series with every loading zero has $d_i = 1$ and its driver is its own
$E_i$. The latent market draws $E_i$ from the series' existing price stream, in
the order and number the process draws today. So the driven step takes one
standard normal in and scales it as before, and a series with no loadings
reproduces today's path for the same seed bit for bit. That identity is the
migration test: nothing changes until loadings are set.

** Cost

A tick reads $K$ factors at the current time, each through at most $D$ levels
of the bridge, so it costs $O(KD)$ and does not depend on the number of series
$N$. The latent market keeps each series' driver value at its last tick, so it
never reads a factor in the past. Memory is the loadings, $O(NK)$, plus one
value a series. A run's total cost is linear in its ticks, which generalises the
rank-one result of the arrival regime from one factor to $K$.

** What the correlation reaches

The loadings fix the correlation of the /drivers/. A published return is the
driver's Gaussian transformed by its process. Two effects separate the two
numbers.

- *Scaling.* If $r_i = \sigma_i Z_i$ and $r_j = \sigma_j Z_j$, where the scales
  are set by past information and by draws independent of the current
  Gaussians (GARCH variance, the mixture component), then
  $\operatorname{Corr}(r_i, r_j) = \rho_{ij}\, E[\sigma_i \sigma_j] / \sqrt{E[\sigma_i^2]\,E[\sigma_j^2]}$.
  The sign is kept, and the magnitude is at most $|\rho_{ij}|$ by the
  Cauchy-Schwarz inequality.
- *Non-linear maps.* The tail tilt maps $Z$ through a monotone function. For a
  bivariate normal pair, no pair of functions has a correlation larger in
  magnitude than $|\rho|$ (Lancaster, 1957), so the tilt can only lower the
  magnitude.

So the stated number is an upper bound on the correlation of published returns,
reached when the scales move together and the tilt is off. A test of the
design measures the drivers; a test of a feed's published correlation states
the bound.

** Measures

The drivers are noise under the real-world measure $P$. Spot and curves are $P$
quantities. The SSVI state of a volatility surface is a $Q$ parameterisation
driven by $P$ noise, as the approach note accepts
([[id:A3F7C2E1-84B9-4D56-9E2A-1F6D05B38C47][Probability measures: P (real-world) and Q (risk-neutral)]]).
Each emitted surface is free of static arbitrage because the generator keeps its
SSVI parameters inside the bounds of Gatheral and Jacquier
([[id:3C43D0CC-3E74-45CA-BC85-A2303D0ADEDC][Paper summary: arbitrage-free SVI volatility surfaces]]).
Nothing in the noise excludes dynamic arbitrage between one day's surface and
the next.

The generator holds static arbitrage itself, so the guarantee does not depend
on what a pricing engine later does with the surface. The mixture-preserving
interpolation of van den Berg
([[id:EC220F01-1844-4EED-A61D-BCF9F01EE00C][Paper summary: mixture-preserving, arbitrage-free vol-surface interpolation]])
is studied here but not part of this design.

** Scope

The first implementation holds block factors only: an FX pair's spot and its
surface. Static loadings come first; per-regime loadings follow. A surface's
strikes are solved against the forward $F(t, T) = S(t)\, P_f(t, T) / P_d(t, T)$
built from the spot and the two discount curves last published.

** Later work

- *Global factors and calibration.* Global factors link asset classes: FX spot
  with rate differentials, equity indices with credit spreads, commodity curves
  with inflation. Their loadings per regime are fitted from public fixings, by EM
  on regime-segmented mixtures as Kienitz fits his, and by principal components
  of regime-segmented daily changes. This is Phase 5 of the approach note.
- *Derived correlation quotes.* With global loadings in place, the generator
  emits =CORRELATION/RATE= quotes from $\mathbf{b}_i \cdot \mathbf{b}_j$ for
  basket and hybrid products.
- *Regime loadings and stress.* Loadings that rise toward one in the active
  regime model correlations that converge in a panic. Factor jumps placed on the
  cell grid give reproducible stress scenarios.
- *Heavier tails.* Kienitz's stretched Gaussian and Student-t mixtures (Kienitz,
  2026b) would change a component's marginal, not the shared noise.
- *Dynamic arbitrage.* For multi-year XVA paths, the $P$ increments would need
  martingale-corrected $Q$ drifts, for example an arbitrage-free dynamic SSVI.

** Open questions

1. *The level of the smile's curvature.* The design gives the curvature its
   shocks, not its level. In the generator the level is the curvature of the
   fitted surface the run starts from, to which its process reverts. Spot
   history cannot identify it: curvature is a $Q$ quantity, the market's price
   of tail risk, and a $P$ series gives at most its real-world counterpart. Two
   sources are possible: the spot model's own return distribution at each
   expiry, whose mixture implies a convex smile, plus a stated risk premium; or
   contributed or vendor option quotes. Kienitz (2026a) generates whole surfaces
   from a mixture fitted to surface history, which needs that history.
2. *Calibration data for surfaces.* Public fixings exist for rates and spot. FX
   option quotes are not public, so the loadings of a surface's state variables
   cannot be fitted from public data alone.
3. *Bridge depth at scale.* The cost of depth 30 a tick at tens of thousands of
   feeds is unmeasured.
