This page documents a [[id:F44766FA-1E9C-416F-849A-E131101B2ECA][user journey]]: A trader builds or refreshes a candidate volatility surface from the day's market, reads its calibration error and its slices, and leaves it unpublished.

A user journey describes what one person is trying to do, before anything is
built. It is a design document that outlives any sprint, so it names the
screens, the entity fields each screen shows, and the operations and messages
each step needs.

* Goal

"I want today's quotes turned into a candidate surface I can price from, and I
want to see how well that surface fits before anyone signs it off." The journey
is done when a candidate surface exists for the chosen as-of, the person has
read its calibration error and its slices, and the surface is still a
candidate: nothing has been published, so pricing still reads the surface it
read before.

* Actor and trigger

The actor is the trader, acting as maker: the person who holds the market-data
management role for this party. The trader owns the surface and is the only
person who builds or refreshes it; an approver owns the decision to publish,
in a different journey. The trigger is the start of a trading day, a market
move, a fresh quote set, or a request from pricing. The journey starts at the
list of curves and surfaces and ends when the trader leaves the surface
unpublished.

A surface is a *derived* object. It is a candidate until it is approved and
published, and only the published object is visible to pricing and reporting
([[id:186B44DD-51EA-45AA-971E-0C2D4D21A6DD][Publication of derived market data sets]]).

* Steps

1. *Find the surface.* The person opens *Curves and surfaces* and filters to
   surfaces. The system lists the party's blueprints, one row per pair and
   smile model, with the output series each writes to and the instant it was
   last calibrated. The blueprint is the surface's recipe and its name; the
   series holds the points.
2. *Choose the surface and an as-of.* The person selects a blueprint and an
   as-of timestamp. The system reads the blueprint's output series and the
   quotes at or before that instant. The calibration takes the as-of as a
   field of its request, so the instant the person chooses is the one the
   quotes are read at.
3. *Read the market it is built from.* The system lists the quotes the surface
   is built from, one row per =oresmd_uri=: the value, the =source= tag that
   published it, and its =observation_datetime=. An observation is evidence the
   market sent; nobody signs it off.
4. *Confirm the parameterisation.* The person reads the blueprint: the smile
   model, the interpolation between expiries, the output expiries and strikes,
   and the pair's convention with its ATM type, delta type and switch tenor.
   The recipe lives on the blueprint, so the screen reads it there rather than
   inferring it from a series.
5. *Fit the quotes to the parameterisation.* The person chooses *Calibrate
   candidate*. The service checks the recipe, reads the quotes the pillars name
   and the party's published forwards, fits the smile model and writes the
   candidate: the grid as observations of the output series, and the checks as
   a calibration record. The reply carries the fit.
6. *Read the calibration error.* The person reads the largest pillar error,
   the fit of each pillar with its error, the converged verdict and the count
   of pillars beyond the blueprint's tolerance. The calibrate reply carries
   them, and the calibration record stores them against the blueprint version
   the fit used.
7. *Read the slices.* The person inspects the smiles across tenors and the
   mesh. The reply carries the grid the calibration sampled, so a smile is one
   row of it and the mesh is the whole of it; the screen draws both without a
   second read.
8. *Refresh or leave.* The person either excludes a quote, changes the as-of
   and refits, or accepts the candidate. Refitting repeats step 5.
9. *Leave unpublished.* The person chooses *Leave unpublished* and returns to
   the list. The system records no publication: the surface stays a candidate,
   and the published object pricing reads is unchanged.

Calibration, if it existed, would be a server-side computation that writes a
derived series, so the journey would not carry state between steps: the reply
would carry the candidate. Nothing in this journey publishes.

* Screens and wireframes

One screen. The surface row and its as-of sit at the top, the quotes and the
parameterisation sit in the middle, and the calibration error and the slices
they produce sit at the bottom, so the person reads cause above effect. The
state line at the foot says the surface is a candidate only.

The calibrate reply carries the whole fit, so the error and the slices are read
in the call that produced them. The calibration record and the surface publish
both register history providers, so =marketdata.v1.history.get= shows what was
calibrated and when. A surface publish is decided under
=marketdata.fx_vol_surface_publish=, one approval by Market Risk, and a raise in
the system tenant closes at once.

* What is missing

1. *A calibration cannot be read after the reply.* The record is stored with the
   grid's metadata, the pillar fits and the violations, and nothing serves it by
   its id. So a candidate inspected yesterday cannot be inspected today, and a
   published set's calibration is reachable only through the publish row's id.
   The row's versions are in the change history, which states what changed and
   when; the fit itself needs a read of the record. The candidate is that read.
2. *There is no candidate grid read.* The points are observations of the output
   series, and the as-of read takes only published sets. A person who wants the
   candidate's grid after leaving the calibration has no read for it.
3. *The mesh and the slices are the reply's grid.* A smile is a row of it, which
   is enough for the reading the journey needs, but there is no slice by expiry,
   no mesh read finer than the output grid, and no view of total variance along
   an expiry axis, which is what the arbitrage check reads.
4. *The read states two of the convention's seven fields.* The ATM type, the
   switch tenor, the long-term ATM and delta types, the risk-reversal preference
   and the butterfly style are held in =ores.refdata= and are not returned with
   the fit, so a strike label past the switch tenor is not explained
   ([[id:9487E120-6D43-4EFB-8E28-E9CEBA7EB43A][Read a published surface]] records
   the same gap from the reading side).
