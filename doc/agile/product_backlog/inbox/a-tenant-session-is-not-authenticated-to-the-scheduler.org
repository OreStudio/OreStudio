* What

Make the scheduler answer a tenant's signed-in session with the truth: forbidden,
not unauthenticated.

Reproduced on 2026-11-28 with a tenant administrator of =acme_corporation= signed
in through the gateway:

| Read | Answer |
|------+--------|
| =/api/operations/housekeeping/stores= | 401 =not-authenticated=, =unauthorized= |
| =/api/operations/housekeeping/capacity?range=24h= | 401 =not-authenticated=, =unauthorized= |
| =/api/operations/housekeeping/tables?limit=3= | 401 =not-authenticated=, =unauthorized= |

The same session reads the rest of the application, and the scheduler's reads ask
for =scheduler::job_instances:read=, which a tenant administrator holds. So the
refusal is not the permission check: it is the request context the scheduler
builds from the delegated token, which the tenant's session does not satisfy.

* Why

Two things are wrong with the answer, and only one of them is cosmetic.

- The status says the person is not signed in when they are. A screen cannot tell
  "ask your administrator" from "sign in again", and the browser may take a 401
  as a reason to drop a session that is perfectly good.
- Because every housekeeping read is refused, a tenant's housekeeping page is
  empty and reads as an installation with nothing to keep. That empty page has
  been read as a design decision — the catalogue is the installation's — when at
  least part of it is this refusal.

The fix decides the design question with it: what a tenant may read of the
installation's housekeeping, and what answer it gets when the answer is nothing.
