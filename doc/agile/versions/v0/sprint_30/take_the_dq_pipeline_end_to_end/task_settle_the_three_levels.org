* Notes

The page is the deliverable of this task, not a by-product. The investigation
showed the four existing documents each answer a part and disagree at the seams;
a fifth partial page would repeat the defect, so this one has to be the single
picture the others point at.

Answers requirement 4, and unblocks every task below it. Defect D3.

The decisions a reviewer has to accept, because every task below depends on
them. All six are settled; the reasons are in the page and in the sign-off
exchange.

1. Three levels, named after the owner of the values: *platform*, *world*,
   *tenant*. The name is *platform* and not *installation*, because
   =data_tiers= gives "installation data" to a tenant's business entities and
   the row-level security model gives "the installation" to the deployment's
   registers. The deployment's registers sit outside the three levels: no
   bundle carries them and no tenant reads them.
2. One copy of a platform row and of a world row, in the system tenant. No
   tenant holds a copy, and a tenant reads them through the shared-reference
   policy and =:system_tenant_visible:=, both of which exist. A shared table
   takes no tenant write path; the restriction is by permission and not by a
   further policy shape.
3. =base= becomes =world=. The tenant's own starting configuration in =base=
   becomes =tenant_defaults=, and holds only what a tenant may change: the
   badges, the badge mappings and the code domains. The ORE parameters and
   their value domains move to =system_core=, because nothing shows a tenant
   editing them and their per-tenant copy was a duplicate for readers.
4. A dataset is a member of one bundle and no more. A given table in a given
   tenant has one mechanism that writes it, which lets a populate script own
   the system tenant's party rows while the publish function owns every other
   tenant's.
5. The bundle publication and the bring-up bootstrap are the two callers of one
   publish function. The bring-up publish is part of database setup and runs
   after every populate script.
6. The bundle carries its level, and the level determines the target tenant; a
   request does not name a target. A caller whose tenant the level does not
   admit is refused, or a tenant administrator could publish a world bundle
   into their own tenant and recreate the second copy by request.

See [[id:16920F25-F0EF-4031-9C2D-DF57B5865072][Investigation: publishing pipeline]] for the evidence behind every claim here.
