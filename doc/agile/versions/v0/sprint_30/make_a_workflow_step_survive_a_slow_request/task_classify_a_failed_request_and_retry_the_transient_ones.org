* Goal

A step that hits a transient transport failure tries again with backoff and jitter while its budget remains, and a refusal fails at once. Each attempt is logged with its number and its reason, and the policy lives in one helper rather than at each call site.

* Acceptance

- The helper tells a timeout and a no-responders failure apart from a refusal, and retries only the first two.
- The delay grows with jitter, so two steps that failed together do not return together.
- The attempts stop at the step's remaining budget, so the step does not outlive its own deadline.
- Every attempt is logged with its number and the reason the previous one failed.
- A refusal is reported at once, with the server's reason.

* Plan

- Make a request timeout carry a kind. =ores.nats= raised it as a bare runtime
  error, so a caller had to match its words; it becomes
  =nats_error_kind::request_timeout=, beside =services_unavailable=.
- Read a failure into three answers: transient (timeout, nobody listening),
  refused (the server's own refusal), permanent (everything else). Only a
  transient failure is retried.
- Own the retry in one helper: the request, the step's deadline, the
  per-attempt deadline and the work in a person's words. The wait doubles and
  is jittered, the attempts stop at the policy's limit or the deadline, and a
  refusal is thrown at once with the server's reason.
- Replace the report scheduler's string match on "NATS request timed out" with
  the kind, since its own suite asserts the code that match produced.
- Use the helper at the five derive sites and the calendar regeneration, and
  leave every other request as it is.

* Result

A request timeout is now a kind on the transport error, not a sentence a caller
matches. =nats_connect_error= gained =request_timeout=, distinct from
=connection_timeout= (a connect that never completed) and from
=services_unavailable= (nobody listening).

[request_retry.hpp](file:../../../../../projects/ores.iam/core/include/ores.iam.core/service/request_retry.hpp)
holds the three answers and the one retry loop:

- =classify_request_failure= reads a refusal, a timeout or a missing responder,
  and everything else.
- =request_with_retry= takes the request, the step's deadline, the per-attempt
  deadline and the work in words. It doubles the wait with jitter, stops at the
  policy's limit or at the deadline, and throws at once on a refusal or a
  permanent fault, naming the work, the attempts and the reason.

The five derive requests and the calendar regeneration now go through it, each
with the deadline of the step that makes it.

The report scheduler's string match is gone: its own suite,
=service_next_fire_tests=, asserts that a timed-out request reads as
=request_timeout=, and it failed with =internal_error= while the change was
stashed. It passes now.

Six new cases cover the classification and the loop, including the refusal that
is not retried, the limit, and the deadline that refuses a retry. The two
=ores.iam.core.tests= failures are the initial-admin database guard, which the
environment's out-of-sync schema causes and this change does not touch.

- On a recreated database and a full build, =ores.iam.core.tests= (348 of 348) and =ores.reporting.core.tests= pass in full (2026-11-13).
