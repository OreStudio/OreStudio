* What

A starting point, or a step kind, that fills a tenant with synthetic parties:
the thing the deleted organisation generator did, expressed in the shape
provisioning has now.

* Why

The generator that was deleted built parties, counterparties, business units,
portfolios, books and contacts inside an existing tenant, from a seed, and its
only caller was a shell recipe that created a tenant called =acme=. It was not
usable post-provisioning (it tried to create a second root party per tenant and
the unique index refused it), it produced identifiers that were not
well-formed LEIs, and it had no concept of staff accounts, so the datasets it
was meant to serve stopped using it.

What is worth having is the other shape: a tenant created from a starting
point, with synthetic parties in it, described by the starting point's own
rows, so both clients get it and neither holds a generator's knobs. That is
either a new step kind (code) or a new starting point whose arguments name a
generator (data), and the four requirements the analysis left behind -- a
fixed shape where a dataset needs one, staff accounts, locale coverage, and
ISO 17442 checksum-valid LEIs -- are the list.
