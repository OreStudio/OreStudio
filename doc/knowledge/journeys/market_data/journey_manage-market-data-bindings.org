This page documents a [[id:F44766FA-1E9C-416F-849A-E131101B2ECA][user journey]]: A person decides which producers of market data a party consumes: sees the bindings, what each feeds and when it last ticked, adds, edits and deletes a binding, and reads who changed it, for synthetic data and, shown and read only, for real data.

A user journey describes what one person is trying to do, before anything is
built. It is a design document that outlives any sprint, so it names the
screens, the entity fields each screen shows, and the operations and messages
each step needs.

* Goal

"I want to say which producers of market data this party consumes, to see what
each one feeds and whether it is still ticking, and to change that without
asking someone to run a script." The journey is done when each binding is
listed with its source, the series it feeds, whether it is enabled and running
and when it last ticked, and the person has added, edited or deleted the
bindings that needed it.

* Actor and trigger

The actor is the market data operator: a person who holds
=marketdata::feed_bindings:read= to look and =marketdata::feed_bindings:write=
or =marketdata::feed_bindings:delete= to change a binding. The system
administrator has the same screen for the whole installation, and the
[[id:986CED57-341F-49DE-8F5D-F186771F10D8][Keep the market data feeds running]]
journey covers the system administrator's view of what is running. Which actor
classes own the screen is an open question; see /What is missing/.

The trigger is one of four events. A party is new and has no market data. A
new synthetic feed exists and the party should consume it. A series has gone
quiet and the person looks for the binding behind it. A report asks why a value
is generated, and the person checks that the series comes from a synthetic
binding and not from a vendor. The journey starts at *Market data*, then
*Bindings*, and ends when the person leaves the screen.

Three facts shape the screen.

- A binding says that a party consumes one producer source. It does not name
  the series. A tick names its own datum by its oresmd URI, and the ingest loop
  stores it, once for each enabled binding of that source, in the party's own
  series. The series a binding feeds is therefore read from what has been
  written, and the screen says that it is inferred.
- A binding says what kind of producer it names: =SYNTHETIC= for a generated
  feed or =VENDOR= for a real one. The ingest loop stamps the series with it.
  The default is =VENDOR=.
- A synthetic feed has a binding mode. A bound feed publishes on the ingested
  subjects and gets a binding when it runs. A sandboxed feed publishes on
  subjects the ingest loop does not read and never gets one. At most one running
  feed may write a series, so two feeds on one pair are never both bound.

Two scenarios run through the journey.

- *Synthetic data* is the supported scenario. A person can follow every step to
  the end.
- *Real data* from a vendor is not supported. The journey and the prototype draw
  a vendor binding so that the design holds it, and they say in words that this
  product does not connect real feeds. No control adds a vendor binding, and a
  vendor binding that already exists is a read only record: it has no Edit, it
  can be deleted, and the page says why.

* Steps

The screens are the record screens of the
[[id:2BF7F03F-6BF4-45FF-8E26-4D3B4E2D943D][Record screen standard]]: a list, a
record page with the tabs Details and History, and dialogs for add, edit, delete
and revert. This journey adds no layout of its own. The steps are the synthetic
scenario. The last block says where the real data scenario differs.

1. *List the bindings.* The person opens *Market data* and then *Bindings*. The
   system lists the party's bindings, with the source name first, then the kind
   (synthetic or vendor), the series the source feeds, whether the binding is
   enabled, the status of the feed behind it, and when it last ticked. The series
   is an oresmd URL drawn as the product draws every reference: a link to the
   series page in a new tab, and an *Inspect* button for the read only view of
   its parts.
2. *Search and filter.* The person searches by source name or series and filters
   by kind. A search that matches nothing says so and offers to clear it.
3. *Open a binding.* A click on a row, or Enter, opens the record page on its
   Details tab. The system shows the source name, the kind, whether it is
   enabled, the status, the last tick, a link to the feed behind the source, the
   feed's folder and tick rate, and the series with its oresmd URL. The last line
   says who last changed the binding, when and why. The header carries the access
   mark and, for a person who may take them, *Edit* and *Delete*.
4. *Add a binding.* The person chooses *Add* on the list. The dialog "Add binding"
   asks for the source, chosen from the synthetic sources the party does not
   consume, and whether the binding is enabled. It says which series the source
   would write and refuses the source, naming the feed, when a running feed
   already writes that series. Until a subject answers that before the write,
   this check is a target, and the system reports the refusal after the write; see
   /What is missing/. A new record is saved with the reason new record, so the
   dialog states it in a note and does not ask. After the write the system opens
   the new binding's page.
5. *Edit a binding.* The person chooses *Edit*. The dialog "Edit <source name>"
   shows the source and the enabled flag. Changing the source rebinds the party
   to another synthetic source without restarting any producer, and the dialog
   warns when the series changes. The person picks a change reason from the
   reasons for an amendment and writes a commentary when the reason needs one. The
   system writes against the version it read and refuses a binding that changed
   since. After the write the page shows the new values.
6. *Open the feed behind a binding.* The person follows the link to the feed. The
   feed's page holds its run state and its *Start* and *Stop*, as the
   [[id:303D85B2-0039-4B1C-83BD-BB866BC0AEE1][Feed screen standard]] says. A
   binding has no start or stop of its own, and starting a folder or a collection
   is the work of
   [[id:A829B804-23A5-493C-BDC6-A3B513EBDFEF][Run a synthetic collection]].
7. *Delete a binding.* The person chooses *Delete*. The dialog names the binding
   and says what is lost: ingestion for the party stops, the series and its
   observations stay, and the binding's history stays. The person picks a reason
   and writes a commentary when it needs one. After the delete the system returns
   to the list.
8. *See who changed a binding.* The person opens the *History* tab. It is the
   shared timeline: a chart of the events over time above a rail of one-line
   entries, newest first. Each entry says whether the version was created or
   changed, the record and its version, the fields that changed, the reason, who
   modified it and for whom, and when, with the commentary below. The detail is
   collapsed and shows the removed and the added line. The newest version carries
   the current-version emblem. *Hide system activity* hides the versions that a
   service wrote. *Revert* is offered on any older version, to a person who may
   write. Its dialog says that the values of that version are written back as a
   new version and that no version is lost. Start and stop are the feed's and are
   not in the binding's history.
9. *See a binding that has gone quiet.* The status column says *Quiet* for a
   binding that is enabled and has not ticked within the interval its tick rate
   implies, and a quiet binding stays in the list. The person sorts by last tick.
   The threshold is provisional: nothing states how long a feed may be quiet yet.
10. *Leave.* The person goes back to the *Market data* area.

*Real data differs at three steps.* In the list a vendor binding shows the kind
*Vendor* and the status *Not supported*. Its page is a read only record, so it
shows no *Edit*, though it shows *Delete* to a person who may delete, and one
notice on the Details tab says that real feeds are not supported. *Add* offers no vendor source and says why, in one
sentence.

* What is missing

1. *No read for the series a source feeds.* A binding names a source, not a
   series. The candidate is a read that resolves a source to the series it has
   written, or a series reference on the binding. Until then the screen shows
   the series of the source's newest observations and says it is inferred.
2. *No read for the last tick.* The ingest loop keeps the count and the last
   tick of each binding in memory, and nothing reads them. The candidate is a
   read over those statistics, or a stored last-observation time. Until then
   the screen uses the newest observation of the series as the nearest answer.
3. *No list of the synthetic sources a party does not consume.* Bind needs the
   producers that have no binding for the party. The candidate is a read that
   joins the generation configs to the bindings. Until then the dialog lists
   every source and marks the bound ones.
4. *No answer to which feed holds a series.* The feed controller knows which
   running feed writes a series and refuses a second one, but no subject says
   so before the person picks. Until then the system reports the refusal after
   the write.
5. *No way to tell the kind of a producer from the source name.* The binding
   carries the kind, but a source with no binding has none. The dialog must
   not offer a source it cannot classify.
6. *The synthetic feeds stopped after a service restart.* The newest synthetic
   tick of AUD/USD stopped advancing after the marketdata service was
   restarted. The task Find why the synthetic feed went quiet looks for the
   cause. The screen needs the answer to say why a feed is quiet.
7. *No threshold for quiet and no alert.* The configs carry a tick rate. Nothing
   compares it with a last tick, and nothing notifies a person. The screen sorts
   by last tick and the person judges, until a threshold exists.
8. *The menu had no entry for bindings.* Decided by Marco on 2026-10-13: the
   screen is in the Market data area, beside the Synthetic folder, with the trail
   Home, Market data, Bindings, then the binding. The
   [[id:339F0405-43AE-4049-B6B3-EB12E5A38CA4][Menu structure]] now says so.
9. *The standard fixes the key, and rebinding edits it.* Decided by Marco on
   2026-10-13: Edit offers the source name, with help under the field saying that
   it switches the ingest source. A binding is the exception to the key rule, and
   the [[id:2BF7F03F-6BF4-45FF-8E26-4D3B4E2D943D][Record screen standard]] names it.
10. *A vendor binding.* Decided by Marco on 2026-10-13: it is a read only record
    with no Edit, and it can be deleted, so a stale one does not stay for ever. The
    standard names the exception.
11. *Who may edit and delete.* Decided by Marco on 2026-10-13: a market data
    operator adds and edits, and only the system administrator deletes. An action a
    person may not take is not drawn.
