* Summary

A surface is three readings of one answer: the picture, the grid and one smile.
The prototype argues they must be drawn from a single response so they cannot
disagree, that the strike column's meaning is part of the surface (a delta,
spot-quoted, not premium-adjusted), and that a surface nobody published is a
notice and not a grid.

It is ported from hedgr's =prototypes/vol_surface_smile.html=. It is the
published surface; the candidate's smiles, mesh and arbitrage checks are the
sibling prototype [[file:inspect-a-surfaces-slices.org][Inspect a surface's slices]].

* Detail

** The question

Is a surface best read as a picture, as numbers, or as one slice? A picture
shows the shape and the smile; the grid shows the value a person prices from; a
smile shows the wing. The variant decides which the page opens on.

** The variants

Switch with the bar at the foot, or =?variant=.

| Variant | Name        | The structural argument |
|---------+-------------+-------------------------|
| =a=     | The surface | A boxed 3D mesh with ticks on three axes, and the axes named under it. |
| =b=     | The grid    | Expiries as rows, strikes as columns, ATM mid stated. |
| =c=     | One smile   | One expiry across the strikes, with the strike codes under it. |

** The states

- =?state=published= — a surface with a publish instant.
- =?state=stale= — the publish is older than the threshold; the badge says so.
- =?state=none= — the blueprint exists and nothing is published. A notice, no
  grid.

** What is fake

The grid is generated by the smile function hedgr's =vol_surface_generator.py=
used, and the mesh is projected with the same maths, so the picture and the
table cannot disagree. Every number is mock; nothing reads a service.

** Where the code lives

=doc/prototypes/market_data/read-a-published-surface/index.html=,
self-contained. The projection and the colour ramp are in its script.

** The verdict

Take it. It answers a question no existing prototype answers: what a person
reads *after* a surface is published, as opposed to what a quant checks before.
The one addition is the strike code on each column header, because a column
called =25D put= is ambiguous until the delta convention is stated.

** What changed from hedgr

| Item | hedgr | Here | Why |
|------+-------+------+-----|
| Tabs | Surface, Grid, Smile | Variants of one page | The prototype switcher is how this group compares arguments |
| Strike meaning | Implied by column labels | =model=, =strike convention= and =delta type= in the facts row | A strike is not a number without them |
| Chrome | A dialog | A page with a facts row | Prototypes read as the product |
| Mesh | Generated once into the HTML | Generated from the grid in the page | The picture cannot drift from the table |
| Staleness | A printed badge | A badge from the server's verdict | The threshold is configuration, not a client constant |
