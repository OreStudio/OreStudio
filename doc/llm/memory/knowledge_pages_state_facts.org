#+title: Knowledge pages state facts, never history
#+description: A knowledge page is a statement of fact about the system as it is. Its summary must not assert that the subject matters, justify a choice already made, narrate how the system came to be, or describe the cluster the page belongs to.
#+type: memory
#+memory_subtype: feedback
#+level: cross
#+filetags: :memory:feedback:
#+created: 2026-09-21
#+updated: 2026-09-21

A knowledge page states what is true of the system now: what a thing is, what it does, and why it is shaped the way it is. It does not narrate how the system came to be that way.

The summary has to place its subject, because that is all a search result or a link preview shows. A reader who has not opened the page must learn from the summary what area it covers. For a structure note that means naming the subject area the cluster addresses and the concerns it separates -- not the pages it gathers.

Three openings are never right: a summary does not assert that its subject matters, does not justify a choice already made, and does not inventory its cluster. Write the subject.

*Why:* The first summary written for the Message Queue structure note had all three faults at once -- messaging playing a central role, NATS chosen for its simplicity, and the cluster listing every document on the topic -- and was rejected for exactly that. The reader who reached the page already decided the subject matters; a justification for a settled choice belongs with the decision that recorded it; and inventorying the collection is the job of the index, not of the page.

The correction then over-shot the other way. It stated facts about the message implementation -- the subject grammar, what a payload may carry, the verbs an entity supports -- all true, and all belonging to a single page of the cluster, and it still left a reader unable to say what the cluster covered. That was rejected in turn. A summary has to place its subject, not merely describe it.

*How to apply:* When writing or reviewing the summary of any knowledge page, and especially when scaffolding one, where the template invites a generic opening. Read it back as a stranger would and ask two questions: does it say what area the page covers, and is every sentence a fact about that area rather than about the page or its past? For a structure note, name the concerns the cluster separates; for any other page, name what the thing is.
