A point-in-time record, written while the story that commissioned it was open, of an
[[id:A7F8D9E2-3C4B-4A5D-B67C-8E9F0A1B2C3D][investigation]] into what each asset class needs
before a volatility can be derived for it. It is a snapshot, not a description of the system. The
durable half -- what a class supplies to the engine -- belongs on the page that owns the
derivation; cite that page from code and recipes, not this one.

* Context

The platform derives a volatility from a history, because no feed offers a volatility quote. The
engine is one measurement with three per-class parameters: the transform, the coordinates a quote is
named by, and the smile model.

That leaves a question the engine cannot answer: for which asset classes does the platform hold a
history at all? A derivation with no series is not a small feature with a fake input. It is a
fabrication, and the platform must refuse it by naming the series it lacks. This survey answers the
question class by class, so the story can say which classes it serves and which it refuses.

* Methodology

Three questions were asked of each asset class, in this order, because each one is cheaper than the
next and a failure at any of them ends the enquiry:

1. *Is a series held?* The adapter catalogue (=marketdata_adapters_artefact_populate.sql=) says
   which public sources the release reads; the simulator's feed kinds say which series it generates;
   the ACME dataset says which curves the groups are configured from.
2. *Can a quote be named?* The datum schema says whether the key grammar has an instrument type for
   the class's volatility, with the coordinates the class quotes.
3. *Is a set configured?* The refdata curve configurations say whether a surface of the class has
   anywhere to land.

* Paths taken

The classes were walked in the order of the taxonomy, and each was carried as far as the first
question it failed.

| Class | Series held | Quote the datum names | Verdict |
|-------+-------------+-----------------------+---------|
| FX | ECB reference spots through =frankfurter= (real, daily), the simulator's spot paths, and the forwards the IR curves imply | =fx_option=, by pair, expiry and a delta label | served today |
| Interest rates | 13 official rate sources (real, daily, one per central bank) and the 35 yield curves' own quote series (generated and stored per observation date) | =swaption= by currency, expiry, term and strike; =capfloor= by currency, index, term and index tenor | servable now |
| Inflation | 10 inflation curves configured, and no source that fixes an index | =zc_inflation_capfloor= and =yy_inflation_capfloor=, by index, term, cap or floor, and strike | waits on a history: an index-fixing adapter |
| Credit | nothing configured | none: the schema names the CDS spread, the hazard rate and the recovery rate, not a CDS option volatility, while ORE's today's market names =CDSVolatilities= | waits on a datum, then a history |
| Equity | nothing | =equity_option=, by name, currency, expiry, strike and option type | waits on a history |
| Commodity | nothing | =commodity_option=, by commodity, expiry, strike and option type | waits on a history |

Three of the rows need to be read carefully.

*Interest rates has many series where FX has one.* An FX pair's ladder hangs off a single series:
every expiry is a window over the same spot. A swaption's underlying is the rate at its term, so the
derivation reads the series of that term and a term the curves do not carry has no history at all.
The refusal a person reads is therefore per pillar and names the term, not the currency.

*A generated series is a history, if it is stored.* The yield curves' quotes are written per
observation date by the simulator, so after a run the store holds the term structure's own history
and the derivation can measure it. A fresh database holds none of it, and then the derivation has
nothing to read until the generator has run. The engine's refusal must say which of the two it is
looking at.

*Credit fails twice, and the first failure is the datum.* ORE reads CDS volatilities as a component
of today's market, and the configuration tables for them exist. The datum schema has no instrument
type for a CDS option, so no key can name the quote the derivation would write. A credit volatility
is a datum story before it is a derivation story.

* Conclusions

1. *Two classes can be served now.* FX is served today; interest rates can be served as soon as the
   derivation has its three parameters. Between them they cover the volatility of every trade in the
   seed population: the FX options, the swaptions and the caps.
2. *Four classes fail, for three different reasons.* Inflation and equity fail on a series and would
   pass with an adapter each. Commodity fails on a series and has no obvious free source to adapt.
   Credit fails on a datum first and a series second.
3. *Inflation is the nearest third class.* Its quotes are already nameable, ten curves are already
   configured, and the missing piece is an index-fixing adapter -- a market data task, not a
   derivation task.
4. *The transform is a log return or a difference, and the class decides which.* A price or a rate
   that stays positive takes a log return: FX, equity, commodity. A rate or a spread that can be
   negative, or that is quoted in basis points, takes a difference: interest rates, inflation, credit.
5. *The coordinates differ more than the measurement does.* FX names a delta label, the other
   classes name a strike. The ladder that a class quotes is the ladder its surface is published on,
   so the coordinates belong to the class and the window rule does not.
6. *The derived provider is FX-named too.* The catalogue row for the =derived= provider describes
   "the FX volatility quotes our own computation states", so a second class renames that row as well
   as the code.

* Recommendations

- Serve FX and interest rates in this story. Refuse the other four by name, with the reason each one
  fails, so an operator reads "no fixing history for the index" and not "unsupported asset class".
- Make the refusal per missing input: the series, the coordinate or the datum. The distinction is
  what tells the next planner whether the work is an adapter, a table or a key grammar change.
- Take inflation as the next class after this story, as a market data task: add the index-fixing
  adapter, then the derivation's three parameters follow with nothing new.
- Treat credit as its own story, because it starts with a datum the key grammar cannot name, and an
  engine that grew a credit branch before its datum would be the exact fault this story avoids.
- Do not serve equity or commodity until an adapter exists. A derived quote with no history behind it
  is indistinguishable from an invented one, and the platform already states it does not invent
  quotes for a party.
