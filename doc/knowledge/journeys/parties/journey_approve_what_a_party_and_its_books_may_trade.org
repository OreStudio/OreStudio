This page documents a [[id:F44766FA-1E9C-416F-849A-E131101B2ECA][user journey]]: A risk administrator states the product types a party permits and narrows each of its books to a subset, reading the two sets against each other and understanding every refusal.

A user journey describes what one person is trying to do, before anything is
built. It is a design document that outlives any sprint, so it names the
screens, the entity fields each screen shows, and the operations and messages
each step needs.

* Goal

"I approve what this party may trade, and then what each of its books may carry,
and I have to be able to see that no book has escaped what I approved. When the
system refuses a book, it tells me which part of the party's approval is
missing, because the correction is always up there and never on the book."

The person is finished when the party's set reads as they intended, every book
of the party carries a subset of it, and nothing is left refused without a
stated reason.

* Actor and trigger

A risk administrator runs this journey, acting inside their own tenant. The
reference data administrator runs the same journey when a desk opens, and the
product owner reads it without changing it.

Three things bring them there: a new desk whose books have to be approved before
it books anything, a product line the firm withdraws, and a report that shows a
book trading a product nobody remembers approving.

It starts on the party record and ends on a book of that party, because the two
sets are one decision read at two levels. Both are the same record screen, so
the person never leaves the record they are working on.

* Steps

1. *Open the party and its approvals.* The person opens the party record and
   chooses the /Market approvals/ tab. The page reads the party, and the tab
   reads the party's set.
2. *Read what the party permits today.* The tab states which of the two states
   the party is in: it permits every product type the catalogue holds, or it
   permits the list shown. A party that states an empty list is drawn as
   permitting nothing, in the words the screen uses for any empty list.
3. *Choose to state a set.* The person switches the party from permitting
   everything to permitting a list. The system writes nothing until the set is
   saved, and the tab draws an empty list with /Add/ under it.
4. *Add the types the party may trade.* /Add/ opens the picker over the product
   type catalogue, searched by code and name. The person adds each type the
   mandate allows; the tab lists them in code order with the name beside the
   code.
5. *Save the party's set.* /Save/ in the tab's header writes the flag and the
   set. The system refuses the save when a book of the party carries a type the
   save would remove, and the refusal names the type and the book.
6. *Move to a book of the party.* The tab links the books of the party. The
   book's /Product types/ panel reads either that the book follows its party or
   the subset it states.
7. *Narrow the book.* The book's picker offers only the types the party
   permits, so a book cannot be given a type outside the approval by picking
   it. The panel draws the party's set beside the book's, marking each type as
   carried or not carried by the book.
8. *Save the book's set.* /Save/ writes the book's flag and its subset. The
   system refuses a type outside the party's set, which reaches the person only
   from a page that has gone stale or from a caller that did not use the
   screen.
9. *Confirm the two sets read as one.* The person returns to the party's tab
   and reads, for each book, whether it follows the party or narrows it, so the
   approval and the books that answer to it are one reading rather than two
   pages.

No step waits on the server after it returns, so the journey carries no state
across a reply and needs no workflow instance.

* Screens and wireframes

Two screens, both on the record screen the standard already draws: the party's
record page gains a tab, and the book's record page gains a panel. The
judgement of each step follows, because a step the standard already draws is not
a design decision, and a step no rule draws is what the prototype must settle.

| Step | Shape on the screen | Judgement |
|------+---------------------+-----------|
| 1 | A tab of the record page, held in the address | Standard: RS-PAGE-09 |
| 2 | A line that states the state, then the list or the empty list | Standard: RS-PAGE-07 for the empty case. The words for "permits every type" have no rule: the design note proposes them |
| 3 | The switch between the two states | Standard: RS-WRITE-01 draws a classification as a select, and this is a select of two labels |
| 4 | A links panel with /Add/ under it, and a picker dialog | Standard: RS-RELATED-01 to RS-RELATED-03, RS-RELATED-06 |
| 5 | /Save/ at the right of the panel header, the refusal under the panel | Standard: RS-PAGE-03, RS-WRITE-05, RS-FEEDBACK-01 |
| 6 | A panel on the book record, read only until edited | Standard: the parts and links panels draw both; reading the party's set inside the book's panel is new |
| 7 | The party's set drawn beside the book's, with each type marked | No rule draws a two set comparison: the design note defines it for the standard to take |
| 8 | The refusal naming the party's set | No rule draws it, and the server does not carry the words: see /What is missing/ |

The two sets are links and not parts: a product type exists on its own and is
carried by the party or the book, and a link's panel carries no history of its
own (RS-RELATED-03).

* Prototype

The prototype goes under =doc/prototypes/parties/approval_matrix/=, and it must
settle four placements the standard does not:

- *P1 Where the set lives.* A tab of the party with a panel on the book, or a
  screen of its own that both record pages link to. The tab and the panel keep
  the person on the record they are changing, which is the standard's habit; a
  screen of its own is the alternative the prototype draws, to be refused or
  accepted.
- *P2 How the two sets are read together.* On the book, the party's set is the
  background and the book's the marks, or the two are two columns of one table.
  A type the party permits and the book does not carry must read as clearly as
  its reverse, which cannot happen and is therefore not drawn.
- *P3 What a party that states nothing looks like.* "Permits every type" is a
  state no rule draws. The prototype proposes its words, and the standard takes
  them if the owner accepts them.
- *P4 Where a refusal stands.* Under the panel, in the words of the operation,
  or in a dialog the person must dismiss.

The states the prototype draws, each at its own address: a party that permits
everything; a party that permits an empty list; a party that permits a list; a
party save refused because a book carries the type; a book that follows its
party; a book that narrows it; a book save refused from a stale page.

* What is missing

- *No operation answers the set a book may carry.* The database resolves it,
  from both sets and both flags, but nothing carries the answer over the wire,
  so the book's panel would read two junctions and apply the rule itself. That
  is the rule in a second place, which is the defect this journey exists to
  avoid. Capture: [[id:8B1C5367-304E-4723-89B9-4FA6C16AAFDF][No operation answers the product types a book may carry]].
- *A refusal does not carry the party's set.* The database refuses the write and
  names the set in its exception, and the write path turns that into a generic
  failure. The screen cannot tell the person which approval is missing. Capture:
  [[id:DEC66AE2-4EB0-46B5-BA47-5D332B1D68B2][A refused approval reaches the person as a database error]].
- *No rule draws two sets compared.* The book's panel needs the party's set as
  its background, and the record screen standard draws one set at a time. The
  design note the prototype carries proposes the shape, and the standard takes
  it at the sign off, as the calendars topic added RS-HIST-01.
- *No words for a set that is not stated.* "Permits every product type" is a
  state and not an empty list, and no rule states how the screen says it.
