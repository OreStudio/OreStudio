* Goal

The planner must know what a party is configured to receive, not what has arrived: a vol quote or a bootstrapped curve can take a while to appear. Give each generator kind one function that lists its outputs: an IR curve its pillar quotes and its index fixing, an FX spot its quotes and its pair fixing, a vol surface its quotes. Add a market data request that computes one party's demand from the catalogue, with compute_party_demand, without ticks or adapters. The expected set is the outputs of each generator that publishes an item the party demands, plus the curves of its bootstrap configs whose pillars it expects. It replaces served_fixing_uris, which is global and IR only.

* Acceptance

- One function per generator kind lists its outputs, and the curve feed and the fixing writer use the same function.
- A market data request returns a party's demand computed from the catalogue, and it answers before any adapter is ready.
- The trade generation scope holds the party's expected set; served_fixing_uris is removed.
- A unit test shows that a party routed to one provider does not expect another provider's outputs.

* Plan

A demanded item is a quote address with a term and no dates, such as
=oresmd://ir/AUD?type=quote&instrument=ir_swap&quote=rate&fwd_start=0D&tenor=1D&term=10Y=.
It is not a series address: =series_of= drops the term. So a generator's
outputs are quote addresses, and they match a demanded item directly.

1. *Outputs of each generator kind.* A new header
   =ores.synthetic.api/feeds/generator_outputs.hpp= holds one pure function per
   kind, from the config and its rows:
   - IR curve: the address of each template entry's pillar quote, written
     by =pillar_quote_datum= as the feed writes it, and the fixing address of
     =curve_fixing_uri=, which the feed hands to the fixing writer. A
     meeting-dated pillar names a quote only once its dates resolve, so it is
     left out (=pillar_quote_needs_dates=).
   - FX spot: the quote, in its canonical spelling. Its fixing items are what the spot
     reads at its start, not what it publishes, so they are not outputs.
   - FX vol surface: the quote of each expiry at ATM and at the risk
     reversal and butterfly of each delta. =fx_vol_surface_feed= names its
     points with the same =fx_vol_point_uri=, and the registry builds the
     feed with the same =fx_vol_surface_deltas=.
2. *A party's demand from the catalogue.* A market data request
   =get_party_demand_request= carries a party id. Its handler reads the
   catalogue and calls =compute_party_demand=, and it answers with each
   route's adapter id, entitlement and demanded items. It needs no tick and
   no adapter.
3. *The expected set.* A pure function in =ores.synthetic.api= takes the
   party's demand and the generators, each with the providers it publishes
   for and its outputs. A spot or a curve publishes for the provider of its
   collection, and a surface for the provider of each contributor
   (=system_generator_offers=). A generator counts when it publishes for the
   provider of an entitled route of the party and one of its outputs is a
   demanded item of that route. The expected set is the union of the
   outputs of the generators that count. =generator_publications= builds the generators from the config
   rows: an enabled config in an enabled collection.
4. *The planner.* =trade_generation_scope::expected_market_data= replaces
   =served_fixing_uris=. The writer fills it from the request and the
   generator configs, and =drawable= asks it for the swap's fixing address.
   =served_fixing_uris= and its tests are removed.

Each step ends in a unit test. Step 3 has the test of the acceptance: a party
routed to one provider does not expect the outputs of a generator of another
provider.

* Notes

expected set yet. No acceptance criterion needs them: the planner asks the set
only for fixing addresses.

the =SyntheticService= role now holds =marketdata::routes:read=.
