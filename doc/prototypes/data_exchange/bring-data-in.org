* Summary

Every import journey begins the same way: name a source and a scope. What
differs is the trigger, the payload and who may act. This screen is the one
entry step, with those three as variants: seven triggers, four actors, the
family picker that shows what the file carries, and the workflow the import
will start. Staging writes no live row.

It is throwaway. It reads no service. Open [[file:bring-data-in/][the source screen]].

* Detail

** What it argues

- The trigger is a variant, not a screen. Onboarding, a seed, a defect
  reproduction, a vendor feed, one corrected file and a promotion differ in
  their source files and their scope; none differs in what the person does
  first.
- The scope comes from the session. The file carries no party, the shell
  refuses a party other than the session's, and the screen shows the party as a
  read-only field rather than a picker.
- The scan is visible before the write. For the sample-folder trigger the table
  lists what the scanner read and what it ignored, with the reason. The seed
  journey says the person cannot see this today.
- The family picker is the missing scope. The onboarding journey needs the
  conventions and the portfolio and nothing else, so the parts the file carries
  but the journey does not want are unchecked and named in the refusal reason.
- Who may act is a panel, not a footnote. The operations user holds the
  conventions write and not the trade or market writes; the screen shows the
  permission that is missing instead of hiding the button.
- The workflow is named before the work starts. A card gives the type, the
  start message, the failure policy and every step the definition would
  declare, so the person can see what the import will do and where progress
  will appear.

** The workflow this import starts

The card reads nothing. The type and the step list are the definition's, and
=workflow.v1.definitions.list= would answer the same list; the stage subjects
and the commit are the draft design's. The budgets are the workflow API's:
=data_step_timeout= for a step that stages, =write_step_timeout= for a step
that writes a record.

The list follows the draft: one stage step per family the file carries, then
one commit step and one record step. A family the server skips is declared as
a step too, so the skip is reported by a step rather than dropped. The engine
builds this list from the request at start and rebuilds the same list on every
advance, so the card is a promise the run can be held to.

Nothing runs until =workflow.v1.ops.start_workflow= is published. The card's
*Follow the run* opens the [[file:one-import-run.org][Run]] screen in its staged
state, where the progress begins at that publish.

** Variants and states

=?case=onboard|seed|defect|feed|one-dataset|promote|party=,
=?actor=admin|ops|maintainer|support=,
=?state=source|families|refused=, =?scan=0=. The bar at the foot switches the
trigger and the actor; the two refusals are the missing permission and the row
the validator refuses.

** What is real and what is not

The subjects and the permission model are the journeys': =ore upload=,
=ore import=, =ore import-run=, =marketdata import=, =compute download-input=,
and the four writes each import borrows. The convention family names and the
27-family count come from the modelling tree; the merge policies (replace by
key, add missing world, skip) come from the draft design. The step states,
their names, the budgets and the failure policies are the workflow engine's.
The workflow type, its stage subjects and the per-family stage replies are the
draft's and are not registered. The row counts, file sizes, request ids and
checksums are invented.

The screen itself has no server. No upload route, no scan route and no staging
verb exists. The journey lists them as gaps, and the screen says so in its
banner.

** Screens the workflow connection touches

The Entry screen names the run; it does not follow one. The
[[file:one-import-run.org][Run]] screen follows the instance and holds the retry,
the [[file:stage-an-import.org][Review]] screen sits between the two instances a
decider-gated import needs, and the [[file:import-register.org][Register]] lists
one row per instance. This screen answers the question the engine cannot: which
workflow will this file start, and what will it do.

** The verdict

Not signed off. The screen asks the owner two things: whether the trigger
belongs on one screen at all, and whether the operations user is given a
permission that means "may import for a party" or stays out of the trade and
market imports.
