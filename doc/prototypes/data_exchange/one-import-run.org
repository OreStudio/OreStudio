* Summary

After the commit there is one question, and it is per family: what was written,
what was refused, what was skipped, and what a failure left behind. Four
journeys ask it about the same attempt record, so this is one screen in
different states. It also draws the difference the design decision turns on:
one atomic commit, or today's write per family.

It is throwaway. Open [[file:one-import-run/][the run]].

* Detail

** What it argues

- A family is a row. The record names every family, including the one that
  refused and the one that was skipped, and every part that crosses a service.
- The mechanism is visible on the screen. The toggle between "one commit" and
  "per-family writes" changes what a failure left behind: nothing, or every
  family written before it.
- The failed family is named. The design draft's reply carries the family and
  the counts; today's save does not, and the gap is printed where the family
  name would be.
- A retry is the same import, not a new file. The applied families do not run
  again, and the retry control sits on the record rather than on a workflow
  screen the person must find.
- The trade retry still doubles the trades. The seed's second run is drawn with
  its counts, because no trade is matched by its ORE id today.
- Who may read the run is scoped. The maker, the decider and the same party
  see it; another party's run is absent.

** Variants and states

=?case=onboard|seed|defect|feed|one-dataset|promote|party=,
=?state=applied|failed|partial|expired=,
=?mode=proposed|today=, =?fail=<part>=, =?attempt=1|2=. The bar switches the
trigger, the outcome, the mechanism and the attempt.

** What is real and what is not

The parts, the family names, the FX skip, the operation subjects and the
atomic-versus-per-family difference are the journeys' and the draft design's.
The ids, counts, timestamps, sample names and duplicate fixings are invented.
The per-family reply and the import record do not exist; the screen shows the
shape they would need.

** The verdict

Not signed off. The owner must answer the question this screen puts in the
middle: is a half-applied ORE file ever acceptable? The trade import says yes
by design and the conventions import says no, and the two cannot both be true
under one mechanism.
