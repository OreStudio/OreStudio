* Summary

This prototype draws the list of pricing engine configurations and the page of one, and nothing else. It invents no layout. The list is the standard list: breadcrumb, plural title, one sentence, Refresh, Workflows and Add, a search, three filters, the table, the pager and the four states. The page is the standard record page: the lead line, the access mark, the actions only for a person who may take them, and the tabs Details, Products, Parameters, Reports and History with Revert.

A party holds few official configurations, one for each cycle, and model risk signs them off. The prototype draws that. Each configuration has a status (approved, in review, draft or retired), and the one that is official for a cycle carries an *Official for* tag. An approved configuration offers *Propose a change* in place of *Edit*, and no *Delete*. A pending proposal shows as a banner and in the History tab. A draft edits and deletes as the standard says.

The two other additions are the two things a market-risk owner asks that the standard does not answer. The Products tab opens with a *coverage strip*: how many of the product types the reports that bind the configuration need have a row, and which do not. The Reports tab opens with the rule and the reach: a report needs a price for every product type in its portfolio, a change reaches the next run of each report named once it is approved, and a run already produced keeps what it ran with. It lists each report with its cycle, its portfolio, and what the configuration leaves unpriced for it.

The page answers to the journey [[id:386DD47E-DA9E-4EA7-B925-852048EE47B7][See the pricing engines a party holds]]. It is throwaway: it reads no service, writes nothing to one, and states which parts of the interaction no operation supports. It follows the [[id:2BF7F03F-6BF4-45FF-8E26-4D3B4E2D943D][Record screen standard]] and the rules in [[file:../index.org][Prototypes]]: plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript, no framework, no build and no backend, with the design tokens of =projects/ores.web/packages/web/src/styles.css=.

* Detail

** The question the screen answers

"Which engine prices my swaptions, and which of my reports use it?" The screen answers it in two reads: the list, to find the configuration, and the page, to read what it prices with and who binds it. The coverage strip answers the third question, what is left unpriced.

** States and switches

- =?screen=list= and =?screen=record&id=c1|c2|c3&tab=...=. =c1= is the approved Intraday pricing, official for the intraday cycle, with eleven products and two reports. =c2= is the approved EOD pricing, official for the EOD cycle, with eleven products, four reports, four versions each signed off by three approval parts, and a proposal for version 5 that waits for sign-off. =c3= is a draft that no report binds.
- =?state=all|empty|error|loading= for the four list states.
- =?actor=viewer|analyst|admin=: a viewer reads, an analyst adds and edits, and only the administrator deletes. An action a person may not take is not drawn.
- Delete is offered on a draft only. A report that binds a draft is refused, and the page says that no service refuses it today.
- On an approved configuration, *Propose a revert* is offered on any version older than the one in force, and writes its values as a new proposal. On a draft, *Revert* writes them back as a new version.

** What is fake

Every value. The product and report counts in the list are fixtures, because no operation returns a count for a list row. The traded product types are a fixture, because no operation returns them. The reports on the Reports tab are names, because no read lists the reports that bind one configuration. The status, the cycle, the sign-off, the proposal and the portfolios of the reports are fixtures: no operation holds them. The first version of each official configuration is marked as provisioned by the seed, with no sign-off. The product, model and engine rows are real triples counted from the ORE example files, and the settings of the Bermudan swaption are the ones most example files carry.

** Where the code lives

=doc/prototypes/reporting/see-the-pricing-engines-a-party-holds/index.html=. One file, plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript, with the style and the script inline, no modules, no fetch and no build step.

** What it argues

- The History tab draws the shared timeline as the [[id:BD492939-118E-4B55-AE5B-300F7A1C3B41][Entity screen specification]] draws it: an event chart split by kind, a rail grouped by day with the time in the left gutter, one header line for each entry, the note on its own line, a collapsed detail, the current-version emblem and the *Hide system activity* toggle. The prototype does not draw batches of entries that are one act, and the toggle is always on, because the fixture has no reader without the account permission.
- The list is drawn as the record screen standard draws it, without a variation.
- The coverage strip belongs on the Products tab, above the table, and states the products left unpriced by name.
- The Reports tab states the rule and the reach before it lists a report.
- An approved version does not change in place. The screen offers a proposal, and shows a pending one.
- The list says that the official configurations are few, and marks which one is official for each cycle.

** The verdict

None yet. This page is on the branch that proposes it and waits for review.

** What it does not settle

- Whether the list columns product count and report count are worth a second read per row. The prototype draws them; a build can drop them.
- Whether the coverage strip should also appear on the list, as a count per row. A configuration that leaves a needed product unpriced is the one a person wants to find first.
- Whether a party may hold more than one official configuration for a cycle. The prototype draws one, and marks it. Model risk decides.
