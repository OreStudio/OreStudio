#+title: ores.iam.external_identity
#+description: Codegen entity model for the IAM external_identities table: the link from an ORE account to the provider account that signs it in.
#+type: ores.codegen.entity
#+component: iam
#+filetags: :model:entity:iam:identity:
#+entity_singular: external_identity
#+entity_plural: external_identities
#+entity_title: External Identity
#+brief: A link from an ORE account to a provider account.
#+created: 2026-11-25
#+updated: 2026-11-25

The link from an ORE account to one account at one identity provider, and the
record that the person proved it. The provider names the person by an issuer and
a subject; the subject is opaque, stable at the provider, and the only thing ORE
trusts to identify that provider account.

The address the provider stated at the moment of linking is kept beside the
subject, with whether the provider said it was verified. It is evidence of what
the person linked, not an identity: nothing in ORE looks a person up by it, and
no link is ever made because two addresses match. A person's address at a
provider can change after the link, so the column records a fact at a time and
is never treated as the current truth.

Two invariants decide the shape. The pair (issuer, subject) names one provider
account, and with the tenant it is the natural key, so the same provider account
links to one ORE account in a tenant. And a link belongs to an account: an
account may hold several links, one per provider, and keeps at least one way in
at all times, which its own operations enforce.

The link's surrogate key, which the screen carries when a person removes a link.

** issuer
:PROPERTIES:
:type:         text
:cpp_type:     std::string
:natural_key:  true
:END:

The issuer identifier of the provider, as the ID token stated it, such as
=https://accounts.google.com=. It is kept on the link as well as on the provider
so that the identity of a provider account survives an edit to the provider's
own issuer.

#+begin_src cpp :name generator
std::string("https://accounts.google.com")
#+end_src

** subject
:PROPERTIES:
:type:         text
:cpp_type:     std::string
:natural_key:  true
:END:

The provider's own identifier for the person, the ID token's =sub= claim. It is
opaque, it is stable at the provider, and it is the only thing that identifies
the provider account. Google scopes it to the client that asked, so the same
person at two tenants of one deployment holds two different subjects.

#+begin_src cpp :name generator
std::string("113456789012345678901")
#+end_src

:PROPERTIES:

The ORE account the provider account signs in. The link is the account's, and
removing it changes nothing else about the account.

** identity_provider_id
:PROPERTIES:
:type:     uuid
:cpp_type: boost::uuids::uuid
:END:

The provider the link was made through. It is what the door looks the link up by
once the flow names a provider, and it is what the screen shows as the provider's
name.

** email
:PROPERTIES:
:type:     text
:cpp_type: std::string
:nullable: true
:END:

The address the provider stated at the moment of linking. Kept as evidence of
what the person linked: it is never used to find an account, and it is never
refreshed silently.

** email_verified
:PROPERTIES:
:type:     boolean
:cpp_type: bool
:nullable: false
:default:  false
:END:

Whether the provider stated that the address was verified at that moment. A link
is only made on a verified address, so this is true for every row the door
writes; it is kept because the answer was a condition of the link, and the
record should say what was true then.

#+begin_src cpp :name generator
true
#+end_src

** linked_at
:PROPERTIES:
:type:     timestamp with time zone
:cpp_type: std::chrono::system_clock::time_point
:nullable: false
:END:

When the person linked the provider account, either by registering through it or
by proving their ORE account first. The screen shows it, because a person
recognises a link by when they made it.

#+begin_src cpp :name generator
std::chrono::system_clock::now()
#+end_src

** last_used_at
:PROPERTIES:
:type:     timestamp with time zone
:cpp_type: std::chrono::system_clock::time_point
:nullable: true
:default_value: {}
:END:

When the link last signed the person in, empty until it first does. It tells a
person which of their links they actually use, and it tells an administrator
which links no longer serve anyone.

#+begin_src cpp :name generator
std::chrono::system_clock::time_point{}
#+end_src
