* What

Account for every relation the installation holds, and not only the ten the
catalogue names.

Measured on this environment on 2026-11-28 with =compass sql=:

| What | Figure |
|------+--------|
| The database | 1672 MB |
| Relations in =public= | 846 |
| Stores in the housekeeping catalogue | 10 |
| Catalogue stores that are relations | 6 (five chunked tables, one table) |
| TimescaleDB hypertables | 26 |
| Hypertables the catalogue does not name | 22 |
| Bytes the catalogued relations hold | about 380 MB of the 1672 MB |

The twenty-two are not stragglers: they are the four telemetry tables, the IAM
session and token tables, =ores_compute_grid_samples_tbl= and its node and work
totals, =ores_marketdata_market_fixings_tbl= and its observations, and the
scheduler's own job instances and retained-store samples.

The largest relations the catalogue does not name are plain tables rather than
hypertables, and static: the IP-to-country map holds 96 MB, its DQ artefact
another 94 MB, the legal entities 41 MB, the images 34 MB and the counterparties
32 MB.

* Why

The story's acceptance said an installation ships no store without a default
bound. Ten stores do not make that true: most of the bytes sit in relations no
catalogue row names, so the screen's "8 of 10 bounded" is a statement about a
tenth of the database.

Nothing else bounds them. =timescaledb_information.jobs= holds one job on this
environment and it is the server's own job-history retention: the
=add_retention_policy= calls in the telemetry and report schemas run only under
the paid licence and are skipped otherwise, and the sweep visits only the
catalogue's rows. A hypertable that is not a store is therefore kept for ever.
