* Goal

A person watching a run sees, at every moment, what each step is, how far into
it the work has come, and what it has done so far; a person reading a run that
has rested sees the same narrative, and why it stopped if it did.

The capability belongs to the workflow component, because every workflow in the
system needs it, and the setup journeys are its first consumer. Today a step
answers once, when it ends: a long step shows nothing while it runs, and what it
did on the way is written to a service log that no user will ever read.

* Acceptance

- Every step states what it is doing in the reader's words from the moment it
  is dispatched: a name, a description, and the arguments it acts on.
- A step that is working states how far it has come, as a completed count
  against a total, or says that the total is not yet known.
- A step's work is readable while it runs and after it rests: an ordered
  journal of what happened, each entry carrying its level.
- A step that delegates to another run names that run, and that run's own
  progress is visible without the nested run's record being copied.
- Nothing about progress or the journal reaches the audit trail or the history
  of the step's own entity: progress is a register and the journal is a log,
  and neither is a business fact.
- A run that has rested keeps its journal, and the diagnostics that explain a
  failure land with the step's state rather than only on the live channel.
- Every sentence a person reads is either a fixed sentence the client may
  translate or a key with values, never a sentence with values baked into it.

* Plan

The design is one distinction. Four things travel with a step, they have four
different lifetimes, and today they share one home: the step's own versioned
row, written once, at the end.

| What | Lifetime | Home | Audited |
|------+----------+------+---------|
| State: the step's machine, its result, its error, its timings | transition-driven, must survive a restart | the step's temporal row, as today | yes |
| Context: what the step is, what it will do to the reader's data, what it waits on, whether it can be retried | fixed when the run starts | the run's materialised step snapshot, which already carries the name and description | yes, with the run |
| Progress: how far into the step | overwritten while the step works, worthless once it rests | a latest-value register keyed by step | no |
| Journal: what happened, in order | appended while the step works and kept after it rests | an append-only log with a per-step sequence | no |

*The rules the split gives.* A step's four things are stored once each, and
never in two places. The engine is the only writer of a step's record:
progress and journal entries reach it as events, because a handler that wrote
directly would put two writers on one step and let a service claim an instance
or a tenant the engine knows better. Nothing a person needs after the fact
travels only on the live channel, so the diagnostics that explain a failure
land with the terminal state in the same write, as they do today; the live
channel carries the narrative of work in flight, and losing its last line is
acceptable. Progress is never audited: it is a reading about work in flight,
and a versioned history of every tick would swamp the history and make the
audit trail say something it does not mean.

*The shapes.* Progress is one non-temporal row per step — the step, its
instance and tenant, a completed count, a total where zero means indeterminate,
a key with its values, and when it was last written — overwritten in place.
The journal is append-only rows in sequence, each carrying its tenant, instance
and step, the time, a level that separates narration from warning and error, a
key with its values, and a message. The read that a page already polls carries,
per step, the state as it does today, the context from the run's own snapshot,
the live progress, and the tail of the journal; a second read pages the whole
journal for a run that has rested, by sequence, so a client can fetch only what
it has not seen.

*The channels.* Progress and journal entries are published while a step works
and the engine records them, coalesced: one write a second, or one per
threshold crossed, rather than one per item, because a step that reports per
party would otherwise write a row per party. They are best effort, which is why
the authoritative diagnostics stay with the completion. The read is polled,
as it is today; the journal is the one thing here that would push cleanly,
because it has a sequence and a cursor.

*Delegation answers itself.* A step that waits on another run — the bundle
publication is one, and it waits on a run of thirty-seven steps — states that
run as part of its context. The nested run is already a run with its own steps
and its own read, and the wait already counts them, so nothing is duplicated:
the page either follows that run or says how far into it the step has come.

*Where it lives.* The entities, the widened step contract and the widened read
belong to the workflow component, which owns runs and steps. The journeys
consume them, and this story is not part of the setup journeys' work.

*The order.* The two stores and their channels first, engine-written and
coalesced, with the read carrying progress and the journal tail. Then the step
contract: a progress call and a note call beside the calls that end a step, and
the completion's per-item entries absorbed into the journal. Then the context in
the snapshot — the arguments rendered, what the step waits on, whether it can be
retried — most of which is a rendering of what the definition and the starting
point already declare. Then the screen: a running step shows its progress and
its latest line, and a rested or failed run expands into its journal.

* Decisions

- Four things travel with a step, and each has one home: state in the step's
  temporal row, context in the run's materialised snapshot, progress in a
  latest-value register, and the journal in an append-only log.
- The engine is the only writer of a step's record. Handlers report; they do
  not write the step.
- Progress is never audited, versioned or replayed. It is a reading about work
  in flight, and the step's outcome replaces it.
- Nothing a person needs after the fact travels only on the live channel.
- The live channels are coalesced and best effort; the durable write happens
  once, with the outcome.
- A step that delegates to another run states which run it waits on instead of
  restating that run's progress.
- The capability belongs to the workflow component, not to a journey. Every
  workflow needs it, and the setup journeys are its first consumer.
- Text a person reads is either a fixed sentence, which a client may translate
  by looking up the sentence, or a key with its values. A sentence with values
  baked into it cannot be translated and is not written.

* Open questions

These are settled when the story is broken into tasks; each names the answer I
would argue for.

1. Does the journal absorb the per-item entries a completion already carries,
   or sit beside them? Two homes for one thing is how they drift, so I would
   absorb them: one store, one read, and the completion still appends in the
   same write as the terminal state.
2. Who coalesces progress, the engine or the handler, and by rate or by
   threshold? I would coalesce by rate in the engine, because the engine is the
   single writer and can see the churn it is creating, and a handler that
   forgets to throttle should not be able to melt the store.
3. Is a journal kept for the life of the run, or purged on a policy? Its value
   is support, and support has a shorter life than the audit trail the run
   keeps.
4. Does the journal push, or is it polled with the rest? It has a sequence and
   a cursor, so it is the one thing here that pushes cleanly; polling first
   costs nothing.
5. Does the screen state run-level progress — the step the run is on out of the
   steps it declares — or is the rail's own shape enough?
6. When a step waits on a run, does the screen follow that run, state how far
   into it the step has come, or both?

* Out of scope

- The journey screens that consume this: the setup journeys own their own
  screens, and this story owns what those screens can read.
- Backfilling journals for runs that have already happened.
- The engine's change events: a run's own event payload stays as it is, and
  the progress read, not the event, remains the contract a page follows.
- A general telemetry pipeline. The journal is about one step of one run, and
  the service logs stay where they are.
