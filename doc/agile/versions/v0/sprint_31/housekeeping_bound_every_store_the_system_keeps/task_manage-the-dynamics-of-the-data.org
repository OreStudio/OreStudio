* Acceptance

- Every relation is measured once a day and the series is kept, so a table's growth can be read and not only its size
- The tables screen states each table's growth over the window it read, and says when it has no history to state
- The housekeeping summary states the database's size beside the size the catalogue bounds, and the former 'Total size' no longer reads as the whole
- The tables screen is reached from a control on the housekeeping screen and from the dashboard, and the dashboard states the largest tables
- A tenant administrator cannot read the installation's relations, and the screen says so rather than failing
- The series is itself a store in the catalogue with a period, so measuring everything does not create the thing nothing bounds

* Result

The screens now state what the data is doing and not only what it holds. Every
relation is measured once a day into a series that is itself a catalogue store
with a 90-day period; the tables page states each table's growth over a week,
its size, its share, and the store that names it; the housekeeping summary states
the database's size beside the size the catalogue bounds; and the dashboard
states the largest tables beside the disk they fill.

Three things the work found and did not settle.

- A tenant's session is answered 401 =not-authenticated= by every housekeeping
  read, before the gate this task added is reached. That is captured: the status
  misleads a screen and hides the design question behind a defect.
- The growth column states nothing until the first two daily firings exist, and
  it says so rather than stating a zero.
- What a table's *kind* of growth means — reference data kept by design, a series
  that needs a period, a versioned table whose growth is churn, scratch to be
  released — is the vocabulary a person needs before a policy can be proposed
  from a table. That is the next task, not this one.
