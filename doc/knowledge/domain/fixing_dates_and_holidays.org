#+title: A fixing date follows the calendar of the rate it observes
#+description: Fixing holidays belong to the observed rate, not to the coupon; an NDF derives its fixing date from its delivery.

* Summary

A fixing date follows the holiday calendar of the rate it observes, not the
calendar of the coupon it settles. A non-deliverable forward is the clearest
case: the parties agree its delivery date, and its fixing date is derived from
it, early enough to book and settle the cash difference, and must respect the
holidays of both the fixing and the settlement currencies. A fixing on a
holiday is normally refused, and any exception needs a recorded reason.

* Detail

** Fixing holidays and coupon holidays

A floating leg generates its fixing schedule from its payment periods, its
fixing period, its day count and its fixing source. Its fixing dates are
adjusted on the calendar of the observed rate, which is the source's calendar,
and its payment dates on the calendar of the payment. A system therefore keeps
both calendars on the leg. The calendar machinery, and how a schedule is
generated from it, are in [[id:A960FE8E-56FA-4655-AA51-54C14882179B][Calendars, Holidays and Events]] and
[[id:DF9EFAF9-1340-48F2-BFD9-1F4ED0ADBC76][Date Rolling and Business-Day Calendars]].

** The fixing date of a non-deliverable forward

Traders agree the delivery date first and derive the fixing date from it, by
going back the spot lag of the settlement currency. Market participants process
holidays differently, so the convention is ambiguous, and it differs from the
convention for non-deliverable options. The fixing date must leave time to book
and settle the cash difference by the delivery date. A forward that reaches its
fixing date without a fixing date or a settlement currency cannot be fixed and
is valued as a deliverable forward until it is corrected. See
[[id:CD3E82C6-AE56-4D06-B949-0B3A5CE7A5FC][Deliverability and non-deliverable instruments]].

** Fixings on holidays

A fixing on a holiday of its source is normally refused, because the source
publishes nothing that day. Some desks allow it for a half-day market, and
whether they do may depend on the product. An override needs a recorded reason,
a sign-off, and an agreement with the counterparty on what value to use, since
the usual source has none. The administration of calendars is in
[[id:381A7747-2786-45E1-8C90-B42EC5A81472][Holiday Calendar Administration]].

** Chains of internal trades

When a risk is moved between books by a chain of internal non-deliverable
forwards, only the trade that faces the market should be fixed. The internal
links are marked so that they behave as deliverable forwards and generate no
fixing trades.
