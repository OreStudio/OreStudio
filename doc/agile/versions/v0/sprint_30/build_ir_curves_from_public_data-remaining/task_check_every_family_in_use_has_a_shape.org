* Goal

A family with no shape row is projected unguarded, by design, so the catalogue can grow family by family. Nothing says when it should have grown: a new family is written unguarded until someone thinks to state it. A check reads the families the stored identities use, by asset class, series subclass and index family, and names each one no shape row states. Raised by the review of PR #4821.

* Acceptance

- A pgTAP suite or a local check names every family in use that no shape row states, and fails when there is one.
- A family that is unguarded on purpose is listed as such, with its reason, so the check passes on a deliberate gap.

* Plan

The check lives in the shape suite, =marketdata_series_identity_shape_test.sql=,
beside the scan that holds each identity to its shape. It reads the stored
identities and the shapes directly rather than the backfill's =unstated_count=.
The count needs a backfill call and a service, and it says how many series fall
in an unstated family, not which family. The suite can name the family.

A family is keyed as the guard keys it: the identity's asset class, the series
subclass, and the identity's =index=, empty for a series. An identity the codec
could not read has no class, so it is no family and the check ignores it.

A deliberate gap is a row of =t_unguarded_on_purpose= in the suite, with its
reason. The list lives in the suite rather than in the shapes table, because a
gap is a decision about this check, and a shape row with no requirement would
also hide the family from the backfill's unguarded count.

The check must be able to fail, and a database whose families all have shapes
cannot show that. The suite copies the shapes it reads into a temporary table,
takes one family that is in use out of the copy, and asserts the check names
it. It then lists that family as a gap and asserts the check passes. A
database with no family in use yet skips both cases.

* Notes

The suite sees what row-level security lets its session see: the system
tenant's identities. A family only a tenant's own data uses is not checked here,
as the scan above it states for the same reason.

The projector already counts what this task asks about: =backfill_result::unstated= is
incremented for every series whose family no shape states, and the backfill response
does not carry it. The check is therefore mostly a reader, and it is taken after
[[id:15A60BBE-102B-40BA-8F6A-83E2FB92E96B][Report what the shape guard refuses]] rather
than beside it.

What the count says at =67803175ce=, which shapes the deliberate-gap list: the system
tenant holds 330 identities in 5 families and every one of them is stated, so the check
passes on today's data. The 66 unstated families in use are a test tenant's fixtures --
the synthetic series generator writes a faker URI and a drawn subclass -- so the list
is not a catalogue gap but a fixture gap, and the answer may be to fix the generator
instead of listing 66 entries. That is
[[id:474E8968-C7B4-4740-839F-9328B93A5FEA][Generate a series that is an identity]].

* Result

The shape suite now names every family in use that no shape row states, and
fails when there is one. A family left unguarded on purpose goes in the suite's
=t_unguarded_on_purpose= list with its reason, and the check then passes. There
is no such family today: the five families the system tenant uses all have
shapes.

Both acceptance points are met, and the suite proves each one. It takes a
family in use out of its copy of the shapes and asserts the check names it, and
it lists that family as a gap and asserts the check passes.
