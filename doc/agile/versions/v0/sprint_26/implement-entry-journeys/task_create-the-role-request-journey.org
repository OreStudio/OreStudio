* Goal

A journey document that answers the question registration leaves open. Registration gives a new account one default role; a person who needs more has today no way to ask, because [[id:3C753578-BC0E-433C-92B5-38031F050ADC][Change someone's access]] is an administrator acting on somebody else's record and [[id:550682A6-6AAC-4939-BB2B-80D53091E4FA][Ask for more access]] does not exist. The journey records how a person asks, who answers, what they see while they wait, and what closes the request.

It is deliberately its own journey and not a step of [[id:33B0C287-8262-4C2A-9F7B-3F74C06586AE][Sign up]]. A registration ends at a confirmation; a role request may be made at any time afterwards, it may be refused, and it is answered by somebody else. Folding it into the door would make one journey with two actors and two endings.

* Acceptance

- The journey document exists in the Access group and is named for the person's goal rather than for the entity, following [[id:CA94C762-B0C8-40E7-85CE-D4C6355023CC][Document type: user_journey]].
- It carries every required section: goal, actor and trigger, steps, screens and wireframes, entities composed, operations and messages, what is missing, and related journeys.
- It names who answers the request, what the requester sees while they wait, how a refusal is stated, and what closes the request.
- It states whether the request holds server-side state. A request that waits on a person is a [[id:FDF3C079-4E18-4DB7-911F-7691AD1F5665][workflow instance]]; one that is answered in the same session is not.
- The Access hub links it, and [[id:33B0C287-8262-4C2A-9F7B-3F74C06586AE][Sign up]] and [[id:0010510B-470E-4B67-98DC-B6DBF44E1680][Know what I may do]] link to it under * Related journeys*.
- Every operation it needs is marked =exists=, =partial= or =missing= against the generated protocol.

* Plan

1. Start from the person's goal: "I cannot do my job with the access I have."
2. Decide the shape with the product owner: a request that an administrator approves, or a request that self-service grants from a catalogue the tenant allows. The existing subjects bound what is possible: =iam.v1.roles.assign= needs =iam::roles:assign= and is an administrator's write, so a self-service grant is new server work.
3. Write the steps the person takes, then the steps the approver takes, as one journey with two actors or two journeys if that is what the design says.
4. Draw the wireframe, name the entities and the fields, and build the coverage table.
5. Record the gaps, including how the requester is told the answer and whether the account can hold a pending request at all.
6. Wire the document into the Access group hub and into the related journeys of the pages around it.

* Result

The journey is [[id:DF0FBED2-59C1-43DE-945F-D8839884C38D][Ask for more access]], in the Access group, with one
wireframe per actor.

- *The shape.* The product owner chose a request that a tenant administrator
  approves. A self-service grant from a tenant-approved catalogue was the
  alternative, and it was not taken.
- *One journey, two actors.* The request is one record with one lifecycle, and
  each actor owns one end of it. The screens stay apart, and the administrator's
  unprompted version of the same write stays
  [[id:3C753578-BC0E-433C-92B5-38031F050ADC][Change someone's access]].
- *The state.* A request waits on a person, so the document states that its
  state is a [[id:FDF3C079-4E18-4DB7-911F-7691AD1F5665][workflow instance]] in
  =ores.workflow=. That is the rule
  [[id:504863AB-C45A-47E8-BB77-AADCB620A305][The entry journeys]] and the
  user_journey contract already state, applied to a request rather than to a
  provisioning run.
- *The coverage.* Five of the ten subjects the journey needs exist:
  =iam.v1.roles.by-account=, =.list= and =.permissions= for the reads,
  =iam.v1.roles.assign= for the grant, and =workflow.v1.instances.steps= for the
  progress. The five request subjects are missing, and the workflow engine
  cannot yet hold a request that waits on a person.
- *Wired in.* The Access hub lists the journey and describes it, and
  [[id:33B0C287-8262-4C2A-9F7B-3F74C06586AE][Sign up]],
  [[id:3CA48F40-B601-497B-BA78-9822DBEBA8E5][Know what I may do]] and
  [[id:3C753578-BC0E-433C-92B5-38031F050ADC][Change someone's access]] each
  link to it under * Related journeys*.
- *A defect in the acceptance.* The criterion names =0010510B= as *Know what I
  may do*; that id is
  [[id:0010510B-470E-4B67-98DC-B6DBF44E1680][Register a service account]]. The
  link was added to [[id:3CA48F40-B601-497B-BA78-9822DBEBA8E5][Know what I may
  do]], which is the journey the criterion means.
