* Summary

A *cut* is the fixed population of deals and market data that one trading
valuation is run against at one timepoint. A cut is not defined by being daily or
by belonging to one centre: the EOD cut is the daily kind, and other kinds of cut
share the one defining property, a timepoint. The word carries a second sense in
FX. The *cut code* fixes the time of day at which an option expires. This page
defines both senses, the kinds of cut, the centres a cut is taken for, what a
cut holds, and the audit trail it leaves. The sourced material rests on the
public references listed under [[*References][References]]. Where a claim has no
public source we say so and mark it as our working convention. A later section
states where ORE Studio stands today, which is a named cut time on a currency
pair and not a cut.

* Two senses of the word

The word names two different things, and most of the confusion around cuts comes
from conflating them.

1. The *market data cut*. A fixed population of deals and market data at one
   instant, used as the input to official valuation for that day. Practitioners
   say "the cut", "the EOD cut", "the COB cut", or "taking a cut" for this
   sense.
2. The *option-expiry cut code*. A named time of day, such as a ten o'clock New
   York cut, that fixes when an FX option expires on its expiry date. See
   [[*Cut codes for option expiry][Cut codes for option expiry]].

/COB/ is close of business, the moment a given centre takes its cut. Some
systems say "snapshot" for the first sense. We do not. In this repository
"snapshot" names two reads over stored market data, described in
[[*Profiles and stored snapshots][Profiles and stored snapshots]], and "cut" names
only the two senses on this page.

* What a market data cut is

A market data cut is an immutable, timestamped capture of two things.

- The *deal population*. Every live deal as it stands at the instant, with its
  state, its book, and any manual correction applied before the cut was
  authorised. A linked trade and the trade it is linked to are captured
  together.
- The *market data*. Spots, interest rate curves, forward points, volatility
  surfaces, fixings, and any market-driven model parameters, as they stand at
  the instant, with any approved override applied on top of the raw feed.

Once authorised, a cut does not change. A later correction produces a new cut,
or a new version tracked separately from the authorised one.

A cut exists so that the day's numbers come from one agreed population. Two
reports run at two moments would otherwise read two different market data sets
and disagree in ways nobody can reconcile. We treat that reproducibility as our
requirement, and [[id:EDAE3C87-7733-4D0C-881F-5AA65DD832AC][Pricing Configuration]]
states it as a contract over the inputs of a valuation.

* The deal cut and the market data cut

The word names two components, and each is frozen by its own act.

- The *deal cut* freezes the deal population.
- The *market data cut* freezes the market data set.

Our working convention, with no public source behind it, is that the two are
taken and authorised independently and in this order. The deal cut is taken
first and retaken until the population is clean, because a person may still
correct a deal in production. The market data cut is taken after it, because a
provisional market data set is needed to value the deals and confirm they are
right before anybody commits the day's rates.

The design argument for keeping them separate is ours. Each has its own owner,
its own review, and its own failure. A corrected deal does not move a rate, and
an approved rate does not move a deal. A single act that froze both would make
every trade fix a re-approval of the day's market data.

* Kinds of cut

Each kind freezes the same shape of population. They differ in purpose, in what
they are built on, and in when they are authorised.

** End of day cut

The EOD cut is the primary, authoritative cut for a centre. It is the input to
the day's official revaluation, to the movement from yesterday's cut to today's,
and to the reports built on both. It is sometimes called the *independent cut*,
because approved rates replace the rates the front office used during the day.
That name should be used with care, because the independent price verification
cut below is the one that is independent by construction.

** Independent price verification cut

The IPV cut is built on the EOD cut. It keeps the EOD population and replaces
selected market data items with values sourced independently of the front
office. The purpose is to test the firm's own marks against data it did not
produce.

A prudential standard fixes what counts as an independent source. It accepts
exchange prices in a liquid market, trades in the same or a very similar
instrument, tradable quotes from brokers and other market participants,
consensus service data, indicative broker quotes, and counterparty collateral
valuations
([[https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/101/chapter/I][EBA RTS (EU) 2016/101 on prudent valuation]], Article 3). The same article
names the independent price verification process as the process whose market
data range those sources serve.

Which items a firm replaces is its own policy. The standard fixes the sources,
not the items. We define the *IPV explain* as the value difference between
valuing at EOD rates and valuing at IPV rates. That difference measures how much
of the day's value depends on the firm's own marks.

Our working convention is that the IPV cut is not one static capture. Coverage
improves as independent data arrives, so several versions may be produced while
the verification runs.

** Flash cut

A flash cut is a tentative cut that a valuation runs against before the day's
cut is authorised. The purpose is to test the market data and the deal
population while both can still be changed. The output is indicative and
lighter than the full run.

Our working convention, again with no public source, is a day with three flash
runs. A morning run near the start of the centre's trading, an afternoon run
before the EOD cut, and a final run against the authorised cut. The final run is
the one published.

** Start of day cut

Our working convention is a start of day cut taken at the opening of a centre.
It carries the previous EOD market data forward, so the new day starts from a
known reference and the day's movement is measured from it. The centre's opening
is a real, stated instant, since a settlement system publishes the start of its
business day
([[https://www.federalreserve.gov/paymentsystems/fedfunds_about.htm][Fedwire Funds Service, operating hours]]).

* The end of day process

No public source prescribes this sequence. We state it as our working
convention for what a cut must settle before it is authorised.

1. /Pre-requisite checks./ Every deal is in a state the day allows, the fixings
   are authorised, and the expiries and exercises are processed.
2. /Deal cut./ The population is frozen. Anomalous deals are corrected and the
   cut is retaken until clean.
3. /Market data cut./ The raw values are frozen, approved rates are applied on
   top, derived curves are realigned, and the checks that follow the cut run.
4. /Flash validation./ A valuation runs against the cut and is compared with the
   earlier flash runs. The exceptions are reported and signed off.
5. /Authorisation./ Finance signs off the rates. Market risk reviews the deals
   above its thresholds. The cut becomes official and immutable.
6. /The day's runs./ The full revaluation, the day-on-day explain, the IPV
   explain where an IPV cut exists, and the regulatory and management reports.

The first five steps settle the data and the sixth consumes it. A run that
starts before authorisation prices a candidate, and its output is not official.

** Approved rates and curve realignment

After the market data cut, finance replaces raw values with *approved rates*.
An approved rate is a rate signed off for official valuation. A central bank
shows the shape: the ECB publishes one reference rate per currency pair per
working day, at around 16:00 CET, after a concertation procedure among central
banks
([[https://www.ecb.europa.eu/stats/policy_and_exchange_rates/euro_reference_exchange_rates/html/index.en.html][ECB euro foreign exchange reference rates]]).
One official value per pair, published at a stated time, is what an approved
rate is.

An override record carries the asset or currency pair, the rates centre, the
tenor, the value, and the date. It also carries who proposed it, who reviewed
it, who approved it, and a mandatory comment.

Overriding one rate leaves its derived values inconsistent. Forward points imply
cross-currency rates through covered interest parity, so raising one currency's
rate tenor implies a change in the rates derived from it against another
currency. Those derived values must be recomputed, and that recomputation is
what *curve realignment* names. See
[[id:49687AD7-396A-4CD8-B96B-B3F6675C9779][Covered Interest Parity and FX Forward Pricing]].

* Multiple centres and rates centres

FX trades around the clock, so a firm runs a day per centre rather than one day
for all of them. Each centre's day has a stated start and end, and the
settlement system for its currency publishes them. Fedwire states its business
day as starting at 21:00 ET on the preceding calendar day and ending at 19:00
ET, with a deadline for third-party transfers at 18:45 ET
([[https://www.federalreserve.gov/paymentsystems/fedfunds_about.htm][Fedwire Funds Service, operating hours]]).

The same pair can therefore carry a different official rate at each centre,
because each centre applies its own approved rates for its own end of day. A
firm that holds one global rate per pair will misprice the deals booked at the
other centres.

In our model the qualifier sits on the entity that revalues, not on the rate. A
book carries a business centre, which is a coded identifier for a trading
location's holiday calendar and its rates centre, inherited down the business
unit tree and overridable at any node. See
[[id:DD828FB9-5901-4B9A-A767-71FD88472F0A][Business Centre]] and
[[id:D18DF500-2C6C-42FF-BBAE-D5A46D410910][Book groups and rates centres]]. The
one market data set per rates centre is the same idea stated from the data side.

Our working convention is that a trade booked after its logical centre's close,
an *after-hours* trade, is valued at the rates of the centre whose window it
belongs to and not the centre whose cut it lands in. The population is the same
and the market data set is not, so a cut has to be able to hold another centre's
market data beside its own.

* What a cut holds

A cut is a versioned, immutable bundle of the three parts below.

** The deal population

Every live deal at the cut instant, with its state, its book assignment, and the
manual corrections applied before authorisation. A linked trade and the trade it
links to are captured together. Each deal carries the state it held at the cut,
so a settled or expired deal is not read as a live one.

** The market data

| Item             | What is captured                                                  |
|------------------+-------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Spot rates       | One per currency pair, from an approved feed or an override       |
| Interest rates   | The discount curve per currency, as discount factors              |
| Forward points   | Per currency pair and tenor, consistent with interest rates       |
| Volatility       | The surface per pair, at its quoted tenors and deltas              |
| Fixings          | The authorised fixings whose date falls on or before the cut      |
| Model parameters | The market-driven parameters the pricing models calibrated from   |

** The metadata**

| Field             | Meaning                                                        |
|-------------------+----------------------------------------------------------------|
| Cut identity      | Unique, and unchanged once the cut is authorised               |
| Kind              | EOD, IPV, flash, or start of day                               |
| Trading centre    | The centre the cut belongs to                                  |
| Rates centre      | The qualifier for the rate values inside the cut               |
| Reference date    | The business date the cut represents                           |
| Cut instant       | When the cut was taken, with its timezone                      |
| Authorised by     | The person and the time of authorisation                       |
| Base cut          | For an IPV cut, the EOD cut it was built on                    |
| Version           | Incremented when a cut is retaken before authorisation         |

* Profiles and stored snapshots

Our working convention is that a cut is not built by a person choosing values.
It is built by resolving a set of rules against whatever the store holds at that
instant. The two names below describe that resolution.

A *profile* is such a set of rules. It maps a logical requirement to a physical
instance, so that a spot requirement resolves to one centre's approved rate and
a forward requirement resolves to one curve's bootstrapped pillars. A profile is
a declaration and holds no values.

A *snapshot* is the profile materialised at one instant, with every rule
resolved to a specific versioned item and the result given a stable identity.
That identity is what a later valuation replays. A *floating* snapshot is
re-evaluated as the reference instant advances. A *fixed* snapshot is pinned to
the values it resolved. An EOD cut is a fixed snapshot.

The word collides with ours. [[id:CEC7844C-B3AE-4858-BF67-71F511007F82][Market Data Snapshot]]
defines the resolution-side snapshot as a concept and records that neither ORE
nor Strata names one. [[id:4EE26B23-003B-4DF1-AA83-1196CC6810C3][Snapshots and cuts]]
defines the reads we serve over stored observations. Neither is a cut. A cut
needs both of them plus an authorisation, an identity, and a deal population of
its own.

* A book revalues against one cut

Our working convention is one EOD cut per book. The book's cut decides which
centre's rates produce its official revaluation, so a book managed from one
centre and booked at another should carry the managing centre. Changing a book's
cut is a governance act rather than a setting, because it changes every number
the book reports.

Whether a book could hold more than one cut is not settled here. Our model gives
a book one business centre and one rates centre, so one cut is the shape that
fits.

* Cut codes for option expiry

An FX option has an expiry date and an expiry time, and the cut code supplies the
time. The time matters in three ways. It fixes how much time value is left, which
drives the price near expiry and near the strike. A wrong code prices the option
systematically wrong. The volatility surface is quoted at cut-time tenors, so the
code and the surface have to agree.

We hold no public source that fixes these times. The times in circulation are
market convention, they differ by centre and by currency pair, and a firm's own
list is the only authority on what it trades. This page therefore records the
sense of the term and no table of times.

In our reference data the cut code is a property of the currency pair. The pair
carries a default, a trade may override it, and an override is worth flagging
because it changes which market data set the trade values against. See
[[id:CC6D86D8-33FC-41D6-AC27-F35FA942F436][Currency pair and currency entity fields]].
The field is designed and not yet built. See [[*Where ORE Studio stands][Where ORE Studio stands]].

* Audit, rerun and time travel

An immutable cut makes three things possible.

- A *historic rerun* re-executes a day's runs against that day's cut. The result
  is identical unless a model changed, and a model change is versioned
  separately.
- *Time travel* shows the market data as it stood at a past cut, with the
  overrides, who applied them, and why.
- The *change history* of every override is kept with the cut. The record holds
  the user, the instant, the value before, the value after, and the comment.

The requirement behind all three is ours and is already written down.
[[id:EDAE3C87-7733-4D0C-881F-5AA65DD832AC][Pricing Configuration]] names the
inputs a reproducible valuation needs, and a cut is the thing that would supply
two of them.

* Where ORE Studio stands

** We have an as-of read over stored observations

=marketdata.v1.curve_snapshot.get= returns the latest value of every node of one
composite object, taken at the service's own clock. The request carries no as-of
field, so there is no way to ask this read for a past instant. A node with no
point at or before the instant is a hole, not a carried-forward value. With
=include_provenance= the reply also says whether a point was quoted, derived or
manual, and for a derived point which recipe and which as-of built it. See
[[id:4EE26B23-003B-4DF1-AA83-1196CC6810C3][Snapshots and cuts]].

That read answers "what does the store hold now". It does not answer "what did we
use on Tuesday", because the instant is not a parameter.

** We have a definition of a resolution-side snapshot, not an implementation

[[id:CEC7844C-B3AE-4858-BF67-71F511007F82][Market Data Snapshot]] defines the
fixed and floating snapshot as the materialisation of a resolved market data
set, and says plainly that neither ORE nor Strata names the concept. No entity,
service or read in this repository implements it. The concept is documented and
the stored-data read is implemented. We should not read the note as a description
of running code.

** We have a cut as a named cut time, designed and not built

[[id:4B4ED152-1670-4E81-895A-24D747382D4C][Model market data cuts as proper ORE Studio reference data]]
designs a lookup entity for a cut: code, name, description, cut time and
timezone, referenced by =currency_pair.default_cut_code= and by anything else
that needs a cut time. The story records that no entity, table or foreign key
target exists today, and that =default_cut_code= was dropped from the currency
pair rather than kept as free text until something could be referenced.

The schema agrees. =ores_refdata_currency_pairs_tbl= carries no cut field, and no
table named for a cut exists
([[proj:projects/ores.sql/create/refdata/refdata_currency_pairs_create.sql][the currency pairs table]]).

The story designs a cut's identifying attributes. Those are the smallest part of
a cut. It does not design an authorisation, a population, or a stored market data
set.

** We do not model a cut as an immutable population

Nothing in this repository holds a frozen population of deals and market data
with its own identity, authorisation, and version. Deals live in versioned,
bitemporal rows, so a past state can be reconstructed by a query, but a query is
not a cut. It has no authorisation, nobody signed it, and its result can differ
between two calls as rows are corrected. The market data side is the same. The
as-of read gives the latest value per coordinate at an instant. A cut would have
to give the value that was used, fixed at the moment of authorisation.

So the two components above, the read and the reference entity, are both thinner
than what this page describes. Either could become the seed of a cut. Neither is
one.

** A report cannot yet name a cut

A report's market binding is one row per market role, holding the role and the
name of a market configuration, in
[[proj:projects/ores.sql/create/reporting/reporting_configuration_market_bindings_create.sql][the configuration market bindings table]].
It carries no cut and no as-of. The target in
[[id:FB8F701C-E56A-49A9-AA90-1B6BD0B02092][A report binds to a published curve, not to a name]]
replaces the free name with a publish record and a pinned as-of, so a run can say
which published set it priced from.

That decision leaves the cut as the dependency it is. A published set fixes which
curve and which instant, and it does not fix the deal population. So a report
bound to a published curve still cannot say which population it valued or which
cut authorised that curve for the day. Binding the population is a second
binding, and it needs a cut to point at. The reason a report cannot name a cut
today is not a missing field. There is no cut to name.

* What the sources establish

Three claims on this page have a public source. That an independent price
verification exists, and which market data sources count as independent, rests
on the prudential standard ([[https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/101/chapter/I][EBA RTS (EU) 2016/101]], Article 3). That an
official rate is published once per working day at a stated time rests on the
ECB reference rates. That one centre's business day has a stated open and close,
published by the settlement system for its currency, rests on the Fedwire
operating hours.

Everything else is either a definition of a term or marked as our working
convention. The order of the deal cut and the market data cut, the flash runs,
the start of day cut, the numbered end of day sequence, one cut per book, the
after-hours rule, and the cut times for option expiry have no public source we
could verify. They are written down here as the convention we work to, not as
established fact. A reader who needs the practice of a particular firm should
ask that firm.
