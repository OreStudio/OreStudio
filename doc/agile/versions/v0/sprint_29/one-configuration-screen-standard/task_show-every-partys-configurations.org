* Goal

A person who works in several parties sees every configuration of every
party their account works in, on Reporting -> Product pricing and on
Reporting -> Engine settings. The party filter offers those parties, and
narrows the list to one of them. A configuration of a party the account does
not work in is not read.

* Acceptance

- The party filter on both screens offers the parties =/api/session= answers
  as =availableParties=, and no other party.
- "All parties" shows the configurations of every one of those parties.
- Choosing one party shows only that party's configurations.
- A configuration whose party the account does not work in is absent.
- A session with no actor (a service context) reads what it read before.

* Plan

The party isolation policy on the configuration tables admits rows whose
=party_id= is in =app.visible_party_ids=, which the login mints as the
selected party and its descendants. An account that works in several parties
therefore reads only the acting party's rows, whatever the filter says.

- =ores_iam_actor_party_ids_fn()= answers the parties the session's account
  works in, read from =ores_iam_account_parties_tbl= for the session's tenant
  and actor, and NULL where there is no actor.
- A new codegen flag, =rls_account_party_isolation=, adds that set to the
  party isolation policy of the tables the two screens read.
- The tenant policy still applies, and the actor's set is the account's own
  parties, so nothing widens beyond them, and trading and other
  tree-scoped business data keep the subtree rule.

* Notes

** Root cause, proved on the running system

The list route reads every row its table admits; the party filter is applied
client side. The scope comes from the RESTRICTIVE party isolation policy,
whose set is =app.visible_party_ids=, minted at login and at party selection
as =ores_refdata_visible_party_ids_fn(tenant, party)= -- the selected party
and its descendants. Choosing ACME Corporation HK Ltd therefore admits HK
alone, even though the account works in five parties.

#+begin_src sql
set role ores_solid_dirac_reporting_service;
select set_config('app.current_tenant_id','<acme tenant>',false),
       set_config('app.current_party_id','<HK>',false),
       set_config('app.visible_party_ids','{<HK>}',false),
       set_config('app.current_actor','tenant_admin',false);
select party_id, count(*) from ores_reporting_configurations_tbl
 where valid_to = ores_utility_infinity_timestamp_fn()
   and configuration_type_code = 'pricing_engines'
 group by party_id;
-- before the fix: HK 8
-- after the fix : Plc 2, HK 8, UK 9, US 6
#+end_src

Through the API, as =tenant_admin= in HK, =GET /api/reporting/pricing-engines=
answered 6 rows, all HK; =GET /api/reporting/engine-settings= answered 2
rows, all HK. After the fix the first answers 23 rows across the four
operating parties and the second answers 7.

** Proved live in the browser

Signed in as =tenant_admin=, chose ACME Corporation HK Ltd, and drove both
lists with Playwright. A party the account does not work in, =ZZ Unlisted
Probe Ltd=, was created under Acme Corporation Plc with a pricing
configuration of its own. The filter offered the account's five parties and
not that one, "All parties" showed 23 configurations across the four
operating parties and did not show the probe, UK plc narrowed to its 9, and
engine settings narrowed from 7 to US Inc's 2.

** Left open**

The report runs a definition owns stay party-scoped, so the Reports screen
shows another party's definitions with no latest run. It needs its own
decision on the run tables, which this task did not take.
