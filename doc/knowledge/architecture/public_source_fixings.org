#+title: Public sources publish daily fixings through an adapter
#+description: The architecture of the public market data sources: each publisher is a provider, an adapter fetches what they published and sends it as durable fixing ticks, the ingest stores them in a class of their own, and a feed starts from the latest one.
#+type: knowledge
#+level: cross
#+filetags: :marketdata:adapter:fixing:calibration:
#+created: 2026-10-23
#+updated: 2026-10-28

* Summary

Central banks and other public bodies publish rates every day. Each of them is a
market data provider like Bloomberg or LSEG, with its own vendor ids and its own
terms. One adapter, the public sources adapter, fetches what they published and
sends it as ticks over the adapter contract, as the simulator does. The values
are official fixings, so they are durable: the ingest keeps them in a tick class
of their own, with no age gate and no expiry. When a synthetic feed starts, it
reads the latest fixing on or before the date of the market clock, so a feed
that stops and starts again takes the most recent published value. The catalogue
starts wide, with many providers that publish the same pairs, so the case of one
pair from several providers is exercised from the first day.

* Detail

** The shape

#+begin_example
 publishers (ECB, Bank of England, Bank of Canada, FRED, IMF, ...)
        |  fetchers (Frankfurter first; Treasury and overnight rates later)
        v
 public sources adapter  -- announce, demand, tick, heartbeat -->  ores.marketdata
        (backfill on start, then daily)                              | map vendor id -> item
                                                                     | durable class: no age gate
                                                                     v
                                                     durable tick table (kept for good)
                                                                     ^
                                                                     |  latest on or before, window
                                                     synthetic feed at start (calibration)
#+end_example

- The adapter knows nothing of stochastic processes. It publishes what it
  fetched, in the vendor's own notation.
- The service knows nothing of HTTP. It maps, checks, stores and delivers, as
  for any adapter.
- A feed never fetches. It reads the store. When no fixing is stored, the feed
  keeps its fixed level and the screen says so.

** Each publisher is a provider

A publisher is a =provider= of kind =reference=, with a source of kind =live=.
The data is real, so nothing marks it as simulated. The provider owns its vendor
ids, and a =market_data_vendor_instrument= names each value in the publisher's
own notation. A =market_data_mapping= maps it to an oresmd item. Nothing new is
needed for this: it is the pattern of every vendor.

The design is generic. The catalogue is data, so a new publisher is a row and a
set of vendor ids, not code. A fetcher is code, and one fetcher serves many
publishers: Frankfurter alone reaches 106 providers.

** The item is an FX fixing

A central bank's daily rate is an official fixing taken once a day, not a quote
of the live market. Its item is an FX index, the ORE index =FX-SOURCE-CCY1-CCY2=:

#+begin_example
oresmd://fx/EUR?type=fixing&index=fx&source=ECB&ccy=USD     (FX-ECB-EUR-USD)
oresmd://fx/EUR?type=fixing&index=fx&source=BOE&ccy=USD     (FX-BOE-EUR-USD)
#+end_example

The publisher is part of the identity, because two banks' fixings of one pair
are two numbers taken at two times. So EUR/USD from the ECB, the Bank of England,
the Bank of Canada and the IMF are four items. Each is a series of its own
provider. A pair covered by several publishers is the duplicate case: the
calibration has several fixings for one pair and chooses by a rank, and the
screens and the demand show several providers for one underlying.

The item being a fixing also keeps the durable rule apart from live FX spot.
The tick class is keyed by the instrument type, so the fixings can be durable
while the spot quotes stay perishable.

** A durable class with its own table

Today the ingest drops a tick whose vendor time is older than its age limit, and
a tick older than the newest one of its vendor id
(=adapter_tick_translator.cpp=). A fixing published yesterday at 16:00 CET, and
every day of a backfill, would be dropped. The durable tier of
[[id:6F3F1A80-5B4C-4F33-ADF0-F42CDE8FF8C2][The tick ingest pipeline]] is the
answer, and the fixings are its first class:

- The age gate does not apply.
- A backfill arrives in order, oldest first, for each vendor id, so the order
  rule still holds.
- Conflation is =none=: each daily value is kept.
- The class has its own points table, of the same shape as the ticks, because
  "a class with a different retention needs its own table". It is kept for good.
  The volume is small: a publisher gives a few dozen rows a day.

** The public sources adapter

One service speaks the adapter contract for every public source. It announces,
answers its demand set, sends a heartbeat, and publishes ticks.

- *Fetchers.* A fetcher reads one access route. Frankfurter is the first. The
  US Treasury par curve, SOFR, SONIA and €STR are later fetchers, for the
  curves. The fetcher takes its transport as an interface, so its tests run on
  stored responses with no network.
- *One publisher a request.* The Frankfurter fetcher asks for one provider at a
  time, =/v2/providers/<key>/rates=, and never for the blended rate. The blended
  rate is Frankfurter's own sum and hides which bank the number came from.
- *Backfill, then daily.* On start the adapter fetches the window that the
  calibration needs for each demanded item, oldest first. After that it polls
  each publisher once a day, after its publication time, from the last day it
  sent.
- *HTTPS.* The only outbound HTTP client today, in =ores.storage=, takes
  =http://= alone. The adapter gets an HTTPS GET built on the Boost.Beast and
  OpenSSL that the build already links.
- *The address is a setting.* The hosted Frankfurter is a convenience. A team can
  point the adapter at its own Frankfurter in Docker without a change of code.

** Terms

Every provider has terms, Bloomberg and LSEG included, so the terms are columns
of =provider=: the terms page, the credit it asks for, whether it allows the
values to be stored, when the terms were accepted, by whom, and the version of
the terms. The system administrator accepts or declines each public provider in
the first run, and again when its terms change. See the task
[[id:4DFF1284-F24A-4815-B723-B7A22B4815E8][Show the public sources' terms in the first run]].

The adapter never fetches for a provider whose terms are not accepted. A
provider with no terms page of its own, 65 of the 106 that Frankfurter lists, is
not offered until someone reads its own site and records what it says.

** Demand

The adapter answers a demand set like any adapter. The system party subscribes
to each public provider and routes to its source, so the items it calibrates
from are in the demand. A tenant may subscribe too, and reads the fixings
through its own series and its entitlement, as for any source.

** The reader

The reader has two questions: the latest fixing of an item on or before a date,
and the fixings of an item over a window. Each answer names the provider, the
vendor id, the vendor time and the time it was received. It follows the snapshot
reader that already returns the latest tick at or before an instant, on the
durable table.

The calibration is its own task: a feed's config names the fixing items it may
start from, with a rank, and a policy (=latest_on_or_before_clock= or
=exact:<date>=). See [[id:A4165C70-0EAB-483D-8055-C1D8BF543113][Calibrate the synthetic ticks from free public sources]].

** Coverage at the start

Frankfurter lists 106 providers, 41 of them with a terms page. Among those 41,
USD, EUR, GBP, JPY, CHF and CAD are each quoted by six or more: the ECB, the Bank
of England, the Bank of Canada, FRED (H.10), the IMF, the Bank of Japan, Norges
Bank, the Czech National Bank and others. The first catalogue takes every daily
provider of the 41 whose terms allow storage, and so covers the main pairs
several times over. The list was read from =/v2/providers= on 2026-10-28.

Three of the providers are not daily: the BIS is monthly, the US Treasury
reporting rate quarterly, and Japan Customs weekly. They wait for the class
policy to say how stale a monthly fixing may be.

** Secrets

A credential is never in a table, a log or a stored URL. A provider row holds a
=credential_ref=, such as =env:ORES_MARKETDATA_FRED_API_KEY=, and a
=secret_source= interface resolves it. The first implementation reads the
service environment, as the database passwords are read. Frankfurter needs no
key.

** How this design changed

- *2026-10-23: a table of its own.* The first design loaded the public values
  into a new table, =published_quote=, with a publisher catalogue, a series
  mapping and a fetch and load provenance: five new entities beside the market
  data service.
- *2026-10-28: the existing tables.* Before any of it was built, the question
  was asked whether the existing tables carry it. The catalogue, the vendor
  mapping and the DQ pipeline held four of the five. An H.10 dataset already
  travels the DQ path into =market_observation= and the feeds read it as a
  vintage.
- *2026-10-28: an adapter.* DQ is for canned data, a snapshot that stays fixed.
  A public source publishes every day, and a feed that starts again should take
  the newest value. So a public source is an adapter that publishes ticks, the
  publisher is a provider, and the values are durable fixings. The new parts
  are the durable class, the adapter, the provider terms and the reader. DQ
  keeps the canned vintages.
