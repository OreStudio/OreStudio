#+title: A volatility surface follows the curve from state to published set
#+description: The path of a synthetic FX volatility surface, mapped step by step onto the IR curve's: generation config, generator kind, contributors, quotes, ingest, surface blueprint, calibration, candidate, publication and the ORE export.
#+type: knowledge
#+level: cross
#+filetags: :marketdata:synthetic:architecture:volatility:
#+created: 2026-11-03
#+updated: 2026-11-03

* Summary

A synthetic FX volatility surface takes the path an IR curve takes, and every
step has its counterpart. A generation config holds a ladder and process
parameters; a generator behind a kind string of its own advances a hidden state
and prices quotes from it; the quotes cross the adapter seam as ticks on oresmd
series; a blueprint constructs an object from them, checks it and writes a
candidate with lineage; a four-eyes publish makes the candidate authoritative;
and a report binds the published set, which the ORE export writes at the
boundary. Two steps differ. Synthetic contributors stand between the state and
the quotes, and a surface is /calibrated/ to all its quotes at once where a
curve is /bootstrapped/ pillar by pillar.

The rows marked "the same" are the point of the map. The surface reuses the
seam, the store, the publication and the binding as they stand.

** Generation

A surface generator joins the simulator by the edits of
[[id:B7A03C80-6566-4E97-9D45-14A69726C381][Adding an asset class]]: a codegen
entity model for its config, a builder in the generator factory, one kind
registration, a permission and its grant, and catalogue rows for each provider
that quotes surfaces. The runner, the subject builders, the adapter contract and
the ingest loop need no edit
([[id:D67C930B-261F-480B-B5EF-020C6D617333][The generator factory and the kind seam]]).

The config holds child rows, as the IR curve config does: the pair and the
refdata =fx_volatility_config= of the surface, which names its
=fx_option_convention=; template rows for the expiries and strike labels; process
parameter rows against a parameter vocabulary; the correlation rows between the
shocks, spot included; and contributor rows, each naming a provider and a noise
profile. Which processes the state uses, and why, is the subject of
[[id:BB2B7C4C-FD4E-4DCA-BF1D-00F929622B87][Synthetic market data generation: approach]].
Where the starting quotes come from is
[[id:099697A6-8526-48E0-A63D-DB3932C06468][A derived source publishes what it infers from stored fixings]].

** Contributors

A curve's quotes are its pillars, so the IR producer publishes them directly. A
surface's quotes reach the seam through synthetic contributors that observe the
state with noise of their own, as
[[id:D4C7C4A7-CD2D-4307-AB06-DA9BE3C3A334][A synthetic contributor publishes noisy observations of a latent surface]]
describes. Each contributor is a provider; the clean one is the truth.

** Quotes

Each quote is named by its oresmd URI. The surface is one series, and a point
adds the two axes, the expiry and the strike label:

: oresmd://fx/EUR?type=series&instrument=fx_option&quote=rate_lnvol&ccy=USD
: oresmd://fx/EUR?type=quote&instrument=fx_option&quote=rate_lnvol&ccy=USD&expiry=1Y&strike_label=25RR

The schema row exists, and a =strike_label= names every point of a delta ladder:
=ATM=, a delta call or put, a risk reversal, a butterfly, or a level
([[id:C3E053CA-0D4B-480B-9119-E11530160EC1][oresmd: ORE Studio Market Data URI]]).
The quoting convention is not in the URI. It is the pair's
=fx_option_convention=, so every source quotes in it, and that is what lets a
vendor replace a synthetic contributor.

** Construction

[[id:446F28DF-155A-47EC-8D66-FDB90A8FBFA7][Bootstrapping a curve: from quotes to a published set]]
is followed step for step.

1. *The recipe.* A surface blueprint names the pair, the series it reads with a
   weight per contributor, its expiries and strike labels, its conventions, its
   smile model and its output series. The smile model is the tenant's: SSVI,
   SABR per expiry, a spline or another
   ([[id:5B513D53-5DF7-4540-99C0-60D909678873][FX Volatility Surface]]).
   The settings are those of
   [[id:F439271D-F8CD-4A6E-A9FE-E523A4419846][User Journey: Author a surface's framework]].
2. *The inputs.* Each pillar reads its series by oresmd identity at or before the
   as-of, as
   [[id:7AE57098-08D4-4D4A-83D6-918FE520B68E][A pillar names the market series it reads]]
   requires.
3. *The calibration.* The smile model is calibrated to all the quotes at once,
   as a desk calibrates its smile model to the day's market. A curve has an
   order in which one pillar's solve feeds the next, which is what makes its
   method a bootstrap; a surface has none, so its method is a calibration. Both
   are /construction/. A butterfly quoted as a broker strangle is matched as a
   strangle price rather than as a smile volatility.
4. *The checks.* The calibrated surface is checked for calendar-spread and butterfly
   arbitrage ([[id:3F7A2C91-84BE-4E12-B5D0-1D6E5F08A432][Vol Surface No-Arbitrage Conditions]]),
   and the calibration error of each point is recorded beside the checks.
5. *The candidate.* The calibration writes the calibrated volatilities on the blueprint's
   grid as observations of its output series, at the as-of, each with a lineage
   record naming the source series and the blueprint version. The blueprint is
   the series' only writer, and the series' identity says its points are
   inferred; the identity is described in the oresmd note.
6. *Publication.* The four-eyes act of a curve makes the candidate a published
   set.

The trader's side of these steps is the journeys
[[id:89537A61-5C48-4A8D-AE3A-8DD4069433DF][Calibrate or refresh a surface]],
[[id:F2E3F72C-60B9-4D23-A51F-B426B481470D][Inspect a surface's slices]],
[[id:28D1545F-1BE8-4C14-8B69-292DC12BC45F][Watch a curve or surface evolve]] and
[[id:478EFA03-AB47-4286-A3CE-583C7438632D][Adjust a curve or surface by hand]].
** Consumption

A report binds the published set as
[[id:FB8F701C-E56A-49A9-AA90-1B6BD0B02092][A report binds to a published curve]]
describes: the binding names the publish record and the as-of, the export
refuses a set that is not published, and the export writes the ORE market
configuration from the set's points. For a surface, the published grid becomes
ORE volatility quotes at the boundary with an ORE volatility configuration that
describes the grid. ORE interpolates between grid points by its own rules, so
the blueprint's grid and the exported configuration together decide how closely
ORE reproduces the calibrated surface at the strikes a trade needs.

** Other asset classes

Equity and commodity surfaces quote by strike or moneyness, and an IR cube adds
the swap tenor. Each follows this path with its own template and its own
schema row.
