#+title: Book types and the controls on each
#+description: Which kinds of book the system holds, which process opens, changes, moves and closes each, and who must approve each act.
#+type: knowledge
#+level: cross
#+filetags: :domain:trading:book:controls:four-eyes:
#+created: 2026-10-10
#+updated: 2026-10-10

* Summary

The system holds two kinds of book, and the controls on a book follow from
which kind it is. An *official book* maps to a book in the general ledger,
is created by Finance, and goes through the heavyweight multi-party process
the lifecycle note describes. A *virtual book* is the one exception: the
trading system creates it inside a [[id:4AB0BC63-D73A-4FC3-B9AF-16C1BB90653F][sandbox]], it is never mapped to the
ledger, and it holds no position of the firm. Within the official books the
classification axes (regulatory type, ledger feed, purpose) say what a book
is for, and they do not change which process opens it. The existing notes
state the controls for opening and closing an official book and for opening a
sandbox. They were silent on changing a book, moving a book under another
portfolio, granting rights and creating a portfolio, and the product owner has
decided each of those below: every one is gated by a second person, and
sandboxes are not.

* Detail

** The two kinds of book

| Kind | Maps to the ledger | Created by | Lives in |
|------+--------------------+------------+----------|
| Official book | Yes. Its ledger feed is automatic or manual | Finance, after the multi-party process | The firm's portfolio tree |
| Virtual book | Never. Its ledger feed is none | The trading system, for the user or process that needs it | Exactly one sandbox's own portfolio tree |

The sandbox note states the split in one sentence: every book the firm trades
in maps to a book in the ledger and is created by Finance, and the trading
system holds a view of them and cannot create one. A virtual book is the one
exception ([[id:4AB0BC63-D73A-4FC3-B9AF-16C1BB90653F][Sandbox]], /Virtual books/). A *dynamic book* is neither: it is a saved
filter that behaves like a book without being one, and it has no lifecycle
of its own ([[id:44C9D99B-979B-43AB-90BC-AF7E7D8BDE7C][Dynamic books]]).

** What a book is for: the classification, not the kind

An official book carries three independent classification fields and one
flag ([[id:74AA46EB-64ED-4FD7-B212-AEC164648B84][Book classification]]). They say what the book is for. None of them selects
a different opening process.

| Field | Values | What it decides |
|-------+--------+-----------------|
| Regulatory book type | Trading, Banking | The capital treatment ([[id:36B8F463-121F-42D6-8048-3C62C21AF557][FRTB boundary]]) |
| Ledger feed type | None, Automatic, Manual | How the ledger balance is fed. A manual ledger book is updated by hand. A virtual book has none |
| Book purpose type | Trading, Reserve, Funding, Wash, Write-off, Test, Sales, Sweep target, Remittance target | The one risk role the book plays |
| Sweepable | yes or no | Whether the spot sweep centralises its cash |

A wash book has an extra property the system enforces: every client trade
booked into it is offset by a generated back-to-back trade that routes the risk
into a designated risk book ([[id:9E364BEF-12EE-42F8-902C-B1E68A3095B8][Wash books and risk routing]]). A test book is an
official book "isolated from the main book population, ideally owned by test
entities restricted to IT users". It is not a virtual book, because it is not
created by the system inside a sandbox.

** The controls the existing notes state

*** Opening an official book

The process is controlled and multi-party ([[id:07CEDE6A-21B7-4F87-9745-708FDCA8C362][Book lifecycle]]):

1. The desk initiates the request. The desk cannot create or enrich a book.
2. A controller reviews and approves it. This step exists so that a desk
   cannot authorise its own new book.
3. Finance, Market Risk and Operations enrich the book in parallel, and each
   approves its own contribution. No one function's sign-off implies the others.
4. The access review begins. Operations sets the book up on the booking
   systems and the ledger counterpart is set up.

The system must track where a book sits in this process and who still needs to
authorise or enrich it.

*** Closing an official book

A book closes only when it is flat, meaning every balance held against it is
zero. Closure runs as a clean-down: all live deals move to a different book and
the closed book is retired, not deleted. Finance executes the closure
([[id:07CEDE6A-21B7-4F87-9745-708FDCA8C362][Book lifecycle]]).

*** Opening and ending a virtual book

A virtual book has no approval chain. A trader opens a sandbox at a node of the
official tree only where they hold the right to open sandboxes there, and the
sandbox's virtual books are created for them. The owner confirms the
sandbox is still needed by its review date, or archives it, and an archived
sandbox is read only. Nothing in it is deleted ([[id:4AB0BC63-D73A-4FC3-B9AF-16C1BB90653F][Sandbox]]).

*** Access to a book

Access to an official book is permissioned at a fine grain and lapses unless
renewed, and Operations administers the recurring review ([[id:C84899BD-5EC8-4AA7-88D7-CE7ED19BC573][Book access and
permissions]]). A right at a portfolio node covers every node below it. A
sandbox's visibility follows its anchor.

** Decisions on the acts the notes do not state

The existing notes are silent on these acts. The product owner took the
recommendation for each on 2026-10-10, and they are the rule the prototypes and
the screens follow. Each gated act raises an approval request, and the person
who decides is never the person who asked.

| # | Act | Decision | Who asks | Who decides |
|---+-----+----------+----------+-------------|
| 1 | Change an official book after it is live | Gated by the function that owns the field. A description-only edit is not gated | The desk or the owning function | Finance for the functional currency, GL account reference, cost center and owner unit. Market Risk for the regulatory type, purpose, rates centre and sweepable flag. Operations for the allowed currencies and products and the status |
| 2 | Move a book under another portfolio | Gated. A move changes who can see the book, because a right at a node covers everything below it | The desk | Operations, who administers book access |
| 3 | Grant or remove a right at an official portfolio node | Gated, as the deferred HBAC capture proposes | The head of desk | Operations |
| 4 | Change a book's classification | Gated. This is the same ownership as row 1, stated for the axes: regulatory type and purpose to Market Risk, ledger feed to Finance | The desk or the owning function | Market Risk or Finance |
| 5 | Open a test, wash, reserve, funding, write-off, sweep-target or remittance-target book | The ordinary opening process. A purpose adds no approver, because Market Risk already establishes the classification and Finance the ledger set-up. A test book is owned by IT users | The desk | The opening chain |
| 6 | Create an official portfolio | Gated, because a portfolio is where rights inherit from | The desk | Operations |
| 7 | The book structure screen's "new book" | It is the desk's request that starts the opening process. It does not create a live book. The book goes live when the chain completes | The desk | The opening chain |

*Virtual books and sandboxes are not gated.* A trader creates a sandbox and its
virtual books at a node where they hold the right to open sandboxes, with no
approval. A sandbox's portfolios and its rights follow the sandbox, not this
table. Nothing in a sandbox reaches the ledger, official risk or reporting.

** Who plays each part in Acme

The Acme Corporation dataset has 58 staff accounts across three legal entities
and a group account, under three roles: Trading (30), Operations (16) and Viewer
(12). The roles the system seeds are SuperAdmin, TenantAdmin, Trading,
Operations, Viewer, Support and Quant. No role is named for a controller, for
Finance or for Market Risk. The table matches each part the controls name to
the accounts Acme holds. It was read from =accounts.json= and the role seed.

| Part | Needed for | Acme today | Gap |
|------+------------+------------+-----|
| Desk requester | Initiating a new book; proposing a rights grant | Head of Desk (9) and Head of Trading (3), role Trading | none |
| Controller | The first independent check on a new book | No account, no job title, no role | *missing* |
| Finance | Wiring the book into the accounting hierarchy; executing a closure | No account. A Group Treasury unit exists, and no account sits in it as Finance | *missing* |
| Market Risk | Classification, capital treatment, rates centre | Head of Market Risk (3) and Head of Risk (3), role Operations | exists, but shares a role with Operations |
| Operations | Allowed currencies and products; set-up on the booking systems; access review | Head of Middle Office (3) and COO (3), role Operations | exists |
| A second person for each part | A decider who is not the asker | The heads above come in threes, one per entity. Controller and Finance have none | *missing* for Controller and Finance |
| Sandbox user | Opening a sandbox and its virtual books | Traders and analysts hold accounts. No account is a quant or an IT user, and the Quant role has no Acme account | *missing* |
| Test-book owner | Owning a test book, "restricted to IT users" | No IT account | *missing* |
| Tenant administrator | Administering the tenant | Not in the dataset. It comes from tenant provisioning | check |

Two further facts matter before the prototypes are drawn. The deciders in an
approval request are chosen by permission, so a Controller and a Finance part
need either their own roles or an agreed permission on an existing role. And
the seeded roles do not separate Market Risk from Operations, so a
Market Risk approval and an Operations approval would be decided by the same
role unless that changes. The task filed for the story adds the missing
accounts. The recommendation, taken as the working rule, is a role for each of
Controller, Finance and Market Risk that holds the permission to decide the
approvals its part owns, and the existing Operations role keeps Operations'.

** How the controls map onto approval requests

Every second-person control above is one case of the approval request: a record
for anything that waits on a person's decision, with a rule that the person who
decides is not the person who asked ([[id:0FE6BDD9-EB24-4B07-8B0C-D816AAF1BEBF][Approval requests]]). Each gated act
becomes a request kind. The multi-party opening is a request that several
deciders must each answer. The deferred [[id:C6D6A0C2-1A62-4C52-8DED-D431E410E89F][four-eyes authorisation framework]]
is what would hold the pending change and apply it when the decision lands, and
it is not built.
