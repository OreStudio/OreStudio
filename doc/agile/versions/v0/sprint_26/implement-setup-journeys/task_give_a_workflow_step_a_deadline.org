* Goal

A step declares how long it may run, and the engine fails the one that outlives
its deadline. A run whose service has died, whose command the bus dropped, or
whose handler wedged stops being =in_progress= for ever, a waiter reads a failure
and a reason instead of a step that looks busy, and a retry resumes it once the
service is back.

* Acceptance

- Every step states how long it may run, and the engine refuses a run whose
  definition built a step without one.
- The engine fails a step that outlives its deadline and stops the run, and the
  run is retryable from that step.
- A deadline never rolls the run back, whatever failure policy the definition
  declares, because a step that went silent is a step whose work is unknown.
- An answer that arrives after the deadline is ignored, and the retry that
  follows re-dispatches the step under its own identity.
- The failure names the subject that never answered and what the engine knows
  about the component that owns it.
- A run that waits on another run states a longer budget than the steps it waits
  for, so the inner deadline is the one that fails.

* Plan

The deadline belongs to the step's definition, because how long a step may take
is knowledge its author has and no default is right for both a write and an
import. It travels with the run, in the snapshot the engine materialises at
start, so a definition that shortened a deadline cannot expire a run started
under a longer one.

The engine decides, on a pass every thirty seconds, over the steps it dispatched
rather than over every run in the store, and it stops the run rather than
applying the definition's failure policy: a rollback is the right answer for a
step that reported that it failed, and a guess about a step nobody heard from.

What a deadline cannot know is why the step went silent. The bus drops a command
whose subject has no subscriber and says nothing, so the engine follows the
service heartbeats instead and states what it saw when a deadline fires: nothing
ever reported, the component was not reporting when the command was published,
or it was still reporting. Evidence, not proof, and the note says so.

* Result

A step declares how long it may run, the run carries the budgets it was started
with, and the engine fails any step whose command it has been waiting on for
longer than that. The failure names the command subject, how long it has been
silent, and what the engine knows about the component that owns the subject.

Three decisions make the behaviour what it is.

The deadline belongs to the definition and not to the engine, because how long a
step may take is knowledge its author has and no default is right for both a
write that answers in milliseconds and an import that publishes tens of
thousands of rows. Definitions read their budgets from two names in the
definition contract -- one for data work, one for a write -- and a step whose
work is neither states its own. A definition that forgot is refused rather than
defaulted: a step with no deadline is a command the engine would wait on for
ever, which is the failure this work exists to remove.

A deadline stops the run and never rolls it back, whatever failure policy the
definition declares. A rollback answers a step that reported that it failed,
because such a step did nothing; a step nobody heard from is a step whose work is
unknown, and undoing a run whose work was in fact done is the worse guess. What
the engine fails is retryable, so a service that comes back is one retry away.

A step that waits on another run states more than the steps inside it. The first
live walk of this change proved why the rule is needed: the party stage and the
bundle step it waited on both stated the data budget, the outer deadline fired
seconds after the inner one, and the run recorded that the party stage had not
finished. That sentence is true and useless, because the cause was one level
down. `orchestrating_step_timeout` is the budget that says so, and the caller's
own patience with a nested run is now a backstop rather than a deadline: the
1500 seconds it stated was the length of a hang a dead service produced, which is
how a timeout came to name a step instead of a cause.

The engine cannot tell a service that has died from one that is slow, and does
not pretend to. A command published to a subject with no subscriber is dropped by
the bus in silence, so the deadline decides and presence only explains: the
engine follows the service heartbeats and says, when a deadline fires, whether
the component that owns the subject ever reported, whether it was reporting when
the command was published, or whether it was still reporting. Evidence and not
proof, because a service that is running but wedged reports too.

An answer that arrives after the deadline is ignored and logged as an answer that
came too late rather than as a duplicate, so the record a person acted on stands.
The retry that follows carries the step's own id, which is the idempotency key,
so a service that already finished replays its outcome instead of doing the work
twice. A retry that overlaps the original leaves one step with two executions in
flight until the first records its outcome, which the journey document states as
the window a step must be safe to repeat inside.

The live walk is in =evidence/step_deadline_live.txt=. With
=ores.marketdata.service= stopped, a party added through the journey's own route
failed after fifteen minutes with:

#+begin_example
The step did not answer within 15m of its command being published (subject
marketdata.v1.market-data-observations.publish-from-dq); it has been silent for
15m. Nothing has reported as the marketdata service since this engine started,
so the command may have had no handler.
#+end_example

The step above it exercised the other branch of the presence text -- a service
that was still reporting and did not finish -- and the run that followed the
budget fix reported the silent step's own sentence, which is the outcome the
change is for.

Two defects were found on the way and are recorded rather than hidden. The pass
that enforces the deadlines judged each entry in its own right only after a
leftover run in a dropped test tenant aborted a whole pass and starved every
deadline behind it; and the engine's own test suite re-dispatches the
deployment's in-flight runs, which restarted a live run's silence three times
and is captured as
[[id:0EBBA137-9A83-4411-A706-DBC5543B1235][the engine tests reset a live run's
deadline]].
