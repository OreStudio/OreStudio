* Acceptance

- The basis configuration is its own part, not ten nullable columns behind a flag.
- No row carries a column that its construction type cannot use.
- The ORE projection writes one typed array per part and the round trip is byte-identical on the fixtures.
- Each part is justified by its own invariant rather than by the entry's member count, and the argument is in the task's Result.

* Plan

** The rules a split obeys

The data-oriented model decides the shape, not the column count. Its rules, and
the reasons behind them, are in
[[id:BBD43BF2-9FEE-42B0-A667-9468AF759DC2][Data-Oriented Design in ORE Studio]] and
[[id:56AD6F46-99E0-482E-B7B5-C9FDEC8A7641][The data classification checklist]]; this task applies them and
does not restate them. In short:

- a type holds one component: fields that share one invariant, hold at one
  instant, and are keyed by the same key;
- a part is joined by key and never nested, so the wire carries one typed array
  per component and no part's fields appear inside another's object;
- a field read on a different cadence, or by a different screen, job or service,
  goes with its readers and not with the row it happens to sit in;
- the classification comes first. A compile error never reclassifies data: if a
  shape has to be denormalised for a compiler, the classification stays and the
  denormalisation is recorded;
- the centre keeps the identity and whatever the parts must agree on. A part one
  reader needs does not stay on the centre.

** This grouping

Two refdata curve entries are one construction per row:

| Type | Members | Columns | The block that should not be a column |
|------+---------+---------+---------------------------------------|
| =default_curve_configuration= | 27 | 27 | =default_curve_type= and one column per construction |
| =commodity_curve_config= | 24 | 24 | =has_basis_configuration= and ten basis columns |

A default curve is built one of several ways, and the columns that state one way
are null for the others. A commodity curve either carries a basis configuration
or does not, and the ten basis columns are meaningless when it does not.

** What proves it

- No row carries a column its own construction cannot use.
- The basis configuration is one part type with its own invariant: all ten of
  its fields are set together or the part is absent.
- The ORE projection round trips byte-identical on the fixtures.

* Notes

** What each construction reads

ORE's own readers are the authority, because the schema states every setting
as optional for every type: =DefaultCurveConfig::Config::fromXML= and
=CommodityCurveConfig::fromXML= at tag 1.8.17.0 dispatch on the construction
and read only that construction's fields.

A default curve configuration's =Type= selects its construction. Every type
reads =DayCounter=, =Extrapolation=, =AllowNegativeRates= and =RecoveryRate=:

| Type                                              | Fields it reads                                                                                                  |
|---------------------------------------------------+------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| SpreadCDS, HazardRate, Price, ConvSpreadCDS, Null | DiscountCurve, Conventions, Quotes, StartDate, RunningSpread, IndexTerm, ImplyDefaultFromMarket, PriceIsUpfront |
| Benchmark                                         | BenchmarkCurve, SourceCurve, Pillars, SpotLag, Calendar                                                          |
| TransitionMatrix                                  | InitialState, States, Quotes                                                                                     |
| YieldCurve                                        | ReinterpretedYieldCurve                                                                                          |
| MultiSection                                      | SourceCurves, SwitchDates, which the projection refuses today                                                    |

A commodity curve's construction is the element it states. Every construction
reads =Currency= and =Extrapolation=:

| Construction  | Selected by                       | Fields it reads                                        |
|---------------+-----------------------------------+--------------------------------------------------------|
| Basis         | =BasisConfiguration=              | the block's nine fields and its quotes                 |
| CrossCurrency | a top-level =BasePriceCurve=      | BasePriceCurve, BaseYieldCurve, YieldCurve             |
| Piecewise     | =PriceSegments=                   | DayCounter, InterpolationMethod, the price segments    |
| Direct        | none of the above                 | DayCounter, InterpolationMethod, SpotQuote, Quotes, Conventions |

** What the corpus states

Every =curveconfig*.xml= under =external/ore= (89 files) was read for a field
its construction does not read. The commodity curves (34 Direct, 1 Basis, 1
Piecewise) state none. The default curves (189 SpreadCDS, 102 HazardRate, 21
Null, 15 Benchmark, 14 TransitionMatrix, 1 YieldCurve) state one pair: all 14
TransitionMatrix configurations state =DiscountCurve= and =Conventions=, which
ORE's reader ignores for that type. The round trip must stay byte identical, so
that pair stays storable for a transition matrix; this is the one recorded
denormalisation.

** The parts

The default curve configuration keeps its identity, its place and the settings
every type reads: =is_inline=, =priority=, =default_curve_type=, =day_counter=,
=extrapolation=, =allow_negative_rates=, =recovery_rate=, =position=. Its parts
are keyed by =default_curve_configuration_id=, at most one of each:

| Part                                  | Columns                                                                       | Types                                                         |
|---------------------------------------+-------------------------------------------------------------------------------+---------------------------------------------------------------|
| default_curve_market_terms            | discount_curve, conventions, has_quotes                                       | the CDS family and Null; TransitionMatrix, the denormalisation |
| default_curve_cds_terms               | start_date, running_spread, index_term, imply_default_from_market, price_is_upfront | SpreadCDS, HazardRate, Price, ConvSpreadCDS, Null        |
| default_curve_benchmark               | benchmark_curve, source_curve, pillars, spot_lag, calendar                    | Benchmark                                                     |
| default_curve_transition_matrix       | initial_state, states                                                         | TransitionMatrix                                              |
| default_curve_yield_reinterpretation  | reinterpreted_yield_curve                                                     | YieldCurve                                                    |

The commodity curve keeps =currency= and =extrapolation=. Its parts are keyed
by =curve_definition_id=, as the entry's own row is, at most one of each:

| Part                          | Columns                                                         | Constructions     |
|-------------------------------+-----------------------------------------------------------------+-------------------|
| commodity_curve_interpolation | day_counter, interpolation_method                               | Direct, Piecewise |
| commodity_curve_direct        | spot_quote, conventions, has_quotes                             | Direct            |
| commodity_curve_cross_currency | base_price_curve, base_yield_curve, yield_curve                | CrossCurrency     |
| commodity_curve_basis         | the nine basis columns                                          | Basis             |

=has_basis_configuration= and =has_price_segments= go: the basis part's
presence states the Basis construction, and the price segment rows, already a
child entity, state the Piecewise one. An empty =PriceSegments= element would
give ORE a piecewise curve with no segments, and the corpus states none.

** Where each part's rule is enforced

A default curve configuration states its type, so each of its parts' insert
triggers checks the type against the types that fill the part. The section
generator writes those checks from the projection's
=import_default_curve_parts=, the one statement of which type fills which part,
as it writes the segment checks from =import_segments=.

A commodity curve states no construction column: the part it holds is its
construction. So no trigger can check a commodity part against a construction,
and the export does instead: it takes the parts of the one construction ORE's
reader would select, and refuses any part left over. The document pipeline
does not store commodity curves today, so a database check would guard no
write.
