#+title: The simulator is one adapter plus the tools that author its generators
#+description: What ores.marketdata.synthetic contains, how it differs from ores.trading.synthetic, and which parts are adapter and which are authoring.

* Summary

=ores.marketdata.synthetic= is an adapter. It implements the adapter contract and
produces ticks from generators. It also holds the tools that author the
generators: collections, folders, generator configs and preview. Trade
generation is a different thing and belongs to =ores.trading.synthetic=.

* Detail

** Two components, two jobs

| Component | Stands in for | Acts through |
|-----------+---------------+--------------|
| =ores.marketdata.synthetic= | The external world: vendors | The adapter contract |
| =ores.trading.synthetic= | Business activity: traders and counterparties | The normal trading API, like a user |

The two share authoring needs such as seeding, collections and preview. A small
shared library can hold them if the duplication grows.

** Inside the simulator

- *Adapter part.* Announce, demand, tick and heartbeat. The generator runner
  turns the demand set into running generators. See [[id:39057B64-8F85-4882-9B66-D2D4FC7468E4][The synthetic runner]].
- *Generators.* FX spot and IR curve processes, with a factory for new asset
  classes. See [[id:D67C930B-261F-480B-B5EF-020C6D617333][The generator factory and the kind seam]] and [[id:B7A03C80-6566-4E97-9D45-14A69726C381][Adding an asset class]].
- *Authoring part.* Collections, folders, configs and the preview operations. See
  [[id:94471941-E1D7-4796-9BB0-44C96667FE57][Preview and simulate]].
- *Fault injection.* See [[id:69287DA4-3558-4EAE-972B-82BC7F9296E9][The simulator stops at the adapter seam]].

** What the simulator never does

- It does not write to the store.
- It does not know a party.
- It does not publish an oresmd URI.
- It does not make up names that mark the data as simulated. Its source names are
  ordinary labels such as =bloomberg-sim=.

** Several simulated sources

The simulator can serve more than one provider. Each provider's simulated source
is its own adapter instance and announces its own provider. One process can host
many instances.
