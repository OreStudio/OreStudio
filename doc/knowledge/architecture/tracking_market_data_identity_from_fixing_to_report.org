#+title: Tracking market data identity from fixing to report
#+description: The target state: identities that distinguish observed, generated, derived and published data, lineage that connects them, and reports that reference published curves.

* Abstract

This document describes the target state of market data provenance in ORE
Studio. Every number a consumer reads will answer three questions: what it is,
where it came from, and which act made it authoritative. It will answer them
because the identification system gives each kind of market data its own
identity, so an observed fixing, a synthetic observation, a bootstrapped curve
point and a published set are never confusable, and because the lineage records
connect those identities through the lifecycle. At the end of the lifecycle a
report references the published curve: the report's market binding names the
publish record, the ORE XML is produced from that set at the boundary, and only a
set in the published state is consumable. The chain is then trackable end to end,
from a report's number back to the public fixing that started it.

* The question

A person reading a curve asks three questions of every number. What is it: a
quote observed in the market, a value a generator produced, a discount factor a
bootstrap derived, or a set a person published? Where did it come from: which
quote, which generator, which curve configuration, which people? Which act made
it authoritative: the decision, the publish, or nothing at all?

The target state answers all three for every consumer. Two conditions make that
possible. The identity must distinguish the kinds of data, because a discount
factor and the swap rate it was bootstrapped from are not the same datum and must
not answer to the same name. The lineage must connect them, because the point
must name the quote it came from and the act that published the set. Identity
without lineage gives distinct names with no history; lineage without identity
gives records that cannot be compared.

* Why identity must distinguish

The market does not supply term structures ready for valuation. A quote is
observed; a curve is constructed from quotes by a bootstrap, and the construction
is a decision about method, conventions and pillars. The target state therefore
gives the observed quote and the bootstrapped curve point different identities. If
they were the same, nothing in the system could say whether a number was observed
or constructed, and the whole question of provenance would dissolve into a naming
convention.

The distinction is visible in the identity itself. An observed OIS quote is a
series of the form =oresmd://ir/USD?...instrument=ir_swap&quote=rate...=; a point
of a bootstrapped discount curve is a series such as
=oresmd://ir/USD?...instrument=discount&quote=rate&curve_id=...=, and its datum
carries the term. The instrument, the quote and the curve identity separate the
two; the provider, the party and the series separate one curve from another. The
identity of the input is not a weaker version of the identity of the output; it is
a different identity for a different kind of thing.

* The identification system

The identification system is the oresmd URI. A series is an item in a provider's
catalogue for a party, and a datum is a series with a coordinate. The definition
is stated in [[id:CE99CD2A-B31F-4306-865F-CBC519FBC1B8][The oresmd datum identity]] and
[[id:E4736D88-03B2-4A40-B7F4-01B06B9B9FD7][A quote series is keyed by item, provider and party]]. Three properties make
it fit every use case.

First, the identity is independent of the vendor's spelling. A series a synthetic
feed publishes and a series a bootstrap reads are the same series when their
oresmd URIs agree, whatever the vendor would call them.

Second, the identity is fine enough to tell kinds apart, as the previous section
shows, and coarse enough to compare them: the same series read at two as-ofs is
one series with two datums, so a time series is a series and its coordinates, not
a new series per day.

Third, the runtime uses oresmd and nothing else. An ORE key is a boundary format:
it names a datum in the corpus' terms and converts to an oresmd datum where ORE
XML is read or written. In the target state no reader, feed or classifier in the
runtime spells a datum the ORE way. The conversion is stated in
[[id:D1A25B9E-B1A0-4E7C-AB5C-1C7C3EF07F90][ORE import and export]].

* The lifecycle and what tracks it

The lifecycle has six steps. At each, the data has an identity, and a record
connects it to what came before.

** Observation

A public source publishes a fixing: the ECB euro reference rates, the Federal
Reserve H.10 through FRED, the US Treasury par yield curve, the New York Fed SOFR
and the Bank of England SONIA, reached for FX through the Frankfurter API. The
official adapter publishes it as a durable fixing per provider, with the
provider's key kept so the credit and the terms apply. The identity is the
fixing's series and datum; a provider without a terms page is not loaded.

Tracks it: the provider catalogue and the source, the series and the observation,
and the provider key on the value.
See [[id:F4BBEF34-D84C-4AE6-A3AA-DC47C3EE06EE][Public sources publish daily fixings through an adapter]] and
[[id:3F9D6EAE-9FB8-4A0D-9BAF-1EA3EA93AB4F][Synthetic prices are calibrated from free public sources]].

** Generation

A generation config names the fixing items it may start from, with a rank, a
policy and a window. The initial value is the latest fixing on or before the
market clock's date, and the volatility is the spread of the log returns over the
window. The generator reads the published fixing and publishes a synthetic series
of ticks. The identity of a synthetic observation is its own series, separate
from the fixing it was calibrated from; the config's fixing items are the lineage
that connects the two.

Tracks it: the generation config's fixing items, the synthetic series and its
observations, and the config version.
See [[id:BB2B7C4C-FD4E-4DCA-BF1D-00F929622B87][Synthetic market data generation: approach]],
[[id:316F0EA0-2DA4-4E96-87D5-F4EAE7490938][The IR curve generation config]] and
[[id:FB57F761-0FC3-4283-AD32-CCF2FE68AD2E][The IR curve producer]].

** Delivery

A party takes a provider's feeds through a subscription that carries its display,
non-display and derived rights. A route names the source the party uses for that
provider. The demand is computed from the subscriptions, and the adapters follow
it. Delivery does not change the identity: the series the party reads is the
series the catalogues named, and the rights decide whether the party may read it,
not what it is called.

Tracks it: the subscription, its rights, the route and the computed demand.
See [[id:31BCA3F8-31AD-49C2-9B6B-F2312EE7DECB][A party takes a provider's feeds through a subscription]],
[[id:37B27CA8-9F44-4A61-9B02-3EAABD8C3818][A party routes to exactly one source for each provider]],
[[id:083D4B54-549B-4D56-AE89-430A8A4277FF][Demand drives what an adapter publishes]] and
[[id:7D1DE23F-AA14-44AB-81D5-9961EF02533C][Market data ticks because ownership, demand and a smoke check agree]].

** Derivation

A blueprint names a party, a currency, an index tenor, a role, the conventions and
the output series it owns. Its pillars name the market series each quote comes
from. The compute reads those series' observations at the as-of, resolves the
tenor dates, and runs the bootstrap; a projection also reads its funding curve's
discount factors. The result is written as observations of the output series,
with lineage naming the source series and the configuration version.

This is the step where identity must distinguish. The curve points are not the
quotes: they are a discount series with a curve identity and a term coordinate.
The lineage is what connects them: the output series' observations carry the
input series and the version that produced them, so a curve point can be traced
to the quotes without pretending to be one.

Tracks it: the blueprint and its pillars, the output series and its observations,
and the lineage record with the source series and the config version. The pillar
names its series, as [[id:7AE57098-08D4-4D4A-83D6-918FE520B68E][A pillar names the market series it reads]]
decides. The records are [[id:73A5FAA1-377C-45A6-999E-4AD4DE406F78][IR Curve Bootstrap Config]] and
[[id:5DC8A225-A2FD-4ECC-8284-C1D333F0EB3A][IR Curve Bootstrap Pillar]]; the journeys are
[[id:FBD32402-C71F-4730-A3D2-70BA1D5F3868][Maintain the curve blueprints]],
[[id:62728AF9-01AD-4FBF-B022-83DCD64204A9][Check that a curve blueprint builds]] and
[[id:F55B08E3-0ADA-4267-8CB9-127FF1814549][Bootstrap or refresh a curve]]; the architecture is
[[id:0220F3F7-C674-47EE-91CE-1C04804B4558][Tenor and Curve Bootstrapping Architecture]], and the
provenance is [[id:573F9165-C20B-463C-B0B3-F324AFE4E81F][Curve Point Provenance]].

** Publication

The maker raises a publish naming the blueprint and the as-of; the subject is a
publish record. A second person decides under the kind's maker and checker
permissions and the record's four-eyes rule. Publishing applies the approved
request once, stamps the publisher and the instant, ends the set it replaces, and
sends the notice. The set then reads as published, superseded or withdrawn.

Publication is an act, and the identification records it without confusing the
data with the act: the publish record names the configuration, the party, the
as-of, the request and the publisher; the points remain observations of the output
series. The set's state is a function of the record's facts, so a published set,
a candidate and a withdrawn set are distinguished by what happened to them, not by
a different series identity.

Tracks it: the approval request, the publish record, the published set and its
state, and the notice. See
[[id:239E4C3C-12E7-4A87-9DE3-16B9F093BC14][Ask for a publish to be approved]],
[[id:A85B6C4E-0F93-4410-BF2E-7167722849ED][Decide a publish]],
[[id:53F31919-86FA-4E35-8594-9E72197C4A3B][Publish the approved set]] and
[[id:B05A31CC-FA22-4534-848E-601A30822992][Withdraw or replace a published curve]].

** Consumption

A report has a definition, a schedule and a run, and the run needs market data. In
the target state the report references the published curve. Its market binding
names the published set: the output series, the as-of and the publish record that
made it authoritative. The reader accepts only a set in the published state; a
candidate is not consumable, and a superseded or withdrawn set is consumable only
when the report explicitly names a historical as-of, which is what makes a rerun
reproducible.

The ORE export turns that set into the ORE XML the compute reads, and the ORE key
appears at the boundary as the boundary format it is. The report's curve is
therefore the published curve, not a separate document: the number in the report
and the set on the published screen share the same output series, as-of and
publish record. The provenance chain then runs unbroken from the report to the
publication, the derivation, the delivery, the generation and the fixing.

* What the target makes answerable

The records support the provenance queries the lifecycle implies, end to end. From
a number in a report, the report's market binding names the publish record and the
as-of; the publish record names the request, the decider and the publisher; the
output series and its observations name the points; the lineage names the source
series and the configuration version; the source series names the quote; and the
quote names the provider and the fixing it was calibrated from.

From the input side the same records answer the reverse question. An observed
fixing names its provider and its terms; a synthetic tick names the configuration
whose fixing items calibrated it; a published set names its act and its state. The
same series read at two dates is one series with two datums, so a history is a
series and its coordinates rather than a set of unrelated records.

* The changes that reach the target

Three changes remain to be built, and each is a change to a named part of the
system rather than an open design question.

The first is the report's market binding. A report will name the published set,
and the published read will refuse a set that is not in the published state. The
binding carries the output series, the as-of and the publish record, and the ORE
export builds the XML from those. This closes the chain described above.

The second is the runtime's ORE spelling. No reader, feed or classifier in the
runtime will compose an ORE key. The pillar names its series, as
[[id:7AE57098-08D4-4D4A-83D6-918FE520B68E][A pillar names the market series it reads]] decides; the
series classifier keys its rules on the oresmd identity; and the oresmd codec
accepts every valid datum rather than only those with an ORE spelling. The work is
recorded as [[id:EAD9F02F-3B4B-495E-B37D-71BF766F7970][Name a curve pillar's market series]].

The third is the creation of the output series. A blueprint's output series will
be created as part of the blueprint's lifecycle, so the series exists by the time
a person needs to choose it, and the Add dialog is not the first place the person
learns that it must already exist.

* Conclusion

In the target state, the provenance of market data is trackable end to end. The
identification system distinguishes the kinds of data, so an observed quote, a
synthetic observation, a derived curve point and a published set each have their
own identity; the lineage and the publish records connect them, so each number
can be traced to its sources and to the act that made it authoritative; and the
report references the published curve, so the chain from a report to a fixing is
unbroken. The three changes that reach that state are the report's market
binding, the removal of the ORE spelling from the runtime, and the creation of the
output series within the blueprint lifecycle.

* Patterns used

- [[id:94C14185-EE64-412F-8681-849AFF04B4D8][Request-Reply]] -- each step is a call that waits for its answer: the screen reads, the compute, the publish, the report run.
- [[id:5C52B5EA-C13B-4149-BC18-DCB2D9AE998B][Competing Consumers]] -- the adapter and every service handler take their messages through a queue group, so one instance of the service handles each.
- [[id:26AE458C-5E23-423B-BEBE-1B55D83A7AA5][Idempotent Receiver]] -- the publish gate applies one request once, and the ingest tolerates a repeated tick, so a retry or a replay cannot move a set twice.
- [[id:D5F77B10-D498-4B56-ADF7-7E5CEFA44FA0][Database per Service]] -- the path crosses the market data, refdata, inbox and reporting stores, and each component owns its own.
