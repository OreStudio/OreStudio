What the service does at start, read from its own log. The first block is a
start against a database that holds no expiry job; the second is a restart
against one that does.

First start -- the setting is read, the job list is read, and the job is
written:

  NATS request: ...variability.v1.ops.get_setting
  NATS request: ...scheduler.v1.job_definitions.list
  NATS request: ...scheduler.v1.job_definitions.put
  [ores.inbox.service.app.approval_expiry_schedule] Registered the approval
    expiry job 'ores.inbox.approval_expiry' from
    inbox.approval_expiry.schedule.

Restart -- nothing is written, because the job already holds the schedule the
setting states:

  NATS request: ...variability.v1.ops.get_setting
  NATS request: ...scheduler.v1.job_definitions.list
  [ores.inbox.service.app.approval_expiry_schedule] Registered the approval
    expiry job 'ores.inbox.approval_expiry' from
    inbox.approval_expiry.schedule.

The row those two runs left behind:

  select job_name, version, schedule_expression, is_active, valid_from,
         modified_by, performed_by
  from ores_scheduler_job_definitions_tbl
  where job_name = 'ores.inbox.approval_expiry'
    and valid_to = ores_utility_infinity_timestamp_fn();

          job_name          | version | schedule_expression | is_active |          valid_from           |           modified_by            |             performed_by
----------------------------+---------+---------------------+-----------+-------------------------------+----------------------------------+--------------------------------------
 ores.inbox.approval_expiry |       1 | */5 * * * *         | t         | 2026-10-08 01:07:00.455944+01 | ores_eager_maxwell_inbox_service | ores_eager_maxwell_scheduler_service
(1 row)

One live row at version 1 is what idempotence looks like here: the restart
found the job by name and left it alone, so valid_from is still the first
write's and the schedule's history holds only the writes the setting has
actually had. The write names the inbox service as the modifier, and the
scheduler stamps itself as the performer, because the scheduler is what wrote
the row.

The registration is the reason the job exists at all: nothing seeds it, so a
build that did not write it would have no sweep, and the service would refuse
to start rather than run without one.

One restart during this walk did not reach the registration at all: it stalled
part-way through registering its entity-change event mappings and never logged
=Service ready.= A restart a minute later completed. That intermittent startup
stall is not this component's, and it is recorded in
is_a_steady_timer_spawned_from_on_started_the_right_design.org.
