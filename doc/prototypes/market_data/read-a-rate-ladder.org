* Summary

A rate ladder is the market, read sideways: maturities as rows, providers as
columns, and one consensus so a person can see agreement and disagreement at
once. The prototype argues that a gap is shown as a gap and never as a zero,
that the maturity dates belong to the server because the calendar does, and that
the curve below the table is the same answer and not a second read.

It is ported from hedgr's =prototypes/rates_ladder_providers.html=. The table,
the spread row and the curve survive; hedgr's History tab does not, because no
read stands behind it yet.

* Detail

** The question

How much of the market does one ladder show before it stops being readable? The
answer decides the variant: every provider, the consensus with a distance, or
one provider at a time.

** The variants

Switch with the bar at the foot, or =?variant=.

| Variant | Name             | The structural argument |
|---------+------------------+-------------------------|
| =a=     | Providers as columns | Every provider's quote, the consensus beside it, and the spread below. The full picture, and the widest table. |
| =b=     | Consensus only   | One value per rung, how many providers it rested on, the best quote and the distance from it. A board, not a wall. |
| =c=     | One provider     | The column a person prices from, with nothing to compare. |

** The states

- =?state=full= — every rung quoted by most providers.
- =?state=gap= — several providers are absent from the middle rungs and one
  short rung loses its longest-standing source. The page lists the thin rungs,
  because a dash is what a person otherwise reads as zero.
- =?state=stale= — every quote is over the threshold. The verdict is the
  server's.

** What is fake

Every number, date and age is mock. The consensus is computed in the page as a
median so the mock cannot contradict its own caption; the server will state it,
with its method, because two clients must not disagree.

** Where the code lives

=doc/prototypes/market_data/read-a-rate-ladder/index.html=, self-contained: one
file with its styles and its script.

** The verdict

Take it, with one deletion and one addition. Delete the History tab until a read
supports it. Add the thin-market list, which hedgr did not have: the prototype
showed gaps but did not name them, and a gap on a ladder is what a person
explains to a desk.

** What changed from hedgr

| Item | hedgr | Here | Why |
|------+-------+------+-----|
| Tabs | Ladder, Curve, History | Ladder and Curve as sections of one page | History has no read; the two that remain are one answer |
| Consensus | A printed number | A computed median with the count it rested on | A consensus must state its method and its sample |
| Gaps | Dashes | Dashes, plus a list of the rungs that are thin | A person acts on a thin rung, not on a dash |
| Chrome | A dialog with a footer party badge | A page with a facts row | Prototypes read as the product |
| Maturities | Printed | Printed, and stated as the server's | The calendar is a server fact |
