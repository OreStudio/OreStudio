* Acceptance

- The test reads every checked-in expected output and passes, or names the file and the reason it is out of scope.
- The test fails when a catalogue family has no reader or no storage shape.
- A sample that omits an open measure, or states a measure the family does not declare, parses: the required columns are the test, not an exact header.
- A family whose file no sample ships is listed with that reason, so an unreadable family cannot hide behind a missing sample.
- The test runs in the local check gate and needs no engine binary or database.

* Plan

One test walks every CSV under =external/ore/examples/*/ExpectedOutput=, asks
the catalogue which family it is, and reads it with the reader that family
states: the metric reader for the families whose row is figures, one of the
three child-row readers for the families whose row is a flow. The family's
shape is a small table in the test, and the seven payload families are listed
with the reason they are not read here: they are market state, and the
marketdata task stores them.

A second test states the same table from the catalogue's side: every family has
a shape, the payload families are exactly the ones listed, and the three
child-row families are exactly the three.

* Result

The corpus gate reads every checked-in expected output: 460 files, no refusal,
994 assertions. The families fall into 50 metric, 3 child-row and 7 payload, and
the gate proves the three counts add up to the catalogue rather than trusting
the table.

Nothing is skipped silently. A family with no shape fails the test, a payload
family must be one of the seven named with a reason, and a child-row family must
be one of the three. When a family is added to the corpus tomorrow the gate
fails until a reader or a stated payload covers it.

The storage half of the acceptance waits: the gate cannot yet check that a
family's figures reach a table, because the two store tasks are still open. That
is recorded on the task rather than claimed.
