#+description: The owner's three requirements for a time series plot: the window's high, low and change in the header with the high and the low marked on the plot, statistics for a selected range with a crosshair readout, and overlays with a difference mode. This page is a [[id:671F18E4-E09C-4B3B-BD24-D33DF8AE38A6][capture]] in the *inbox* bucket of the product backlog — a pre-sprint idea, not yet pulled into a sprint as a story.

* What

A time series plot must answer three questions about one series. The window it
draws states its high, its low and its change in the header, and the plot marks
the high and the low on itself. A range the person selects reports the statistics
of that range alone, and a crosshair readout states the value under the pointer as
the pointer moves. The plot carries overlays, which are Bollinger bands and
realised volatility, and a mode that plots the difference against history rather
than the level.

These are the owner's requirements, quoted below as given. They are requirements
and not a design: no control, no read, no shape and no arithmetic is settled here.
No screen in the repo does any of the three today.

* Why these three belong together

Each answers a different question about the same series:

- The window figures and the high and low markers answer *what happened in the
  window*. One number for the change, and two extremes a reader can point at.
- The selected range's statistics and the crosshair answer *what happened in the
  part I care about*. The window is the screen's choice; a range is the person's.
- The overlays and the difference mode answer *how does this compare with what
  the series usually does*. A level alone has no scale; a band and a difference
  against history give one.

A plot that answers only the first is a sparkline. It shows the shape and leaves
both the detail and the comparison to a table, and a person who wants either
leaves the plot to build it by hand.

* The requirements

** The window's figures in the header, and the high and the low on the plot

The requirement, in the owner's words:

#+begin_quote
The plot states its window's high, low and change in the header, and marks the
high and the low on the plot itself.
#+end_quote

No screen does this today. Both reporting prototypes state a title and a unit in
the plot head, and the value axis range in the caption. Neither states a high, a
low or a change there, and neither marks an extreme on the plot. The marks both
plots draw name the run in view, the baseline, or the latest run, never a high or
a low.

** The statistics of a selected range, and a crosshair readout

The requirement, in the owner's words:

#+begin_quote
Selecting a range of the series reports the statistics for just that range, and a
crosshair readout states the value under the pointer as it moves.
#+end_quote

No screen does this today. Neither prototype selects a range of a series, and
neither handles a pointer event. A point carries an SVG title element, so a
browser shows its own tooltip on hover, and that is not a crosshair readout.
There is no rule under the pointer, no value read as the pointer moves, and no
stated precision. The nearest control either page has is the baseline picker on the run
prototype, and it chooses another run to compare against rather than a range of
one series.

** Overlays and comparison modes

The requirement, in the owner's words:

#+begin_quote
Bollinger bands, realised volatility, and a mode that plots the difference
against history rather than the level.
#+end_quote

No screen does this today. No Bollinger band and no realised volatility is drawn
anywhere in the repo. The nearest piece of the third item is the change plot on
the run prototype, which draws each book's NPV change against a previous run of
the same definition as signed horizontal bars. That is a difference against one
previous run, across books rather than over time, so it answers a different
question from the one the requirement asks.

* What the two reporting prototypes do today

Both pages were read, not assumed: their HTML, their =prototype.js= and the plot
comments in them.

** Read what a run produced

- *Requirement 1, not met.* The plot head states a title, a unit and a partial
  tag, and no figure. The value axis range is in the caption and in the
  aria-label. No high, low or change is stated in the header, and neither extreme
  is marked on the line; the only marks are the run in view and the baseline.
- *Requirement 2, not met.* The page has no range selection and no pointer
  handler; its listeners are =change=, =click= and =keydown= on controls. Nothing
  follows the pointer and nothing reports the statistics of a range.
- *Requirement 3, partly met, and only the comparison.* =changePlot= draws each
  book's NPV change against a previous run of the same definition, signed and
  drawn from a zero rule that is the point of no change. That is a difference
  against history, but over books and against exactly one run, never against the
  window's mean and never with a band. No Bollinger band and no realised
  volatility is drawn.

** Look back over a report's history

- *Requirement 1, not met.* The plot head states a title, a unit and a partial
  tag. The caption and the aria-label state the value axis range and the span of
  dates the points run over, and the latest run's point is marked and named. No
  high, low or change is stated, and neither extreme is marked.
- *Requirement 2, not met.* No range selection and no pointer handler. Each point
  carries an SVG title element with the run id, its start and its value, so the
  browser shows its own tooltip. That is a tooltip and not a readout. It draws no
  rule, reads no value as the pointer moves, and states no precision.
- *Requirement 3, not met.* No overlay is drawn and no difference series is
  plotted. Variant C puts the latest run beside one earlier run and states what
  differs, which compares two runs as figures rather than as a plotted
  difference.

The one piece of the three that exists anywhere is the difference against a
previous run in the run prototype.

* What the repo states about plotting

No document under =doc/knowledge/= states a plotting convention that a reporting
plot can follow. The tree was searched by file name and by content for a plot, a
chart, an axis, a sparkline, an overlay, a baseline and a partial mark. Most hits
are the domain sense of the word, a curve axis or a market data overlay. Three
documents hold a rule a plot can use:

- The two reporting journeys state the plot vocabulary, the rule that chooses a
  form, and the honesty rules. They are
  [[id:969C14D2-4280-4D7C-8F51-1D395B521C83][Read what a run produced]] and
  [[id:465CA244-C488-4C69-ACA3-C694D104A42A][Look back over a report's history]].
- [[id:303D85B2-0039-4B1C-83BD-BB866BC0AEE1][Feed screen standard]] states one convention for a chart card: the card
  heads with what it shows and the high and the low, and an optional EMA over
  twenty ticks is drawn dashed over the line. That is a precedent for the header
  figures and for an overlay, on the market data Ticks tab and not on a
  reporting screen.

So the header's high and low and an overlay both have a precedent in the
knowledge base, under a screen that draws a price series. The change figure, the
range selection, the crosshair, the Bollinger band, the realised volatility and
the difference mode have none. A plot built from this capture either extends the
feed screen's convention and the two reporting journeys, or needs a convention of
its own.

* The constraints any implementation inherits

** The plots are inline SVG with no library and no network

Both prototypes say why in the comments at the top of their =prototype.js=. A page
opened from =file://= has no build, no CDN and no network, so a chart library
cannot be loaded and none is copied in. Every plot is written by hand as SVG:
domains, ticks, segments, point marks and the axis rule are computed in the page.

The three requirements can be built the same way, and no library decision is
needed to meet them. A high, a low, a change, a mean and a standard deviation are
arithmetic passes over a series the page already holds. A range selection is a
pointer hit-test against the x scale the plot already computes. A crosshair is a
vertical rule and a label moved on =pointermove=. The two prototypes already
handle the parts a library would otherwise supply.

A library decision is needed only past these requirements, when the plot must pan,
zoom, draw a series too dense to hit-test cheaply, or animate. That decision
belongs to the work that needs it, not to this capture.

** The honesty rules the existing plots follow

Both prototypes state them, in the plot comments and in the caption each plot
draws:

- *An axis baseline is stated.* A bar is drawn from the zero rule, and a negative
  value draws a bar on the other side of it. A line states the range of its value
  axis and says in words that the axis does not start at zero, so the reader is
  told rather than left to the eye.
- *A partial result is marked and never drawn as if it were whole.* A figure that
  excludes a trade the engine could not build is a floor. A bar or a point drawn
  from it is dashed or hollow, carries the same partial tag the figure carries,
  and the caption names what it leaves out.
- *An excluded part is drawn rather than dropped.* A run that produced no figure
  breaks the line at a drawn gap, and the caption says a failure is not a value
  of zero. A donut draws what the errors left out as a hatched slice named
  excluded, so the whole is never silently smaller than the run.
- *The exact numbers stay available in text beside the plot.* A table under the
  trend carries every plotted number, the by-book table carries every bar, and
  every SVG carries a text alternative in its aria-label. A plot never replaces
  the figures a reader checks.

** What the new features inherit from those rules

- *A Bollinger band drawn from a partial series must say so.* A band states what
  the series usually does, and a window holding one point drawn from a floor is
  not the whole answer. The band must carry the partial mark and the caption must
  name the points that make it partial. Realised volatility carries the same
  rule, because one partial point in the window changes it.
- *A crosshair readout must not imply a precision the data does not have.* The
  readout states a value in the precision the source carries and no more, names
  the run or the timestamp it belongs to, and states the state of that run. It
  never interpolates a value between two runs and prints it as measured, and it
  never prints a figure that a failed run does not have.
- *The statistics of a range inherit the same rule.* A mean or a high over a
  range holding a partial point is partial, and says so in the same words the
  existing plots use.

* Open questions

These are questions, not answers. The future work settles them before a control
or a read exists.

- *What is the realised volatility window, and is it stated or chosen?* The
  window the plot draws, a fixed count of runs such as the last twenty, or a
  period such as the last thirty days? The screen fixes it and states it in the
  header, or the person chooses it and the header states the choice?
- *What does the difference against history compare against?* The previous run, a
  fixed baseline such as the first run of the window, or the window's mean each
  answers a different question, and each draws a different difference series.
- *Are the Bollinger bands two standard deviations, and of which series?* The
  requirement gives the name and not the arithmetic: the standard deviation of
  the level, of the change, or of the log return, with a sample or a population
  divisor, and around an arithmetic or a geometric mean.
- *Do hidden series participate in the window's high and low?* When a series is
  toggled off, or the scope narrows, is the header's high the high of what is
  drawn or the high of what the window holds? The two answers differ wherever the
  hidden series holds an extreme.
- *What does a range selection do when the range spans a partial result or a
  gap?* Report the statistics over the points it holds and mark them partial, or
  refuse and say why? A range holding a failed run has no value for that run, and
  a mean over the rest is not the mean of the range.
- *Does the crosshair snap to a point or read the line between points?* A readout
  that snaps states a measurement the data holds. One that interpolates states a
  value nobody recorded, which the honesty rules above cannot allow without a
  word that says it is interpolated.
- *What is the change of a window holding one point?* The existing plots refuse to
  draw a trend of one point because it states a direction the data does not have.
  The header's change has the same problem and no stated answer.

* Why it is deferred

This is captured for a future decision. No plotting work is scheduled. No task
under [[id:6F7F7843-0A12-443A-A8FB-1A0FFA25E6CC][Manage reports from the list a party
runs]] plans a plot or a chart, and that story's screens are the list of reports a
party runs and the arc of one report. The requirements also need the answers above
before a control can be designed. The deepest of them, the comparison baseline,
decides what the band means.
