* Summary

The practice of fixings implies a small set of entities: a /source/, with its
administrator, method, calendar and status; a /definition/, which is one
source's series for one asset, such as the WM/Reuters 4pm fix of AUD/CAD; an
/observation/ of a definition on a fixing date, at system level; a /party
fixing/, one party's adoption of an observation, with its status and any
override; a trade's /schedule/ of fixing dates; and the /event/ that authorises
or undoes a party fixing. ORE Studio already holds definitions, as ORE indices,
and observations, as versioned fixing series. It does not yet hold the source
as an entity, the status of a source, or the party fixing.

The three invariants of [[id:C6A619F3-B2B6-42D5-9C41-5269A84BC96C][observation storage]] apply: an
observation is never stamped on a trade, its history is append-only, and the
official fixings of a party for a definition form an unbroken prefix in time.

** A source reaches the store through its publisher

Every source has a publisher, and every publisher reaches the store through an
adapter. The model holds the publisher and not the adapter, so nothing in it
depends on which adapter serves a publisher. A derived source is the one source
without a publisher of its own: its observations are computed from its
parents'.

A ceased source, such as a LIBOR setting, keeps its definitions and its past
observations. ORE Studio also keeps the LIBOR settings as legacy sources for
teaching; their status records that they have ceased.

** What ORE Studio holds today

| Entity | Held | Where |
|--------+------+-------|
| Definition | Yes | The oresmd fixing URI and its =market_index= model, one per ORE index. See [[id:C3E053CA-0D4B-480B-9119-E11530160EC1][oresmd: ORE Studio Market Data URI]]. |
| Observation | Yes | A versioned row of the fixing series of the definition, under the system party. See [[id:F4BBEF34-D84C-4AE6-A3AA-DC47C3EE06EE][Public publishers publish daily fixings through a supplier session]]. |
| Source | In part | The FX publisher is a field of the FX index (=source=ECB=); the public publishers are a catalogue with terms. No source holds a method, a calendar, a status or a version, and an interest rate index names no source at all. |
| Publisher | Yes, for the central banks | The public publishers are a catalogue with terms, reached through adapters. The administrators of the other sources are not yet publishers in the catalogue. |
| Party fixing, override, event, schedule | No | |

The gap that matters most is the missing source. Without it the system cannot
answer which definitions the market fixes and who fixes each. Trade generation
and fixing checks both need that answer: a generated trade should name only a
definition whose source has a publisher.

The supported sources are listed in
[[id:5AF9CEEF-A878-443A-8C32-C6E9F6C26FC9][Fixing sources for option cuts and non-deliverable currencies]].

** Open questions

- How a derived observation is timed when its parents fix at different times:
  whether it waits for the later parent, and what it shows until then.
- How a definition records the version of its source in force on a trade date,
  so that a trade reads the terms it was agreed under.
- Whether a keyed future observation is an observation with a status, or a
  separate record that becomes an observation when it takes effect.
