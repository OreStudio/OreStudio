* Summary

The Acme tenant is the platform's fully populated demonstration: four legal
entities, 73 staff, a market data subscription each, a trade population and the
approval requests the trades raise. Two questions about it are hard to answer from
the code, and this note orders the pages that answer them. First, where does an
Acme party's market data come from, and who installs it. Second, who may decide
the approvals the demonstration raises, and why the person who decides is never
the person who asked.

The cluster is six pages. They are read in the order below, because each one
assumes the vocabulary of the one before it: the providers first, then the
catalogue they belong to, then the two rows that tie a party to a provider, then
the profile that writes those rows, and only then the two pages about people —
the roles they hold and the decisions those roles let them take. A reader who
wants only the approval side can start at section five.

* Where the market data comes from

Read these three to be able to say, for any value on an Acme screen, which
provider produced it, which source delivered it, and which row tied the party to
that source.

   Seven providers, six sources, and which of them each of the four entities
   takes. After this you can say why London reads =demo= and New York =lseg=.
2. [[id:F6094D03-3914-4046-8E57-3FF4AA14764B][The Acme catalogue is installed by the bundle, and the feeds generate the
   series]]. The 447 instruments, 447 mappings and 24 rate card components the
   system bundle installs, the feed collections that then run, and the 341 series
   they produce. After this you can say what a tenant may and may not write.
3. [[id:9E8D00D3-5ADD-48E0-85AB-CC19693DEFFF][A subscription names the provider and the route names the source]]. The two
   rows, the rights on them, the flow a demand follows to a tick, and who may
   change them. This page carries the demand and subscription diagram. After this
   you can trace a run's market data end to end.
