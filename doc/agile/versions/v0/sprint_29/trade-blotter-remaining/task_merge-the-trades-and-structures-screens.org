* Goal

Settle whether a structure is a row in the trade blotter or a screen beside it,
and design the one screen that reads trades and structures together from more
than one perspective: the portfolio and book tree, the counterparty, and the
book.

Today the arc has two screens with no shared surface. The trade blotter lists
trades and cannot show a deal; the structures list shows deals and cannot show a
trade's economics; the deal's page repeats the legs that the blotter already
holds. A person who wants "everything we did with this counterparty today" has to
read two lists and join them by eye.

The deliverable is a decision, not a screen: which shape the screens take, what
each perspective asks, and what the read that serves them states. A prototype
follows the decision, and the screen follows the prototype.

The data layer already agrees. The trade summary view emits rows of two kinds —
=trade= and =structure= — and the blotter's own read filters on =row_kind=. The
screen beside the blotter is a screen that reads the same rows with a different

** The shape, as the owner decided it on 2026-10-29

One screen, not two. It opens on a portfolio tree on the left and the trade list
beside it. The list's rows are what a person books and sees at the top level: the
top-level trades, and the structures — packages and the other rungs of the
composition ladder — as single rows. A structure's legs are not rows of that list,
because they are inside it.

A row opens what it is: a trade that belongs to no structure opens the trade view,
and a structure opens the structure view. The two record screens survive as what a
row opens; they stop being places a person browses to on their own.

A *flat* option turns the same list into leaves: the structures expanded to their
legs, so every row is a trade. The option is an option and not the default,
because the flat list loses the commercial unit the customer bought while it makes
the risk readable trade by trade.

The two screens this arc built are therefore replaced by one: the structures list
folds into the blotter, and the structure's page becomes the structure view a row
opens. The existing blotter's structure view — a mini-blotter of the selected
structure's legs, tabbed by product type — is the same idea at a smaller scale and
is folded in with it.

** Where a structure sits, decided

The book question is settled by
[[id:1E035C4E-119B-472E-96FA-310D8B7C7CD8][The book of a structure]] and adopted
here: one book per structure is a booking convention and not an invariant, so it is
not enforced. The blotter places a structure under the book its legs share when
they share one, and otherwise at the lowest common ancestor of their books — the
desk when they share one, the party when they do not — and the row says *spans
books*. The prototype draws both cases.

** Perspectives, and why the word needs exploring

The owner's framing: a trade set has two or three topologies it can be read
through, and each answers a different desk's question.

| Topology | The question | Serves | What the read needs |
|----------+--------------+--------+---------------------|
| The portfolio tree, with or without structures | What does this book hold, and how does it roll up? | Trading | The portfolio, book and desk hierarchy, a roll-up per node, and the flat option |
| The counterparty topology | What have we done with this counterparty, and when? | Sales, and credit risk exposure | Counterparty on every row, a roll-up per counterparty, and the netting set |
| The instrument and asset-class topology | What risk are we carrying in this family, and where? | Trading, and risk | The instrument family and asset class on every row, rolled up |

The first is the default and the one that ships first: it is the hierarchy the
books already model, and it is what a trader opens the screen for. The second is
for sales and credit risk; the third is the risk cut. The task explores what a
perspective actually is before building one — a saved view of one list, a
different grouping and column set over the same read, a separate screen, or a
different read — and states the test that tells them apart. *Perspective* is
therefore a working word in this task, not a decided concept.

The prototype settles the first reading of the word: a perspective is a *topology
over the same rows and not a different read*. The rail changes — the party's desks
and books, the counterparties, the asset classes — the rows narrow, and the
columns stay the ones a trade has. It is chosen by a control on the screen and
carried in the address, so a perspective is shareable.

Two things follow and are answered by the prototype rather than assumed: the left
rail changes with the perspective (it *is* the perspective), and the control and
the address both name it — a control that is not in the address is not shareable,
and an address that the control does not show is not discoverable.

** The read

The blotter's read is one page of =trade_summaries= with a filter. A structure
row already arrives from it. What the screen needs beyond today's read is: the
row kinds it may ask for (trade, structure, or both), the grouping the view
states (portfolio, book, counterparty), and the roll-up a group row carries. The
decision states which of those join the existing read and which need a new one;
it does not add a route per perspective.

** The prototype

[[id:5C1D6A0B-5A0C-4E8C-9F58-2F0B7C7A1D31][Trade blotter prototype]] is the
artefact: a portfolio tree beside the list, the row kinds, the flat option, the
three perspectives, and the trade view and structure view reached from a row. It
is the shape the screen is built from, as with the pages this arc already built.

** The build, in units

The prototypes are signed off, so the work is the screens. The summary view already
states both row kinds, and =GET /api/blotter= reads one page of them with the tree
beside it — the party, its portfolios and their books, each with the count of the
rows under it. What remains, in order:

1. *The read, finished.* The page narrows after it is read, because the summary has
   no structure column a filter can name, and the tree asks for a count per node
   rather than one grouped read. Both want the summary view to state the structure
   a trade is a leg of, and a grouped count per book and portfolio.
2. *The blotter screen.* A tree panel on the left and the list beside it, a flat
   option, and the perspective control. It reuses =RecordList= for the rows, so
   the pager, the search and the empty and error states are the house ones.
3. *The routes.* A trade row opens =/trading/trades/:id= and a structure row
   =/trading/structures/:reference=, which already exist and stay.
4. *The removal.* The trades list and the structures list become redirects to the
   blotter, and the Trading area offers one card, *Trade blotter*, whose permission
   is the trades read and the structures read together.

** What this task does not do

It does not build the screen, move the routes, or change the read. It decides,
prototypes, and leaves the build to the tasks it files.

  serves them and the states on it.

- The question came from the owner on 2026-10-29, after the structures screens
  were reviewed: "we created a complete separation between trades and structures
  but to me that is not how a real trading system behaves", and "we need a screen
  that combines the portfolio tree and the trades display, possibly from
  different perspectives (e.g. counterparty perspective rather than portfolio
  tree and so on)". The task exists to answer that with evidence rather than
  preference.
- The owner then fixed the shape in the same conversation, and it is the one this
  task designs: "we need a trade blotter which opens up with the default view of a
  portfolio tree on the left hand side, and the trade list in the middle of the
  screen. the trade list contains top level trades, structures packages etc. if a
  user clicks on a trade which is not in a structure, it shows the trade view. if
  the user clicks on a structure it shows the structure view. legs are not in the
  trade list as they are inside the structure. however, a user may want to see a
  flat view, in which case we only show the legs for structures plus leaf trades."
- The same conversation fixed the perspectives' purpose: the first is for trading,
  the second for sales and credit risk. The owner also asked for the word itself to
  be explored, which this task does rather than assuming a control.
- The owner ruled out *deal* as a term in the same breath. It is recorded as a
  memory, and this task's acceptance makes the screens it designs use the
  composition ladder's words. Today the English copy says it in ten keys under
  =trading.structure= and =trading.booking=, the server's refusal messages say it,
  the browser cases say it, and the story's own pages say it.
- The two screens that exist today were built in
  [[id:2D3C5B23-E6DB-46D5-8732-ACF54987A33A][Build Book a trade]] and its
  structure screens; their shapes follow the prototypes, and the prototypes did
  not answer this question.
- The summary view's two row kinds are also why the blotter's search finds a deal
  by its own reference: a structure is already a row there.
- The counterparty rule is a separate, open question: the store enforces the party
  of a structure's legs and not the counterparty, while the screens state both.
  Whichever shape this task picks has to state what it can enforce.
- The book question — whether all trades under a structure share one book, and
  what the tree does when they do not — was investigated separately:
  [[id:1E035C4E-119B-472E-96FA-310D8B7C7CD8][The book of a structure]]. Its
  verdict: the rule is a booking convention and not an invariant, so a structure
  row is placed at the lowest common ancestor of its legs' books and says so when
  it spans.
- What is not prior art: no public Murex or Calypso documentation describes their
  internal trade-package model. The vendor pages are cited for the shape of the
  answer, and the schema documentation for the data model.
