#+title: The derived source cannot warm a tenant inside the blueprint step's wait
#+description: A surface blueprint waits for the volatility quotes its derived source owes it, and gives up after a few minutes. At ten pairs and a year of fixings the wait was met; at 39 pairs and ten years it is not, because the derived source materialises daily quotes over the whole fixing history, per tenant. 156 blueprints waited and none was given while the derived source was still deriving. The cost is the backfill times the pair count, so the wait and the history are the same knob.

This page is a [[id:671F18E4-E09C-4B3B-BD24-D33DF8AE38A6][capture]] in the *inbox* bucket of the product backlog — a pre-sprint idea, not yet pulled into a sprint as a story.

* What

The derivation's cost is fixed: one pass over the returns fills the whole grid, and the feed keeps
a five-day tail instead of a history. Measured after that change, a full 39-pair pass takes two to
three seconds in the service log, where it took hours. What remains is the step that waits on it,
and two pairs that no source quotes.

=give_party_surface_blueprints= gives each party a copy of the system's surface blueprints and
waits for the quotes each one reads. The wait is a few minutes -- it asked again every ten seconds
and gave up about nineteen tries in. With ten pairs and one year of fixings the derived source
answered inside it. With 39 pairs and ten years of fixings it does not:

#+begin_src text
  [11/14] give_party_surface_blueprints: failed — 156 surface blueprint(s) were not given.
          ... no series of derived oresmd://fx/NZD?type=series&instrument=fx_option&quote=rate_lnvol&ccy=JPY
#+end_src

Every one of the 39 pairs' series exists, for the tenant that asked first: after the run aborted,
all 39 were there. The series are created per tenant, and the derivation walks the whole fixing
history for each pair, so the work a new tenant owes the derived source grows with the backfill's
length and the pair count together.

* Why

The ten-year grid is not what fails; the *materialisation* is. The derived source builds each
expiry from a window of daily returns as long as the expiry itself, so a ten-year quote needs ten
years of fixings, and it derives every day of that history rather than the current day alone. Ten
times the history is ten times the derivation, per tenant, inside a wait that was sized for one
year of it.

Measured on this branch: at 400 days of backfill and 10 pairs, a fresh tenant provisions in about
three minutes and publishes 40 surfaces. At 3650 days and 39 pairs, the same step exceeds its wait
and the run fails at step 11 of 14.

* What to read next

- =projects/ores.synthetic/api/src/feeds/derived_fx_vol_feed.cpp=: the derivation loop, and its
  own comment that it publishes the current day only because a past day can be computed again.
  The store is what makes the first tenant expensive.
- The step's wait: a workflow step's timeout and its own retry count, versus the derivation's
  throughput. Either the wait becomes a function of the work, or the derived source is asked to
  warm itself before the blueprints need it, or the history the store keeps is bounded to what a
  consumer asks for.

* What it looks like now

After the one-pass derivation, the first run of the step still fails for one party and names two
pairs that cannot be quoted at all:

#+begin_src text
  progress: ACME Corporation US Inc: surface blueprints with their quotes 37 of 39
  progress: ACME Corporation HK Ltd: surface blueprints with their quotes 37 of 39
  [11/14] give_party_surface_blueprints: failed — 45 surface blueprint(s) were not given.
          Acme Corporation Plc: ... 39 blueprint(s) ...
          ... USDCLP_SSVI, USDCOP_SSVI for the other three parties
#+end_src

Two distinct things, and only the first is new:

- The first party is given nothing while the other three are given 37 of 39 each, although all
  four read the same per-tenant series and all 39 series exist by the time the run ends. The step
  asks a party again every ten seconds and gave up eighteen times.

  The two halves of that sentence sit in two different places, and the gap between them is the
  defect. =plan_party_surfaces= -- =ores.iam.core/messaging/party_surface_blueprints.hpp= -- looks
  the quotes up among the *party's own* series, =own_series.find({oresmd_uri, provider_code})=,
  and refuses the blueprint when the party holds none. The derived adapter that writes those
  series -- =ores.synthetic.service/src/derived_fx_vol_adapter.cpp=, =make_store()= -- creates and
  writes every one of them **under the tenant's system party**, from =system_party()=. A business
  party's own copy therefore has to come from somewhere else, and for the group entity it does
  not: the other three parties got theirs. The next reader should find who copies a provider's
  series onto a party -- the curve path has the same shape -- and why the first party in the
  profile misses it.
- USD/CLP and USD/COP have no volatility quotes and never will from this source: the ECB publishes
  no CLP or COP fixing, so the derivation says so by name -- =not_found: The fixing item
  oresmd://fx/EUR?type=fixing&index=fx&source=ECB&ccy=CLP does not exist=. Their blueprints should
  not be in the catalogue, or the pair needs a source that quotes it.

* Resolved

On a recreated database at 35 pairs and 3650 days of fixings, the blueprint step gives every
party, the first one included, all of its blueprints, and the run completes all 17 steps. The
one-pass derivation took the cost out of the wait. The two pairs no source quotes are left out of
the catalogue until the derived source reads each pair from the source that quotes it.
