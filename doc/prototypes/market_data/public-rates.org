* Summary

The Public rates screen does governance today: it lists the public publishers,
their terms and their stored fixings, and it lets a person accept the terms at
the version shown. It does not answer the operational question a system
administrator asks next: is the data actually arriving, and what do I do when
it is not.

This prototype argues the shape of that answer before any control is built, and
it is the shape the story will implement: six surfaces under one title. The
publisher is the object over time, and each tab answers one question about it or
about the underlying it fixes.

#+ATTR_HTML: :class hug-leading
| Surface | Question | Reads |
|---------+----------+-------|
| Publishers (list) | Who is arriving? | publishers, feed state, latest fixing per item |
| Publisher record · Overview | What does this publisher's record say? | publisher, feeds |

| Publisher record · Fixings | What did it publish for this pair, over time? | fixings by series, feed start |
| Publisher record · History | Who changed it, and when? | publisher history |

| Availability (area tab) | Which publishers hold this item? | fixings by series |
| Compare (area tab) | How far apart are their values? | fixings by series, arithmetic in the page |
| Fetch (area tab) | Ask for data now | the refresh request, not yet on the contract |

* A publisher, a feed and a route

A value belongs to its publisher. A feed is one publisher on one service through
one session, and the route is the party's choice of one feed for one publisher
and one asset class. A second feed of the same publisher is another way to the
same fixing, not a second fixing: the item keeps its identity, and the feed is
provenance.

The list carries the count of underlyings, never the pairs themselves: a
publisher fixes tens or hundreds of pairs, and the record's Underlyings tab is
where they are read. The record page is the standard shape: a breadcrumb, a
header with the terms and the state, the acts, and tabs.

The publisher and the availability surfaces do not need a new read: a fixings
read answers a window of up to twelve series, and the coverage and the distances
are arithmetic over it. Fetch needs the refresh request the design task names,
and a publisher's expected rhythm, which the record does not carry yet.

* What it asks

1. What does a paused publisher look like, and what happens to its fixings, its
   chart and its pair on the comparison while it is paused?
2. While a backfill of a chosen range runs, what does the person see: a
   progress, the table filling day by day, or nothing until it ends?
3. How does a late fixing read differently from a feed that is down, and
   how does a publisher that never had a fixing read, without three colours
   meaning the same thing?
4. Does accepting terms that withdraw permission to store show deleting the
   publisher's fixings as a consequence, or as a command the person must
   remember?

* What it argues

- *One row, four questions.* The row answers who the publisher is, whether we may
  fetch it, whether it is arriving, and what a person may do about it. The five
  badges that follow from those questions are mutually exclusive: accepted terms
  and no fixing yet is =never fixed=; accepted, serving and no value today is
  =late=; a stopped heartbeat is =not serving=; a person's pause is =paused=.
- *Freshness is not tick age.* The session read's last-tick age is useless for a
  publisher that publishes once a day, so the row states the last fixing's date,
  the time it was received, and whether the next publication is late. The
  session's heartbeat stays in its own column, where a stopped feed reads as
  one.
- *The paused row stays.* A paused publisher keeps its fixings and the feed that
  started from one, so hiding it from the list loses the reason a feed's start
  names it. The prototype keeps it, dimmed, and draws the hidden variant beside
  it for comparison: the hidden variant needs a count and a way back, and it
  makes the pair comparison lose a column silently.
- *A backfill is a request, and the screen says whose.* The dialog names the
  session as the only party that can answer it, and says that until the contract
  carries the request the screen offers only the window the service fetches
  itself. The done state says what the store did: asked for 300 days, stored
  280, skipped 20 it already held.
- *Deleting fixings is a consequence, not a command.* The prototype does not
  draw it as a row action. It is offered after a terms version withdraws
  storage, in the same place the person accepts the new version, and it names
  the feeds that started from a value that goes.

* What is fake

- Every publisher, item, fixing, date and time is invented.
- The monthly coverage matrix is invented for EUR/USD only; every other pair
  falls back to empty month cells, which is the shape, not a measurement.
- The session's state and heartbeat are written into the mock data; the real
  read answers them per session id, with the ids it serves and refuses.
- The fetch progress and the counts in its done state are fixed numbers.
- The expected rhythm on the configuration surface is the answer the record
  cannot give yet, and the =not modelled yet= chip says so.
- Nothing is written. Accepting terms and deleting fixings are inert buttons.
  Pause and resume move the query string. Fetch moves the address to the running
  state and no request leaves the page.
- The Pause act carries a =design pending= note: the pause is a demand decision
  the story has not taken yet.

* Verdict

The shape is agreed for review: the row carries terms, freshness, next
publication, the feed and the acts; the three failures have three sentences;
the paused row stays unless the hidden variant's count and the lost comparison
column are wanted; the backfill is a dialog that says the feed answers it.

The paused row stays, and the hidden variant's only gain is a shorter list
whose pair comparison silently loses a column. The withdrawn-terms state draws
the deletion where the person accepts the new version, never as a row command.
The availability and compare surfaces read the same fixings the publisher page
reads; neither needs a new operation.

What the prototype cannot settle is what this story's two decision tasks own:
whether the pause is a demand fact or a publisher state, and what form the
backfill request takes on the session contract. The prototype's choices are the
input to those decisions, not their substitute.

* The setup is a screen of its own

The publishers, sessions, feeds, scopes, schedules and routes are configured in
[[file:connectivity.org][Connectivity]]. This prototype reads the values; it
names the feed that serves each publisher, and links to its record, but it
configures none of it.
