#+title: Pin risk in the expiry of near-the-money options
#+description: Near the strike, the spot used flips the decision and the position; such options are set aside and decided late.
#+type: knowledge
#+audience: user

* Summary

An option whose strike is close to spot at its cut is hard to expire. A small
difference in the spot used, or in the moment it is read, flips the decision
between exercise and expiry, and with it a position worth the full notional.
There may be no single undisputed spot, so two options on one pair expiring at
one cut can be decided on different spots. Practice is to decide the clear
cases in bulk and to set aside those within a tolerance of the strike, deciding
them later on the spot recorded at the cut.

* Detail

** Why near the money is hard

Just before expiry a near-the-money option has a small delta, so the desk holds
a small hedge. At expiry the position becomes either nothing or the full
notional in the underlying. The decision is therefore large, and it rests on a
spot that the market may not agree on at the second it is needed. The
literature calls the related concentration of prices at strikes on expiry days
/pinning/: Ni, Pearson and Poteshman (2005) document the clustering of stock
prices at option strikes on expiration dates, and Avellaneda and Lipkin (2003)
give a mechanism by which the hedging of option positions drives it.

** Tolerance and the deferred decision

A tolerance around the strike defines /close/: an absolute interval for one
pair, or a percentage or a number of pips for any pair. Options inside the
tolerance are set aside, the others are exercised or expired in bulk, and the
set-aside options are decided once the cut has passed, on the spot at the cut.
A trader may decide them much later, so the spot at every cut must be recorded
and kept. A set-aside option must also be signed off, so that none is
forgotten.

The holder keeps the final say. For a European vanilla, moneyness sets the
default, but a client may exercise an out-of-the-money option, or let an
in-the-money one lapse to avoid a delta it would then have to hedge. The
parties may agree the conditions for exercise in advance.

** Working through a cut

Traders expire their own books. They need to see, for each cut, the options
still to decide; to exclude a book with a recorded reason that reports show;
and to see which of the day's cuts are complete. See
[[id:FE1C63AB-A242-4B02-86AA-8035EEC8DF54][Cut-by-cut expiry management]].
