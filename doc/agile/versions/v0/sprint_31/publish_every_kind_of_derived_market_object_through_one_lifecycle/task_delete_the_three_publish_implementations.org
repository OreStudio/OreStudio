- The other two rows this task started with are now their own tasks, because neither is a deletion in market data: [[id:4ED5A5FE-7BBF-4378-AEFD-C22004F14D39][Serve or retire the series slice subject]] crosses the protocol, the BFF and the gateway, and [[id:12B71000-B339-4807-815B-5FD290383EA3][Fail the gate on a declared subject with no handler]] changes the check tooling.

* Plan

The three files held two things each: the kind's implementation of the
interface, and the per-kind publish facade the operations called. The second is
what the story removes, so the files are replaced by one file per kind that
holds only the kind plus its reads.

The approval kind codes and the publish status words move to
publication_kind_codes.hpp, which has no dependencies and is what the handlers
read. =raise_permission_of= moves into the lifecycle, because it asks the same
question the raise asks. The two reader classes are named for what they do:
=fx_forward_published_reader= and =fx_vol_surface_published_reader=.

The handlers answer the request themselves now: the raise, or the apply of an
approved request, through the two acts, with the response mapped where the
message shape is known. The provisioning step does the same, and its two set
raises become calls to =raise_publication=.

The tests keep their cases and change their doors. The old exported functions
are gone, so the suite gains =tests/curve_publish_steps.hpp=: the same six steps
(resolve, verdict, raise, raise a set, record, live as-of) built on the two acts.
The curve's regression is therefore still the curve's behaviour, stated through
the one implementation, and the cases themselves are untouched.

Two acceptance rows are not this task's work, and the pattern was already
visible in the tree: =get_series_slice= is declared, generated and called by
both the BFF and the gateway, so retiring it is a protocol change across three
components and serving it is a new handler; and the gate that fails a declared
subject with no handler is a change to the check tooling, not to market data.
Each is scaffolded as its own task, and the reason is recorded in the Result.

* Result

The three files are gone. Each kind now lives in one file named for what it
implements -- curve_publication_kind.cpp, fx_forward_publication_kind.cpp,
fx_vol_surface_publication_kind.cpp -- and holds the kind plus its own reads,
because the reads were never a publish implementation and the story only
removes the acts.

What moved rather than disappeared: the approval kind codes and the publish
status words to publication_kind_codes.hpp, raise_permission_of into the
lifecycle, and blocking_failure_of into the surface's own header. The two
readers are named for what they do, fx_forward_published_reader and
fx_vol_surface_published_reader.

The handlers answer their own requests now: the raise, or the apply of an
approved request, through the two acts, with the response mapped where the
message shape is known. The provisioning step does the same, and its two set
raises call raise_publication.

The suite kept every case and changed its door. tests/curve_publish_steps.hpp
states the six steps the old exported functions gave it -- resolve, verdict,
raise, raise a set, record, live as-of -- on top of the two acts, so the curve's
regression is still the curve's behaviour and the cases are untouched.

The gate: build clean, database recreated, non-heavy checks 9 of 9, ticks-smoke
and step-consumers pass -- so the handlers this task rewired and the
provisioning step that raises sets still run end to end.
ores.marketdata.service.tests passes, which is where the refactored cases run,
and ores.reporting.core.tests passes too. ctest reports 105 of 108: the three
that fail are ores.inbox.core.tests, ores.refdata.core.tests and
ores.workflow.core.tests, the pooler and shared-tenant race the previous
branches also hit. Re-running them needs the fleet the gate has already
stopped, so this task leans on the experiment recorded on task B153E922: on a
fresh database, serially, all three pass.

Two rows of the original acceptance became tasks instead of code, and the
reason is in each: get_series_slice is called by both the BFF and the gateway,
so the subject is not dead from the caller's side -- the service is -- and
retiring it touches three components; and the gate that fails a declared
subject with no handler belongs to the check tooling, not to market data.
