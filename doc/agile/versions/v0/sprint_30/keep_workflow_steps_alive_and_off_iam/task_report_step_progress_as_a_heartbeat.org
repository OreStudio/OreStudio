* Acceptance

- A step's heartbeat carries its progress, and the engine records the latest.
- The engine fails a step only when its heartbeat has stalled for a bounded time, with no fixed per-step deadline.
- The shell prints each running step's progress in place of a countdown.

* Plan

Today a worker renews its step's claim lease in the background, and each
renewal moves =lease_expires_at= forward. The engine's deadline pass ignores
the lease: it measures silence from =claimed_at= and fails the step when that
passes the budget, so a live step is failed twenty minutes after its claim.
The renewal carries no progress, so nothing can tell working from stuck.

The user decided that reported progress is the stall signal: a step stuck
inside a call keeps renewing its lease but stops progressing, and that is
the stall to catch.

1. The claim row gains the step's latest progress: a phase, a done and a
   total count, and when the progress last changed. A claim starts with no
   progress, changed at its claim.
2. A step reports progress through its context, as it advances, in the
   units natural to it. The renewal the worker already sends carries the
   latest report, and the repository stores it, moving the changed time
   only when the progress differs.
3. The engine fails a claimed step when its progress has not changed for
   the step's budget, which becomes a stall budget, or when its lease has
   lapsed past a grace, because then its worker is gone. Elapsed time since
   the claim no longer fails a step. An unclaimed step keeps today's queue
   rule.
4. The workflow's step status carries the progress, and the shell prints it
   for a running step.
5. The provisioning steps report progress: generate_trades per entry and
   per trade written, set_staff_passwords per page, and the curve and
   photo steps per item.
6. Prove it on Acme provisioning under load, and with a test step that
   stops progressing while it keeps its lease.

* Result

Merged as [[https://github.com/OreStudio/OreStudio/pull/4660][#4660]], =a4488c0d54=.
The claim the worker renews now carries what the step last reported, and the
engine measures silence from the last time that report moved.

- The claim row holds the step's latest progress — a phase, a done and a total
  count — and =progressed_at=, which moves only when the report differs from the
  one stored. Six repository cases hold it: a claim starts with no progress at
  its claim, a renewal reporting new progress moves the time, one reporting the
  same progress leaves it, one reporting nothing keeps the stored progress, and
  a takeover starts it afresh
  (=projects/ores.workflow/core/tests/repository_step_claim_repository_tests.cpp=).
- The engine fails a claimed step for standing still, not for taking long. The
  silence it measures runs from the later of the dispatch and the last progress
  the step reported, so a step that waited in a queue gets its whole budget once
  a worker takes it, and every report starts that budget again
  (=workflow_engine.cpp=, the deadline pass). Elapsed time since the claim no
  longer fails a step; the case "workflow_engine gives a claimed step its whole
  budget" proves a claim restarts the clock after two minutes of queueing, and
  the unclaimed-queue rule still refuses a command no service has picked up.
- The step summary carries the progress, and the shell prints it as it moves
  rather than a countdown (=workflow_run_commands.cpp=, =progress_line=).
- The provisioning steps report as they go: =generate_trades= per trade and per
  entry, =set_staff_passwords= per page, and the curve, forward and surface steps
  per item. The wire carries it to both clients.

What this does not prove: no case drives a step that keeps renewing its lease
while its progress stands still, so the stall path itself rests on the engine
code and the claim repository cases rather than on a test of its own. The live
Acme run on a recreated database is recorded in
[[id:BA56F4F5-AA1F-468E-AA7B-B322F20B6382][Make a workflow step survive a slow request]],
which shows the progress arriving while a step runs.
