#+title: Sandbox
#+description: Importing and experimenting without disturbing official data: the sandbox portfolio, virtual books that never reach the ledger, the states their trades may hold, how a sandbox trade becomes real, and the regulatory rules that bound it.
#+type: knowledge
#+level: cross
#+filetags: :knowledge:domain:finance:trading:book:sandbox:
#+created: 2026-10-02
#+updated: 2026-10-02

* Summary

Users need two things that must not disturb the firm's official data:

1. /Importing/ trades and market data to look at them — a set of ORE
   samples, a counterparty's portfolio, a file from another system.
2. /Experimenting/ — changing trades and market data, running reports
   against the changes, and comparing the results with the official
   ones.

The *sandbox* meets both without a second copy of the firm's data.
Trades are isolated by where they are booked and by what they are; market
data and configuration are isolated by what a report selects. Nothing is
hidden inside a container, and no official row is ever edited.

- The sandbox is a top-level [[id:5FC58A79-DBC9-46AF-9AAA-2C80B3A47850][portfolio]]. Its books are
  *virtual books*: books the trading system creates itself, which are
  never mapped to the ledger and hold no position of the firm.
- A trade in a virtual book is a draft, a test or a hypothetical. It is
  never live, so it is never a concluded agreement, never in a capital
  calculation, never reported and never in the ledger.
- A sandbox trade becomes real only through the ordinary lifecycle: a
  draft is executed into a real book, and a test or hypothetical is
  dealt as a new trade, linked to it.
- Imported and perturbed market data sit in the market data universe
  under their own source; the official configuration never resolves to
  them.
- A report runs against explicit inputs — the trades in scope, the
  market data configuration and snapshot, and the analytics
  configuration — so an experiment is a report whose inputs point into
  the sandbox.

* Detail

** The sandbox portfolio

Each tenant has one top-level portfolio named *Sandbox*. Under it:

| Portfolio | Holds | Typical content |
|-----------+-------+-----------------|
| Experiments | One sub-portfolio per user | What-if copies of official trades, hypothetical new trades, trial structures |
| Samples | One sub-portfolio per sample set | The trades of an imported ORE sample |
| Imports | One sub-portfolio per import | Trades imported to inspect, reconcile or rehearse a migration |

The name follows market usage: a sandbox is a space where actions have
no effect outside it. The analysis notes use the same word for the
holding area where broker-originated trades wait for a human check.

The portfolio tree is the trading system's own hierarchy and never
reaches the ledger (see [[id:5FC58A79-DBC9-46AF-9AAA-2C80B3A47850][Books and portfolios]]), so the
sandbox needs no accounting set-up. An experiments sub-portfolio is
visible to its owner only, in the way the analysis notes describe
personal portfolios: users experiment freely, but not where the firm's
official risk is computed.

** Virtual books

Every book the firm trades in maps to a book in the ledger and is
created by Finance; the trading system holds a view of them and cannot
create one. A *virtual book* is the one exception, and the rules that
make it safe are what define it:

1. /It is created by the trading system/, under the sandbox portfolio,
   by the user or process that needs it.
2. /It is never mapped to the ledger./ Its ledger feed is none, and no
   ledger book corresponds to it.
3. /It lives only under the sandbox./ A virtual book cannot be placed
   in any other portfolio, and a real book cannot be placed under the
   sandbox.
4. /It holds no position of the firm./ The next section states what
   its trades may be.
5. /Nothing official reads it./ Official P&L, risk, capital, the
   ledger, settlement, confirmation and regulatory reporting exclude
   virtual books by construction, because the trades in them cannot
   reach the states those processes act on.

A virtual book has a [[id:74AA46EB-64ED-4FD7-B212-AEC164648B84][classification]] like any other book,
because valuation needs one — a what-if is meaningless if it does not
know whether the trade would sit in the trading or the banking book.
The classification describes the book the experiment imitates; it does
not make the book part of either regulatory book.

** What a trade in a virtual book may be

| Booking nature | State | What it is | Example |
|----------------+-------+------------+---------|
| =hypothetical= | its own machine | A question; never an agreement | A what-if copy of a live trade, a trial hedge, an ORE sample trade |
| =test= | its own machine | An exercise of the system; never an agreement | A rehearsal of a migration, a test of a new product form |
| =actual= | =draft=, before any agreement | Recorded before any agreement exists | A request taken down over the phone, a quote being explored |

The [[id:03C12558-69AC-4925-AAE1-85FFF75B10BA][trade classification]] decides which machine a trade
runs through, and the hypothetical and test machines hold no transition
that confirms, settles or reaches the ledger. A draft is not an
agreement either (see [[id:B8D4F2A1-9E37-4C6B-A8D5-2F1E0C9B7A3D][Trade lifecycle]]).

The lifecycle also files a /pre-live/ trade — an agreement struck but
missing details — under =draft=. That trade is an agreement, so it
belongs in a real book, not in the sandbox; only a draft recorded
before any agreement may sit in a virtual book.

*A trade in a virtual book can never be live.* That is the single rule
the schema enforces, and the regulatory rules below follow from it.

** How a sandbox trade becomes real

Every route out of the sandbox goes through the ordinary lifecycle, so
a trade that becomes real is checked, authorised and confirmed exactly
like any other.

| From | How it becomes real | What happens |
|------+---------------------+--------------|
| A draft | /Execute/, =draft= to =live= | The agreement is struck. The same transition books the trade into a real book, so it is never live in a virtual one. It enters the [[id:785C68A0-DD94-404A-BF16-57814554DA12][operational authorisation queue]] as a new trade. |
| A hypothetical or a test | A new trade | A hypothetical never becomes anything else. Dealing it books a new =actual= trade in a real book, linked back to the hypothetical, which keeps the question that justified the deal. |

Leaving a virtual book is therefore not a [[id:6CB4879F-3F4B-48CE-A01E-E1B9AEE866B0][book move]]: a sandbox
trade has no position to move and no ledger history to carry. A live
trade cannot enter a virtual book at all; to experiment on it, a user
makes a hypothetical copy.

** Experimenting on official trades

A what-if on a live trade never edits it. The user makes a
/hypothetical copy/ in their experiments portfolio, linked to the
original, and changes the copy. The link lets a report set the copy
beside the original, and lets a reader see what the experiment was
about. A structure or a hedge can be tried the same way, from copies.

** Market data and configuration

Market data needs no sandbox of its own, because the
[[id:FD427B5B-BDC5-4803-AFCD-97A59E31FE25][resolution pipeline]] already separates what exists from what a
valuation uses:

- /Imported market data/ enters the [[id:842487B3-BEA2-4E74-8EA4-E1007FD559F3][market data universe]] under
  its own source. The official [[id:3F7F814B-E96F-4A62-99E2-76A3074AC832][configuration]] does not resolve to
  that source, so official valuations never see it.
- /Changed market data/ is a new item with its own identity, which
  declares its source and the change applied. The analysis notes state
  the rule: market data is immutable, and anything derived by
  perturbation is a new element, never an edit.
- /Run-time scenarios/ — the shifts ORE applies in a stress or
  sensitivity run — change nothing that is stored.
- A /snapshot/ fixes the values a run used, so an experiment can be
  repeated (see [[id:CEC7844C-B3AE-4858-BF67-71F511007F82][Market data snapshot]]).

Analytics configuration — conventions, curve and pricing engine
configurations — works the same way: an ORE sample brings its own
configuration under its own name, and nothing official refers to it.

Making imported market data official is a decision, not a copy: the
data's source is admitted to the official configuration, and that
admission is recorded as an activity with who and why.

** Reports

A [[id:D4938956-1A5E-4F67-BCE8-7A951CE957E9][report definition]] states its inputs explicitly: the
trades in scope, the market data configuration and snapshot, and the
analytics configuration. An experiment is a report whose scope is a
sandbox portfolio, or whose market data or configuration points at
sandbox data. A comparison is two runs that differ in one input. A
report whose scope reaches into the sandbox is marked unofficial, and
an official report cannot include the sandbox.

** Imports

An import is an act with an identity: who ran it, when, from what
source, and what it created (see [[id:2AF58405-4F3D-4B74-AAD0-6A97E7DA215F][Trade sets, groups and links]]).

- /An import to inspect, reconcile or rehearse/ creates hypothetical or
  test trades in the import's virtual books, its market data under the
  import's source, and its configuration under the import's name.
  Discarding it cancels what it created; nothing is deleted.
- /An ORE sample/ is such an import: one sample set, one sub-portfolio
  of Samples, one source, one configuration, and a report definition
  that ties them together.
- /Real deals/ are not imported into the sandbox. A concluded deal is
  booked into a real book at once, and held as unauthorised until
  Operations releases it; the authorisation queue is the controlled
  staging area for agreements. The next section explains why.

** The regulatory boundary

The sandbox stays outside every regulatory obligation by holding
nothing that the obligations apply to. Each rule below is met by the
single rule that a trade in a virtual book is never live.

| Rule | What it requires | How the sandbox meets it |
|------+------------------+--------------------------|
| Basel RBC25.1-2 | Every instrument the bank holds is in the trading book or the banking book; "all other instruments must be included in the banking book" | A virtual book holds no instrument the bank holds: no agreement is ever live there. Every concluded deal is in a real book from the moment it is struck |
| Basel MAR12 | Each trading account belongs to exactly one trading desk, which is the level of model approval and of the P&L attribution and backtesting tests | The sandbox is not a trading desk, and its results never feed desk P&L, attribution or backtesting |
| EMIR Article 9 | Every derivative contract concluded, modified or terminated is reported no later than the following working day | A sandbox trade is never a concluded contract, so it is never reportable; a concluded deal is never parked in the sandbox, so it cannot miss the deadline |
| MiFIR Article 25; MiFID II Article 16(6) | Records of all orders and transactions are kept for five years; records of all services, activities and transactions are kept | Drafts — quotes and requests — are records. They are cancelled, never deleted, and every sandbox act keeps its audit trail |
| Books and records | The firm's books are complete and its ledger reflects every position | A virtual book is never mapped to the ledger and holds no position, so the official books are complete without it |
| Market conduct | No order or trade without a genuine change of interest | Hypothetical and test trades have no transition that reaches a counterparty, a venue, a confirmation or a settlement |

Two further controls follow from [[id:74AA46EB-64ED-4FD7-B212-AEC164648B84][book classification]]:
test trades in production use test counterparties and entities, so
nothing can mistake them for real business; and a sandbox report is
never a source for official P&L, capital or regulatory figures.

** What follows for the model

This section is analysis rather than reported practice.

- A book carries whether it is virtual. A virtual book has no ledger
  book, sits under the sandbox portfolio, and the portfolio tree rejects
  a virtual book elsewhere and a real book under the sandbox.
- A trade's booking may name a virtual book only while the trade is
  =draft=, =test= or =hypothetical=. The transition to =live= requires a
  real book in the same write.
- The links /what-if copy/ (original to hypothetical) and /dealt from/
  (hypothetical to actual) join a sandbox trade to its official
  counterpart.
- An import is an activity with participants, like every other act.
- Market data and configuration carry their source; the official
  configuration names the sources it admits.
- Report definitions mark whether they are official, and an official
  definition cannot select sandbox inputs.

** Open questions

- /Sample counterparties./ An ORE sample names its own counterparties
  and netting sets. Creating them as test counterparties keeps them out
  of real business but adds them to the tenant's reference data; a
  dedicated demonstration tenant keeps the tenant clean at the cost of
  switching tenants to explore a sample.
- /Retention of experiments./ Whether hypothetical trades in an
  experiments portfolio fall under the record-keeping rules — they are
  neither orders nor transactions — decides whether a user may discard
  an experiment completely or only cancel it.
