This page states the deal component of a cut. The
[[id:B5214F3A-A72E-421E-82BC-79338B2E95CB][Cuts]] structure note gives the
definition, the kinds and the body; this page takes the half of the body that
carries the deals.

* Summary

A trade cut holds the deal population a valuation values at the timepoint: every
live deal as it stands at that instant, with the state it holds, the book it
belongs to, and the corrections applied before the cut was authorised. The
definition of a cut fixes a body of data at one instant, and the deals are the
part of that body which the valuation prices. Two properties follow from the
purpose. The population must be complete, because a valuation states what a book
is worth and a missing deal makes that statement false. The population must be
identified as of the instant, because a deal's state, its book and its terms
change, and the record must say which version each of those facts had. The
population also carries its authorisation, since an unsigned population is a
candidate. The deals are one half of the body; the market data, the settings and
the engine version are recorded beside them, as the
[[id:B5214F3A-A72E-421E-82BC-79338B2E95CB][Cuts]] note sets out. A query over
the trade store yields a population and carries none of these properties with it.

* What it fixes

- *The deals*. Every live deal at the instant, by identity and by the version
  that was current.
- *The state each holds*. Live, expired, fixed or settled as it stood at the
  cut, since the state enters the valuation and a settled deal valued as live
  reports a position the book no longer has.
- *The book and the centre*. The book that owns the deal, which selects the
  centre's market data the deal values against.
- *The corrections*. Any manual correction applied before authorisation, with
  the reason, since a correction changes the number and therefore belongs inside
  the body rather than in a log beside it.
- *The linked deals*. A deal and the deal it links to, captured together,
  because the value of a hedge depends on the position it hedges and a
  population holding one of the pair reads half the position.
- *The identity and the authorisation*. The label, the timepoint, the signatory
  and the version.

A record of the first entry alone reports the trade store at a moment. The
remaining entries are what turn that report into a statement a valuation can
rely on.

* Why the component is a separate act

The hub states our working convention that the deals are fixed first and the
market data second. The division of labour explains the order. The two
components differ in owner, in review and in mode of failure. A deal is reviewed
by comparing it with what the desk booked, which is an act of completeness and
identification. A rate is reviewed by comparing it with an external source,
which is an act of verification. A reviewer of deals holds no view on a curve,
and a reviewer of curves holds no view on a booking. One act covering both would
place the two reviews in one signature, and the resulting signature would certify
work that no single party performed.

The order follows from the same premises. The market data set exists to value the
deals, so the population must be settled before the final rates are committed,
and a provisional market data set serves the review in the interval.

* Where ORE Studio stands

Deals are stored as versioned rows. Each row carries an identity, a version and
the interval for which it was current, in
[[id:4304A441-E532-45FB-837A-378F13693CAE][ores.trading.trade]], so the store can
reconstruct a past state through a query over those intervals.

A query yields a population and satisfies the definition only in part. It fixes
a body of deals at an instant, which is the first constraint. It supplies none of
the properties the purpose requires: the query carries no identity of its own, no
party signs it, and the interval a row states is rewritten when a correction
arrives, so two executions of the same query at different moments can return
different bodies. The repository therefore holds the reconstruction and leaves
the identity and the approval unbuilt.

The nearest object in the repository is scoped to a run rather than to a day. A
report's trade population is partitioned into trade sets, recorded against the
run, and each set's market data is resolved at its as-of. See
[[id:AC05783F-3917-4401-902B-40C5277300A7][Gather each trade set and its market data]].
That object fixes a body of deals at an instant for one run and records it. The
day's population, signed once and reused by every report of that day, is a
different object, and the repository holds the first.
