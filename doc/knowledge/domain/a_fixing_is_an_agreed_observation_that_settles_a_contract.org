#+title: A fixing is an agreed observation that settles a contract
#+description: A fixing is a rate observed at an agreed time from an agreed source; contracts settle or decide on it.

* Summary

A *fixing* is the value of a market rate observed at an agreed time from an
agreed source, which a contract uses to settle an amount or to decide what
happens next. A floating coupon of an interest rate swap settles on an
interest rate fixing; a non-deliverable forward settles on an FX fixing; a
European digital option pays or not according to a spot fixing at expiry. A
fixing differs from a quote: a quote is a source's continuous price of an
instrument, while a fixing is one authoritative value of an index for one
fixing date.

* Detail

** The vocabulary

| Term | Meaning |
|------+---------|
| Fixing source | The source of the observation, agreed between the counterparties when the trade is done. See [[id:6E566C97-F2A4-4AE8-B509-BDE23568A0B8][A fixing source is identified by its rate source definition]]. |
| Fixing rate | The value the source gives: an interest rate, an FX rate or another observable. |
| Fixing date | The date and time at which the observation is made. |
| Fixing schedule | The set of fixing dates of a trade or a leg. |
| Fix | The act of applying an observation to a trade. |
| Unfix | The reversal of a fix that was applied in error. |
| Reset | In rates markets, a synonym for a fixing: a floating rate /resets/ on its fixing date. |

The terms follow the market's definitions: the 2006 ISDA Definitions define
the floating rate options of rates trades, and Annex A to the 1998 FX and
Currency Option Definitions defines the settlement rate options of FX trades.

** Kinds of observable

Three kinds of observable are fixed in practice:

- *rates*, such as a three-month Ibor or an overnight risk-free rate. The two
  families and their difference are in [[id:5170B17B-188B-45CA-A1B6-1F27A97E1CF8][Interest Rate Benchmark Types: IBOR vs. RFR]];
- *spot FX rates*, such as the WM/Reuters 4pm London rate of a pair. See
  [[id:7B09CA1B-606D-493F-A784-676BAE701FC8][The WM/Reuters 4pm fix is the dominant FX benchmark]];
- *realised statistics*, such as the realised volatility of a volatility swap.
  These are not observations of a source but calculations over a series of
  spot fixings, so they are called fixings only by extension.

** Official and unofficial

A fixing is /official/ when operations have authorised it and it has been
applied to the trades that need it; until then it is /unofficial/. The
distinction belongs to the [[id:CA61B445-B6DC-41D3-ACF9-D3FB32D0D342][fixing process]], not to the
observation itself.

** A fixing is not a quote

ORE Studio keeps the two apart. A quote is a tick of a source's stream; a
fixing is a value of an index for a date, stored durably in the fixing series
of the index. [[id:E4736D88-03B2-4A40-B7F4-01B06B9B9FD7][A quote series is keyed by item, provider and party]]
describes the quote side, and [[id:F4BBEF34-D84C-4AE6-A3AA-DC47C3EE06EE][Public publishers publish daily fixings through a supplier session]]
describes the fixing side, including why the publisher is part of an FX
fixing's identity. The oresmd URI names a fixing with =type=fixing=; see
[[id:C3E053CA-0D4B-480B-9119-E11530160EC1][oresmd: ORE Studio Market Data URI]].
