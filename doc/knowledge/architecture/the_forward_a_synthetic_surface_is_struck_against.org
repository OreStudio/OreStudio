#+title: The forward a synthetic surface is struck against
#+description: How a synthetic volatility surface's forward is built from the run's own latent curve: the interface between curves and surfaces, who publishes what, how the design compares to Kienitz and van den Berg, and the four choices it settles.
#+type: knowledge
#+level: cross
#+filetags: :market_data:synthetic:surfaces:curves:architecture:

#+updated: 2026-11-13

* Summary

A volatility surface is not a free-standing object. Its parameters are
expressed relative to the forward of its underlying, so the forward is part of
the surface, not an input that can be replaced by a flat guess. The synthetic
run already holds a latent curve for every currency it generates: each IR curve
feed steps a short-rate model whose discount factors are analytic at any
horizon, and it prices every pillar quote it publishes off that one state. So
the curve the surface needs exists, and the work is to share it inside the run,
not to build it, and not to publish it.

The rule this page fixes: *the surface is struck against the forward the run's
own latent curves imply, read from a market board the run holds; the vendor
publishes raw quotes and never a curve.* The three frameworks the surface is
judged against -- Kienitz (2024), and van den Berg's two papers -- all state the
surface relative to a forward, so they agree with the rule and none of them asks
the vendor to publish the curve.

* Detail

** The interface, stage by stage

A curve reaches a surface in five steps. Each step names the code that holds it.

1. *The latent curve.* One =i_yield_curve_process= per currency, chosen from the
   five seeded engines ([[id:D6FDEFC6-ECF9-4C9A-A566-9BB61FCDBAC1][Vasicek, CIR, Hull-White, Black-Karasinski and the
   two-factor Gaussian]]). Its interface documents itself as "the latent curve a
   single process state implies at every maturity, not just the next scalar
   tick", and =discount_factor(ticks_ahead)= is that curve. The IR curve feed
   prices every pillar it publishes off that one state
   (=price_ir_curve_entry=, deposit, FRA and swap), so a currency's quote set and
   its curve are two views of one object. Nothing is inferred: the model that
   made the quotes is the model that answers for the discounts.
2. *The market board.* When a curve feed ticks, it publishes the dated discount
   factors its model implies to the run's board: the pair of a date and a
   discount factor, from the spot date at one outwards. Dated, because the model
   counts its horizon in ticks and a surface's expiries are calendar tenors; the
   conversion is the publisher's, done once, and no reader repeats it.
3. *The pair's market at an expiry.* A surface feed reads its pair's spot and the
   two currencies' boards and builds one market per expiry:
   =fx_market{spot, domestic_discount = P_quote, foreign_discount = P_base}=, the
   mapping the calibration already uses. The quant block
   =math::pair_market_at= does the resolution, and it is the same block the
   calibration uses, so the two sides of the system resolve a market by one rule:
   an exact date takes the row's discounts, a date between two rows takes both
   interpolated log-linearly in days from the spot date.
4. *The forward and the strikes.* =F(t,T) = S(t) P_f(t,T) / P_d(t,T)=. The ATM
   strike is that forward for a =forward= ATM convention and the
   delta-neutral strike otherwise (=fx_atm_strike=), and each risk reversal and
   butterfly is read at the strike that carries its delta
   (=fx_strike_from_delta=). The smile is then read at those strikes
   (=read_smile=, =fx_vol_surface_quotes=).
5. *What travels.* The wire carries the volatility at a delta label -- =ATM=,
   =25RR=, =25BF=, =10RR=, =10BF= -- never a strike. So a reader of the quotes
   must repeat step 4 with its own forward, which is why the two sides resolving
   the same market matters more than the size of the difference between them.

** Why the forward is not optional

The forward enters the quoted surface twice, and the second entry is the one
that catches people out:

- *In the strike.* The ATM strike is the forward. A wrong forward displaces every
  strike of the expiry by the same factor, and a displaced strike reads a
  different point of the smile, so every quoted volatility is read at the wrong
  moneyness.
- *In the delta.* Spot delta is =P_f * N(d1)= with
  =d1 = (ln(F/K) + sigma^2 T / 2) / (sigma sqrt(T))=. The foreign discount
  factor is outside the normal and the forward is inside it, so the forward
  shapes the delta as well as the strike. A surface quoted with no rate
  differential has no coherent spot-delta reading at all.

Two measured facts bound the cost and say what the surface actually needs:

- The quoted volatilities are *invariant to the level of the spot*, to =1e-12= at
  all points, when the two discount factors are held: a smile is read at a
  moneyness, so a common scale cancels. The emitter's test "a surface's quotes do
  not depend on the level of its spot once its discounts differ" pins it, and it
  is what lets a surface feed quote without first knowing the pair's spot.
- They *do* depend on the ratio of the two discounts: moving it from one to
  =0.99/0.97= moves the quotes by =7.8e-05=, about =0.008= volatility points,
  which the test "a surface's quotes do depend on the ratio of its discounts"
  pins.

So what a surface needs from the curve is the discount ratio at each expiry, and
nothing else. That is a small ask, and it is not the same as saying the choice of
curve does not matter: the ratio *is* the forward in units of the spot.

** Who publishes what

| Object | Who holds it | Published as market data | Through the publish lifecycle |
|--------+--------------+--------------------------+-------------------------------|
| Raw quotes: FX spots, IR pillars, volatility quotes | the vendor, which the system tenant plays | yes, as series by provider and party | no |
| The vendor's latent curves | the run, on its market board | *no* | no |
| A party's curves, forward sets and surfaces | the party | yes | yes, with maker-checker |

The middle row is the one this page adds. The vendor's curve is not market data:
it is the state the vendor's own quotes come from, shared with the other feeds in
the same run so that a surface can be struck against the same forward its rate
quotes imply. It needs no approval, no lineage and no record, because it is not
a claim about the market -- it is how the vendor knows what it is quoting.

The last row is unchanged: a party derives its own curves from the quotes it
receives, and publishes them as derived market data. The consistency between the
two is *bootstrap consistency*, not identity, and the latent-surface note already
states the reason: "the clean pillar quotes and their bootstrap give the true
curve". A party bootstrapping the vendor's pillar quotes recovers the vendor's
latent curve to the fit of its own bootstrap. So a surface struck against the
vendor's curve and re-read against a party's forward set differ by the bootstrap,
which is a number the round-trip test states rather than a design defect to
argue about.

** How this compares to the literature

The three frameworks the surface is judged against are summarised in
[[id:84E7CEA8-9654-431C-A8D1-C8B97E35B6D5][Kienitz (2024)]],
[[id:EC220F01-1844-4EED-A61D-BCF9F01EE00C][van den Berg (2026a)]] and
[[id:3F849D36-022B-49CC-AD71-791481F36581][van den Berg (2026b)]]. The table
places our design beside them.

| | Kienitz 2024 | van den Berg 2026a | van den Berg 2026b | ORE Studio |
|---+--------------+--------------------+--------------------+------------|
| What is generated | a joint observation vector, by a Gaussian mixture | an interpolated surface between quoted pillars | option prices from a terminal-density surrogate | live price paths and their quotes, on one clock |
| Measure | P throughout | Q | Q, under the model's terminal density | P for the states, Q for the surfaces -- the split the approach note documents |
| Where the rate state lives | a coordinate of the observation vector | not modelled; the forward is an input | the drift of the underlying process | a short-rate model per currency, inside the run |
| How the forward is obtained | implied by the joint draw, through the closed-form conditional | an input: the strikes are moneyness | an input: the density is of =S_T / F= | derived by covered interest parity from the run's own latent curve |
| What the surface is | a component of, or a conditional of, the joint law | a mixture of densities over the terminal underlying | the terminal return density of a GARCH process | an SSVI parameterisation held inside the no-arbitrage bounds |

What we take from each, and where we differ:

- *Structure from Kienitz.* His method's point is the conditional: "given this
  yield curve, what vol surface is consistent?". A surface generated with no
  reference to the curve is not that conditional; it is an independent draw in
  the same parameterisation. We adopt the conditional structure and the regime
  reading of it -- a regime that carries its own loadings is the mixture's
  components in continuous time -- and not the GMM itself: a daily joint draw is
  the right shape for a batch of snapshots and the wrong shape for a live
  simulator whose feeds tick at their own times, which is why
  [[id:8D5466B2-74B5-4C0D-B6DC-528CD466A47F][the joint-market investigation]] chose shared Brownian factors instead.
- *Constraints from van den Berg (2026a).* His interpolation is arbitrage-free
  because the mixture family is closed and the peacock condition holds along the
  weight path. Those are conditions on the terminal distribution, and the strikes
  they compare are moneyness, so the forward is an input to the construction. We
  do not use the interpolation -- we sweep SSVI parameters and rely on the
  closed-form conditions of Gatheral and Jacquier -- but we inherit the same
  obligation: the surface must be struck against its true forward, or the
  conditions are being enforced on a different object. If a day comes when we
  interpolate between quoted pillars rather than sample a parameterised surface,
  his method is the one to use.
- *The object from van den Berg (2026b).* His surface is the terminal return
  density of the underlying, so the drift and the discounting come before the
  smile: there is no version of that object that does not know what it is a
  return on. Our design says the same thing with a different mechanism: the
  forward is not a drift we model but a curve we hold.
- *Where we are in a stronger position than any of them.* We do not have to infer
  the curve from a fitted joint law, nor take the forward from the market: we
  hold the short-rate model the quotes came from. The forward is not estimated;
  it is the generator's own state.
- *Where we are weaker.* Kienitz's joint GMM gives a fitted, regime-segmented
  joint law; our loadings are stated by a person until calibration lands. And
  van den Berg's guarantees are per-interpolation; ours are per-emitted-surface,
  so a day's surface and the next day's are not excluded from dynamic arbitrage.
  The approach note records both limits.

** The four choices this page settles

Each choice lists what was weighed, what is recommended, and whether it can be
changed later. The shape that makes a later change cheap is stated first, because
that is the part that is expensive to get wrong.

*** What the board carries

| Option | Weight |
|--------+--------|
| Curves only | the smallest thing that answers the question the surface asks; one member kind |
| Curves and every derived quantity the run could share | equity forwards, commodity strips, the =CAPFLOOR/SHIFT= that constraint 5 needs; more consumers, more surface to get wrong |
| A general derived-quantity bus, designed up front | no consumer can be named yet beyond these two, so the design would be guessed |

*Recommended: curves only now, in a shape that takes more members without a
redesign.* The board is a map from a member id -- a kind and a code, such as
=curve:USD= -- to a series of dated values. A second kind is then a new key and a
new publisher, not a new structure.

*Is it changeable later?* Yes, and this is why the shape matters more than the
choice. Adding equity forwards is additive: a new key, a new publisher, and the
readers that want it. What would not be cheap to change is a board built as a
curve cache with curve-specific accessors, because every consumer would then
learn "curve" and a second kind would have to be bolted on beside it. The page
fixes the general key for that reason, and the choice of members stays open.

*** What one currency's entry holds

| Option | Weight |
|--------+--------|
| The curve's own pillar dates, from the spot date at one | exactly the shape =pair_market_at= consumes and the shape a party's forward set has, so both sides read one rule |
| A fixed tenor grid to thirty years | more dates than any surface needs today; adds a second set of dates to keep consistent |
| Both | more to keep consistent for no consumer |
| The model's state, so each reader evaluates the curve itself | hands the engine to every consumer and couples them to it; a change of short-rate model breaks every reader |

*Recommended: the curve's own pillar dates.* A surface expiry past the last
pillar is refused when the feed starts, naming the date and the currency, rather
than extrapolated: an expiry beyond the curve is a configuration error, and a
silent extrapolation is the class of defect this page exists to remove.

*Is it changeable later?* Yes. Adding a fixed grid is adding dates, which
=pair_market_at= already interpolates between, and it is a publisher-side change
with no reader change.

*** When the surface re-solves its strikes

| Option | Weight |
|--------+--------|
| Every tick | what a desk does, and what the approach note's step 4 says: the surface advances its SSVI state and solves its quotes' strikes against the new forward |
| On the surface's own volatility tick only | fewer solves, and a stale forward between vol ticks, which is the defect again at low frequency |
| At construction, as today | one solve for the life of the feed; the reason this page exists |

*Recommended: every tick.* The cost is one strike solve per expiry per quoted
label, not per contributor: a contributor's observation is the clean point plus
its noise profile, so the clean surface is solved once and observed many times. A
delta solve is a Newton iteration on a monotone delta, so a handful of evaluations
of the normal CDF; seven expiries by five labels is thirty-five solves a tick,
which is microseconds of work and is not the bound on a run of thousands of
surfaces.

*Is it changeable later?* The code would accept a coarser schedule, but the
behaviour is the fix: a surface that re-solves moves when its rates move, and a
surface that does not is quoting against a forward nobody holds. Treat it as a
choice of frequency, not of design, and keep it at every tick.

*** The tolerance of the round trip

The round trip is: the surface struck against the vendor's latent curve, then
calibrated by a party from the same quotes. It cannot be exact, because the party
bootstraps with its own engine.

| Option | Weight |
|--------+--------|
| Assert identical strikes | false, and it would hide the bootstrap difference as a tolerance of zero |
| Assert a stated tolerance, measured once | honest, and it names which side owns the number |
| No test | leaves the only cross-side consistency claim unstated |

*Recommended: a stated tolerance in volatility points, measured once*, with the
measurement and the command recorded on the task that lands it. The number is a
property of the party's bootstrap, not of this design, so it moves when the
bootstrap changes -- which is the argument for asserting it beside the bootstrap
rather than inside the surface's tests.

*Is it changeable later?* Yes, it is a constant in a test, and it should be
re-measured whenever the bootstrap changes.

** What this page does not settle

- *The spot's own drift.* Covered interest parity says the spot's drift is the
  rate differential of its pair. The FX spot feed instead measures an annual
  drift from the pair's own window of fixings and steps that
  ([[id:46FF8EF4-3874-4C9A-9222-69DA691E793F][The FX spot producer]]), so a spot
  path and a curve path can disagree about the carry. The same board answers it,
  and it is deliberately not in this story's scope.
- *Dynamic arbitrage.* The guarantee is static, per emitted surface. A path of
  surfaces is not excluded from dynamic arbitrage, as the approach note and the
  shared-factors design both record.
- *Regime-dependent loadings.* The factors exist; loadings per regime do not.
- *Who quotes the strike.* Contributors quote volatilities at delta labels. A
  contributor that quoted a strike instead would need the forward on its own side,
  which no document asks for.
