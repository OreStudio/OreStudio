* Summary

The scheduler runs jobs on cron schedules. It has three domain types. A
*job definition* is configuration: a name, a cron expression, an action
type with its payload, an active flag, and an optional tenant and party.
A *firing* is the record of one execution of a job: the job it belongs
to, the instant it was triggered, its start and end times, its status,
and its error. An *action* is the operation a firing performs; the
scheduler registers one handler per action type and selects the handler
by the action type the job declares.

The scheduler has minute granularity: a cron expression resolves to whole
minutes and the loop fires a job at the minute it names, so the shortest
interval between two firings is one minute. An action runs either a database
statement or a message delivery. The scheduler does not wait for the work a
message starts, and it does not retry an action that fails.

Read the pages below in the order given. The first is an end-to-end
example, the next five describe one type each in the order of the path,
and the last section is the boundary with the workflow cluster.

[[id:01083101-A83A-4294-81A4-DDF3192CEB14][Report Processing]]. Read it

* What this cluster does not cover

[[id:A960FE8E-56FA-4655-AA51-54C14882179B][Calendars, Holidays and
Events]] owns calendar reference data: business days, holidays and market
events. The scheduler does not read it. A cron expression defines
wall-clock instants, so a firing is due at a stated minute and not on a
business day, and a recurrence that must follow the business calendar
cannot be expressed as a cron expression.

[[id:1805BAFC-7E43-4D9C-822F-17C3455C6E00][Compute job lifecycle]] owns
the grid's jobs: batches, workunits, results and hosts. The word job has
two distinct meanings here. A scheduled job is a definition that fires; a
compute job is a unit of work submitted to the grid.

The workflow cluster owns the contents of a run, and this cluster owns
only the point at which a run may be started. The division is why either
cluster can be read without the other.
