* Plan

Five units, and the reconnaissance found they are not the same size.

1. The roll rule on =tenor_convention=. One column, mirroring =measured_from=
   and =resolution_algorithm=: a non-nullable text column, a row in the insert
   trigger's =* Validations= table, and rows in the detail form and the list.
2. The writes on =tenor_convention_resolutions=. The junction's C++ flags carry
   =read_only: true=, which is what suppresses the write surface; removing it
   generates =put=, =put_many=, =delete= and =delete_many=, and the two
   permissions they need.
3. The change reason on the tenor write. Verify before building: every refdata
   write takes a =change_intent= and the service copies it onto the row, so this
   may already be carried.
4. The resolve operation, which is a new hand-written handler with its own
   protocol pair, registration and permission.
5. A swap's rolls as a refdata row, which the journey's own text says is not a
   write target.

Units 1 and 2 are built here, unit 3 is verified as already carried, and unit 5 is
recorded as answered. Unit 4 is filed as
[[id:CFE1A720-6BB6-4D94-AA26-28FBC1A2978D][Resolve a tenor over the wire, with
the refusal it carries]], together with the refusal unit it belongs with. The
resolve operation cannot be written before the roll rule exists, because the roll
rule is what its reply reports, so the two are sequenced rather than split for
convenience.

* Notes

Two findings shaped the work. The roll rule is a real gap and is one column. The
change reason on the tenor write is not a gap at all: =put_tenor_request= carries
a =change_intent=, and =tenor_service.cpp= copies its =reason_code= and
=commentary= onto the row, so the journey row that said otherwise was stale and
is corrected rather than built. The cheap check that settled it was reading the
service's =prepare_change=, which is where every refdata write's intent lands.

The junction's =read_only= flag is the whole of unit 2. It is on the C++ flags
and it suppresses the write surface for a junction whose model does not want one,
which is the documented shape: "a model that states the surface has no writes
should not emit messages for operations that cannot happen."

* Result

Three of the five units are settled, and the two that are built are the two the
resolve operation depends on.

*What landed.*

- *The convention carries its roll rule.* =tenor_convention= gained a
  =business_day_convention_type= column, non-nullable and defaulting to
  =ModifiedFollowing=, validated by
  =ores_refdata_validate_business_day_convention_type_fn= in the insert trigger
  and drawn on the detail form as a picker and in the list as a column. The
  lookup holds seven rules; the four the journey draws -- =Following=,
  =ModifiedFollowing=, =Preceding=, =ModifiedPreceding= -- are among them. The
  roll rule was a choice on the screen with no row behind it, and now the write
  can store it and the resolve reply can report it.
- *The resolution junction takes writes.* =tenor_convention_resolutions= had
  =read_only: true= on its C++ flags, which is what suppressed its write surface.
  Removing it generates =put=, =put_many=, =delete= and =delete_many= beside the
  three reads, five new shell scripts, and the =:write= and =:delete= permissions
  they need. The set membership, the anchor override, the SPECIAL tenor's offset
  and a SCHEDULE_STEP row's schedule code and step count all write now, with the
  version check every other refdata write makes.

*What was verified rather than built, and one thing withdrawn.*

The tenor write was recorded as carrying no change reason and no commentary. It
carries both. =put_tenor_request= takes a =change_intent=, and
=tenor_service.cpp= copies its =reason_code= and =commentary= onto the row's
=change_reason_code= and =change_commentary=. No code was needed, so the journey
row that asserted otherwise is corrected instead.

*A swap's rolls are recorded as answered, not open.* The journey's own text says
the schedule step draws the axis expansion and is not a write target. The
closed-form axis is evaluated code-side and an event-lookup axis reads
=calendar_event= rows, so a repository of swap ladders would be a second source of
truth for a date the schedule already derives. The gap is closed with that reason
rather than left dangling.

*What is filed.*

The resolve operation and the refusal payload are one unit, filed as
[[id:CFE1A720-6BB6-4D94-AA26-28FBC1A2978D][Resolve a tenor over the wire, with
the refusal it carries]]. They are filed together because there is nothing to
carry a refusal until the operation exists, and they are sequenced after this
change rather than split from it for convenience: the reply reports the roll rule,
so the roll rule had to exist first. The new task's Notes carry the
=resolve_end_date= signature and the conditions its doc comment says throw, the
model-declared message and custom-handler pattern to copy, with the party
composite as the worked example, and the open question of whether the operation's
subject sits under =refdata.v1.ops.= or the generated =refdata.v1.tenors.= space.

*Verification.*

Eleven checks pass: handler-permissions, subject-conformance, open-reads,
protocol-twins, generated-column-refs, sql-hygiene, format, reachability,
sql-reachability, populate-refs and shell-menus. The cmake component files are up
to date, the shell recipe and physical space inventories are up to date, and the
tangle left five new scripts, all staged.

Journey 9's table lost every =partial= row and two of its five =missing= rows. The
three that remain are the resolve operation twice over -- the resolution column
and the resolved-dates step -- and the refusal that operation would carry.

*What is not verified.* No database or C++ test exercises the new column or the
new writes: the box shares one PostgreSQL across eleven worktrees, so =compass db
recreate= would disrupt the others, and the local =.env= is at format version 27
against a required 29. The build result is recorded when it lands.
