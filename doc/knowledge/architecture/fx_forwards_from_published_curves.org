#+title: ORE Studio publishes FX forwards derived from published curves
#+description: The design decision: a forward is derived from one spot and two published funding curves, then published with its provenance.

* Summary

ORE Studio derives each FX forward from one spot and two published funding curves, and it accepts no keyed points. The result is a parity forward, so it holds no cross-currency basis. A forward set is a published record, written to the database with its provenance. It is computed into a candidate, raised, decided and applied through the lifecycle every derived market object shares ([[id:186B44DD-51EA-45AA-971E-0C2D4D21A6DD][A derived market object is published through one lifecycle]]), so it supersedes the set it replaces, tells its readers, and is withdrawn through a request of its own.

* Detail

** Why

The calculation is a pure function of its inputs, and the curves already pass through a publish per currency. The published set records what the firm used at an as-of: the numbers, the spot, both curve publishes, the convention, the method and the approval. A later change to an input does not alter it. The set is a record of a decision, so it does not go stale. It can only be superseded.

The calculation uses dates and discount factors, so different day-count conventions do not enter it. See [[id:9CDD445B-964A-5C7E-8E65-DB9967E99F55][FX forward calculations run on discount factors and not quoted rates]].

** What this costs

- A market forward holds the basis, and the BIS documents a persistent basis since 2007. A quoted forward and an ORE price under a cross-currency curve will differ.
- Points are outputs. See [[id:F1F326CE-5384-559E-AF4A-88DBBC4EC557][Swap points and interest rate differentials are two views of one number]].
- A withdrawn funding curve leaves forward sets built from it, so the set must show that. [[id:75B40B64-FF31-5381-922B-0EB1F521BC38][An end-of-day FX rate set must be complete and independently verified]] needs such a record.

** Scope

A forward set belongs to a party, and so do the two funding curves it reads. The raw spot is the system's, reached through the party's subscription. USD-hub pairs come first. A pair's spot days are the larger of its two currencies, as [[id:267DD5E8-F095-47C1-B42B-733565306776][FX spot date and settlement]] states.
