* Goal

A provider names the publisher that owns an item: Bloomberg for a ticker, the ECB for a reference rate, WM/Reuters for a fix. Being computed rather than observed is not a publisher, yet the store holds a provider called derived, which states both that we made the value and, by omission, that no one owns the item it belongs to.

The confusion is visible where it costs most. USD SOFR 1M is an index CME Group administers; we compute it from our own overnight curve, and the row names derived, so a reader cannot tell whose index it is and the real publisher cannot replace it by rank because the rank belongs to another provider. The same holds for the 2,280 derived FX volatility quotes and for the 194 series and 2,612 fixings already filed under derived.

The annotation already exists and is unused for this purpose. market_series.derivation_kind says whether a series is OBSERVED or produced by a named derivation (IR_CURVE_BOOTSTRAP, FX_VOL_DERIVATION, FX_VOL_SURFACE_CALIBRATION), and observation_lineage records the point's source kind and the configuration that produced it. A derived value should be a value under the provider that owns the item, marked by that annotation, so a real source of the same item replaces it by rank, as it does for every other provider.

This contradicts the page that blesses a derived source of our own (Derivation of FX volatility quotes from historical fixings: publishes under its own identity, so a vendor of the same quotes replaces it by rank). The task settles which of the two statements the store follows.

* Acceptance

- No provider named derived and no source of it: every derived value sits under the provider that owns its item, and its derivation_kind and observation lineage say how it was made.
- The 194 series, 2,612 fixings and 2,280 vendor ids filed under derived are re-filed under the owner of their item, or deleted where the item has no owner.
- A reader of a series, of an observation and of a screen tells whether a value was observed or derived, and which derivation made it, without reading a provider name.
- A real source of a derived item still replaces it by rank, and a test proves the replacement.

* Plan

1. *Inventory and ownership.* List every series, vendor id and source filed under
   =derived=, and name the publisher that owns each item: CME Group for Term
   SOFR, Banco de la República for IBR, GPW Benchmark for WIBOR, and for the FX
   volatility quotes the vendor whose quote the item stands for. Where no
   publisher owns the item, decide between the provider =ore=, which the bundle
   already installs for the platform's own values, and deletion.
2. *The annotation.* =market_series.derivation_kind= already says how a series
   was made (=OBSERVED=, =IR_CURVE_BOOTSTRAP=, =FX_VOL_DERIVATION=,
   =FX_VOL_SURFACE_CALIBRATION=), and =observation_lineage= already records the
   point's source kind and the configuration behind it. Add what is missing: the
   field on the reads that answer a series, and the line on the screen that shows
   one.
3. *Re-file.* Move the series and the vendor instruments to the owning provider,
   and retire the sources =derived-a= and =derived-fx-vol=, or rename them as
   sources of that provider. The 19 fixing series and the 175 FX series move
   together.
4. *Rank.* Keep the source ranking that lets a real source replace a derived
   value, and prove it with a test that adds a live source of a derived item.
5. *The contradiction.* Settle it against
   [[id:099697A6-8526-48E0-A63D-DB3932C06468][Derivation of FX volatility quotes from historical fixings]],
   which says a derived source publishes under its own identity, and update
   whichever page states the losing rule.
6. *Proof.* A fresh provision with no =derived= provider, every series naming its
   owner, its derivation visible in the API and on a screen, and the rank
   replacement working.
