This page documents a [[id:F44766FA-1E9C-416F-849A-E131101B2ECA][user journey]]: Each approval part that model risk names reads a proposed change to an official pricing engine configuration, approves or refuses it, and a proposal that every part approved is made official. The person who proposed a change cannot sign it off.

A user journey describes what one person is trying to do, before anything is
built. It is a design document that outlives any sprint, so it names the
screens, the entity fields each screen shows, and the operations and messages
each step needs.

* Goal

"I must decide if this change to the way we price is safe, and I must leave a
record that I decided." The model settings of a party are under model risk
control. A party holds few official pricing configurations, one for each cycle,
and every official report of that cycle runs with one of them. A change to an
official configuration therefore changes the numbers of every report that binds
it. Each approval part that model risk names must read the change, and the system
must keep who approved it and when. The journey is done when the proposal is in
force, or refused, or withdrawn, and the record says which and who decided.

* The approval is the inbox's

The platform already has an approval engine in [[id:EE61043C-CBCE-44B5-85E9-A3E433FBA3EF][ores.inbox]], and this
journey uses it. It does not invent a second one.

- A proposal is an approval request ([[id:6A2D9C47-1B8E-4F35-A0D2-7E9C4B1F5A68][ores.inbox.approval_request]]) of a
  kind ([[id:3C1E8B52-7A44-4F0E-9D61-2B5F0A7C9E13][ores.inbox.approval_kind]]). The kind here would be
  =analytics.pricing_engines_change=. The component that owns the kind owns the
  detail row, which holds the proposed version.
- A part ([[id:B4BE73C7-E847-46BA-82A5-9A411E275B88][ores.inbox.approval_part]]) is a function whose approval a request
  needs. A part is not a role. It names a permission, such as
  =inbox::approvals:decide_market_risk=, and a tenant grants that permission to
  whichever role does the work. Each part has an answer order. A part with a lower
  order approves first. Parts with the same order answer together.
- A decision ([[id:9B3D6E81-4F2A-47C5-8E19-0A5C7D3B2E96][ores.inbox.approval_decision]]) is one row for each act: approve,
  refuse, hold, resume, withdraw, reverse. A decision is never edited.
- A refusal from a part that can answer ends the request. The requester alone may
  withdraw it.
- The schema enforces four eyes. A trigger refuses a decision from the person who
  raised the request, and a unique index lets one person approve a request once.

Four parts exist today: =controller=, =finance=, =market_risk= and =operations=.
This journey needs three. *Market Risk* is the seeded part. *Quant* and *Model
Validation* are new, and their names are *assumed* until model risk confirms them.
The prototype draws Quant first, then Market Risk and Model Validation together.
That order is also an assumption.

* Actor and trigger

Four people take part. All act inside their own tenant, for their own party.

#+ATTR_HTML: :class hug-leading
| Actor | What they do |
|-------+--------------|
| The proposer, usually a market-risk owner | Raises the request, answers a refusal, rebases an out-of-date proposal, withdraws it. Cannot decide it, even when the proposer holds one of the parts. |
| A signer, one for each part | Holds the part's permission. Reads the proposal and approves it or refuses it for that part. |
| Whoever makes a proposal official | Any person who may write the configuration. They do it after every part approved. |
| A viewer | Reads the queue and the proposals. Changes nothing. |

The trigger is a proposal. It comes from
[[id:D998F36A-9716-48E7-BC70-BC4813115591][Change how a product is priced]], from
[[id:BC2066E8-C595-47C0-97CE-19DF182F616E][Bring in a pricing engines file]], from
a proposed revert on the History tab, or from a candidate in
[[id:0D39E4B7-75FD-480A-A996-2234BCC7BCC8][Author a pricing engine configuration]].
A signer starts from the queue under *Workflows* on
[[id:386DD47E-DA9E-4EA7-B925-852048EE47B7][See the pricing engines a party holds]].
The journey ends on the proposal's page, with the proposal in a final state.

* Steps

1. *Open the queue.* The person opens *Sign-off*. The screen is a record list as the
   [[id:2BF7F03F-6BF4-45FF-8E26-4D3B4E2D943D][Record screen standard]] draws one.
   A row is one proposal: the configuration, the cycle it serves, the change as
   =version N to N+1=, who proposed it, when, the parts that approved so far, its
   state and what it needs from the reader. A tag says *Needs your sign-off* when the
   reader holds a part that can answer now and did not propose the change. A search
   runs over the configuration and the change. A filter shows the open proposals,
   the ones that need the reader, or all, with the closed ones.
2. *Open a proposal.* The title names the configuration and the proposed version.
   The lead line states the state and that the version in force stays in force
   until the proposal is approved. The tabs are *Sign-off*, *Changes*, *Reach* and
   *History*.
3. *Read the changes.* The Changes tab compares the proposal with the version it
   was written on, as a diff, and shows the proposer's reason and commentary. It
   lists the evidence the proposer attached, such as a back-test.
4. *Read the reach.* The Reach tab states the rule: a report needs a price for
   every product type in its portfolio. It lists the reports the configuration
   serves, with each report's cycle, portfolio, the products it holds that
   change, and whether the proposal still prices every product type the report
   needs. It states that a run made after the change is in force is not comparable
   with an earlier run for the changed products.
5. *Read the sign-off.* The Sign-off tab lists each approval part, its decision, the
   person who decided, when, and the comment. A part that must wait for an earlier
   part says so: *Waits for Quant*. It states the four-eyes rule: the person who
   raised the request cannot decide it, even when they hold one of the parts, and one
   person approves a request once.
6. *Approve.* A signer who holds a part that can answer now sees *Approve* and
   *Refuse* on that part's row. *Approve* opens a dialog that asks the signer to
   confirm that they read the changes and the reports they reach, and takes an
   optional comment. After the last part approves, the proposal moves to
   *Approved, not in force*.
7. *Refuse the proposer.* When the signer proposed the change, the row shows no
   button and says: you proposed this change, another person who holds the part
   must sign it off. The inbox refuses the same decision already.
8. *Refuse.* *Refuse* opens a dialog that needs a reason. The request ends and the
   proposal moves to *Refused*. The version in force stays in force. The proposer reads the reason and
   changes the proposal and submits it again, or withdraws it. A refusal never
   deletes the proposal.
9. *Make it official.* When every part approved, any person who may write the
   configuration sees *Make version N+1 official*. The dialog names the reports it
   reaches and states that no report is rebound and that the previous version stays in
   the history. After it, the version is in force, and the next run of each report that
   binds the configuration uses it.
10. *Rebase an out-of-date proposal.* When another proposal became the version in
    force first, this proposal sits on an old version. The page says so and offers
    the proposer *Rebase*. A rebase moves the proposal onto the version in force and
    clears the approvals, because the parts approved a different base.
11. *Withdraw.* The proposer may withdraw an open proposal. It changes nothing and
    stays in the history.
12. *Read the history.* The History tab lists what happened to the proposal, newest
    first: who proposed it, each approval and refusal with its comment, and when it
    was made official.

* Prototype

The [[file:../../prototypes/reporting/sign-off-a-pricing-engine-change/][sign off a pricing engine change prototype]] owns this journey, and
[[file:../../prototypes/reporting/sign-off-a-pricing-engine-change.org][its design note]] says what it argues, which states it can reach and what is
fake. It belongs at =doc/prototypes/reporting/sign-off-a-pricing-engine-change/=.

It must draw the queue and the proposal page as the Record screen standard draws
them. It must switch the state of the first proposal behind
=?proposal=pending|partly|ready|rejected|superseded= and the person behind
=?actor=proposer|quant|risk|validator|viewer=. The proposer must hold the Market
Risk part, so that the four-eyes refusal is reachable. It must show the answer
order: Quant answers first, and the other two rows say *Waits for Quant*. It must
show an approval, a refusal with a reason, the point where every part has
approved, the making of a proposal official, a rebase and a withdrawal. It must
state that the kind, the Quant part and the Model Validation part do not exist, and
that their names and order are assumed. Every value is a fixture.

* What is missing

1. *The kind and its detail.* No approval kind names a pricing engines change, and
   no detail row holds the proposed version. A proposal must not replace the rows
   that a run reads, so the detail holds it apart.
2. *Two parts.* =market_risk= exists. A =quant= part and a =model_validation= part
   do not, and neither do their decide permissions. Model risk must confirm the
   names, the answer order and which role holds each permission. The kind says how many approvals close a
   request, and one person approves once.
3. *The policy row.* The inbox policy says which part must approve a put of an
   entity. A row for =pricing_model_config= makes a direct write to an approved
   version go through a request. Without it, a person who holds a part can write
   the configuration directly, and the sign-off controls nothing. The service must
   also read the policy.
4. *Make it official.* An approved request applies through the kind's apply
   workflow, inside an apply window. The prototype draws a person who makes the
   proposal official instead. The choice is open.
5. *The version a run used.* No operation says which version of a configuration a
   run was made with. The sign-off proves that a version was approved, and the run
   cannot prove it was made with that version. Model risk needs both.
6. *A report binds a configuration, not a version.* =report_configuration= holds
   the configuration. Making a version official must change what the next run
   reads, in one step, with no rebinding.
7. *A candidate that replaces the official configuration.* A candidate from
   [[id:0D39E4B7-75FD-480A-A996-2234BCC7BCC8][Author a pricing engine configuration]]
   also moves the reports of a cycle from one configuration to another. That is a
   rebinding step, and the prototype does not draw it.
8. *Rebase.* The inbox has no operation that moves a request onto a newer base.
9. *The evidence.* A proposal carries a back-test or a note. No operation attaches
   a document, and the prototype draws the files as a fixture.
10. *Hold, resume, expiry and reversal.* The inbox has these decisions and an
    expiry. The prototype draws approve, refuse and withdraw only.
