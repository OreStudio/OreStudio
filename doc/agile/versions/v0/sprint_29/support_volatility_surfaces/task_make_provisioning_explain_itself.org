* Goal

A person reading the logs of any provisioning run can say which step is running, what it is waiting on, how long each part took, and why it ended. Each step logs its start, its end, its duration and its outcome; generate_trades logs each entry's phases and timings; the trade writer logs its progress; the step stream logs each delivery and claim; and the workflow engine warns about a step still in progress past its expected time. With that, the stall of 2026-11-03, where generate_trades stopped after ACME Corporation HK Ltd's bonds at 21:01:10 and the run never finished, is found and fixed.

* Notes

** What the stall was

The run of 2026-11-03 did not stall. The user journal dropped most service lines under load, so the log stopped while the work went on. The database showed every step complete and the tenant active. Setting =LogRateLimitIntervalSec=0= did not stop the loss, so the rate limit is not the cause; that change is reverted. The fix is a file sink per service replica at =publish/log/<service>.<replica>.log=, on by default; =ORES_SERVICE_LOG_TO_FILE=0= turns it off.

** A step longer than its lease, before the fix

The claim lease is 120 s and generate_trades runs for minutes. NATS redelivered the command every 30 s. The redeliveries queued behind the running handler, because the subscription delivers serially. When the handler finished, one redelivery took over the claim before the engine released it. The idempotency check replayed the recorded outcome, so no work ran twice. This was safe by accident, not by design.

** A step longer than its lease, after the fix

While a claimed handler runs, a renewer thread runs every quarter lease (30 s). It sends an in-progress ack to NATS and renews the claim through =workflow.v1.ops.renew_step_claim=. The renewal is conditional on the owner, so a holder that lost its claim cannot take it back; the renewer then logs an error and stops. The renewal does not move =claimed_at=, so the step deadline still caps the total run time.

** Limits

- The step deadline caps a step's whole run, renewed or not. generate_trades has 1200 s; at the measured 10,000 trades in 516 s, about 23,000 trades would exceed it.
- A step queued behind a long step waits unclaimed. The engine exempts an unclaimed step while its service is alive, up to ten times its deadline. In the 10,000 run, write steps queued for up to 495 s against a 120 s deadline, and were not failed.

* Result

Provisioning now explains itself, and a long step keeps its claim.

- Each provisioning step logs its start, its return with its duration, and any exception of any type, which now fails the step.
- generate_trades logs each phase of each entry with its timing; the trade writer logs its plan, its progress every 25 trades, a failure naming the trade, and its end.
- The step stream logs each claim and each handler's duration; each step logs its answer to the engine; the claim handler logs takeovers as warnings.
- The engine warns every two minutes about a step still in progress, with its time against its deadline and whether a worker holds it.
- The claim of a running step is renewed every 30 s, and NATS is told the message is in progress, so no redelivery takes the step over.
- Services write their logs to =publish/log=, because the journal drops lines.

Acme provisioned on a fresh database with 10,000 trades: all 9 steps completed, the tenant is active, and the logs show no takeover, lost claim or deadline failure.
