#+title: Five tenant-scoped IAM tables carry a tenant and no isolation policy
#+description: iam_rls_policies_create.sql covers the IAM tables a person would list from memory, but five tables that carry a tenant_id have no policy at all and no mention in that file: seed_profiles, seed_profile_steps, seed_profile_parameters, auth_events and run_token_issues. The seed profiles are platform configuration that any tenant can currently read across the boundary, and auth_events is audit data. Whether the generator never emitted them, an exemption list names them, or the file predates them is not yet known.

* What

=projects/ores.sql/create/iam/iam_rls_policies_create.sql= enforces tenant
isolation for accounts, account credentials, account parties, account roles,
login info, roles, permissions, role permissions, sessions, session samples,
run grants, role grant requests and tenants. Five IAM tables that carry a
=tenant_id= are not mentioned in it and have no policy at all:

| Table | What it holds |
|-------+----------------|
| =ores_iam_seed_profiles_tbl= | the starting points a tenant is built from |
| =ores_iam_seed_profile_steps_tbl= | the steps each starting point orders |
| =ores_iam_seed_profile_parameters_tbl= | the values each starting point declares |
| =ores_iam_auth_events_tbl= | authentication audit |
| =ores_iam_run_token_issues_tbl= | run-token audit |

The four closed vocabularies (=account_statuses=, =account_types=,
=tenant_statuses=, =tenant_types=) are also without policies and plausibly
belong that way. These five are not obviously in that category: the seed
profiles are the platform's own configuration, and any tenant can currently
read them across the boundary.

Found while chasing a first-run screen that offered one starting point
instead of four. That turned out to be an unrelated client-side fault; the
census was a side effect of checking whether row-level security explained it,
and it does not.

* Why

A tenant-scoped table without a policy is readable across the tenant boundary
by any role that holds =select= on it, which is the state every other IAM
table's policy exists to prevent. Whether that reach is a defect or a
deliberate exemption has not been established, and the answer decides two very
different pieces of work:

- if the generator is meant to emit a policy per tenant-scoped entity and never
  did for these, the hole is in the generator and every future entity inherits
  it;
- if =iam_rls_policies_create.sql= is hand-written and simply predates them,
  the work is five policies and a note about how the next entity gets covered.

The seed profiles are the ones worth deciding first: they are read by the
super administrator during setup, so a policy would have to admit the system
tenant without pretending the rows belong to the caller.

* What to find out

- Whether the RLS script is generated at all, and if so from what: the entity
  models carry a tenant flag, and something must decide whether a policy is
  emitted.
- Whether an exemption list names these entities, and who put them there.
- Why =auth_events= and =run_token_issues= are unprotected while the rest of
  the session and audit tables are not.
