#+title: Products that settle on fixings
#+description: Swaps, NDFs, NDOs, digitals, discrete barriers, averages and volatility swaps each need fixings for a different result.

* Summary

Many products cannot be settled or valued without fixings, and each uses its
fixings for a different result. A floating swap leg turns a rate fixing into a
coupon; a non-deliverable forward turns an FX fixing into a cash settlement; a
digital option, a discretely monitored barrier and an average rate option
decide their payoff from spot fixings; a volatility or variance swap computes
its payoff from a daily series of them. A continuously monitored barrier has
no fixing, because no source publishes a continuous fixing.

* Detail

** What each product fixes

| Product | What is fixed | What the fixing produces |
|---------+---------------+--------------------------|
| Interest rate swap, floating leg | The floating rate of each period | A coupon, once the observation is authorised |
| Compounding swap, overnight index swap | Several rate fixings per period | A coupon from the compounded rate at the end of the period |
| Non-deliverable interest rate swap | A rate fixing, and an FX fixing per non-deliverable leg | A coupon in the non-deliverable currency, converted into the settlement currency at the FX fixing |
| Non-deliverable forward | The FX rate on the fixing date | A cash settlement of the difference in the settlement currency |
| Non-deliverable option | The spot rate at expiry | If in the money, a cash settlement |
| European digital | The spot rate at expiry | A fixed amount if in the money |
| Discretely monitored barrier | The spot rate at each monitoring time | Whether the barrier is triggered |
| Average rate option, target redemption forward | Spot fixings over a schedule | The payoff after the last fixing |
| Volatility, variance and correlation swaps | Daily spot fixings of one or more pairs | The payoff from the realised statistic |
| Exchange-traded option | The exchange's settlement price | Exercise, reconciled against the exchange's files |

** Notes on the products

- *Overnight rates.* A compounded overnight rate is known only at the end of
  its period. Lyashenko and Mercurio (2019) extend the forward market model to
  such backward-looking rates. The difference between term and overnight
  benchmarks is in [[id:5170B17B-188B-45CA-A1B6-1F27A97E1CF8][Interest Rate Benchmark Types: IBOR vs. RFR]].
- *Non-deliverable trades.* The mechanics, and the currencies that need them,
  are in [[id:CD3E82C6-AE56-4D06-B949-0B3A5CE7A5FC][Deliverability and non-deliverable instruments]]; the
  valuation is in [[id:B60031AD-CE57-5F9D-885C-1DAF504E6004][An NDF pays a cash difference on its settlement date]].
  A non-deliverable option must be exercised before its settlement can be
  fixed.
- *Digitals.* A European digital can be struck without a fixing source, which
  leaves the outcome to the calculation agent; regulators now expect such
  trades to name a source. See [[id:C880CEB4-4FB7-41AA-8A4A-A650B6FC388C][FX Digital Option]].
- *Discrete barriers.* A barrier monitored at fixings is less likely to be
  triggered than a continuous one. Broadie, Glasserman and Kou (1997) correct a
  continuous barrier formula for discrete monitoring by shifting the barrier.
  See [[id:6CAED5F9-21CB-4300-B1B0-89599CFBFC7C][FX Barrier Option]] and [[id:4BAF88FA-7FDF-4B01-9A52-7AAE733A02B5][FX European Barrier Option]].
- *Average rate options.* The payoff depends on the average of the fixings, so
  the fixings already observed reduce the uncertainty that remains; Kemna and
  Vorst (1990) give the classical pricing method for an average.
- *Volatility and variance swaps.* The realised variance is computed from the
  log returns of the daily fixings; Demeterfi, Derman, Kamal and Zou (1999)
  derive the replication of the variance swap. See
  [[id:7A2186FB-33C3-4916-B62C-A14468EE713F][FX Variance Swap]] and [[id:D69C0964-9F15-43BC-8A13-66ECED718BED][Equity Variance Swap]].
- *Path-dependent trades priced by Monte Carlo.* Every new fixing changes the
  state of the trade, so the pricer runs again whenever a fixing is applied.

** Life cycle of a swap with non-deliverable legs

A swap whose legs pay in non-deliverable currencies needs both kinds of
fixing. Each coupon is fixed in its own currency, then converted at an FX
fixing taken some days before payment. The two legs may convert into different
settlement currencies and settle on different dates. The order is fixed:
authorise the observation, store it, then update every affected trade, generate
its coupons and net the cash. See
[[id:CA61B445-B6DC-41D3-ACF9-D3FB32D0D342][The fixing process observes, authorises, applies and can undo an observation]].
