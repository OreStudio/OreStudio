* Summary

This prototype draws the product pricing screen: the list of configurations,
and one configuration with five tabs. The Products tab is a tree of the
configuration's product types grouped by asset class, beside the product
itself. The product takes the rest of the width: what it is, its model with its
parameters, and its engine with its parameters.

The screen is read-only until *Edit* or *Propose a change*. In edit mode the
values become controls and the change reason and commentary appear.

It is throwaway. It reads no service and writes nothing to one.

* Detail

** The question the screen answers

"Which model and engine price my Bermudan swaptions, and how are they set?" The
tree finds the product; the pane answers with the choice, the parameters and
the documentation of each.

** The list

Three configurations: the approved EOD pricing, the approved intraday pricing
and the SABR draft. The columns are name, status, official for, variant,
products, reports and last changed. A row opens the configuration.

** The configuration page

Five tabs, in this order:

| Tab | Holds |
|-----+-------|
| Details | The configuration's own fields: name, status, official for, version, signed off by, variant, products, bound by, description, reporting row, last changed |
| Global parameters | The document-level parameters, with their documentation collapsed |
| Products | The tree and the product workbench |
| Reports | The reports that bind the configuration, and the reach rule |
| History | The shared timeline |

** The Products workbench

- The tree groups the configuration's product types by asset class, with a
  count per class. Selecting a type swaps the pane.
- The pane opens with the product, its asset class and instrument, and one
  documentation disclosure for all three.
- *Model*: a definition of what a model is, the select, a description of the
  chosen model, then the model parameters.
- *Engine*: the same for the engine, the select, a description, whether the
  pair is known to work, then the engine parameters.
- A parameter shows its value in read mode, and a control in edit mode, with
  its documentation collapsed under it. Simple and Advanced govern the depth;
  Advanced adds the ORE argument, the format and the release.
- A quiet sentence at the top of the tab says that this page sets how each
  product is priced, and that how the engine runs every product is the engine
  run settings, with a link.

** Edit and propose

Read mode shows values and names, and no change fields. *Edit* on a draft, or
*Propose a change* on an approved configuration, enters edit mode: the values
become controls and the change reason and commentary appear with *Propose* and
*Cancel*. Propose or Cancel returns to read mode.

** States and switches

=&tab=details|globals|products|reports|history= picks the tab; =&product=<type>=
picks a product; =&mode=simple|advanced= sets the documentation depth;
=&doc=<name>= opens one documentation entry.

** What is fake

Every value. The status, the cycle, the sign-off, the proposal, the report
counts and the portfolios are fixtures: no operation holds them. The product,
model and engine triples are counted from the ORE example files. The product,
instrument and asset-class paragraphs are fixtures; the asset class text follows
the seeded descriptions. The model and engine descriptions are written for this
page.

** What it argues

- The product takes the width because the parameters are the density; the tree
  is navigation, not content.
- A model and its parameters are one group, and an engine and its parameters are
  one group, as the ORE document arranges them.
- The page is read-only until Edit, and the change fields appear only there.
- One disclosure carries the product, the instrument and the asset class.
- One scoped sentence points at the engine run settings, at the top of the tab;
  there is no family card.

** What it does not settle

- Whether a model or engine change is edited per group or for the whole
  workbench. The prototype edits the workbench.
- Whether the edit dialog for the configuration's own fields belongs on Details
  or in the page header.
