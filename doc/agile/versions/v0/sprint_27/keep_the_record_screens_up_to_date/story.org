* Acceptance

- A list, a record page and a relationship view show a change made elsewhere without a reload, through the change events.
- A Refresh button stands in the same place on each list and record page.
- A screen with an edit open tells the person when the record changed, shows what changed, and lets them keep their edit or take the new values.
- A save made against an old version is refused with a clear message and no data is lost.
- The "Updates and refresh" section of the entity screen specification and the Change notification page describe what was built.

* Decisions

What ores.qt does, read from =EntityListMdiWindow=, =RecencyPulseManager= and =RecencyTracker=:

- A notification marks every open list of that entity stale. The list never reloads by itself.
- The Reload icon changes to a gold colour (=stale_indicator=, 255 215 0, readable in light and dark themes) every 500 ms for six cycles, then stays gold until the person reloads. The tooltip changes to say why.
- After a reload, a row whose =recorded_at= is newer than the last load is a recent row. Its text pulses in the same gold for six cycles, then the mark is cleared.
- The first load only sets the baseline. Nothing is marked.

What the web does the same way:

- One accent colour for "changed", used for nothing else.
- A slow pulse that stops, and a stable stale colour after it. A reader who asks for less motion gets the colour and no pulse.
- The payload is a time, not a list, so a bulk import makes a screen stale once.
- The rows that changed come from the data after the reload.
- No automatic reload of a list, a form or an open dialog.

What the web does differently:

- The web has a record page, which Qt has as a modal dialog. A record page that is being edited shows a line above the form with the newer version, and the person chooses Load newer or Keep my edit.
- Dependent screens (a book showing a country name) stay out of scope, as the Change notification page states.

Decided by the product owner:

- A record page waits for Refresh, as a list does. It never reloads its cards by itself.
- The history waits for Refresh too. A new entry makes the Refresh button stale, and the entry is marked once the history is read again.

Known limit, found in task 1:

- A published entity event names no tenant, in its subject or its payload, and ores.qt ignores the tenant it logs. The web follows ores.qt. A change in one tenant can mark a screen stale in another, which costs one refresh that reads only the reader's own data. The product owner finds this unacceptable: a session may listen only to the tenants and parties it may see. The task "Analyse who may listen to which tenant and party events" decides how.

Open decisions for the product owner:

- The stale colour. The proposal is the existing =warn= amber, which the timeline also uses for "Answered". A separate token would keep "changed" meaning one thing.
