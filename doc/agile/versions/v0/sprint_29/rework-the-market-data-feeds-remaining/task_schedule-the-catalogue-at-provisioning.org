* Plan

The decision that shaped the work: the step schedules what each party
*publishes* rather than a list the profile states. A profile row that named
definitions would be a copy of the catalogue that goes stale, and the catalogue
is the thing that already says when each report runs.

1. The notation: =schedule_reports= as a step kind in
   =provision_tenant_workflow.hpp=, with its words, its budget
   (=orchestrating_step_timeout=) and its need. It needs =load_staff=, because a
   party's report definitions arrive with the bundles the staff step names; a
   run that orders no staff step resolves the need through the party chain.
2. The rule: a definition is scheduled when its schedule is a cron the calendar
   reaches. The on-demand placeholder =0 0 31 2 *= is not, and neither is a
   malformed expression. The rule lives in
   =ores.iam.core/messaging/report_schedule_selection.hpp= as a pure function
   over (id, schedule, holds-a-job), so the selection is testable without a
   server.
3. The executor: one client per party, because the reporting operation makes the
   run grant for the session's party and refuses a definition of another one (the
   live refusal is in the evidence file); read that party's definitions a page at
   a time; select; call =reporting.v1.ops.schedule_report_definitions= once per
   party with a budget above the client's default, because one call can carry
   thirty definitions and each costs a grant, a scheduler write and a save.
4. Idempotence: check the operation first. =schedule_one= returns early on a
   definition that already names a scheduler job, before it creates a grant, and
   the run-grant table holds a unique index on the natural key, so a repeat
   cannot mint a second grant. The step therefore only has to not ask twice,
   which is the selection's =holds_scheduler_job= branch.
5. The profile: the =acme_demo= row at order 70, naming the four parties the
   staff step loads and nothing else; =generate_trades= moves from 70 to 80,
   because scheduling reads the parties' published data and not their trades.
6. The tests: the kind is declared, executed and ordered after the staff; the
   arguments name parties and nothing else; the selection picks a real cron,
   skips the placeholder and a non-cron, and asks for nothing a definition
   already holds; the profile row is ordered between the staff step and the
   trades and names the four parties the staff step names.
7. The gates: the codegen suite reads the kind table and requires every sentence
   it declares in the three web catalogues, so the two sentences are translated
   in the same branch.

* Notes

- The step acts as the run's administrator because that is the identity the
  reporting operation makes the run grant for, and the same identity the
  tenant's own setup run already runs as. No password is added: the handler
  mints the token from the run's administrator, as it does for every other
  step.
- The operation requires the caller's session party to be the definition's
  party. That is why the step mints a client per party; acting as the
  administrator's own party for all four was refused live, and the refusal is
  in the evidence file.
- The nine on-demand definitions are the catalogue's desk maps and the
  ladders' spot report. They hold no job and no grant, which is the point: a
  person asks for them when they want them.
- A report fires and its run does not yet complete. The engine refuses the
  book the population generated (trade XML it cannot parse), which is the
  subject of the task "Complete the ACME report inputs and prove they run".
  This task's claim is only that the catalogue fires by itself.
- A second provision of the same tenant is not a path the product offers: the
  provisioner refuses a tenant code that exists, and the backlog holds the gap
  as "a provisioning run cannot be resumed". Idempotence was therefore proven
  at the two layers a re-provision would reach, the step's selection (unit
  test) and the operation (live re-issue).

* Result

Shipped, on this branch:

- =schedule_reports= is a step kind of the provisioning notation: a kind
  constant, its words and budget in =words_for_step_kind=, membership of
  =provision_step_kinds= and =provision_executed_step_kinds=, the need that
  orders it after the staff, its action in =classify_step_kind= and its
  argument parser. It is code like every other kind, so a profile that orders
  it is refused by a build that cannot execute it.
- The executor reads each named party's published definitions as that party,
  selects the ones whose schedule is a real cron, calls
  =reporting.v1.ops.schedule_report_definitions= once per party under the run's
  administrator, and reports per party what it scheduled, what already held a
  job, what is on demand and what is not a cron. It never fails a run because a
  report is on demand.
- =iam.core= links =ores.reporting.api= and =ores.scheduler.api=, declared in
  its component model so the generated build file states them.
- The =acme_demo= profile orders the step at 70, naming the four parties the
  staff step loads and nothing else; =generate_trades= moves to 80.
- Tests: the notation (declared, executed, not system-scoped, ordered after the
  staff, its command carries the parties), the arguments, the selection's four
  rules including idempotence, and the profile row (ordered after the parties
  and before the trades; names exactly the parties the staff step names).
- Docs: the profile page now states ten steps and why the schedule comes where
  it does; the report families note states how a schedule reaches the
  scheduler, what the placeholder means, and that the UI Schedule action is
  still a separate feature; the three web catalogues carry the kind's
  sentences.
