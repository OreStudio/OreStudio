* Summary

A modeller classifies a set of data before choosing any shape for it. Three
pages carry that decision: the closed set of classifications, the form that
records a classification and the pattern it selected, and the one transport a
classification needs when its owners are several.

The order is the argument. The classifications define the words and the
invariant that sanctions each one. The checklist turns the definition into a
decision recorded before a model is written. The transport assumes the
decision, and says how a bundle crosses owners that cannot share a
transaction. Read out of order, the transport is a mechanism with no reason
for existing and the checklist is a form with no definitions behind it.

* Detail

** Reading order

1. [[id:B2D74E96-0353-4A3D-8CE5-3FC3C30D864C][Data classifications:
   entity, list, family, bundle and artefact]] — start here. The set is
   closed, which is what makes a set that fits none of it a finding rather
   than a shape to invent. This page states the anti-type, the sentence
   that refuses it, the decision rule, and the rule that a compile error
   does not reclassify data.

2. [[id:56AD6F46-99E0-482E-B7B5-C9FDEC8A7641][The data classification
   checklist]] — next, the form. Page one says which classification a set
   is; this page asks for the invariant, the owners, the consistency point
   and the forbidden shape, in a fixed order, so the answer can be
   reviewed. Its worked examples apply the definitions to a list, a
   family, a bundle and an ORE conventions file.

3. [[id:0BA30CC1-18C8-48CC-8243-8912880F8DEC][Transporting a bundle]] —
   last, and only when page two answers Bundle. The classification states
   that per-owner atomicity is the guarantee and global atomicity is not;
   page three is the mechanism that keeps that promise — the manifest, the
   archive and its parts, one message per owner, and the transactionality
   the classification cannot supply across owners.

** Where the classifications are applied

The definitions settle what a set is. The catalogue settles which sets
this system actually has:
[[id:BA57E2D2-553B-4A06-9295-E792D1E29487][The bundle catalogue]]
enumerates every candidate bundle in the code and the models, with its
invariant or the finding that none exists, its owner count, the atomicity
it has today with a file reference, the atomicity it requires, and its
status. Read it after the three pages above: it is the evidence that the
classification is decidable once, per set, instead of per feature.

** Why the order earns a structure note

Step two cannot be filled in without step one's vocabulary, and step three
is safe to read only after step two has said the set crosses owners. A
reader who searches the transport first, as the mechanism with the most
detail, gets a design with no test for when it applies; a reader who starts
at the checklist gets questions whose answers are on another page. The
three pages settle in one reading when they are read in the order above,
and that order is authored here rather than computed from a title match.
