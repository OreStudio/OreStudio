This page documents a [[id:F44766FA-1E9C-416F-849A-E131101B2ECA][user journey]]: A reference data administrator keeps a holiday calendar's rules, exceptions and events correct, and checks the business days they produce.

A user journey describes what one person is trying to do, before anything is
built. It is a design document that outlives any sprint, so it names the
screens, the entity fields each screen shows, and the operations and messages
each step needs.

* Goal

"I want this calendar to say exactly which days are holidays, so that every
date the system rolls, settles or fixes on lands where it should." The journey
is done when the business days the calendar produces match the published
holidays, for every year the desk needs.

* Actor and trigger

The actor is the tenant administrator: an account with the =TenantAdmin= role,
acting inside their own tenant. No narrower role holds the calendar
permissions today; see /What is missing/. The trigger is a newly announced
holiday, a revoked one, a central bank meeting schedule, or a run that settled
on a wrong date. The journey starts at the calendar list, or at a calendar link
from a currency or a currency pair, and ends when the person leaves the
calendar.

A calendar has one of three shapes, and the screen follows the shape:

- *QuantLib-sourced* (=source= =quantlib=, =is_editable= false). Its rules are
  QuantLib's published ones. The row is read-only; the person changes the dates
  by deriving a calendar from it.
- *Derived* (=source= =user= with a =base_calendar_code=). A delta on top of its
  base, carried as exceptions only.
- *Bespoke* (=source= =user= with no base). Built from its own recurring rules,
  plus exceptions.

* Steps

1. *Find the calendar.* The person opens *Calendars*. The system lists the
   tenant's calendars: code, name, type, country, source and whether it is
   editable. The person filters by name, country or type.
2. *Read the calendar.* The person selects a row. The system shows one screen:
   the calendar row, then its rules, its exceptions and its diary events, then
   the business days it produces. Panels that do not apply to the calendar's
   shape are hidden, not shown empty: a derived calendar has no rules panel.
3. *Derive a calendar.* On a QuantLib-sourced calendar the person chooses
   *Derive a calendar*, names it, and confirms. The system writes a new
   =user= calendar whose base is this one. The person continues on the new
   calendar.
4. *Correct the calendar row.* On an editable calendar the person changes the
   name, type, country or image, states a change reason, and saves. The system
   writes it with =must_match_version=.
5. *Maintain the rules.* On a bespoke calendar the person adds, changes or
   removes a recurring rule. The form follows the rule kind: a fixed date asks
   for month and day; the nth weekday of a month asks for month, weekday and
   occurrence; the last weekday of a month asks for month and weekday; an
   Easter offset asks for the offset in days. Every kind asks for the weekend
   shift and the first and last years it applies.
6. *Add an exception.* The person adds a one-off date, says whether it is a
   business day or a holiday, and describes it. An extra holiday is an
   exception that is not a business day; a cancelled holiday is an exception
   that is a business day. Retiring a recurring holiday from a year onward is a
   change to the rule's last year, not a deletion, so the history keeps it.
7. *Record diary events.* The person adds a dated event that does not change
   whether the day is a business day, such as a central bank meeting, with its
   diary entry type, name and description.
8. *Rebuild the business days.* The person chooses *Rebuild business days* and
   says up to which year. The system builds the materialised dates for this
   calendar from its rules, base and exceptions, for the days it has not built
   yet. A day already built keeps its value; see /What is missing/.
9. *Check the business days.* The system shows the year's holidays, one month
   per block. Each holiday shows what produced it: a rule, an exception or the
   QuantLib base. The person can add a second calendar, such as the other
   currency of a pair. The system then shows the union of the two. The union is
   the business-day definition a pair settles on.
10. *Check the history.* The person opens the history of the calendar row or of
    one rule, exception or event. The system shows every version with the
    field-level difference from the one before, and the person can revert to an
    older version, which the system writes as a new version.
11. *Export for ORE.* The person exports the calendar's adjustments, which ORE
    reads as a =CalendarAdjustments= document.
12. *Leave.* The person goes back to the list.

Step 8 is the only one whose effect is not immediate in the rest of the system:
until the dates are rebuilt, every consumer reads the old business days. The
journey still keeps no state of its own; the rebuild completes within the reply.

The history request is the generic one, with =entity_type=
=ores.refdata.calendar=, =ores.refdata.calendar_rule=,
=ores.refdata.calendar_exception= or =ores.refdata.calendar_event=; all four
register a history provider. The pair view reads the dates of both calendars
and unions them on the screen; it needs no operation of its own.

* What is missing

1. *The business days are not rebuilt when their inputs change, and a rebuild
   never rewrites a built day.* Writing a rule, an exception or a base does not
   regenerate =calendar_date=, and =refdata.v1.ops.regenerate_calendar_dates= only
   adds days past the last one built: a changed rule or a new exception never
   reaches a year already built. The candidate is a rebuild that rewrites the
   days whose inputs changed. Until then the screen does not rebuild after a
   save, because that would change nothing; it says what a rebuild does, and
   it marks each exception that the built days do not show.
2. *A QuantLib calendar builds without its holidays.* The rebuild reads only
   stored rules, and a QuantLib calendar has none: its holidays live in
   QuantLib. Its days, and the days of a calendar derived from it, mark
   weekends only. The candidate is for the rebuild to ask QuantLib for the
   base's holidays. Until then the screen hides the business days of a
   QuantLib calendar and warns on a calendar derived from one.
3. *The dates cannot be read by year.* The =calendar_dates_filter= takes only
   calendar codes, so showing one year means paging through every materialised
   date in order. The side-by-side view doubles that cost, because it pages
   through every date of both calendars. The candidate is a date range on the
   filter. Until then the screen pages from the first date it holds to the end
   of the year it needs.
4. *The ORE export is data, not a document.* =calendar_adjustments.export=
   returns the adjustments, and the caller is expected to write the
   =CalendarAdjustments= XML. A browser has no ORE writer. The candidate is for
   the operation, or the web back end, to return the document. Until then the
   screen offers no export.
5. *Externally sourced holidays are not gated.* The administration model
   expects a holiday from an untrusted source to wait for authorisation before
   it takes effect. Nothing models a pending holiday or a trusted source. The
   candidate is a status on the exception, or a workflow over imports. Until
   then every written exception takes effect when the dates are rebuilt.
6. *A holiday has no name.* A rule has no name column, and a materialised date
   carries only whether it is a business day and how it was produced. The
   screen cannot say "Good Friday"; only exceptions (description) and diary
   events (name) carry words. The candidate is a name on the rule, carried onto
   the materialised date. Until then the screen describes a rule by its shape,
   such as "1st Monday of May".
7. *Only a tenant administrator can run this journey.* No role below
   =TenantAdmin= holds the calendar, rule, exception, event or date
   permissions. The candidate is a reference data role.
