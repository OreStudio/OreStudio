* Goal

The FX derivation already holds every piece of the measurement: a window as long
as the pillar, the moments of a return series, a level multiplier, a shape shrunk to a
prior, the reach a sample supports, and the method and version a series is stamped with.
None of it is FX. Make the transform, the coordinates and the smile model the only
per-class inputs, and prove the lift by the FX quotes coming out as they were.

* Acceptance

- The derivation is one engine. Its inputs are a series, a transform, a coordinate set and a smile model; nothing else in it names an asset class, a pair, a currency or a label.
- The engine keeps the rules it already has: the window is the pillar's own length and never shorter than a stated minimum, the level is a realized volatility times an authored multiplier, the shape is shrunk to a prior and clamped, and the reach states what the sample does not cover.
- The FX quotes are unchanged. The same history produces the same ATM, risk-reversal and butterfly values before and after the lift, cell for cell, and the existing derivation tests pass with their expected values unaltered.
- A stored series still states the method and the version that computed it, and the version rises when the computation changes.
- The engine measures and a class names its quotes. There is no plugin interface behind the engine,
  because one implementation would be the only one behind it; the interest rate derivation is where
  the seam is proven, and it adds a difference, a currency with an expiry and a term, and a smile
  across strikes. The bullet this replaces asked for a refusal at a call that does not exist, and was
  amended after review.

* Notes

- The engine's three per-class inputs are stated in its header: the transform is the engine's, the
  coordinates belong to the instrument type, and the smile belongs to the class.
- The transform is an argument of =returns_of= and not a field of the settings, because a setting a
  caller states and nothing reads is a setting that lies about what it looked at.
- A log return leaves out a level that is not positive, which is what the FX path did before the
  lift: a missing price spans the gap instead of producing an invented move. A difference keeps a
  series that crosses zero, which is the transform interest rates needs.
- The reach is the engine's, by a pillar's place in a ladder, so a class maps its labels to the
  places and nothing more.
- =fx_derivation_window= remains as the FX name for the engine's window rule, because the synthetic
  layer reports a pair's reach with it and the reach and the level must not disagree.
- The method and its version stay with the class's computation: a different computation is a
  different method, so =derived_fx_derivation_kind= names the FX one. The stamping itself is the
  store's and is generic.
- =derive_grid= keeps its prefix-sum arrangement of the same rules. It is the one place where the
  arithmetic is written twice, and the test that the grid equals the day-at-a-time answer is what
  holds the two together.
- =fx_derivation.hpp= and =derived_fx_vol.hpp= hold the pieces the FX class supplies: the ladder,
  the convention, the reach labels and =derived_fx_method_version=.
- The FX case stays the regression: its tests carry literal expected values, so a lift that changes
  a number fails a test rather than passing a new one.

* Plan

The engine was cut where the class stops being named. =volatility_measurement= holds the transform
that turns a level series into returns, the window rule, a series' shape read once, and a pillar's
level read over its own window; the FX derivation keeps its coordinates, its convention and its
smile, and maps its settings onto the engine's. The FX path that built its own log levels and
differences now calls the engine's transform, so the seam is used and not merely declared.

The batched grid was left where it is. It computes the same rules over prefix sums, because a day
of a grid must cost the same whether its window is a week or ten years, and it is pinned by the test
that the grid equals the day-at-a-time answer. Folding it into the engine would trade that test for
a slower pass.

* Result

The lift is committed at =f306a1152c=. The engine is =volatility_measurement=, the FX derivation is
one of its callers, and the FX quotes are the ones it produced before: the derivation tests carry
literal expectations and none was changed.

What the next class supplies is now exactly three things: a difference instead of a log return, a
currency with an expiry and a term instead of a pair with an expiry and a delta, and a smile across
strikes instead of a smile across deltas.

Not done, and deliberately: the batched grid keeps its own arrangement of the same rules. It is the
one place where the derivation is written twice, and the equality test is what holds the two
together.
