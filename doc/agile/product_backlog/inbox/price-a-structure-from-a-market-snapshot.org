This page is a [[id:671F18E4-E09C-4B3B-BD24-D33DF8AE38A6][capture]] in the *inbox* bucket of the product backlog — a pre-sprint idea, not yet pulled into a sprint as a story.

* What

The structure page's Price tab states, in the tab itself, that pricing is not
read yet. The signed-off prototype asks more of it: read a market snapshot, say
when it was read, name the shifts that were applied to it, and price the deal
from it — as a whole for a structure, leg by leg for a package. It explicitly
does not tick: the price is indicative, and Refresh is what reads a newer
snapshot.

Nothing in the product prices a trade. The reporting side runs ORE over a
report, which is a different question: a batch, a run, a set of results. The
structure page needs one number for one deal, against a named market, with the
snapshot it used.

* Why

The tab is the last part of the structure page's design and the only one that
cannot be built from what the store holds. Leaving a notice in its place is
honest but it is also the end of the line: no later task in the arc makes a
price appear.

It is also the first place the product would answer a question a trader asks
about a deal they are looking at. The blotter, the structure page and the trade
page all draw what a deal *is*; none of them says what it is *worth*.

* Shape of the work

Not designed here. The pieces that appear necessary:

- A market snapshot with an identity and an as-of, so a price can name the
  market it was struck against. The curve snapshot read exists for published
  curves; a trade needs the set of curves, surfaces and fixings its instruments
  take, which is a selection rather than a lookup.
- A pricing call that answers one deal against that snapshot, and states the
  shifts applied to it.
- The page: the figure, when the snapshot was read, and Refresh reading a newer
  one without ticking.
- A decision about what is priced: a structure as a whole, and a package leg by
  leg, which the prototype already distinguishes.

* What done looks like

The Price tab states a number, the market snapshot it came from and when that
snapshot was read, and Refresh reads a newer one. A package states one figure per
leg and the sum the customer is quoted.
