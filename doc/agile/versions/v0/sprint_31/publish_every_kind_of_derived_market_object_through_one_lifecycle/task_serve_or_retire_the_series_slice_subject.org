* Acceptance

- Either the market data service subscribes to =marketdata.v1.ops.get_series_slice= and answers it from the reads it already has, or the subject is gone from the protocol, the codec, the BFF, the gateway and the web client.
- A test proves the choice: a served subject answers, or a retired one has no subject constant and no caller.
- The knowledge page that calls the subject unreachable states what happened to it.

* Plan

The task was premised on =get_series_slice= being declared and unserved. The
premise does not survive a check of the tree. =registrar::register_handlers=,
which the market data service runs at start-up in =application.cpp=, subscribes
=marketdata.v1.ops.get_series_slice= to =series_slice_handler=, and
=client::make_subject= prefixes the subject for the environment. The claim came
from one page: the journey =journey_inspect-a-surfaces-slices.org= said the
subject was "declared, unserved", the task and the story repeated it, and no
one probed it.

So the disposition is **serve**. The work is the proof and the record, not the
handler, which has answered since sprint 27:

- *A probe on the running environment.* A request on the prefixed subject
  reaches a responder. A subject nobody serves answers =No responders are
  available= instead.
- *The handler's answer*, which =messaging_tick_read_party_tests.cpp= already
  drives over real NATS on a test-scoped subject.
- *Correct the records that call the subject unserved*: the journey page, the
  story's acceptance and task rows, this task's description, the closed
  deletion task's Result and Plan, and the architecture analysis that repeated
  the claim.

A registration test was written and withdrawn. Calling
=registrar::register_handlers= from a test breaks its own precondition, "only
ever called once per service process", because it reaches
=serve_step_commands=: that creates a JetStream stream, claims the stream in a
process-static set that throws on a second claim, binds the service's durable
consumer and prunes the other consumers. A test that provisions infrastructure
in the shared NATS store to prove one string is in a list is the wrong lever,
and the right one is a check over the registrar, which task 12B71000 owns.

Retiring was the other branch and was not taken. The read works, the gateway
and the BFF both route it, and deleting it would remove a typed read, its
reader and its tests to repair a sentence. What is missing is a caller: no web
screen posts to =/api/curves/slice=. That is recorded on the journey page and
is not this task's work.

* Notes

The probe needs the environment's prefix and its mTLS certificates;
=build/scripts/nats.sh= fills both from =.env=. A probe on the unprefixed name
answers nothing at all, because every subject carries the prefix; that is a
wrong probe, not a dead subject.

A responder is not the same as an answered caller. The handler also requires
=marketdata::curve_snapshots:read= and a session that names a party, so a
request without them is refused with a sentence rather than a reply. The probe
shows the subscription; the handler suites show the rest.

* Result

The subject is served and always was. The market data service subscribes it
through its registrar, and a request on the environment reaches a handler and
is refused only for want of credentials. The claim that the service subscribed
nothing was inherited from one journey page and went three records deep before
anyone sent a request.

The proof is the probe, and it is a probe because the durable form of this
check belongs somewhere else. The handler's answer is already held by
=messaging_tick_read_party_tests.cpp=, which drives =series_slice_handler= over
real NATS on a test-scoped subject; what no test held was the registration. The
test written for it here called =register_handlers=, and that function creates
a JetStream stream, claims it in a process-static set, binds the service's
durable consumer and prunes the stream's other consumers, so a single run of a
test binary provisions infrastructure in the shared NATS store. The case is
withdrawn rather than kept with that side effect. The check that holds the
registration without calling it, a comparison of the declared subjects against
what the registrar subscribes, is task 12B71000, and its description now says
the registrar cannot be called to find that out.

The record is corrected in five places: the journey page (the subject is
served, and no screen calls it yet), the story's acceptance and its task rows,
this task's description, the closed deletion task's Result and Plan, and the
architecture analysis that repeated the claim.

The gate reported two failures and neither is this branch's:

- =answer-shapes= reads live tick traffic, and =feeds[].last_at= is null until
  a feed has ticked. It passed on its own a minute later.
- =ctest= failed =ores.refdata.core.tests=, =ores.inbox.core.tests= and
  =ores.workflow.core.tests=, all three on the pooler at port 20607 refusing
  connections; the log says =Connection refused= at each failure, and the
  suite's own connection pool is what reports it. No test case failed on an
  assertion.

Run alone with the pooler up, the three behave as follows. Refdata and
workflow pass. The inbox suite passed once, then failed on a re-run with
=Invalid or inactive tenant_id= for a tenant the database had already
terminated, and then passed twice in a row. The second failure is unexplained
rather than diagnosed: the suite provisions its own tenant, so a terminated
one should not reach it. Both failures belong to the gate and the harness, not
to a market data file this branch touches.
