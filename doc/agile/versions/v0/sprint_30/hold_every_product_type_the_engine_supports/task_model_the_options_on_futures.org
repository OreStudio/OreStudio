* Acceptance

- each of the two types names an instrument table carrying the option block and the future's terms
- the projection reads and writes each element, value for value
- each type has a builder, and the catalogue routes both
- the generator books one trade of each type through its builder, and the recorded trades hold it
- the ORE engine test reads and builds each, with no error of the trade kind
- every fact the element states is a column or a row: no list, basket or schedule is held as text

* Plan

Both elements are flat: an option block, the underlying (or the contract), and
scalar terms. They route to the scripted instrument table through the
class-read path. A native home would need a new bond fact entity for the option
on a bond future and a future expiry column on the equity option row; the
flat element needs neither.

- The future's terms are parameters: =FutureExpiryDate= for the equity future,
  =ContractName= and =ContractNotional= for the bond future.
- Builders: =equity_future_option_builder= (European, cash settled; the future
  expires on or after the option) and =bond_future_option_builder=.
- The generator books the equity option in the equity family, its future a
  month after the option, and the bond option in the bond family on the
  contracts BondFuture names, expiring a month out. A bond future option slot
  states the slot's security as every bond slot does, with a scripted batch.

* Result

EquityFutureOption and BondFutureOption are held in the scripted instrument
table, the option block in the option rows and the future's terms as
parameters. The round-trip test reads the user guide's examples of both and
writes them back. Each type has a builder, the catalogue routes both, and the
generator books one trade of each. The ORE engine builds the recorded trades
with no error of the trade kind; both raise an engine configuration error,
because ORE's Products example configuration names no engine for them, which
the known list holds and the valuation story owns.

Adding the bond type moved the bond slots and showed two planner defects, both
fixed: a forward bond could mature after its bond, and a callable bond drew its
credit curve by slot, so two slots holding one security restated its issue with
different curves.
