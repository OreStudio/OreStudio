* Goal

An arrival law is a value: given its state and its stream, it returns the next gap in operational time, and its state changes only when an event happens. The periodic phase is an explicit start offset, and a Hawkes law starts at its stationary intensity.

* Acceptance

- Periodic, Poisson and Hawkes laws each have exact tests on a fixed stream and statistical tests on fixed seeds: gap mean and variance, phase uniformity, and the time-rescaling check.
- Drawing a gap without an event leaves a Hawkes law's state unchanged.
- The Hawkes law runs in operational time: no market factor reaches it, so its branching ratio is the same in any market.

* Plan

The laws are added beside the old processes, not in place of them.
tick_clock and tick_scheduler still call set_baseline_multiplier, and
the clock that replaces that call arrives with the market clock and the
heap scheduler. Deleting i_arrival_process before then would leave the
feeds with no scheduler, so the move and the deletion happen together in
[[id:5AEEAC8B-5435-46FD-B9C4-250F634682D3][Schedule every feed on the market clock]].
The panel help that says a burst fades faster in a busy market moves there
too, because it is only true once a feed runs on the market clock.

- =domain/arrival_law.hpp= holds one variant of three laws. Each law
  carries its own state, so a law cannot be paired with another law's
  state.
- =draw_gap= takes the law as const and the stream by reference. Only
  =record_event= changes a law.
- A periodic law's offset is the drawn phase before its first event and
  one after it. A Hawkes law starts with the stationary excess, m n.
- =make_arrival_law= reuses validate_arrival_process_parameters and the
  ticks-per-hour conversion.

* Result

=domain/arrival_law.hpp= makes each arrival law a value. A law holds all the
state that decides its next gap. =draw_gap= cannot change a law, so the
phantom jump that a dropped Hawkes draw added is gone by construction, and a
test proves a hundred dropped draws leave the law as it was. A periodic law
starts at the phase it draws, and the phases of many series are uniform. A
Hawkes law starts at its stationary excess, so the first second of a run has
the long-run rate. The compensated gaps of both random laws pass a
Kolmogorov-Smirnov test against Exp(1). No market factor reaches a law. The
scheduler moves onto the laws, and the old processes go, in
[[id:5AEEAC8B-5435-46FD-B9C4-250F634682D3][Schedule every feed on the market clock]].
