* Acceptance

- The 1,000-element bound is one named constant that the service template and the callers read.
- Every hand-written caller that can send more than 1,000 elements in a put-many or delete-many sends it in parts of at most 1,000.
- A generated put-many or delete-many of more than 1,000 elements is refused as =invalid= with =batch_too_large= before any row is read or written. A batch of exactly 1,000 is served.

* Decisions

* Out of scope

- The hand-written account-party handler in iam, which does not use the generated service. It writes one row at a time and is not a batch in the store.
- A change to the get-many refusal code. It stays =filter_too_large=, which PR 4739 shipped.
- The shell and HTTP surfaces. They pass a caller's request through, so the service refusal is their answer.

** What

The generated put-many and delete-many refuse a batch of more than 1,000 elements as =invalid=, as the protocol states. Callers that send larger batches chunk them first.

** Why

The [[id:C650CBC9-FDED-4C68-981F-7902E296928E][NATS Entity Protocol Specification]] bounds every batch at 1,000 elements so a caller can size its chunks and one request cannot hold the store without limit. Get-many enforces its bound since PR 4739, which restored the trade-id services' refusal for every service. The write paths do not, and importers, seeders and the derived sources may rely on that today, so the callers come first.
