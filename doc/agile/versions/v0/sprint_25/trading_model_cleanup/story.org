* Goal

The trading schema says what it holds, enforces what it relies on, and can
express the products a desk trades. Today it does none of those three
consistently.

Sixteen defects were found while building trade populations, and they were found
by loading every table rather than by reading the model. They fall into four
kinds, and the kinds matter more than the count because each wants a different
repair.

*Type defects* hold a value in a type too wide for its meaning, so the database
cannot constrain, index or store it compactly. Schedules and baskets live in
=text= columns holding JSON; the C++ declarations widen =date= and =numeric= back
to strings and doubles on the way out.

*Integrity defects* leave a relationship undeclared. There are no foreign keys at
all across the 41 tables, enumerations are free text with no reference table, the
=_code= suffix promises a constraint that mostly does not exist, and one concept
carries two names.

*Placement defects* put a rule in the wrong place. Referential integrity lives in
PL/pgSQL rather than in a foreign key, and it is the largest single cost on the
insert path. Enumerations live in trigger bodies rather than in the reference
tables that already hold their values.

*Expressiveness defects* lose information rather than discipline, and they are
the ones that bound what the system can hold. An underlying is a scalar, so no
table can carry a basket. An observation schedule is a frequency and two dates,
so no table can carry an irregular one. Together these account for 28 of ORE's
112 trade types, across equity, fx and commodity.

The goal is not to repair all sixteen. It is to repair the mechanisms the rest
depend on, in an order where each step makes the next one smaller.

* Acceptance

Ordered by dependency, not by size. Each step makes the next smaller, and the
first is a prerequisite for sizing the rest.

1. *Every instrument table declares which products it holds.* Twelve of the 30
   carry no =trade_type_code= constraint today, covering 51 of the 112 trade
   types, so coverage cannot be computed at all. Declaring it converts an unknown
   into a list, and the list is what says whether a table is missing.
2. *One name per concept.* =instrument_id= against =id= for an instrument's
   identity, and =day_count_fraction_code= against =day_count_code=. This is
   cheap, and the foreign keys in the next step need one column name per
   referenced table.
3. *The reference tables that the =_code= convention promises exist, and foreign
   keys point at them.* Currency, day count, payment frequency and change reason
   have no table; the enumerations enforced inside triggers move to the tables
   that already hold their values.
4. *An underlying is a row carrying an oresmd URI, not a name in a column.*
   Sixteen trade types across three asset classes stop being unstorable, and
   =basket_json= is deleted.
5. *A schedule is a table, shared across the instruments that need one.* Twelve
   more trade types stop being unstorable, and the JSON schedule columns are
   deleted.
6. *Bulk population does not pay the per-row insert trigger.* Whether by
   separating validation from bitemporal versioning, or by a set-based path, the
   25x is the largest measured cost in the schema.

Two things are explicitly not required for this story to close: every missing
product having a table, and the generic bond, commodity and credit tables being
split. Both are follow-on work that these steps make tractable.

* Decisions

* Out of scope

- Changing how any product is priced. This is a storage and integrity change.
- Trade population generation, which is paused pending this work and resumes
  against the repaired model.
- Adding the remaining unstorable products. The mechanisms come first; the
  products that then need a table are a much shorter list than the count
  suggests today.

* Notes

The defects, their evidence and a proposed repair for each are recorded in the
trade population story's =Investigation: Trade model defects=. That document is
on the =feature/ore-trade-generator-trade-populations= branch and merges with
PR #2034; link it from here once it lands, since a link to an unmerged document
would break the site build.

Two defects carry a measurement rather than an argument. The JSON schedule column
costs 4x on the load path, and referential integrity in PL/pgSQL costs about 25x.
