#+title: A generated FX variance swap breaks its own check constraint
#+description: generate_trades fails at step 8 of 8 on acme_demo: the booked FX variance swap violates CHECK (end_date > start_date), which the product's own constructor refuses to build, so the row reaching the database did not pass through it.
#+type: capture
#+level: cross
#+filetags: :code:trading:seeder:
#+created: 2026-10-31
#+updated: 2026-10-31

This page is a [[id:671F18E4-E09C-4B3B-BD24-D33DF8AE38A6][capture]] in the *inbox* bucket of the product backlog — a pre-sprint idea, not yet pulled into a sprint as a story.

* What

The `acme_demo` seed profile fails on its last step. `generate_trades` books a
population of trades and one of them is refused by the database:

#+begin_example
[8/8] generate_trades: failed — Booking trade fc77dd4a-ef13-5f4d-b590-101ed75bad75
failed: Repository error: ores_q_34: ERROR:  new row for relation
"ores_trading_fx_variance_swap_instruments_tbl" violates check constraint
"ores_trading_fx_variance_swap_instruments_tbl_check1"
#+end_example

`ores_trading_fx_variance_swap_instruments_tbl_check1` is
`CHECK ((end_date > start_date))`. The tenant is left in `bootstrapping` with
everything else loaded, and `compass smoke ticks --fresh` — which the gate's
`ticks-smoke` check runs — cannot pass while this holds.

* Why

The interesting part is not that a generated row was bad but that it should be
impossible. The product's own constructor refuses the condition:

#+begin_example
if (!(terms.end_date > terms.start_date))
    refuse("fx variance swap", "an end date after its start");
#+end_example

(`projects/ores.trading/api/include/ores.trading.api/domain/fx_instruments.hpp`,
`construct_fx_variance_swap`.) The planner that generates the population calls that
constructor (`construct_fx_batch`, `trade_population_planner.cpp:1523`), and it
computes the two dates one from the other:

#+begin_example
const auto start = add_months(scope.as_of_date, -static_cast<int>(slot % 6));
const auto end = add_years(start, static_cast<int>(tenor_years));
#+end_example

So a row that violates the constraint reached the database without passing through
the check, or had its dates changed after it. Both are worth finding, and the
second is the more likely: the booking path, the batch splitter
(`instrument_batch_mapper.cpp`) and the wire protocol each carry the two dates, and
a swap anywhere on that road is invisible to a unit test that checks one of them.

The next step is to make the failure name the two dates — the error stops at the
constraint, so the reader cannot tell whether the generator drew an inverted pair
or something downstream swapped it.
