#+title: Asset Classes
#+description: Six classifications call themselves asset class or risk class and none agree; an analysis of what each classifies, and a proposed hierarchical taxonomy that every use case can cut.
#+type: knowledge
#+level: cross
#+filetags: :trading:refdata:taxonomy:
#+created: 2026-09-15
#+updated: 2026-09-15

* Summary

Wikipedia defines an asset class as "a group of marketable financial
assets that have similar financial characteristics and behave
similarly in the marketplace". The definition is not in dispute; the
enumerations derived from it vary substantially.

Seven classifications are in play across ORE, ISDA, Basel and ORE
Studio. No two share a value set, they range from five values to nine,
and they disagree in both directions: some omit classes the others
carry, and some subdivide a class the others treat as atomic.

The variation has a cause. Each enumeration classifies a different
subject — a product, a trade underlying, a market data series, a risk
sensitivity — and each requires a different granularity. A single flat
list cannot serve all seven subjects simultaneously.

One difference is structural rather than terminological. A product
carries one class, while a trade's risk carries as many classes as it
has kinds of sensitivity. That difference in cardinality cannot be
collapsed into a single attribute.

The proposal is therefore one hierarchical taxonomy, referenced once
by a product and many times by a trade's risk, with each use case
cutting the tree at the depth it needs.

* The problem

| Classification | Values | Classifies |
|----------------+--------+------------|
| ISDA product taxonomy | 5 top level, then nested | OTC derivative products |
| FpML asset class scheme | 8 | Products |
| ISDA SIMM =ProductClass= | 6 substantive | Products, for margin |
| ISDA SIMM =RiskClass= | 6 | Risk sensitivities |
| FRTB risk classes | 6, with credit spread split three ways | Risk sensitivities, for regulatory capital |
| ORE =AssetClass= | 9 | Trade underlyings |
| ORE =RiskClass= | 6 | Risk sensitivities, for reporting |

Each is named an asset class, a product class or a risk class, and the
names do not correspond to the distinctions between them. Two
enumerations named =RiskClass= within one codebase hold different
values, as do two enumerations that classify products. The name is
therefore not a reliable guide to a classification's subject or
granularity.

* State of the art

** The ISDA product taxonomy

The ISDA product taxonomy, published as an FpML coding scheme, is
hierarchical. Values are colon-separated paths that descend from an
asset class through sub-category to instrument type and settlement
method: =InterestRate:IRSwap:FixedFloat=,
=Credit:SingleName:Corporate:NorthAmericanCorporate=,
=Commodity:Agricultural:Dairy:Swap:Cash=,
=ForeignExchange:SimpleExotic:Barrier=,
=Equity:Option:ParameterReturnVolatility:SingleName=.

Its five top-level classes are =Commodity=, =Credit=, =Equity=,
=ForeignExchange= and =InterestRate=.

Two properties matter here. The taxonomy is /open at the leaves/: a
consumer that needs only the asset class reads the first segment and
ignores the rest, while a consumer that needs the instrument type
reads further. And it covers /OTC derivatives only/, which is why it
has no bond class: a bond is a security rather than a derivative
contract, and falls outside the scope ISDA set.

** The FpML asset class scheme

FpML publishes a separate, flat asset class scheme, which ORE Studio
registers as =FPML_ASSET_CLASS=: =ForeignExchange=, =InterestRate=,
=Credit=, =Equity=, =Commodity=, =Inflation=, =Bond= and
=SecuritiesFinancing=.

It extends the taxonomy's five in three places, and each extension
marks a scope boundary rather than a disagreement. =Bond= appears
because FpML covers securities as well as derivatives.
=SecuritiesFinancing= appears because repo and securities lending are
a product family FpML represents. =Inflation= appears as a sibling of
=InterestRate= rather than beneath it.

That last one is the first real disagreement in the material, and it
recurs. Inflation is treated as a top-level class by FpML, as a
top-level class by ORE's risk filter, and as no class at all by the
ISDA taxonomy and by SIMM.

** ISDA SIMM

SIMM holds two classifications simultaneously and treats them as
distinct, which makes it the most informative of the seven for present
purposes.

=ProductClass= carries =RatesFX=, =Credit=, =Equity= and =Commodity=
as its four substantive values, with =Rates= and =FX= as separate
values for the IM Schedule extension, plus =Empty=, =Other= and two
add-on markers.

=RiskClass= carries =InterestRate=, =CreditQualifying=,
=CreditNonQualifying=, =Equity=, =Commodity= and =FX=.

Comparison within one framework isolates the source of divergence.
=ProductClass= merges rates and FX into =RatesFX=, because margin is
computed on the pair together. =RiskClass= distinguishes qualifying
from non-qualifying credit, because the two attract different risk
weights and correlations.

The same framework, applied to the same trades, therefore requires a
coarser classification for one purpose and a finer one for another,
and neither enumeration is a subset of the other. The second of these
differences is examined below and proves not to be a matter of
granularity at all.

** FRTB

The Basel market risk framework classifies for regulatory capital, and
its sensitivities-based method uses six risk classes: general interest
rate risk (=GIRR=), credit spread risk (=CSR=), equity (=EQ=),
commodity (=COMM=) and FX.

Two of its features bear on the taxonomy proposed below.

Credit spread is divided three ways, into non-securitisation
(=CSR_NS=), securitisation outside the correlation trading portfolio
(=CSR_SNC=) and securitisation within it (=CSR_SC=). That is the
second framework to subdivide credit, after SIMM, and it subdivides it
along a different axis: securitisation status rather than qualifying
status. That two frameworks require two incompatible subdivisions of one class
indicates that neither subdivision belongs in the taxonomy, and that
both are properties of the exposure.

FRTB confirms this in its own record layout. A CRIF row carries
=creditQuality=, =longShortInd=, =coveredBondInd=, =trancheThickness=
and =bb_rw= as separate fields, so the framework already treats this
kind of attribute as a column rather than as a class.

FRTB treats neither bond nor inflation as a class. Inflation is a risk
factor within =GIRR=, which places FRTB with SIMM and against FpML and
ORE's risk filter. Bond is absent for the reason common to every risk
classification surveyed here, namely that a bond's sensitivity
decomposes into interest rate and credit sensitivity.

FRTB also carries two charges that are not sensitivities and so have
no place in a sensitivity taxonomy: the default risk charge (=DRC=),
itself split the same three ways as credit spread, and the residual
risk add-on (=RRAO=).

** ORE's AssetClass

ORE holds =enum class AssetClass { EQ, FX, COM, IR, INF, CR, BOND,
BOND_INDEX, PORTFOLIO_DETAILS }= in =ored/utilities/parsers.hpp=.

It attaches this to /underlyings/, not to trades. =Trade= declares
=virtual std::map<AssetClass, std::set<std::string>>
underlyingIndices(...)=, so a trade reports the indices it requires
grouped by asset class, and one trade can report under several classes
at once.

ORE defines neither the concept nor a rationale for the values. The
user guide uses the term only descriptively, as in "depending on the
underlying asset class" when explaining why a barrier option is an
=FxGenericBarrierOption= rather than a =CommodityGenericBarrierOption=,
and constrains some products so that "all underlyings must be from the
same asset class".

Three of the nine values are not asset classes in any other scheme
here. =BOND_INDEX= separates an index from a single bond;
=PORTFOLIO_DETAILS= marks portfolio-level data rather than a market.
The user guide itself counts six asset classes, listing IR, FX, EQ,
COM, CR and INF as ORE's product coverage and describing the scripted
trade module as covering "five of the six asset classes covered in
ORE". The enumeration and the prose do not agree, and nothing
reconciles them.

** ORE's RiskClass

=orea/engine/riskfilter.hpp= holds =enum class RiskClass { All,
InterestRate, Inflation, Credit, Equity, FX, Commodity }=, used to
filter sensitivities for reporting.

Its six substantive values are a strict coarsening of ORE's
=AssetClass=: bond and bond index disappear, because a bond's
sensitivity decomposes into interest rate and credit sensitivity
rather than forming a risk class of its own, and portfolio details
disappear because they carry no risk.

Inflation remains a sibling of interest rate, which places ORE with
FpML and against SIMM and FRTB on the placement of inflation.

** The Common Domain Model

CDM defines a product from three attributes, one of which is a
taxonomy that classifies it, alongside an identifier and the economic
terms. It does not define its own asset class list, and delegates
classification to a taxonomy reference.

CDM therefore treats classification as a property referencing an
external scheme rather than as an enumeration embedded in the product,
which is one structural response to the variation described above.
[[id:AB71AFF8-0B08-4610-9F12-C5A84D8AB892][Trade modeling]] covers CDM more fully.

* Analysis

** Two dimensions of variation

The seven classifications differ along two independent dimensions.
Treating the two as one accounts for much of the apparent disorder.

The first dimension is *the subject classified*. A product, a trade
underlying, a market data series and a risk sensitivity are four
distinct subjects, and a classification of one is not a classification
of another. ORE's =AssetClass= and ORE's =RiskClass= differ principally
because the first classifies underlyings and the second classifies
sensitivities.

The second dimension is *granularity*. SIMM demonstrates it within a
single framework: =RatesFX= is one node where =InterestRate= and =FX=
are two. A second apparent instance, the division of credit, is
examined below and proves to be a property rather than a finer
granularity.

** Is risk class a genuine dimension?

Whether risk class constitutes a genuine requirement, or two names for
one concept arrived at by accident, determines whether the proposal
below needs one attribute or two.

The test is cardinality. A product carries exactly one product class,
whereas a trade carries as many risk classes as it has kinds of
sensitivity.

- A fixed-rate bond is one product of class =Bond=. Its risk is
  interest rate risk and credit risk, which is two classes.
- A cross-currency swap is one product of class =InterestRate=. Its
  risk is interest rate risk in two currencies and FX risk.
- An equity option denominated in a foreign currency is one product of
  class =Equity=. Its risk includes FX risk for a book based
  elsewhere.

A one-to-one relation and a one-to-many relation differ structurally
rather than terminologically. No single attribute holds both, so a
model serving both purposes through one field is incorrect for one of
them.

Two independent sources corroborate this. SIMM holds both
classifications simultaneously, over the same trades and within one
framework, and requires both; a framework able to collapse them would
have done so. ORE encodes the same distinction in a signature:
=Trade::underlyingIndices()= returns =std::map<AssetClass,
std::set<std::string>>=, a map keyed by class, because one trade
reaches into several classes. A classification that was a property of
the trade would have required a single return value.

The dimension is therefore a requirement. The surrounding
inconsistencies are not, and constitute modeling errors: two
enumerations named =RiskClass= within one codebase holding different
values, an =AssetClass= enumeration carrying =PORTFOLIO_DETAILS=,
which satisfies no definition of an asset class given here, and a user
guide counting six values where the code holds nine.

The conclusion is narrower than it first appears. Risk classification
and product classification are distinct attributes with distinct
cardinality. They are not necessarily distinct taxonomies, and the
proposal below adopts that position: one taxonomy, referenced once by
a product and many times by a trade's risk.

** Neither subset nor superset

A subset relation holds in one case and fails in another. ORE's
=RiskClass= is a strict coarsening of ORE's =AssetClass=, so risk
reporting is served by omitting the values it does not require.

SIMM does not stand in that relation to any product classification.
Its =RiskClass= omits bond and inflation, which is coarsening, and
distinguishes qualifying from non-qualifying credit, which resembles
refinement. The following section shows the second of these to be a
property rather than a refinement, but the coarsening alone already
prevents the two from being reconciled by selection from a single
list.

** What SIMM adds is properties, not classes

SIMM appears to require two classes the other schemes lack,
=CreditQualifying= and =CreditNonQualifying=. Its =RiskType=
enumeration shows otherwise.

The SIMM risk types are =Commodity=, =CommodityVol=, =CreditNonQ=,
=CreditQ=, =CreditVol=, =CreditVolNonQ=, =Equity=, =EquityVol=, =FX=,
=FXVol=, =Inflation=, =IRCurve=, =IRVol= and =InflationVol=. These
values form a cross-product of an asset class, a greek (delta or
vega), and, on credit alone, a qualifying flag.

Qualifying status designates no market. It describes a reference
entity's eligibility under the framework's rules, is observed from the
same data as any other credit exposure, and exists because SIMM
assigns the two categories different risk weights. =RiskClass=
flattens class against flag for a purpose its own declaration states:
"the ordering here matters. It is used in indexing in to correlation
matrices". The enumeration therefore functions as an index rather than
as a taxonomy.

=RatesFX= admits the same reading, being a grouping adopted because
margin is computed on the pair together.

FRTB supports the conclusion from a different direction. It also
divides credit, but by securitisation status rather than by qualifying
status, and it carries =creditQuality=, =coveredBondInd= and
=trancheThickness= as fields on the record. A class that two
frameworks divide along two incompatible axes is better modelled as a
class carrying properties than as a class with subclasses.

This removes one of the three disagreements identified below, together
with the credit subclasses proposed in earlier drafts of the taxonomy.
[[id:327EFD17-5708-4A54-AF4B-52CF60E87C01][Risk classes]] examines the evidence in detail.

** What the disagreements actually are

Stripped of naming differences, the substantive disagreements are
three.

- *Inflation*: a top-level class for FpML and ORE's risk filter, no
  class at all for the ISDA taxonomy and SIMM, where inflation
  sensitivity is interest rate sensitivity.
- *Bond*: a class for FpML and ORE's =AssetClass=, absent from the
  ISDA taxonomy because it is not a derivative, and absent from both
  risk classifications because a bond's risk decomposes into interest
  rate and credit.
- *Credit subdivision*: apparent rather than real, and doubly so.
  SIMM splits credit by qualifying status; FRTB splits it by
  securitisation status. Two frameworks needing two different
  subdivisions of one class means neither is a subclass. Both are
  properties of the exposure.

Each disagreement reflects a use case requiring a different depth
rather than a difference of opinion about the domain.

* A proposed taxonomy

** One taxonomy, two cardinalities

The proposal separates the taxonomy from what references it.

- The *taxonomy* is one tree of asset classes, shared by every
  consumer.
- A *product* references exactly one node. That is its asset class.
- A *risk sensitivity* references exactly one node, and a trade's risk
  profile is therefore a set of nodes, one per sensitivity.

No enumeration is named risk class under this arrangement. A risk
class is the taxonomy read from a sensitivity rather than from a
product, and the difference in cardinality is carried by the reference
rather than by a second enumeration.

** The shape

One tree, with asset classes at the root and subclasses beneath, in
the ISDA taxonomy's style. Every consumer selects a /cut/ through the
tree: a set of nodes, at whatever depth it needs, such that no node is
an ancestor of another.

#+begin_example
InterestRate
  InterestRate.Inflation
ForeignExchange
Equity
Commodity
Credit
Bond
#+end_example

A consumer that regards inflation as interest rate risk cuts at
=InterestRate= and inherits its subclass. One that regards it as
separate cuts at =InterestRate= and =InterestRate.Inflation=. The
disagreement between FpML and ORE on one side and SIMM on the other
stops being a disagreement and becomes two cuts of one tree.

Inflation is the only node the surveyed material justifies nesting.
SIMM settles its placement directly: its =RiskType= declaration notes
that "the risk type inflation has to be treated as an additional,
single tenor bucket in IRCurve", so inflation sits inside the interest
rate curve rather than beside it.

** How each use case cuts it

| Use case | Cut |
|----------+-----|
| Product classification | The root: six classes |
| Market data filtering | The root, plus =InterestRate.Inflation= |
| Risk reporting, ORE style | Root minus =Bond=, plus =InterestRate.Inflation= |
| SIMM margin, product side | Root with =InterestRate= and =ForeignExchange= merged |
| SIMM margin, risk side | Root minus =Bond=, with inflation inside interest rate, plus a qualifying property on credit |
| FRTB capital, sensitivities | Root minus =Bond=, with inflation inside interest rate, plus a securitisation property on credit |
| ISDA product taxonomy | Root minus =Bond=, then deeper product levels |

All but one use case is a cut of the same tree. The exception is
SIMM's =RatesFX=, which merges two nodes, whereas a cut selects nodes
without combining them. That case requires a grouping declared over the
tree rather than a node in it.

** Cross-asset is not a class

ORE Studio currently holds =cross_asset= for market data that relates
two classes, documented as "cross-asset correlations". The proposed
taxonomy does not carry it, because a correlation between an equity
index and an FX rate is not a third kind of thing: it is a relation
between two classes.

A correlation series should therefore reference /two/ asset classes
rather than one special value. That removes the only value in the
current market data enumeration that has no counterpart in any
external scheme, and removes the need to explain it.

** Bond stays

Bond is absent from both risk classifications for a good reason: a
bond's risk decomposes into interest rate and credit risk, so it earns
no risk class of its own. It is nevertheless a product class in FpML
and an underlying class in ORE, and ORE Studio holds 11 bond products.

The tree keeps it, and the risk cuts drop it. That is precisely the
case the tree exists to handle.

** The resulting list

The analysis yields seven nodes: six at the root and one nested.

| Code | Name | Parent | Covers |
|------+------+--------+--------|
| =rates= | Interest Rate | — | Deposits, forward rate agreements, interest rate swaps and swaptions, caps and floors, and cross-currency swaps. The curve every other class discounts against |
| =inflation= | Inflation | =rates= | Zero-coupon and year-on-year inflation swaps, inflation caps and floors, and inflation-linked bonds, with the index curve and its seasonality adjustment |
| =fx= | Foreign Exchange | — | FX spot, forwards, swaps, and vanilla and exotic options. Present as a secondary exposure wherever a position is denominated outside the book's base currency |
| =equity= | Equity | — | Equity spot and forward positions, and vanilla and exotic equity options, with dividend curves and volatility surfaces |
| =commodity= | Commodity | — | Commodity forwards, swaps and options, with forward curves driven by storage cost and convenience yield rather than interest-rate parity |
| =credit= | Credit | — | Credit default swaps, single-name and index, and credit-linked notes, with spread curves, hazard rates and recovery assumptions |
| =bond= | Bond | — | Fixed and floating-rate bonds, callable and convertible bonds, and bond futures and options, with issuer spread curves |

Codes retain their present spelling, so =rates= and =fx= are kept in
preference to =InterestRate= and =ForeignExchange=. The external
spellings are recorded in the correspondence below rather than adopted,
since renaming imposes a cost on every existing consumer and resolves
no ambiguity.

*** Correspondence with external schemes

| ORE Studio | FpML | ISDA taxonomy | ORE =AssetClass= | ORE =RiskClass= | SIMM =RiskClass= | FRTB |
|------------+------+---------------+------------------+-----------------+------------------+------|
| =rates= | =InterestRate= | =InterestRate= | =IR= | =InterestRate= | =InterestRate= | =GIRR= |
| =inflation= | =Inflation= | — | =INF= | =Inflation= | within =InterestRate= | within =GIRR= |
| =fx= | =ForeignExchange= | =ForeignExchange= | =FX= | =FX= | =FX= | =FX= |
| =equity= | =Equity= | =Equity= | =EQ= | =Equity= | =Equity= | =EQ= |
| =commodity= | =Commodity= | =Commodity= | =COM= | =Commodity= | =Commodity= | =COMM= |
| =credit= | =Credit= | =Credit= | =CR= | =Credit= | =CreditQualifying=, =CreditNonQualifying= | =CSR_NS=, =CSR_SNC=, =CSR_SC= |
| =bond= | =Bond= | — | =BOND= | — | — | — |

Three cells record the findings of the analysis rather than a mapping.
Credit maps to several values in SIMM and FRTB because both carry a
property the taxonomy does not; inflation maps inside interest rate
for both; and bond has no risk counterpart because its sensitivity
decomposes.

*** What changes from the present enumeration

The current =ores::marketdata::api::domain::asset_class= holds eight
values. Two change.

=cross_asset= is removed. A correlation between two classes is a
relation between them rather than a third kind of thing, so a
correlation series references two classes. No external scheme surveyed
here carries an equivalent value.

=inflation= becomes a child of =rates= rather than a sibling. SIMM and
FRTB both place it there, and SIMM states the reason directly: the
risk type inflation is treated as an additional single tenor bucket in
=IRCurve=. Consumers that report inflation separately cut at both
nodes and are unaffected.

* Open questions

- Whether one nested node justifies a hierarchy at all. Inflation is
  the only nesting the material supports, now that the credit split
  has turned out to be a property. A flat list plus a rule that
  inflation sensitivity is reported as interest rate would serve every
  surveyed use case, at the cost of stating that rule somewhere other
  than in the taxonomy.
- Whether =SecuritiesFinancing= should be a root class. FpML carries
  it; no other scheme here does. It depends on whether repo and
  securities lending become products in ORE Studio.
- How a grouping such as SIMM's =RatesFX= is declared. It is not a
  node and not a cut, so it needs a third mechanism, or SIMM's product
  side is treated as outside the taxonomy.
- Whether a trade's risk profile is stored or derived. Storing it
  duplicates what a sensitivity run computes; deriving it means the
  classification is unavailable before pricing.
- Whether the taxonomy should adopt the ISDA taxonomy's deeper levels
  wholesale for products, rather than stopping at the asset class.
  That would make the product classification and the ISDA taxonomy the
  same object, at the cost of adopting a scheme that covers only OTC
  derivatives.
