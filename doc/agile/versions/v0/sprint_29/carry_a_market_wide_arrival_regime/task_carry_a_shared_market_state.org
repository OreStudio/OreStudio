* Acceptance

- A two-state chain with a transition rate, whose long-run occupancy matches the rate.
- One clock per run drives every feed; a feed no longer owns a clock.
- A row's rate is multiplied by the state and by the row's sensitivity.
- A scenario can pin the active state, and a per-series check still holds in it.

* Plan

Three slices, each verifiable on its own.

1. *The state.* =domain::market_regime=, a two-state continuous-time chain: a
   calm market turns active at one rate and back at another, so the share of time
   spent active is fixed by the ratio. It exposes =current()=, =multiplier()=,
   =advance(elapsed_seconds)=, =pin()= and =unpin()=. It keeps no clock of its
   own, so a caller that sleeps in slices and one that jumps to the next event use
   the same object, and a scenario pins a state without waiting for a draw.
2. *The rate.* Every arrival process gains a baseline multiplier, applied to its
   mean rate: the chain scales it, the row's sensitivity scales it again. The
   multiplier touches the baseline and not the branching ratio, because a busy
   market raises how often events arrive, not how strongly they excite.
3. *The clock.* The clock consults the market before each gap, so every feed
   follows it.

   Two corrections, both forced by scale.

   First, a single clock thread is *not* needed for the exogenous state: a state
   that is a function of time can be shared. Second, sharing it must not cost a
   lock — at ten thousand feeds a mutex on the tick path is the bottleneck the
   factor exists to avoid. So the published view is two relaxed atomics that a
   feed reads without a lock, and one owner thread advances the chain and
   publishes, a few times a second. The clock re-rates its process only when the
   multiplier changes, so a steady market costs one load and one comparison per
   tick.

   The strictly single clock becomes necessary only for the endogenous factor,
   where an event in one series must reach another, and that is the next task.

Tests follow the slices: the chain's occupancy against its own ratio, a pin that
holds however much time passes, the multiplier reaching the rate, and the clock
passing the factor to every feed.

* Result

The market is one per run and every feed follows it. The application creates it
and hands it to the adapter fleet and to every handler that builds a feed, so a
config handler, the folder verbs and the selection verbs all produce feeds that
consult the same state.

The state itself is a two-state chain whose active share follows the ratio of its
two rates, measured at 0.83 against 0.83 over 200,000 one-second slices. A
scenario pins either state, so a demonstration or a test does not wait for a draw.

One thing this task planned and then argued out of: the single clock thread. The
exogenous state needs a shared *market*, not a shared *thread* — a function of
time can be shared by several clocks if it advances with wall time, which it now
does, under a mutex, with a test that two callers advance it by the wall time they
spend rather than twice it. The single clock is required by the endogenous factor,
where an event in one series must reach another, and it belongs to the next task.
