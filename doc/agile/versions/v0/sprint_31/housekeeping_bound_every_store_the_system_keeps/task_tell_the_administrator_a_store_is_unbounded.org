* Goal

A person who can act learns that a store has no bound, or no effective one,
without opening the housekeeping screen. Housekeeping raises a notification into
=ores.inbox= of the administrator who owns the store — the system's for a
platform store, the tenant's for one a tenant writes — when a store has no
policy, when a sweep records a policy skipped or failed, and when a store passes
a size budget. The notification names the store, says what happened and links to
its page; the numbers a person needs to act stay on the page, where the plot and
the policy are.

* Acceptance

- Housekeeping adds a notification kind for each case it raises, with its message key and its arguments, as a seed row and no change to the subsystem
- The audience is the holder of the administrator permission in the store's own tenant: the system tenant for a platform store, a tenant for a store that tenant writes
- A notification names the store and links to its page, and carries no size, no growth rate and no policy period
- One sweep that skips four policies raises one notification, not four: the inbox groups by kind and subject
- Raising never fails the sweep: a delivery that fails is recorded and retried, and the sweep completes
- A store that comes back under a bound raises nothing, so the count of open housekeeping notifications follows the count of unbounded stores

* Plan

Raising a notification is already a solved problem in the inbox: a kind is a
seed row, the raise writes four rows in one transaction, and a permission
audience is resolved to accounts. What housekeeping adds is the *reading of its
own state*, which the database already states in the bounds view and the ledger.

1. *Three kinds, one per case the screen distinguishes.* =store_unbounded= for a
   store no policy names, whose policy is off, or whose action the sweep cannot
   perform; =store_sweep_failed= for a store whose last run failed, because that
   is an incident and reads differently; and =sweep_skipped= for a sweep that
   left policies unapplied, raised once per sweep rather than once per policy.
   A store takes one of the two store kinds and never both, so the open
   notifications of a tenant follow the unbounded stores of its catalogue.
2. *A scheduled pass that reads the view, raises what is true and clears what is
   no longer true.* The sweep applies policies; this pass only looks. It runs as
   the definer, because it reads across the tenants that hold stores and writes
   the inbox rows of each of them. The ledger's =ran_at= is =now()=, the
   transaction's instant, so every policy one sweep applied shares it: that is
   the key the skipped case groups by, and the reason one sweep tells a person
   once.
3. *The audience is the permission to act, in the store's tenant.* The holders of
   =scheduler::retention_policies:write= — which the catalogue's own write
   requires — resolved through the IAM's effective-permission function, so a
   wildcard role counts. A platform store is the system tenant's, and the
   catalogue states every store's tenant, so a tenant's store is told in its own
   tenant without a change to the pass.
4. *The message never carries the numbers.* It names the store and links to its
   page; the size, the rate and the period are what the page is for. The skipped
   notice names how many policies a sweep left, because that is the thing a
   person decides about, and no store has a page for it.
5. *A pass that fails never fails the sweep.* It is a job of its own, its outcome
   is the scheduler's own record, and the inbox write is the inbox's business:
   the pass records what it raised and what it resolved, and a raise that throws
   costs only its own case.

* Result

The installation now tells the person who can act, and stops telling them once
there is nothing to act on.

- *Three kinds.* =scheduler.store_unbounded= for a store no policy names, whose
  policy is off, or whose action the sweep cannot perform; =scheduler.store_sweep_failed=
  for a store whose last run failed, because that is an incident; and
  =scheduler.sweep_skipped= for a sweep that left policies unapplied. A store
  takes one of the two store kinds at a time, so the open notices follow the
  unbounded stores.
- *One pass,* =ores_scheduler_raise_housekeeping_alerts_fn()=, run hourly by
  =ores.scheduler.housekeeping_alerts=. It reads the bounds view and the ledger,
  raises what is true, clears what is no longer true, and answers one row per case
  with how many notices it raised and how many it cleared. It is the definer's
  because it reads every tenant's stores; it states the store's tenant around a
  raise and restores the job's own afterwards.
- *The audience is the holder of the permission to act* —
  =scheduler::retention_policies:write= — resolved through the IAM's
  effective-permission function, so a wildcard role counts, and restricted to a
  person, so a service account is never told. Both codes the retention policy
  handler has always asked for are seeded at last; nothing held them but a
  wildcard, which is why the editor was reachable only by the superuser.
- *One sweep, one notice:* the ledger's =ran_at= is the transaction's instant, so
  the rows one sweep wrote share it, and the skipped notice groups by it. Its
  arguments state how many policies were left, which is the fact a person decides
  about, and no store has a page for it.
- *The notice links to the store's page.* The inbox's bell now appends the id for
  =/operations/housekeeping=, so a notice opens the store it names rather than the
  list.
- *The knowledge page* that owns the subject states the pass, the kinds and why a
  notice carries no numbers.

Proved by 15 pgTAP assertions on a recreated database — one notice per unbounded
store, one person told, no size in the arguments, no second notice on a second
pass, a store that returns under a bound cleared, and one sweep's skips told once
and cleared by a later sweep — and by the job firing in the running fleet.
