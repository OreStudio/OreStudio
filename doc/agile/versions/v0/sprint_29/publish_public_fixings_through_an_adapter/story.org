* Goal

A synthetic feed that starts takes the most recent rate that a central bank or public body published, and every publisher it can draw on is a market data provider like any other, with its terms accepted, its vendor ids mapped, and its fixings stored for good.

* Acceptance

- A durable tick class keeps the FX fixings with no age gate and no expiry, in a table of its own, and a backfill in order is stored.
- Every provider carries its terms, and no public provider is fetched until the system administrator accepts them.
- The public sources adapter speaks the adapter contract, fetches through Frankfurter one provider at a time, backfills the window on start and polls daily.
- The catalogue holds every daily Frankfurter provider whose terms are read and allow storage, so the main pairs are covered by several providers.
- A reader returns the latest fixing of an item on or before a date, and a window, each with its provider and time.
- A synthetic feed starts from the latest fixing of its best-ranked item, and the screen shows the provider and the time of the value.
- No vintage remains: a generator takes its level and volatility only from its ranked public fixing items, a fixed level is a stated fallback, and the notes, the seeds and the default no longer describe a vintage.
- The calibration screen states, for one generator, the ranked items, the item and provider chosen, the value and time taken, the measured volatility over the window, and why a run kept its fixed level.
- Calibration is per asset class, with one policy and one list per class behind a class switch.
- The class list is the landing and is just a list: a policy line with a link, the counts, the filters and every feed with its start and state. Nothing on it selects a subset for calibration, because every feed is calibrated.
- A feed opens its detail with two tabs: *Feed record* for its start, candidates, measures and exceptions, and *Flow* for how it resolved. The class policy is its own class-level screen, not a feed tab.
- Calibration needs no configuration: with no policy the defaults (provider catalogue order, a 90-day window, inverse allowed, keep the fixed level) calibrate every feed. A class policy and a feed's pin are optional refinements.
- A class policy sets the window, the inverse rule, the provider order and the fallback. A feed derives its candidates from the policy or the defaults and its pair; it pins an item or a window only as a stated exception. A feed may also name its items explicitly, but no feed needs to.
- A recalculation of a class resolves every feed and records each start, with the coverage of resolved, fallback and blocked feeds.
- The calibration shows the measures behind a start — realised volatility over the window, annualised drift, daily range, sample coverage, jump count, fixing age and provider dispersion — and says that only the volatility changes the calibration today, the rest being recorded for a later policy.

* Decisions

- A public source is an adapter that publishes ticks, not a DQ dataset (2026-10-28). DQ is for canned data, a snapshot that stays fixed. A public source publishes every day, and a feed that starts again should take the newest value. DQ keeps the canned vintages.
- Each publisher is a provider of kind =reference=, with a source of kind =live=. Frankfurter is an access route, a fetcher, and not a provider (2026-10-28).
- The item is an FX fixing, =FX-SOURCE-CCY1-CCY2=, so the publisher is part of the identity and the durable rule, keyed by instrument type, leaves live spot alone (2026-10-28).
- One adapter serves every public source, with Frankfurter as its first fetcher (2026-10-28).
- The durable fixings are kept for good, in a table of their own (2026-10-28).
- The catalogue starts wide: every daily Frankfurter provider whose terms allow storage, so each main pair has several providers from the first day (2026-10-28).
- Calibration belongs to the market, not to each feed (2026-10-31). With nothing configured, the defaults calibrate every feed; a policy per asset class and a pin on one feed are optional refinements. A list authored on every feed does not scale and hides the market's actual coverage, so no feed needs one, though a feed may still state one as an exception.
- The measures are recorded, and only volatility calibrates today (2026-10-31). Realised volatility scales the mixture; drift, jumps, dispersion, freshness and coverage are visible and stored for a later policy that learns from them. A measure never blocks a start.
- Vintage is decommissioned, and public fixings are the only calibration (2026-10-31). The old path read an observation from a stored vintage; it is gone from the code. The notes, the seeds and the config default follow. A pair with no fixing keeps a fixed level, and the screen says so: that is a stated fallback, not a second source.
- It supersedes the task [[id:74F33C8C-AF28-4A6E-812A-A2D443A77E40][Load published quotes from the public sources]], whose five new tables are not built. The design and its history are in [[id:F4BBEF34-D84C-4AE6-A3AA-DC47C3EE06EE][Public sources publish daily fixings through an adapter]].

* Out of scope

- The curve sources: the US Treasury par curve, SOFR, SONIA and €STR. They are later fetchers of the same adapter.
- The monthly, weekly and quarterly providers (the BIS, Japan Customs, the US Treasury reporting rate), until the class policy says how stale such a fixing may be.
- A Frankfurter provider with no terms page of its own, until its own site is read.
