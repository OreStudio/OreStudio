* Goal

One mechanism dates every FX expiry: the pair's roll, month-end rule and calendars applied by Calendar::advance. The ladder publishes the date, the calibration reads it and refuses a tenor the ladder does not carry, and the surface feed dates its quotes with the same rule, so a producer and a consumer cannot disagree.

* Acceptance

- The rule lives in quant and is tested in isolation: a weekend rolls, a holiday moves, month-end snaps only when the pair says it does, and an unread rule refuses.
- The forward ladder dates every tenor through the rule and keeps publishing the same ladder it did.
- The calibration reads the ladder's value date for each tenor and refuses one the ladder does not carry, naming the expiry and the horizon.
- The surface feed dates its expiries through the same rule and refuses to start without the pair's convention.
- One reader builds the rule from refdata, so a surface and a ladder cannot read the pair's convention differently.
- A provisioning run completes publish_party_surfaces with every surface converged.

* Plan

1. State the rule once, in quant: =fx_expiry_date_rule= holds the pair's roll, month-end flag and
   calendars and answers the date a period values on and the nth business day after a date, by
   calling =calendar_arithmetic::advance=. A rule built without a calendar refuses to date
   anything, so a caller that skipped refdata is told.
2. Read the rule once: =read_pair_expiry_date_rule= in refdata.core reads the pair's convention
   and calendars and returns the rule with the calendar codes. Both the ladder and the surface
   feed call it, so neither reads the pair's convention on its own.
3. Move the call sites in one wave: =build_ladder_dates= takes the rule, the ladder service builds
   it with the reader (the duplicated joint-calendar builder and convention parse leave the
   service), and the surface feed's build input carries it; =fx_vol_expiry_markets= dates each
   expiry with it and refuses a rule with no calendar.
4. Test in the two layers the story asks for: isolation on the rule, then the ladder's dates, the
   feed's tick and the round trip through the calibration.
5. Prove it where it broke: a provisioning run of a new tenant must complete
   =publish_party_surfaces=.

Decisions taken:

- The unit of the mechanism is a *rule value*, not a free function. It carries the convention
  inputs, so there is no call site that can pass a different roll, month-end flag or calendar.
- The rule is read once when a feed or a ladder starts and held by value. A tick must not depend
  on a store that may have moved since.
- A surface whose pair has no convention refuses to start, naming the pair, rather than quoting
  on whatever date the arithmetic lands on. A test tenant is provisioned without conventions, so
  the synthetic service fixture now states one.

* Result

The expiry date is dated one way. =fx_expiry_date_rule= is the only mechanism, =read_pair_expiry_date_rule= is its
only reader, and three call sites use it: the forward ladder that publishes the date, the
calibration that reads the published date and refuses a tenor the ladder does not carry, and the
synthetic surface feed that strikes its quotes at it.

The 1W refusal that started this ("the row dated 2026-11-17 is before the spot date 2026-11-18")
is gone: a fresh tenant provisions end to end and publishes 40 surfaces, all converged and
arbitrage-free.

Still open, and captured rather than fixed:

- [[id:002F8F0F-2429-4838-864D-5B29DB571CBF][A surface the checks refuse is withheld, so
  downstream has no numbers]] -- the policy that would publish a degraded surface with its
  findings instead of withholding it. The refusal this task made explicit (a tenor the ladder does
  not carry) is its first case.
- The withdrawn =stop= of the environment's smoke run: =compass smoke ticks= refuses a tenant code
  that already exists, so it cannot be rerun against an existing environment. The provisioning
  proof above used a new tenant code instead.
