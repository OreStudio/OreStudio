* Goal

ACME's books hold foreign exchange trades: forwards, swaps, vanilla, barrier and digital options, touch options, Asian options, variance swaps and the rest of the sixteen routed types. The family carries 25% of the population. Every trade names two currencies, so the planner draws a pair whose spot and forward points the tenant holds.

* Acceptance

- Every FX trade type the catalogue routes books a trade.
- The ten unrouted types are routed or named out of scope.
- The class share of the population matches its weight within tolerance, and every routed type appears at least once.
- The family exports as the ORE portfolio the engine reads, and reverse-maps without loss.

* Plan

** What the tree already holds

- *The routing.* Sixteen types reach seven tables: =fx_forward_instrument=
  (FxForward, FxSwap), =fx_vanilla_option_instrument= (FxOption),
  =fx_digital_option_instrument= (FxDigitalOption, FxTouchOption,
  FxDoubleTouchOption, FxDigitalBarrierOption),
  =fx_asian_forward_instrument= (FxAverageForward, FxTaRF),
  =fx_barrier_option_instrument= (FxBarrierOption, FxDoubleBarrierOption,
  FxEuropeanBarrierOption, FxGenericBarrierOption, FxKIKOBarrierOption),
  =fx_variance_swap_instrument= (FxVarianceSwap) and
  =fx_accumulator_instrument= (FxAccumulator).
- *The writer, service side.* =write_instrument_batch= writes every one of those
  arrays, so no trading service change is needed.
- *The vocabulary.* The currency set is seeded and holds 179 currency pairs,
  each with its convention calendars.

** How FX differs from rates, and why that matters

A rate trade was one header plus one fact row plus legs, and the store rebuilt
it from the pair. An FX row is the whole instrument: the routed table carries
the trade, the parties, the currencies and the economics in one row, so there is
no fact row to forget. The lesson the rate family learned the hard way does not
repeat here, and the writer is simpler for it.

That simplicity costs variety instead. The sixteen types fall into three
idioms, and a payload has to state all three:

| Idiom | Types | What it states |
|-------+-------+----------------|
| bought and sold | FxForward, FxSwap, FxOption, the five barrier options | a bought currency and amount against a sold one, a date, and for the options a type and an exercise style |
| digital | FxDigitalOption, the touch options, the digital barrier | a foreign and a domestic currency, a payoff currency and amount, a strike, a side, and barriers where the type has them |
| index and notional | FxAverageForward, FxTaRF, FxVarianceSwap, FxAccumulator | an underlying index, a reference notional, a fixing or strike, a side, and the barrier an accumulator knocks out on |

** Decisions

- *D1 — one payload, three idioms.* =fx_terms= carries the union of the fields
  the seven tables state, most of them optional, in the way =commodity_terms=
  already does for its eight shapes. The writer dispatches on the trade type to
  the table the router names and fills that table's fields. Splitting the
  payload into three variants would put the routing decision in the type system
  and then repeat it in the writer anyway.
- *D2 — the pair comes from the catalogue.* A pair is the tenant's reference
  data, not this configuration's, so the scope carries the pairs the tenant
  holds and the planner draws from them. It prefers the pairs that name the
  book's own currency, because a desk books what it quotes; a tenant holding
  none of those falls back to the whole set rather than refusing.
- *D3 — every shape gets an export case.* The rate family shipped one export
  test against its simplest product and two of its products exported wrong
  data. Here the seven tables get seven cases, and the review of this task does
  not begin until they exist.

** Steps

1. *The payload.* =fx_terms= and its variant entry, plus the pair list on the
   scope.
2. *The planner.* =fx_trade_planner= registered under =fx=, cycling the sixteen
   routed types, drawing the pair, the dates and the economics that type needs.
3. *The writer.* One arm per routed table, filling the fields the table states
   and no others.
4. *The profile.* A config for an entity whose books quote FX, so a provisioning
   run produces the family.
5. *The proof.* Seven export cases, one per table, each asserting the document
   names the instrument and its economics; then a population run measuring the
   type coverage and the share.
6. *The record.* Measurements, Result, and the gaps this task cannot close.
