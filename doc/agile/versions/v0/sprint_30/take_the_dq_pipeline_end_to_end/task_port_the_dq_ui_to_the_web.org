This page documents a [[id:31C868B9-306E-4282-BA8C-07E647021B34][task]] in the [[id:7D2BBBBC-36A3-4BFB-9673-0F9005991D94][Take the DQ pipeline end to end, from a seeded artefact table to a published system]] story. It captures the goal, current status, acceptance, and any notes or results.

* Goal

Give DQ a better experience in ores.web than the Qt client had, designed from the
journeys a person actually runs rather than from the screens Qt happened to
ship.

The Qt area is input, not a specification: =ores.qt/dq= holds 88 translation
units, 27 screens (18 entity detail dialogs, 9 history dialogs) and a publishing
surface (Librarian window, bundle wizard, publish dialog). None of that is a
requirement in itself. A screen may answer one journey or several, and a journey
may need a screen that has no Qt ancestor at all.

The refdata work already shows the method: 63 Qt reference-data entities became
three web shapes — 12 record screens, 24 rows of one classification screen and 9
kinds of one convention screen — because the entities were grouped by the
journey that uses them, not by their Qt window. See
[[id:C82ED7AD-7E13-46FA-B7E9-C4E66E823478][Qt to web reference data matrix]] for
that precedent and its field tables.

* Acceptance

- The DQ journeys are written as journey documents under
  =doc/knowledge/journeys/dq/=, in the shape
  [[id:CA94C762-B0C8-40E7-85CE-D4C6355023CC][the user journey document type]]
  sets out, and judged before anything is designed. They cover at least:
  seeing whether the system's reference data is complete, publishing a dataset,
  publishing a bundle, correcting one artefact, and finding which trade a
  published value belongs to.
- Each journey names the screen that answers it. The mapping is many-to-many
  where that is honest, and
  [[id:CA94C762-B0C8-40E7-85CE-D4C6355023CC][one screen, not one per entity]]
  is the default, not the exception.
- Prototypes are on main under =doc/prototypes/dq/=, as plain HTML, CSS and
  JavaScript with the design tokens copied from the product, listed on the group
  page, with a review recorded. No React prototype and no route in ores.web.
- The prototypes are signed off before a screen is built from the shared parts.
- The built screens follow the Record screen standard for records, and group the
  maintenance vocabularies the way the refdata work grouped its 63 entities into
  three shapes.
- Publish state and the publish action are reachable from the journey, not
  hidden behind a wizard only an administrator knows about.
- A person who has never seen the Qt client can complete every journey.

* Plan

1. Read the Qt area as evidence of what the domain holds, not as a screen list.
2. Write the journeys as documents, and judge them before designing anything.
3. Prototype the screens that answer them, as static pages under
   =doc/prototypes/dq/=, and get the prototypes signed off. The inventory check
   (=build/scripts/check_prototype_inventory.py=) keeps the group page honest.
4. Build from the shared parts, following
   [[id:61C6B0A5-03A5-4424-8EA3-EE7E587629FD][Take a topic through the screen
   pipeline]].
5. Prove each journey on a fresh database.

* Notes

Publish state is the point of the screens. The Qt client showed it because the
Librarian was where publishing happened; ores.web has neither the screens nor the
action today, which is why an empty catalogue after a recreate went unnoticed.

The prototype stays on main once accepted
([[id:708C2462-C1F8-455B-B091-9D9690FEEAAB][keep journey prototypes on main]]):
it is the design record, and the
product is written afterwards from the shared components, never from the
prototype's markup.

This task deliberately does not aim at parity. The measure is whether a person
can complete the journeys with less friction than the Qt client imposed, and
whether the vocabulary screens are fewer and clearer than the 18 detail dialogs
they would otherwise become.
