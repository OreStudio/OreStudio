* Summary

The hand adjustment prototype argues that a hand adjustment is a
first-class point with an actor and a reason, and that it must read as a
point the market did not send. It draws the candidate, the forms that
apply a correction, and the state of every correction at the foot, so the
distinction can be judged on the screen that has to make it.

It is throwaway. It is not production code, it reads no service, and every
value on it is mock data. It exists because the
[[id:478EFA03-AB47-4286-A3CE-583C7438632D][journey]] settles what a correction
does and why it carries a reason; nothing yet settles how a person, reading
the candidate, tells a hand point from a point the feed sent.

Open the [[file:adjust-a-curve-or-surface-by-hand/][hand adjustment]].

* Detail

** The question

The journey gives one screen: the candidate and its points at the top, the
correction forms in the middle, the state of every correction at the foot.
The open question is what a hand point *is* on that screen. The journey
records the defect it is written against: a point carries no actor and no
marker that a person wrote it, so a feed write is indistinguishable from a
hand value. The prototype asks whether a point can carry that fact — who
keyed it, why, and when — and what survives when the feed ticks again.

The group's answer, and the one this page argues, is that the adjustment is
a point in its own right, not a shadow of the observation it replaces: it
has an actor, a reason and a time, and it stands until it is cleared.

** The variants

Switch with the *Apply* buttons in the bottom bar, or =?variant=. The
variant is how the adjustment is applied, which is the journey's central
design question.

| Variant | Name      | The structural argument |
|---------+-----------+-------------------------|
| =a=     | one node  | *Set manual point — edit one node*: one tenor, one value, one reason. The candidate table reads each point as *as fed* or *override*. The narrowest act, and the one the journey names first. |
| =b=     | spread    | *Apply spread overlay — over a segment*: a kind (*Parallel* or *Twist*), the segment it applies to (=2Y-10Y=, =5Y-30Y=, or all nodes), a shift and a reason. The candidate table gains an *Overlay* column, and the curve chart bands the segment. One act moves many points without touching their levels. |
| =c=     | paste     | *Paste a set of nodes*: a block of =tenor value= lines, and one reason for the whole block. The candidate table gains a *Pasted* column, and the chart bands =1Y-10Y=. Several manual points in one act. |

The variants disagree about the unit of correction, not about colour. A
corrects one coordinate. B corrects a segment by a delta, so the feed
levels survive. C brings levels from outside the screen.

** The states

Switch with the *Corrections* buttons, or =?state=. The state is the mock
market the screen reads against.

| State       | What it shows |
|-------------+---------------|
| =none=      | No manual point. The feed values stand and nothing is published. |
| =some=      | =5Y= and =10Y= keyed by =a.reyes= (*Desk mark*, *Thin market*), and a *Twist* overlay over =2Y-10Y= keyed by =m.oduya=. |
| =overwrite= | The =5Y= manual point, and a feed tick at =14:07= that tried to write =3.8825= over it. The point shows the feed's attempt beside it, marked as the feed's write, and the corrections table states that the hand value is held. |

The *Survives a feed write* column is the argument in one cell. In =none=
and =some= it reads *no precedence rule*, because none exists and the
screen will not invent one. In =overwrite= it reads *held, hand wins until
cleared*, the candidate the journey states.

** Driving it

The forms are live, but no button writes. Each correction button reports
that it is inert, with the subject it is missing, in the strip at the top.
The variant and state buttons rewrite the query string, so any combination
is a URL. The curve chart is drawn only for *b* and *c*.

** What is fake

Everything. The eight feed pillars, their values, roles, sources and times;
the manual points; the overlay; the actors, reasons and clocks are
constants in the page, and the candidate's derivation is a hard-coded
config id and version. There is no server, no session and no build step,
and nothing is published or stored.

** Where the code lives

=doc/prototypes/market_data/adjust-a-curve-or-surface-by-hand/=. One file,
=index.html=, holding its markup, styles and script, with its plotting
helper =PC=, and no framework and no build step. The site copies the
directory as it stands and adds nothing to it.

** The verdict

The group accepted the argument rather than a layout: a hand adjustment is
a first-class point with an actor and a reason, and the screen must mark it
so no reader mistakes it for a point the market sent. The point carries a
kind, an actor, a reason and a time, and it is drawn apart from the feed's
points — the amber marker in the chart, the *manual* tag in the table. A
feed write against it reads as the feed's attempt, not a replacement.

What stayed on the page as the record: the three variants and the three
states, kept because the argument must hold in all six, and the surface
panel, kept as the record that a surface was asked about but unanswered.

** What it does not settle

Which variant applies a correction. One node, spread and paste are drawn
and none is chosen; the choice belongs to the operations the server will
carry.

The precedence rule. The page states the candidate — a manual point wins
for its coordinate until it is cleared — and the overwrite state beside it,
but no rule is agreed.

How a surface point is addressed. There is no strike vocabulary and no
surface coordinate, so the strike picker is disabled and the mock surface
holds one value.

Where the actor, reason and time live, and what a clear leaves behind. The
journey leaves open whether they sit on =market_observation= or an
override entity of its own; there is no clear operation and no history
provider, so a removed correction leaves no trail.
