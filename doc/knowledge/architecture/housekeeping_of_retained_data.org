#+title: Housekeeping of retained data
#+description: Every retained store is made subject to a retention policy, applied by one executor and recorded as telemetry.
#+type: knowledge
#+level: cross
#+filetags: :housekeeping:retention:storage:scheduler:architecture:
#+created: 2026-11-24
#+updated: 2026-11-24

* Summary

A store is housekept when three parts hold together: a retention policy declares
what the store keeps and for how long, an executor applies the policy, and a
telemetry record states what the executor did. A policy with no executor leaves
the store growing, and an executor with no telemetry cannot be shown to have run.
Both failures are present in the platform today. The second is the commoner: the
schema installs a retention policy only where the storage engine reports the
licensed edition, and reports the interval without installing anything where it
does not. The page gives the shape of a retention policy, the actions an executor
takes, the path an executor follows where the engine will not apply a policy
itself, and the telemetry a policy reports.

* Detail

** The three parts

| Part | Question it answers | What its absence produces |
|------+---------------------+---------------------------|
| Retention policy | what is kept, and for how long | a constant in code, invisible to the operator and changed only by a rebuild |
| Executor | who applies the policy | rows, objects and directories that accumulate while the policy reads as satisfied |
| Telemetry | whether it ran, and what it freed | no way to tell a working policy from an inert one |

** The policy is a record

A retention policy names its target, the action to take on it, the retention
period or threshold past which the action applies, the cadence at which it is
considered, whether it is enabled, and the fallback to take where the engine will
not act. The policy is stored as data: a period changes without a schema rebuild,
and one screen shows every policy beside the size of what it governs.

** The actions

Four actions treat a store by removing what it no longer needs:

- drop the chunks of a time-partitioned table that lie beyond the retention
  period;
- delete the rows of an ordinary table that lie beyond the retention period;
- expire the objects under a key prefix, which is the object store's lifecycle
  rule;
- release a working directory once what it produced is stored elsewhere.

Two further treatments hold a store without removing anything:

- overwrite a counter instead of appending a new version of it;
- recompute derived data instead of keeping a copy per owner.

The action is a property of the class of data, not of the table. Which class
takes which action is the subject of
[[id:21D539CD-AF3B-4479-81D5-F195756C064F][Classification of retained data]].

** One executor

A single scheduled job reads the policies and performs their actions, taking its
cadence from the configured setting that the platform's other sweeps already use
([[id:DFC57D4F-9AB2-4428-BDEC-BF44D106A75D][Scheduler]]). One executor serves
every class because the classes differ in what they hold and in when their
contents stop being useful, not in how a sweep runs. The established example is
[[id:F06175E8-CA73-4A21-BEC5-898D0A388E17][Approval request expiry]], whose
sweep a service registers at runtime from a system setting.

** The fallback path

A time-series engine applies a retention policy as a background job, and the job
exists only where the engine's licensed edition includes it. Where that edition
is absent the policy is never created and the declaration reads as satisfied. The
executor therefore performs the action itself wherever the engine will not, and
records which path ran, so that one declaration is honoured on either edition and
the difference is visible rather than inferred. This is the gap recorded for the
tick store in
[[id:A79C25FA-9F70-453B-A52E-780B4402ABAA][Bounded retention in the tick store]],
generalised to every class.

** The telemetry

Each policy reports, per sweep, the size of its target before and after, the
rows, chunks or objects removed, the time taken and the outcome. The measured
size over time yields a growth rate, which is the quantity a capacity decision
needs and the one an operator cannot obtain from a single reading.

The telemetry sits beside the declaration rather than in a separate console: a
policy's row shows what it governs and what the last sweep freed, together, so
that a policy which is enabled and freeing nothing is visible as such. The size
comes from the database's catalogues for tables and chunks, from the object
store's listing for key prefixes, and from a directory walk for the caches and
working directories a node holds.
