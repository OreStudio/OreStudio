* Goal

When ticks arrive slowly, the loop prepares, copies and republishes each batch in the worker, as the serial loop did. When ticks arrive fast, the three stages run as they do now. A slow stream gets the wait of the serial loop and a burst keeps its throughput.

Measured in the parent task (evidence file =overlap_age_at_republish_output.txt=, 5 runs at a load of about 6 on 8 CPUs): a stream of 500 ticks, one every 2 ms, was republished with a median age of 6.0 ms by the serial loop and 13.9 ms by the pipelined one, and a p99 of 16.9 ms against 42.1 ms. The cause is a guess: each handoff wakes a thread that waits for a free CPU. A burst shows the opposite, with a p99 age of 373 ms against 478 ms.

* Acceptance

- When the loop is idle, the worker runs the three stages of a batch itself, in order, and the stream test shows a median age within 2 ms of the serial loop.
- The order of the batches stays the one of arrival. A batch is not republished before a batch that came before it, and a test says so while a burst and a slow stream alternate.
- The burst keeps a median above 110,000 rows per second through NATS over ten runs.
- A failed copy still loses no tick and stores none twice, and the existing test passes.

* Plan

1. The worker counts the batches it handed to the stages that the third stage has not finished.
2. A batch of 64 ticks or fewer, taken when that count is zero, is prepared, copied and republished
   by the worker. Only the worker adds to the count, so a zero stays zero until the worker adds.
3. Any other batch goes through the stages, as before.
4. A test sends bursts and slow streams in turn, with the number of each tick as its value, and
   checks that the republish keeps the order.
5. The age test of the parent task measures the result.

* Notes

- In one of the five pairs the pipelined loop gave fewer rows per second than the serial loop
  (108,081 against 116,142 at a load of 6.4). One of the ten runs through NATS gave 96,100.
- The limit of 64 ticks is a first value. A batch above it goes through the stages. No run here
  tried another limit.
- The order test is =a_small_batch_does_not_pass_the_batches_that_are_in_the_stages=. A writer
  holds the copy of a large batch at a gate, and the test sends one tick. It checks that the tick
  is not republished while the large batch is held, and that the order holds after the gate
  opens. The writer records the thread of each copy, so the test also shows that the small
  batches were written by the worker and the large batch by the copy stage. With the check of
  =in_flight_= removed, the test failed in 10 of 10 runs. With it, the test passed in 10 of 10.
- The first version of the test, which sent bursts and slow streams in turn, failed in 3 of 5
  runs with the check removed, so it was not a reliable guard. The gate replaced it.
- At a load of 16 to 43 the test of a failed copy from #3623 failed in 3 of 10 runs with the quiet
  path turned off and in about 3 of 9 with it on. That is not caused by this task. The task
  =Wait on the backoff in the failed copy test= holds it.

* Result

A slow stream waits as long as in the serial loop again. The median age of a tick at republish is 5.6 ms in both loops, against 13.9 ms in the pipelined loop before. A burst keeps its gain: the median through NATS is 135,200 rows per second over ten runs, and the age of a burst at republish is lower than in the serial loop. A batch that the worker takes while the stages are idle and that has 64 ticks or fewer skips the handoffs. The republish keeps the order of the ticks.
