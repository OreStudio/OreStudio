#+title: Book moves
#+description: Moving a trade from one book to another: which moves the regulatory and accounting rules allow, why some systems cannot move a trade at all, how the ledger sees a move, and what follows for the model.
#+type: knowledge
#+level: cross
#+filetags: :knowledge:domain:finance:trading:book:
#+created: 2026-10-02
#+updated: 2026-10-02

* Summary

A *book move* changes the book a trade is booked in. Whether a trade
can be moved at all is often described as an accounting impossibility,
but no accounting standard or regulation forbids moving a trade between
two books that share a legal entity, a branch, a regulatory book and an
accounting classification. The restrictions apply when a move would
cross one of those four lines.

That gives one rule:

#+begin_quote
A trade moves between two books by amending its booking when the two
books have the same legal entity, branch, regulatory book and
accounting classification. Any other change of book is not a move: the
trade is closed out and a new trade is booked, or the risk crosses by
internal risk transfer trades.
#+end_quote

The accounting constraint people remember is real, but it governs the
ledger postings, not the trade. Up to the move the trade's P&L belongs
to the source book and after it to the destination, so the ledger
closes the position in one book and opens it in the other at fair
value. A system that keeps only the current book on the trade can show
that only as a back-out and a rebooking, which is why a move can look
impossible without new trades.

* Detail

** What the rules forbid

| Boundary crossed | Rule | Effect on a move |
|------------------+------+------------------|
| Legal entity | A trade sits on one entity's balance sheet | Not a move. The trade is closed out and rebooked, or novated |
| Branch | The analysis notes: where the target book has a different owner, such as another branch, "a direct move is not allowed" and the deal is closed out and rebooked with an explicit link | Treated as a change of legal entity |
| Trading book and banking book | Basel RBC25.14-16: reassignment only "in extraordinary circumstances", such as "the permanent closure of trading desks"; approved by senior management and the supervisor, documented, publicly disclosed and irrevocable; RBC25.15 imposes any capital benefit as a Pillar 1 surcharge. Risk otherwise crosses only as internal risk transfer trades, RBC25.19-25 | Not a move. Risk crosses through new trades |
| Accounting classification | IFRS 9 paragraph 4.4.1: financial assets are reclassified only when the business model for managing them changes, prospectively. Derivatives are measured at fair value through profit or loss and cannot be reclassified | Not a move, except a firm-wide change of business model |
| Trading desk | Basel MAR12.4(a)(iii): each trading account is assigned to a single desk. RBC25.28: internal risk transfers between desks receive capital recognition. Nothing forbids reassigning a position between desks | Allowed. Desk-level P&L and backtesting see the change |
| Two books with the same legal entity, branch, regulatory book and classification | No rule restricts it | Allowed |

The trading-desk row is the one that surprises. A move between two
desks of the same entity and classification is permitted; the desk's
P&L attribution and backtesting see the position arrive or leave, and
the move must not be made to improve the outcome of those tests.
[[id:36B8F463-121F-42D6-8048-3C62C21AF557][FRTB trading book / banking book boundary]] covers the regulatory
classification itself.

** How the systems the analysis notes describe treat a move

The analysis notes on books record what the front office expects:

- A deal can change book freely when the move keeps it within the same
  logical grouping — same legal entity, same branch — so that there is
  no legal impact, and the system should support that automatically.
- A move to a book with a different owner goes through a close-out and
  a rebooking, linked.
- A move should be a single transaction pairing a deal that backs out
  the original trade with the new trade in the destination book.
- Moves can be made in bulk, "with or without historic cash flow
  moves", and an erroneous move is reversed by an explicit /Undo Book
  Move/.

The same notes list book moves first in the priority order of /reasons
for update/ — the reasons a trade /version/ records — and the notes on
confirmations list them among the internal changes that are "not the
customer's business". In those systems an allowed move is therefore an
internal amendment of the same trade: the trade keeps its identity, its
internal version moves, and its external version does not.

The back-out and the new trade belong to the ledger side of that
amendment. They are the postings that close the position in the source
book and open it in the destination, written in one transaction so that
no report sees the position in neither book or in both.

** Why some systems cannot move a trade

Three properties, common in older systems, make every move look like an
accounting impossibility:

1. /The sub-ledger is keyed by book./ Accruals, realised P&L and cash
   are held per book, so moving the trade without moving them leaves
   the source book holding a balance for a trade it no longer has.
2. /Only the current book is kept./ The analysis notes call book moves
   a source of P&L breaks that are hard to explain, because "only the
   current book ID is reported": a P&L explain has to find the previous
   day's record to learn where the trade was.
3. /Books are mapped to ledger entities./ Where books map to different
   ledger entities or general ledger accounts, a move that changes the
   mapping changes the balance sheet, and is genuinely a new trade.

The third is the real restriction, and the boundary table states it.
The first two are limits of the system, not of the domain: a booking
that keeps its history, and a move that carries its own valuation,
remove them.

** Leaving the sandbox is not a move

A trade in a virtual book of a [[id:4AB0BC63-D73A-4FC3-B9AF-16C1BB90653F][sandbox]] is a draft, a test or a
hypothetical, and it holds no position. It leaves its sandbox through
the lifecycle — a draft is executed into a real book, and a hypothetical
is dealt as a new trade — not through a book move. A live trade never
enters a virtual book.

** Moving risk without moving a trade

Two mechanisms move risk rather than trades, and neither is a book
move:

- /Intra-book hedging./ A trader moves an amount of risk between two
  books they manage by booking a deal in the source book and an
  offsetting deal with the inverse direction in the destination book,
  both against an internal counterparty. The two deals are booked and
  tracked together.
- /Risk routing./ Routing rules split and aggregate client trades to
  reach the desk that manages the risk, through wash books and
  back-to-backs. See [[id:9E364BEF-12EE-42F8-902C-B1E68A3095B8][Wash books and risk routing]].

** What follows for the model

This section is analysis rather than reported practice.

- /A book carries the four attributes./ Legal entity, branch,
  regulatory book (trading or banking) and accounting classification
  are attributes of the book, not of the trade.
- /An allowed move amends the booking./ When the source and destination
  books agree on all four, the move is a new version of the trade's
  booking, with /book move/ as its reason. The trade keeps its id, its
  internal version moves and its external version does not.
- /Any other change of book is refused as a move./ The schema can
  enforce it: a booking change whose books differ on any of the four
  attributes is rejected, and the change must be made as a close-out
  and rebooking linked by a [[id:04F5EFF1-D4B5-4BD0-8CBC-4EF5DDF97921][book move link]], or as
  internal risk transfer trades.
- /The move carries its valuation./ The move records the trade's fair
  value at the moment of the move, and whether historic cash flows move
  with it, so the ledger can post the close in the source book and the
  open in the destination without new trades.
- /The booking keeps its history./ Because the booking is versioned in
  time, the book a trade sat in on any date is a plain read as of that
  date. That removes the P&L break the analysis notes describe.
- /Undo is a version./ Undoing a move is a further version of the
  booking that restores the source book, with its own reason; the
  erroneous version stays in the record.
- /Bulk moves are one trade group./ A bulk move of many trades is one
  operation over a set of trades. The trades it moved are one
  [[id:2AF58405-4F3D-4B74-AAD0-6A97E7DA215F][trade group]], and the run records who moved them, when,
  why, and whether historic cash flows moved.
