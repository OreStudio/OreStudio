What the comparison teaches

The two curves answer different questions. One says what a secured overnight deposit earns; the other says what the market expects an unsecured three-month rate to be.

Underlying
AONIA averages secured overnight transactions. LIBOR 3M was a survey of unsecured interbank term borrowing, so it carries bank credit and liquidity.
How it fixes
Overnight fixes backwards: each day's rate is known after the day. Term fixes forwards: the 3M rate is quoted today for a period that starts today.
What it is for
Discounting: the overnight curve is the risk-free leg for present-valuing cash flows. Projection: the term curve forecasts the rate a floating leg will pay.
Why the shape differs
The gap is not only expectation. A term curve adds a term premium and the credit of the panel that quotes it; a compounded overnight curve does not.
A curve is never “the” rate. Reading two together is the lesson: which one discounts, which one projects, and what the gap is made of.

The data fixed

Twelve pillars, the ladder the system builds on. The AONIA column sits around its configured seed level of 4.30%; the LIBOR column is fixed for the design. Every number is a constant in this page — nothing is read from the server.
