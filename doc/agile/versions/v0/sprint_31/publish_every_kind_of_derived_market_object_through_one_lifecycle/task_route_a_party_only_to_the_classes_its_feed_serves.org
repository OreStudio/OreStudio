* Goal

A party that subscribes to a provider is routed to exactly the asset classes the
feed it reaches actually serves, so a subscription step never asks for a route the
store refuses and finishes without a warning.

* Acceptance

- [X] The subscription step asks for a route only for an asset class the chosen
  feed serves, and the run records no warning.
- [X] A provider that serves interest rates through one feed and FX through
  another gets both routes, each on the feed that serves it.
- [X] A test covers the case: a provider whose catalogue holds two classes, reached
  through a feed whose scope holds one.

* Plan

1. *Reproduce.* Provision the smokehouse tenant. The step ends
   =completed_with_warnings= and the warning is the store's own refusal:
   =Invalid route: feed afd34349-1c6c-56b2-a686-97b623ee051a serves no ir scope=.
2. *Read the cause.* The step took the asset class from the authority of every
   =oresmd= address the provider maps. A provider's catalogue is not a feed's
   scope: =derived= maps the FX volatility quotes and, since the term indices
   became ours, interest rate fixing items too, while the feed =derived-fx-vol=
   serves FX alone.
3. *Fix.* The route's class is the catalogue's, kept to the classes the feed
   states when it states any. A feed that states no scope serves whatever its
   publisher maps, and a feed whose scope matches none of the mapped classes is
   routed by its own scope, because the store refuses a class the feed disowns.
   The decision is =app::routed_asset_classes=, a function of two sets.
4. *Prove.* Four cases over the function, and a fresh tenant that completes every
   step with no warning.

* Notes

- The warning cost the tenant nothing today: the party does not read the derived
  term indices, and its surface published from the FX route. It is still a defect,
  because every future provision records it and a reader cannot tell a harmless
  warning from a load-bearing one.
- The route table enforces the rule, so the store was right and the step was
  wrong. The refusal is on =ores_marketdata_routes_insert_fn=.
- A feed that states no scope is not an oversight: the simulated feeds serve
  whatever demand asks of them, so their class comes from the catalogue alone.
