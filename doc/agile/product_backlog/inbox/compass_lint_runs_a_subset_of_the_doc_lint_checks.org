#+title: compass lint runs one of the six checks in the doc-lint job
#+description: The doc-lint job runs six checks and only the first is compass lint; the other five are committed build scripts the workflow invokes by path. A contributor who runs the verb named after the job gets a green result while CI can still fail, and the trigger filter names only the generate_skill scripts.

This page is a [[id:671F18E4-E09C-4B3B-BD24-D33DF8AE38A6][capture]] in the *inbox* bucket of the product backlog — a pre-sprint idea, not yet pulled into a sprint as a story.

* What

The =doc-lint= job runs six checks, and =compass lint= is one of them. The
other five are committed scripts under =build/scripts/=, and the workflow
invokes each by path:

- =rename_skills_to_compass_namespace.py --check=
- =generate_skills_catalogue.py --check=
- =generate_skill_type_tables.py --check=
- =apply_skill_levels.py --check=
- =link_skill_recipes.py --check=
- =link_structure_notes.py --check=

The command named after the job is therefore a subset of the job. A local
=compass lint= pass does not mean the job passes. Measured on 2026-09-26: a new
knowledge page tagged =structure_note= left seven ordered pages without their
back-link; =compass lint= passed on that branch, and the job failed on its last
step.

The trigger has the opposite fault. The =paths:= filter names
=build/scripts/generate_skill*.py= and the other four scripts not at all, so a
change to one of those four does not start the job that owns it. It names
=projects/ores.compass/src/compass.py=, so it does start for a change to the one
check that never moved.

* Why

Nothing recorded says the split is deliberate. The job grew one check at a time,
each as a script plus a step:

- =f27fcfedf7= — the namespace check
- =433ce5053b= — the catalogue and the type tables
- =1b07eaa137= — the skill levels
- =c96b21dda7= — the recipe links
- =6f17f685ac= — the structure-note back-links

=compass lint= predates all of them, in =e6fd15664d=, and no commit afterwards
moved a new check into it. Two facts make that easy to leave alone: =compass
lint= lives inside the compass package and scans the corpus with no other
dependency, while the five checks are repo scripts outside that package. To
consolidate, compass must either shell out to files it does not own, or absorb
those files.

The cost is a mismatch between the name and the contract. All six are repo-wide
consistency checks over the org corpus, so a contributor has one reason to run
them, and the tooling offers two places to find them.

The simplification is one entry point. Either =compass lint= runs the five
scripts as well, with a flag for the fast corpus-only subset, or the scripts
move under compass and the workflow keeps a single step. Whichever way it goes,
the =paths:= filter widens to =build/scripts/**= so a change to a check starts
the job that runs it.
