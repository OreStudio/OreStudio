#+title: Knowledge pages state facts, never history
#+description: A knowledge page is a statement of fact about the system as it is. Its summary must not assert that the subject matters, justify a choice already made, narrate how the system came to be, or describe the cluster the page belongs to.
#+type: memory
#+memory_subtype: feedback
#+level: cross
#+filetags: :memory:feedback:
#+created: 2026-09-21
#+updated: 2026-09-21

A knowledge page states what is true of the system now: what a thing is, what it does, and why it is shaped the way it is. It does not narrate how the system came to be that way.

Three openings are never right: a summary does not assert that its subject matters, does not justify a choice already made, and does not describe the document or the cluster it belongs to. Write the subject.

*Why:* The first summary written for the Message Queue structure note had all three faults at once -- messaging playing a central role, NATS chosen for its simplicity, and the cluster listing every document on the topic -- and was rejected for exactly that. The reader who reached the page already decided the subject matters; a justification for a settled choice belongs with the decision that recorded it; and describing the collection is the job of the index, not of the page.

*How to apply:* When writing or reviewing the summary of any knowledge page, and especially when scaffolding one, where the template invites a generic opening. Read the first sentence back and ask whether it describes the subject or the page. Rewrite anything that argues for the subject, records how it came to be, or describes the collection.
