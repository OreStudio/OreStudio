Why the FX and IR fixings are missing: the publishing setup, read against the tree
==================================================================================

Measured on the provisioned acme_demo tenant at commit b0add7a53e. Every count
below names its query inline; the tenant ids are
acme = 5c0e22e7-4893-4c3b-a06b-17dcb7860f86 and
system = ffffffff-ffff-ffff-ffff-ffffffffffff.


1. What the design says
-----------------------
Read in this order:

- =doc/knowledge/architecture/market_data_architecture.org= — the structure note.
- =doc/knowledge/architecture/public_source_fixings.org= — a public publisher's
  daily rate is an FX index whose identity carries the publisher:
  =oresmd://fx/EUR?type=fixing&index=fx&source=ECB&ccy=USD= is ORE's
  =FX-ECB-EUR-USD=. The system party subscribes to each public provider and
  routes to its source "so the items it calibrates from are in the demand"; a
  tenant "may subscribe too, and reads the fixings through its own series and its
  entitlement".
- =doc/knowledge/architecture/market_data_demand.org= — a party's items come from
  its subscriptions and routes; the demand set is the union over the parties
  routed to an adapter.
- =doc/knowledge/architecture/market_data_catalogue_install.org= — the catalogue
  is the system bundle; "an id in the catalogue and not in the universe cannot be
  demanded. The source screen lists it."
- =doc/knowledge/domain/market_data_requirements_resolution_hub.org= — the step
  this story has been missing. A requirement is symbolic and independent of any
  source. Resolution, rho, maps it to exactly one physical identifier under a
  *market data configuration*, and the physical fields it fills in include the
  *source*. The resolved set is intersected with the universe U, and "a production
  system needs to surface the difference … as an explicit error, rather than
  letting the intersection quietly discard it".
- =doc/knowledge/architecture/tracking_market_data_identity_from_fixing_to_report.org=
  — the target chain, observation through to consumption.

So the design expects two things of the FX and IR fixings. The publisher is part
of an FX fixing's identity and is chosen by resolution, not by the trade. And a
fixing may be read by item, from the installation's store, under the party's
entitlement.

3. What the tree does not do
----------------------------
Four findings, in order of how far each one reaches.

** a. No acme party is entitled to a public publisher, or to any interest rate
      feed at all.

    compass sql -- -c "select p.short_code as party, r.publisher_code, r.asset_class
      from ores_marketdata_routes_tbl r
      join ores_refdata_parties_tbl p on p.id = r.party_id and p.valid_to > now()
      where r.valid_to > now() and r.tenant_id = '<acme>' order by 1,2,3"

    ACCOHK  bloomberg fx | derived fx | ice fx | tradeweb fx
    ACCOUK  bloomberg fx | demo fx | derived fx | ice fx | tradeweb fx
    ACCOUS  bloomberg fx | derived fx | ice fx | lseg fx | tradeweb fx
    ACMCOR  bloomberg fx | derived fx | ice fx | tradeweb fx
    acme_corporation_system  bloomberg fx

Nineteen routes, every one =fx=, every one to a vendor publisher. The system
party routes twenty-one (publisher, asset class) pairs, including =ecb/fx=,
=imf/fx=, =boc/fx=, =tcmb/fx= and sixteen interest rate publishers. The
subscriptions say the same: ACCOUK subscribes to bloomberg, demo, derived, ice and
tradeweb, and to nothing else.

So the installation receives and stores every public fixing, and no acme party is
entitled to one. That is harmless only if a fixing is read by item under an
entitlement derived from the party's *products*, which is what the target says.
The export does not read by item: =series_to_export= selects the party's series
and, for fixings, the system tenant's series, and then
=market_fixing_repository::read_latest_for_series= reads by series id. A party
with no public route holds no fixing series, so it reads no fixing.

** b. The FX trades name a publisher the catalogue has never held.

    projects/ores.synthetic/core/src/service/trade_population_planner.cpp:1937

states =fx_fixing_source = "TR20H"=, with a comment that names the gap:

    The fixing source ORE's examples state for every pair. The source belongs to
    the tenant's market data configuration, which the market data task gives a
    home; until then every generated trade observes this one.

    compass sql -- -c "select count(*) from ores_marketdata_providers_tbl
      where valid_to > now() and code ilike '%tr20h%'"                       -- 0
    compass sql -- -c "select count(*) from ores_marketdata_market_data_vendor_instruments_tbl
      where valid_to > now() and external_id ilike '%TR20H%'"                -- 0

There is no TR20H provider, source, session, vendor instrument, mapping or feed
scope. Per the catalogue-install rule the item cannot be demanded, so no adapter
can serve it and the demand reply has nothing to reject it against. Nine of the
twenty-four pairs are quoted by a feed under that feed's own source, and the
trades name none of them.

** c. The reference data the trades draw from is not intersected with the market
      data universe.

The IR index comes from the swap convention catalogue's own address
(=swap_choices_of=, =trade_population_planner.cpp:433=), and the FX pair from the
currency pair catalogue (=pair_for=, line 1911). The simulator's universe is
separate: =ores_synthetic_ir_curve_generation_configs_tbl= states one curve per
(currency, family, tenor). It holds no =HKD hibor=, no =PLN wibor=, no
=JPY eytibor=, nothing for AOA, BWP, IDR, KRW, MYR, OMR, SAR or THB, and its
=COP ibr= and =USD sofr= curves state no tenor where the trade states 3M and 1M.
The refdata catalogue, meanwhile, offers 92 =GENERIC= Ibor conventions whose own
addresses state no tenor.

    compass sql -- -c "select currency_code, index_family, tenor, role
      from ores_synthetic_ir_curve_generation_configs_tbl
      where valid_to > now() order by 1,2,3"

So the 15 IR gaps are the difference between the two catalogues, and nothing in
the tree compares them.

** d. There is no market data configuration for rho to read.

    compass sql -- -c "select table_name from information_schema.tables
      where table_schema = 'public' and table_name like 'ores_refdata_%configuration%'"

returns curve configurations and nothing else. The resolution function, rho, the
per-tenant configuration it resolves under, and the universe it intersects with
have no rows, no schema and no code. In their place the trade stores rho's output
— a publisher, or an index address with no publisher — frozen at booking time.


4. Verdict
----------
The publishing setup is not the broken part. Feeds, scopes, catalogue, the
durable fixing store and the derived fixing writer all do their jobs, and the
system party receives 325,571 fixings from them.

Three of the four findings are implementation defects:

1. The demo provision gives the acme parties no public publisher route, and no
   interest rate route at all. Every party's entitlement is FX-only and
   vendor-only. (Finding a.)
2. The FX trade generator freezes a publisher, =TR20H=, that no catalogue holds,
   for every pair. The code says so and defers it. (Finding b.)
3. The trade draw and the simulator universe are two catalogues with no check
   between them, so the generator books indices and pairs nobody serves.
   (Finding c.)

One is an architectural gap: rho. The design names it, the resolution hub argues
it, and one code comment states where it will live, but the schema and the code
do not have it. Until a trade's requirement is resolved against the market data
catalogue and the per-tenant configuration, every trade carries a frozen physical
identifier, and the difference between what the trades ask for and what the
market holds can only be detected at the export, as the refusal the previous task
added — never at booking, where a person could act on it.

The smallest change that removes the most of the gap, in order:

1. Route and subscribe the demo parties to the public publishers for FX and IR,
   the way the system party already is. Nothing else can work while a party holds
   no entitlement to the data the installation already stores.
2. Give the FX fixing source its home: resolve it per tenant from the catalogue,
   defaulting to a publisher the tenant routes, instead of the constant.
3. Intersect the trade draw with the simulator's universe, so a booked trade
   always names an item the market answers, and refuse the rest with a reason at
   generation time.
4. Then widen the universe, or derive the crosses, for the pairs and indices no
   publisher quotes: fifteen FX pairs are crosses of two published bases, which
   is what the composite mechanism is for.


5. The join that exists in code, and the one that does not
----------------------------------------------------------
Reading =demand_calculator.cpp= settles what the subscription machinery does
today.

- =compute_party_demand= walks the **routes**, and for each (party, feed) it
  takes the items the feed's provider maps to, keeps those the feed scopes, and
  keeps those the policy names. Entitlement is a subscription of that party to
  that provider with any of the display, non-display or derived rights, plus the
  provider's terms being accepted; otherwise it refuses the demand with
  =not_entitled= or =terms_not_accepted=.
- =demand_policy= carries a hook for exactly the join the user asks for:
  =requirements(party, provider) -> optional<set<oresmd uri>>=, whose doc says a
  function that returns nothing leaves the party with every item of the provider
  a mapping reaches.
- =adapter_supervisor.cpp:229= calls =compute_demand(snap->catalogue, now)= —
  with no policy. So no party's demand is ever narrowed by what its trades need,
  and every entitled route demands its provider's whole scoped catalogue.

So the machinery is built and the subscription is checked. What is absent is the
input: nothing computes a party's required items from the trades it carries, and
nothing checks that the parties' subscriptions cover those items.

    compass sql -- -c "select v.provider_code, count(*) from

        on v.id = m.vendor_instrument_id

Every FX fixing mapping belongs to a public publisher, and to no data vendor.
There is no supplier of a *vendor* FX fixing anywhere in the catalogue, so a
trade naming the 4pm London convention (TR20H) names a convention with no
supplier at all. The vendors supply FX spot only: bloomberg 936 items, ice 910,
tradeweb 910, lseg 14, demo 11, derived 2275.


6. The two axes, and where the model already carries them
----------------------------------------------------------
The vocabulary matters here, because one word is doing two jobs.

- *The fixing convention* is the instrument: who calculates the rate, at what
  time, by what method. The ECB's 14:15 reference rate, WM/Refinitiv's 4pm
  London fix (ORE spells it TR20H), ICE's LIBOR, the New York Fed's SOFR. A trade
  references a convention, because that is what its cashflows settle on.
- *The fixing supplier* is who sells us that convention's values: LSEG, Bloomberg,
  the ECB's own portal, or the platform's own derived feed. A firm may hold one
  convention from several suppliers; the supplier is a commercial and operational
  choice, not an economic one.
- *The fixing series* is the stored data for one convention from one supplier for
  one party.

The model already carries both axes: the convention is inside the oresmd URI
(=source= for FX, =name= and =tenor= for IR) and the supplier is
=market_series.provider_code=. The word "source" names the convention in
=market_series.oresmd_uri= and the supplier everywhere else, which is the reason
this reads as one confusing field.

So the end-to-end rule the tree is missing is a closure, in this order:

    trades -> conventions -> catalogue items -> suppliers -> entitlements

A party may book a trade only if, for every convention the trade names, some
supplier in the catalogue serves it and the party holds a subscription with the
rights that supplier requires. The demand is then the party's items, not the
provider's catalogue. The supplier for each convention is chosen once, by a
stated rank, and recorded, so a report reads the same value twice.

None of the four joins in that chain exists today: the catalogue has no supplier
for the vendor conventions (5), the generator states the convention and no
supplier (3b), the demand is never narrowed by trades (5), and the read resolves
by series rather than by convention plus supplier (3a).
