* Plan

1. Replace the nineteen section tabs with one *Curves* tab, drawn as the
   product pricing screen draws its products: a rail on the left, a workbench on
   the right.
2. Group the rail by the section that declares each curve, because the section
   decides the entry's table and its leaves even though it is not what a person
   looks for.
3. Name the sections that hold nothing once, at the foot of the rail, rather than
   giving each a tab that opens empty.
4. Draw the chosen curve in the workbench: its identity apart from its
   parameters, its documentation, the filters and the elements it holds.
5. Test the tabs, the grouping order and a rendered curve.

* Notes

- *Why the section is the group and not the tab.* The 89 =curveconfig*.xml=
  files ORE ships fill a median of three sections, the smallest one and the
  largest sixteen; the environment's four stored configurations fill one. A tab
  per section therefore opens mostly empty, and asks a person to find the chapter
  before the curve. As a group it still says which family a curve belongs to,
  which is what decides its table and its leaves.
- *The prototype is the argument.*
  [[file:/doc/prototypes/reporting/curve-configuration-sections.org][Curve configuration sections]] draws the shape over a one-, four- and
  sixteen-section document, and this task draws the page from it.
- *The identity is drawn apart from the parameters.* A curve's =CurveId= and
  =CurveDescription= live on =curve_definition=, which every family shares, and
  the rest live on the section's own table. The workbench draws them as two
  cards, as a product's workbench draws the product apart from its model and
  engine settings.
- *The standard needs an amendment.* The configuration screen standard states
  one tab per section. This task breaks that rule deliberately, and the
  amendment is "one tab for the collection, and the section is the group inside
  it". It belongs to the story that owns the standard,
  [[id:AB74EE5D-4E3B-499A-A964-BDCFF7853663][Make every configuration screen follow one standard]], and is raised
  there rather than changed around.

* Result

The curve configuration page now draws Details, **Curves**, Used by and History.
The Curves tab is the product pricing shape: a rail of the configuration's
curves grouped by the section that declares each, with a count on every group, a
filter that matches a curve's name, its description or its section, Expand all,
and the sections that hold nothing named once at the foot. The chosen curve is
read beside the rail: what it is, its identity, its parameters with their
documentation and filters, and the elements it holds.

The nine browser tests cover the four tabs, the grouping order, a rendered
curve's value and words, a leaf the contract does not document, a flag read as
Yes or No, the elements a curve holds, the reports that bind the configuration
and the history. The nested collections of a curve are still named and not
drawn, which is the task of their own.
