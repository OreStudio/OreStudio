* Acceptance

- The report page offers Schedule and Unschedule, gated on the permission, calling the existing operations.
- An on-demand report can be activated: a real schedule is set and scheduled, and deactivated back to on demand.
- The screen states the outcome and the tests are green.

* Plan

- Read the operation, not a new one: =schedule_report_definitions= and
  =unschedule_report_definitions= already carry the ids, the count, the failed
  ids and the words, and the shell already drives them. The task is the app
  action, so the BFF relays those operations and the page states their answers.
- Put the permission on the route as the operation states it. The handler checks
  the write of a definition to schedule and its delete to unschedule, and the
  page's two buttons follow the same pair, so a person who may change a
  definition is not offered a way to take its schedule away.
- Activate an on-demand report with one action rather than two forms. The
  scheduler reads the expression from the definition, so the expression has to
  be stored before the schedule is made; the BFF does both in one call, at the
  version it read, so the page never asks a person to save and then schedule.
- Let the list and the page agree on one word. A definition with no scheduler
  job is on demand whatever expression it keeps, and the expression that never
  fires is on demand as well, so both read =isOnDemand= rather than a rule each.
- Do not hide a refusal. The operation answers a refusal as a result, so the
  page reads the count and states nothing changed, or states the server's reason
  as an error; no toast says success for a write that did not happen.

* Result

A person who holds the permission schedules or unschedules a report from the
report's own page, and activates an on-demand report by stating the schedule it
runs on.

** The routes

| Route | Operation | Permission |
|-------+-----------+------------|
| =POST /api/reporting/reports/schedule= | =reporting.v1.ops.schedule_report_definitions= | =reporting::report_definitions:write= |
| =POST /api/reporting/reports/unschedule= | =reporting.v1.ops.unschedule_report_definitions= | =reporting::report_definitions:delete= |

Each takes ={ids: [...]}= and answers ={changed, failedIds, message}=, which is
the operation's own count, its own failed ids and its own words. The person's
session makes the call, so the service reads only the definitions that session
may see and acts for the party that owns them; a definition of another party is
refused in the operation's words. The permission the route gates on is the one
the operation checks, read before the route writes, because a schedule writes
the expression the job will fire on.

The schedule route takes an optional =schedule_expression=, which names one
definition. The scheduler builds a job from the expression the definition holds,
so activating an on-demand report writes that expression first, at the version
the route read, and then schedules it. A refusal of the operation is an answer
and not an error: the page reads the count and the words, and a refusal it does
not hide.

** The page actions

The report page draws =Schedule= on a definition with no scheduler job and
=Unschedule= on one that has, each gated on the permission its own operation
checks. Neither is offered when the page can tell that the definition's party is
not the session's, and the page then says whose report it is and which party to
act for.

=Schedule= opens a dialog over the existing =CronField= and builder, so a person
who does not know cron states the schedule in controls and one who does opens
the expression. The expression and the schedule are one action. =Unschedule=
withdraws the schedule and leaves the report on demand; the run grant stays,
because consent to run is the report's and not the schedule's.

Every outcome is in words above the tabs: "Scheduled", "Nothing changed. The
report already had a schedule", "Unscheduled", "Nothing changed. The report had
no schedule to remove", and, for a refusal, "The server refused this change:"
followed by the operation's own reason. A count of zero reads as nothing changed
and never as a success.

** On demand

A definition reads as on demand when it holds no scheduler job, which is the
fact that says nothing will fire, and when the expression it holds is the one
that never fires (=0 0 31 2 *=). The list's Schedule column and the page's "When
it runs" detail read the same rule from =isOnDemand=, so the two cannot disagree
about the word.

** What was verified

- The BFF: the ids the operation is called with, the expression the write
  carries and the version it holds, a refusal surfaced as the answer, both
  permission gates and the refusal of an expression that names two definitions.
- The page: the actions offered by permission, the delete gate that keeps
  =Unschedule= from a person who may only write, an on-demand report and a
  scheduled one, and every outcome in words.
- Live, in the demo tenant: an on-demand report was activated from its page and
  unscheduled again, and the scheduler job, the definition's =scheduler_job_id=
  and the IAM run grant were read from the services between the two. The party
  guard was seen on a report of another party. The evidence is
  [[file:evidence/report_schedule_live.txt][evidence/report_schedule_live.txt]].
- The harness check =report-schedule.spec.ts= drives the same round trip and
  leaves the report as it found it.

What could not be verified: that a scheduled report fires. The checks leave the
demo tenant as they found it, so the evidence that a schedule will fire is the
scheduler's live job row with its expression and not a run the schedule
produced.
