* Goal

Give every ACME staff account its party, so a trade can name the person who
entered it. Today a staff account carries no =default_party_id=, and its party
lives only in the many-to-many =ores_iam_account_parties_tbl=, which lists the
three larger entities for everyone. "Which party does this person trade for" is
therefore unanswerable from the data, and the trade population has to name one
of the 30 =Trading=-role holders.

* Plan

The assignment lives in the publisher, =ores_iam_publish_accounts_from_dq_fn=
(=projects/ores.sql/create/iam/iam_publish_from_dq_create.sql=). The target
party is already the function's =params.party_id=, required, and the entity
bundle already publishes each accounts dataset against exactly one party
(=acme.acme_uk.accounts= under =acme_uk=, and so on -- see
=projects/ores.sql/populate/acme/acme_bundle_populate.sql=). The rules were:

- *Why not a seed change in the profile.* The =load_staff= step's arguments
  take =name=, =bundles= and =default= only, and =default= sets the default for
  the *acting* account, the tenant administrator -- =set_account_default_party=
  acts as the caller. There is no per-bundle or per-staff default in the step,
  so a profile change would need new step machinery for data the publish
  already holds.
- *Why not a change to how staff are loaded.* The load is the bundle publish.
  Accounts are created by the publisher, so the publisher is where the account
  row is built.
- *Why the target party is the right party.* It is a fact, not a judgement. A
  staff dataset belongs to one legal entity and is published against that
  entity's party, so the party the publish already targets *is* the employing
  entity. No derivation from role or desk is needed, and none is invented.

* Notes

*The investigation's account counts do not hold on the live database.* It
records 61 current accounts over 59 =user= rows and 2 with a party. The
organisation dataset the ACME tenant is built from holds 58 staff, and the
recreate stages exactly those 58. The 2 with a party are =tenant_admin= and the
system party's account, which are not staff and are not members of the
organisation dataset. So the staff figure is 58, not 59, and the live database
currently holds no ACME tenant at all (2 tenants: =system= and the
half-provisioned =acme_corporation=), so the counts were re-established from the
dataset that produces them.

*What =default_party_id= means and who reads it.* It is the party an account
signs into, "party to log into automatically when quick-login is enabled"
(=projects/ores.iam.api/.../account.hpp=). It is a soft reference to a party
owned by =ores.refdata=. Readers:

- =auth_handler= returns it as =login_response.default_party_id=, but only when
  the account is also a member of that party, so a stale default is dropped.
- =tenant_provisioning_handler::actor_of= scopes a provisioning step's token to
  it, falling back to the account's first party link.
- =account_operations_service::set_my_default_party= is the writer; the migrate
  path in =tenant_provisioning_handler= also writes it.

Null is legal and not itself an error: the fallback keeps working. What null
loses is the ability to *choose* a person's party deterministically, which is
what the population needs.

* Result

The change is one insert column. =ores_iam_publish_accounts_from_dq_fn= now
writes the target party into =default_party_id= on the account it creates, and
its doc comment states why that party is the account's own party.

*The fix is not verified end to end, and a green build must not be read as a
working provision.* =compass db recreate -y -k= succeeds and the build is green,
but a tenant cannot currently be provisioned through the normal path, so the
"recreate then provision" proof has not been made. The cause is the defect
already on =main=: the workflow's step-command dispatch moved to JetStream and
most step subjects have no stream. Evidence from this run:

- =bootstrap setup ... --profile acme_demo= ran to shell exit 0 and reported
  "All 2 step(s) completed" for the *parent* run (=system_provision= +
  =start_tenant_setup=). That is the handover, not the work.
- The child =tenant_setup_workflow= steps never ran. Every row in
  =ores_workflow_workflow_steps_tbl= has =command_published_at= set and
  =completed_at= NULL -- for example step 0 =synthetic.market_data_providers= on
  =synthetic.v1.ops.publish_market_data_providers_from_dq=, published 16:08:36.
- Tenant =acme_corporation= was created and holds one account and one party
  (=acme_corporation_system=). It holds no staff and no books.
- The subjects that *do* have a stream and a live consumer work. Only
  =synthetic_steps= is registered by a merged change (=5ee8749df7=, on this
  commit): it carries =publish_feeds_from_dq= and
  =publish_market_data_providers_from_dq= and has two consumers.
  =iam_steps= carries =provision_tenant_step= and has one.
- The commit that fixes this, =4dfacbef59= "Cover every step-command subject
  with a stream", is *not* an ancestor of this commit. It is on
  =origin/feature/audit-the-nats-call-sites=.
- =WORKFLOWSTEPS_bright_faraday= exists but reports "No Consumers defined". It
  was created by hand with the =nats= CLI on an earlier task, so it is
  environment state and not code. It was left untouched.

Verification was therefore made at the SQL-function level against the staged
ACME datasets, which does not depend on the dispatch path at all. The harness
creates two probe tenants in a transaction, loads the pre-change publisher into
one and the edited publisher into the other, publishes every ACME staff dataset
against its own party, reads the counts, and rolls back. The live database was
left as it was found: two tenants and two parties before the harness ran, and
the same after.
