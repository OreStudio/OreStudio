* Summary

The [[id:B5214F3A-A72E-421E-82BC-79338B2E95CB][Cuts]] structure note fixes
what a cut is, what it holds and why. It settles no state, no act, no
authorisation and no identity, and the screens cannot be drawn without those
four. This page settles them. A cut records the body it fixed and the acts that
made it, and nothing else; its state is derived from those facts and stored
nowhere; a cut kind declares whether a cut of its occasion needs an
authorisation at all, and where it does the authorisation is an approval request
of [[id:0FE6BDD9-EB24-4B07-8B0C-D816AAF1BEBF][the inbox]] whose detail the
reporting component holds; its label is derived from the kind, the scope and the
timepoint, and never typed. The cut is then the object a run names, which is what
the report binding lacks.

The authorisation is not a mechanism this cluster invents. One record already
holds everything that waits on a person, four eyes is a rule of its schema, and
the queue, the notifications and the history are written once. A cut is a kind of
that record, with a detail table of its own, and the only thing the cluster adds
is the declaration that says which occasions need one.

Everything on this page is our working convention. No public source states how a
firm should model the authorisation of a cut, and the
[[id:B5214F3A-A72E-421E-82BC-79338B2E95CB][Cuts]] note records which claims in
the cluster rest on a source and which do not.

* The facts a cut records

A cut records six groups of facts. The parts are the body the
[[id:B5214F3A-A72E-421E-82BC-79338B2E95CB][Cuts]] note argues for; the other
five groups name the occasion, the scope, what the assembly could not fill and
who acted.

| Fact | What it holds | Why the cut records it |
|------+---------------+----------------------------|
| The kind | The occasion, from the closed set [[id:33E879B7-496A-406D-B3F2-72637379AD02][Kinds of cut]] classifies, and the instant a process names where no kind applies | The kind fixes the instant, the recurrence and the centre, and it declares whether a cut of the occasion needs an authorisation |
| The timepoint | The instant, with its timezone, and the cut time it came from where a named cut time supplied it | A body fixed at an instant is not fixed until the instant is stated with its clock |
| The scope | The books whose deals the valuation values, and the rates centre whose market data they revalue against | A body that names no population values nothing, and a market data half with no centre denotes whichever centre answered first |
| The parts | One reference per part of the body: the fixed market data set, the deal population, the versions of the reference data and the settings, and the engine and code versions | The four inputs are what the reproducibility argument requires, and a reference is what a rerun replays |
| The gaps | Every part the assembly could not fill, with the item, the part and the reason | A body that could not be assembled is a fact a person acts on, and silence makes the cut look smaller rather than incomplete |
| The acts | Who took the cut and when, and the authorisation: the request the take raised, the decision it reached, the decider and the instant, or the refusal and its reason — or, where the kind requires none, the fact that none was required | The taking identity and the authorisation are not inputs to the valuation: an unsigned body is a candidate, and an occasion that needs no signature still has to say so rather than look unsigned |

Two of these facts are ours alone and no prior art supplies them. The gap list
follows from the definition: a cut missing a part is incomplete rather than
smaller, so the absence is recorded where the body is recorded rather than left
to a run to discover. The act of taking follows from the same sentence:
something fixes the body, and the something is a person acting at an instant.

** What is derived, and why nothing is stored

The state, the consumability verdict and every word the screens print about a
cut are functions of the facts above. None is stored.

The precedent is the published set.
[[id:9BC6FEDE-01E5-43B8-8A04-ACF0A5AB12B6][ores.marketdata.curve_publish]]
states that the state a reader sees is a function of the stored facts and is
stored nowhere: withdrawn when an instant holds, superseded when an instant
holds, published when an instant holds, and a candidate otherwise. A cut has the
same shape and the same reason. A stored state follows a write that may fail, may
be repeated or may be missed, so the row and its state can disagree; a derived
state cannot disagree with the facts it is derived from.

** What the kind declares

A cut kind is a row, and it carries five facts beyond its code and name.

| Declaration | What it decides | The end of day kind | The flash kind |
|-------------+-----------------+---------------------+----------------|
| What it fixes | The instant, the recurrence and the centre | The centre's close | An instant the process names |
| =requires_authorisation= | Whether the take raises an approval request at all | Yes | No |
| =output_is_indicative= | Whether a result priced from a cut of the kind carries the indicative mark | No | Yes |
| =approval_kind_code= | Which inbox kind authorises it, where it requires one | =reporting.cut_authorisation= | None |
| =display_order= | The order the take offers the kinds | 10 | 40 |

Two of those facts are independent, and the start of day kind is the case that
proves it. A start of day cut is carried forward from the previous end of day
cut, so it needs no authorisation at the opening, and its result is not
indicative because the body was approved when it was first fixed. The flash kind
needs none and is indicative, because its occasion is before the day settles.
Neither fact implies the other, so the kind states both.

* The states and what derives them

A cut is in one of seven states. Each is stated by the facts that hold, and the
order of the tests is the order a reader needs them.

| State | The facts that hold | What a run may do | The mark |
|-------+---------------------+-------------------+----------|
| Draft | No taking instant holds, and no gap stands; the assembled body is saved before the take | Nothing. It is not a cut yet | Grey, the mark of an inactive record |
| Incomplete | No taking instant holds, and one or more gaps stand; the attempt is saved as a draft | Nothing. The gaps are the answer | Red, because the body failed its own standard |
| Taken | The body is fixed, the kind requires an authorisation, and no approval is recorded | Nothing. It is the body the decider reads | Blue, the mark of a pending item |
| In use | The body is fixed, and the kind requires no authorisation | Price from it | Green, the colour of a record that is active and in order |
| Authorised | The body is fixed and an approval is recorded | Price from it | Green |
| Refused | The authorisation was refused, with the reason | Nothing. A refusal is terminal for that body | Red |
| Superseded | The cut was authorised or in use, and a later cut of the same kind and scope holds its place | Price from it, and say a later cut is in use | Grey, as the published lists draw an ended set |

The table is a derivation and not a lifecycle: a state is one of the seven and
never a mixture of two. Two facts decide which of the three usable states
applies, and neither is a matter of taste: whether the kind requires an
authorisation, and whether one was recorded.

Six consequences follow, and each is a decision the screens draw.

**The gate follows the kind, not a single rule about cuts.** A run prices from a
body that is fixed and signed where a signature was required. A cut of a kind
that requires none is usable the moment it is taken, and a cut of a kind that
requires one is not usable until the approval is recorded. A gate that demanded an
authorisation of every cut would refuse the occasions that need none, and a gate
that accepted any taken body would price from an unsigned one.

**A draft is a record, not a scratchpad, and a gap is what separates it from an
incomplete body.** The gaps a body cannot close name a person to chase — a series with no value at or before the instant, a deal at no
version, a curve nobody has approved, an engine version nothing recorded. A take
that threw the attempt away would make the person remember the list. The draft
therefore holds the attempt, each gap with its reason, and the cut's own page is
where the list is read.

**An incomplete body is refused before it is signed.** The take raises no request
while a gap stands, because a decider would be asked to sign a body that does not
meet the definition. The refusal is at the take and not at the authorisation, and
the screen says what would close each gap.

**A held request leaves the cut where it was.** The inbox's hold state parks the
request and not the body, so the cut stays *Taken*, its card reads that the
request is held, and the waiting time is the request's to report.

**Supersession does not withdraw authorisation.** A superseded cut is still a
record of an instant, and a run that priced from it still reproduces from it. The
published-set rule that an ended set is refused unless its as-of is pinned does
not carry over, because a curve is a value in use and a cut is a record of what
was used. The one word the screen must say is that a later cut replaced it: a
person who reads a result must not take it for the day's cut.

**The indicative mark is the kind's, and it is not the state's.** A result priced
from a flash cut is indicative because the kind says so, and the mark is drawn on
the cut, on the picker's row and on every result it prices. A cut of a kind that
requires no authorisation is not therefore indicative, and the start of day kind
is the case that shows it.

* The authorisation is the inbox's

A cut that requires an authorisation raises an approval request of a kind the
reporting component registers. Nothing about the wait for a person is the cut's
own: the record, its lifecycle, its rules, its queue, its notifications and its
history are
[[id:0FE6BDD9-EB24-4B07-8B0C-D816AAF1BEBF][the approval request's]], and the
cut adds a detail table and a descriptor.

| What the cut needs | Where it lives | The precedent |
|--------------------+----------------+---------------|
| The record of the ask, the decision and the wait | An =ores.inbox= approval request: its kind, state, asker, reason, decision and expiry | [[id:0FE6BDD9-EB24-4B07-8B0C-D816AAF1BEBF][Approval requests]] |
| What the request is about | A detail table in the reporting component, keyed by =request_id=: the cut and the counts of the body's parts | [[id:9BC6FEDE-01E5-43B8-8A04-ACF0A5AB12B6][ores.marketdata.curve_publish]] |
| Whether a cut needs one at all | The cut kind's =requires_authorisation=, and the take raises nothing where it says no | [[*What the kind declares][What the kind declares]] |
| Who may decide | The inbox kind's =decide_permission_code= | [[id:A85B6C4E-0F93-4410-BF2E-7167722849ED][Decide a publish]] |
| How many approvals close it | The inbox kind's =approvals_required=; one for a cut | [[id:A85B6C4E-0F93-4410-BF2E-7167722849ED][Decide a publish]] |
| Four eyes | A rule of the schema: a decision by the asker is refused, and one person counts once | [[id:0FE6BDD9-EB24-4B07-8B0C-D816AAF1BEBF][Approval requests]] |
| The queue | The shared Approvals read, filtered to the kinds the caller may decide | [[id:0FE6BDD9-EB24-4B07-8B0C-D816AAF1BEBF][Approval requests]] |
| One open request per subject | The detail table's natural key on the cut, which is what the inbox means by the same subject | [[id:0FE6BDD9-EB24-4B07-8B0C-D816AAF1BEBF][Approval requests]] |
| The screen the decider reads | The request page, which draws the kind's detail as its own section | the inbox's =RequestPage= and its =kinds.tsx= descriptor |
| Notifications | The inbox's: the deciders are told a request waits, the asker is told the decision | [[id:0FE6BDD9-EB24-4B07-8B0C-D816AAF1BEBF][Approval requests]] |
| Applying the decision | The reporting service stamps the cut's authorisation facts when the request reaches approved | the shape IAM uses for a role grant |

**The take raises the request, in one act.** The body is frozen when the request
is raised, so what a decider reads is what gets authorised. The detail row is
written with the body and keyed by the request, as the curve publish detail is
keyed by its request. A take that wrote a body and then asked for a signature
would leave an interval in which the body may move.

**The taker cannot authorise, and the rule is not the cut's.** The inbox refuses a
decision by the person who raised the request, and a unique key on the request and
the decider keeps one person from counting twice. A cut adds nothing to that rule
and depends on it, which is why the take and the decision are two permissions and
not one. A firm with one person who holds both cannot authorise a cut at all: the
screen draws the refusal and names what is missing, which is a second person, and
it does not draw the button.

**A refusal is terminal for that body.** The body stays readable, the reason is on
the request and copied to the cut's page, and the inbox allows no second decision
on a refused request. The remedy is a new take, which is a new cut and a new
request, because an approval is a decision on one body and a decided body does not
become another.

**The apply is a reconciliation, not a second screen.** When a request reaches
approved, the reporting service writes the cut's authorisation facts and marks the
detail row applied. It does so from the request's change event and from the state
the request holds, so a missed event, a repeated one, or an approval made while
the service was down all converge on one outcome, as a role grant does. The
alternative — an apply workflow — is named in
[[*What this page does not settle][what this page does not settle]], because the
work is one row and idempotent.

**The cut's page links the request and never the reverse.** The request is the
record of the wait and the cut is the record of the body; the request page draws
the body through the kind's detail, and the cut's page names the request. Neither
copies the other's facts, because two copies of one act drift.

* The permissions

Three permissions decide what a person sees and does. Two are the cut's own, and
the third is the inbox's.

| Permission | What it governs | Who holds it, in the fixture |
|------------+-----------------+------------------------------|
| =reporting::cuts:read= | The list, the record, the picker over cuts, and the run's card that names one | Market risk, finance, a reader |
| =reporting::cuts:take= | Assembling and taking a cut, which raises its authorisation where the kind requires one | Finance, for the end of day kind |
| =inbox::approvals:decide_finance= | The decision that authorises an end of day cut: the inbox kind's own decide permission | Finance, and a different person from the taker |

The first two names are proposals and follow the platform's namespace for a
component's own record. The third is not new: it is a permission the inbox has
already seeded, and the cut's kind names it rather than inventing one.

**One permission decides every cut that needs a signature.** A cut body is signed
by one function whatever the occasion that produced it, so the inbox kind carries
one decide permission and every authorisation cut kind names that same kind. The
occasions differ in whether they require a signature and in how many approvals
close it, not in who signs. A firm that splits the duty grants the permission to
the role that does the work, which is the inbox's own rule: a part is decided by a
permission and not by a named job.

* The identity

The cut's key is a generated identifier. The label a person quotes is derived
from the three facts that name the occasion, and it is never typed:

#+begin_example
  <kind code>.<scope code>.<timepoint date>[.<take ordinal>]
  EOD.ACME-LONDON.2026-10-06
  EOD.ACME-LONDON.2026-10-06.2
#+end_example

The derivation is the rule the
[[id:FB8F701C-E56A-49A9-AA90-1B6BD0B02092][report binding decision]] applies to
a market configuration name: the screen draws what the record will say and no
field asks for it. Three reasons carry the rule.

A typed label is a name, and a name is what the binding decision rejects: it says
nothing about which body it means, and two bodies can carry one. A derived label
states the kind, the scope and the instant, and no second cut can carry it.

The take ordinal is the hub's numbered sequence made visible. A retake of the
same day, kind and scope is the second cut of that day, and the ordinal says so
in the one place a person looks. The published sets carry no such ordinal because
a curve is one value in use; a cut is one act among several on the same day.

The label is not the authorisation. A person reading =EOD.ACME-LONDON.2026-10-06=
knows which day's body is meant and not whether anybody signed it, so every
screen that shows the label shows the state beside it, and a cut of a kind that
requires no authorisation shows that basis in the same place.

* How it meets the report binding

The [[id:FB8F701C-E56A-49A9-AA90-1B6BD0B02092][report binding decision]] makes
a market role name a publish record and its as-of, and records that it settles
the curve and leaves the deal population open: *a report that must state both
needs a second binding, to a cut.* This page supplies the second binding and
says how the two fit.

**They are not two names for one thing.** A binding is a rule: one market role,
one published set, one as-of, and a pin where a rerun needs an earlier result. A
cut is the materialisation of every rule at one instant, beside the deals, the
reference data and the engine. The
[[id:DD59B04C-DAD2-458C-B35E-3F6B0B3D6DC6][Market data cut]] page supplies the
vocabulary: a *profile* is a set of rules and carries no values, a *snapshot* is
the profile materialised at an instant, and the market data half of an end of day
cut is a fixed snapshot. The market role bindings are rules of the profile; the
cut records the identity of the fixed snapshot that resolved them.

**The run settings configuration gains the cut it prices from.** One row, one
choice, shared by every report bound to that configuration, exactly as the
market roles are. The bindings stay where the decision put them, as the rules the
cut resolves; the cut is what a run names.

**The run keeps the cut, and the cut keeps the publish records.** The run records
the cut it priced from, and the market card the binding decision settles reads
its role, curve, party, as-of, publisher, published instant, points and publish
record *through* the cut. A result therefore names one object that reaches every
input, which is the promise the cluster exists to keep. The run does not copy the
cut's parts; it names the cut, and the cut is immutable once its body is fixed.

**The gate is one sentence, spoken in three places.** The picker refuses a cut
that is not consumable where it is picked, the run refuses it before the engine
is reached, and the run's output message carries the same sentence so a scheduled
run that nobody watched still says why it did not price. The sentence states what
the cut's kind requires: a body awaiting its kind's authorisation is refused with
the request and the person it waits on, and a body of a kind that requires none is
never refused on that ground.

**What the deal population becomes.** Today the gathering step reads the trade
store by valuation date and the run records the date it priced at. After this
page, the gathering step reads the cut's deal population, which is a copy of what
the store held at the cut instant and not a query re-run against it. The
[[id:19630B5D-A163-42B5-B9C4-61A87BAE8F5F][Trade cut]] page states why: the
interval a versioned row states is rewritten when a correction arrives, so two
executions of the same query at different moments can return different bodies.
The cut's population is a fact of the cut, and a rerun replays it.

* What this page does not settle

**The entity.** No cut component exists, no table holds one, and no service
answers a read of one. This page settles what a cut must record, which states
follow, who may act and what a run may name; it does not design the columns, the
generated types or the subjects. That is the next work, and it starts from this
page and from [[id:B5214F3A-A72E-421E-82BC-79338B2E95CB][Cuts]].

**One inbox kind, or one for each occasion.** This page takes one inbox kind,
=reporting.cut_authorisation=, whose detail row names the cut and therefore the
occasion. The alternative is a kind per occasion, which buys a decide permission
per occasion and a queue grouped by occasion, at the price of a seeded kind for
every kind of cut. The choice is the firm's, and the screens draw one kind.

**The apply.** The decision is applied by reconciliation, as a role grant is. The
alternative is an apply workflow, which the inbox already offers a kind through
=apply_workflow_type= and which buys a visible run and a retry where this page
buys one idempotent row. The cut's apply is one row, so this page takes the
reconciliation; a firm that wants the apply on the workflow screens changes one
column of the kind.

**The scope of a cut.** The convention taken here is that a cut's scope is the
set of books that revalue against one centre's market data, which
[[id:D18DF500-2C6C-42FF-BBAE-D5A46D410910][Book groups and rates centres]]
supplies the reason for: a body whose market data half names one centre cannot
value books that read another. The hub's narrower convention, one cut per book,
is the smallest such scope and not the only one. Whether a group report prices
from one cut of the group or from a composition of its books' cuts is open, and
the prototype draws the single scope only.

**The reference data half, by version or by value.** The part holds the versions
of the conventions, the calendars, the settings and the curve configurations the
run reads. Whether the cut stores those versions or the identifiers of the
documents is open. The difference is what a rerun needs when a document is
withdrawn: a version read back from a store that no longer serves it is not a
reproduction.

**The ordinal, and whether a retake is a new cut.** This page takes the retake to
be a second cut of the same day with its own ordinal and its own authorisation.
The alternative is one cut with versions, and it is rejected here on the ground
that an approval is a decision on one body: a versioned cut would let a decided
body change under the signature. The alternative is named rather than dismissed
because a firm that keeps one cut per day and amends it would read this
differently.

**Which occasions need no authorisation.** The cluster's classification gives the
start of day kind and the flash kind, and the fixtures draw those two. Whether a
process-named instant needs one is the process owner's answer, and the kind
declares it rather than the design assuming it.

**Who takes a cut where a party has one person.** The four-eyes rule makes an
authorisation impossible, and this page leaves that as a refusal to draw rather
than a rule to bend. A kind that requires no authorisation sidesteps it, which is
a fact about the occasion and not a route past the rule. Whether the installation
should let a named second account authorise across parties is not settled.

**The relation to the run's settings.** A cut holds the settings in force.
Whether the cut names the engine run settings configuration and its version, or
the individual settings, is open, and it belongs to
[[id:EDAE3C87-7733-4D0C-881F-5AA65DD832AC][Pricing Configuration]] rather than
to this page.

* What the sources establish

No claim on this page rests on a public source. The definitions of the states,
the kind's declarations, the permissions, the derivation of the label and the
relation to the binding are ours, and the pages they rest on are cited above. The
rules of the authorisation are not ours: they are the inbox's, stated on
[[id:0FE6BDD9-EB24-4B07-8B0C-D816AAF1BEBF][Approval requests]] and enforced by
its schema. The [[id:B5214F3A-A72E-421E-82BC-79338B2E95CB][Cuts]] note records
which claims of the cluster do rest on a public source, and this page adds none
to that list.
