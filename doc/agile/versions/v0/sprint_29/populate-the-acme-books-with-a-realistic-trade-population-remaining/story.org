* Goal

ACME's books hold a realistic trade population: every asset class the
platform can price, in the proportions a dealing institution holds, dated
relative to the day the tenant is provisioned, and referencing the
parties, books, counterparties and staff the tenant already holds.

The synthetic trade generation machinery produces it. That machinery is
scoped by tenant and by party and is driven by a trade generation
config, so ACME is the first tenant it serves and Northwind is the next.
No task in this story may name a tenant, a party, a book or a
counterparty. Those arrive from the config row. The only place that
names ACME is the ACME seed data.

The investigation this story commissioned is
[[id:6E648B05-2ADB-4E03-89CB-D4670CB72FC3][Investigation: Populating ACME with a trade population]]. It is the record of what was measured and which mechanism was chosen. It is a snapshot, not a description of the system.

* Acceptance

- One asset class at a time. A planner exists for every asset class in
  the refdata catalogue, and the planner for a class books the trade
  types the catalogue routes to that class.
- The population is a function of (seed, as-of). A review is a rebuild
  and a diff, and a second run books nothing.
- The product mix matches Trade Population Weights within a stated
  tolerance, and every routed product appears at least once.
- Every trade names a party, a book, a netting set and a counterparty
  the tenant holds.
- The population exports as the ORE portfolio the engine reads, and
  reverse-maps without loss.
- A second tenant runs the same machinery from its own config rows with
  no code change.

* Decisions

- One config row per tenant, party and asset class. The config already
  carries =asset_class_code= from the refdata catalogue: =interest_rates=,
  =fx=, =equity=, =credit=, =commodity=, =inflation= and =bond=. Coverage
  is therefore a row per family, and not a branch in the code.
- The plan is the seam, and not the writer. Today =trade_population_plan=
  and =trade_population_slot= are bond-shaped, holding =security_id=,
  =issue_id=, =issuer_id=, =tenor_years=, =calendar= and
  =reference_curve_id=, and =plan_population= refuses every asset class
  but =bond=. The plan must carry a payload that belongs to its family,
  each class must sit behind one planner interface, and one writer must
  execute any plan.
- The population size is an input. =trades_per_book= is a compile-time
  constant of 8 today. Move it into the config, so a tenant sizes its own
  population.
- The order follows [[id:A1310399-0ACD-4FA1-9645-AB24B3C8BEB8][Trade
  Population Weights]]: interest rates 42%, FX 25%, equity 12%, credit
  6%, commodity 5%, inflation 3% and scripted 3%. Bonds are 4% and are
  already delivered. Commodity is the exception to the order. It lands
  inside the seam task, because it is the only class every declared type
  of which routes, so it is the only one that tests the plan alone and
  not also the routing work.
- Unrouted trade types are in scope per class. 30 of the 121 catalogue
  types route nowhere: equity 13, FX 10, interest rates 3, credit 2,
  bond 1 and scripted 1. The task for a class either routes its holes or
  names them out of scope.
- Each class states the market data it prices on. A class whose curves or
  surfaces are absent still books and exports, and its task says which
  pricing it cannot prove yet.
