#+title: Synthetic prices are calibrated from free public sources
#+description: Which free public sources set the initial values and the volatility of the synthetic ticks, what each source's terms say, and what is still unchecked.
#+type: knowledge
#+level: cross
#+filetags: :marketdata:synthetic:calibration:
#+created: 2026-10-23
#+updated: 2026-10-23

* Summary

A synthetic tick needs an initial value and a volatility. The values come from free public sources, never from a vendor, and each generator config names its source. The sources are the ECB euro reference rates and the Federal Reserve H.10 release for FX, and the US Treasury par yield curve with the central banks' overnight rates for curves. A retrieval step reads the value that is as close to today as the source allows, stores it with the time of retrieval, and the screen shows that time. The terms of some sources are read from the official pages. The terms of the Treasury and of most Bank of England series are still open.

* Detail

** Why a calibration source and not a vendor

The synthetic ticks are native to the vendor: a Bloomberg generator sends Bloomberg ids. The level of the price has no link to the vendor. The level comes from the world, so it comes from a public source that anyone can check. The calibration source is a property of the generator config, and it does not depend on the provider.

** What this page does not settle

- Whether the Treasury feed carries a current licence. The next step is to read the current site policy of the Treasury.
- Which series of the Bank of England database other than SONIA fall under the Open Government Licence.
- Whether the FRED terms allow a copy of a series in a stored snapshot. The H.10 notes of each series say whether the word "Copyright" appears. The other route is the Board's own H.10 download, which the Board's public domain notice covers.
- Which of the 26 FX pairs the ECB and the H.10 do not cover. The pairs that no source covers keep a fixed level, and the config says so.

** How a generator uses a source

Each generator config carries a calibration entry: the source code, such as =ecb.eurofxref=, =fed.h10= or =ust.par_yield=, and a window for the volatility. The initial value is the latest value that the source has published. The volatility is the spread of the log returns over the window.

The value is retrieved by a step that is separate from the simulator. The step writes the value with the time it was retrieved and the as-of date of the source. The feed screen shows both, so a reader sees how old the level of a feed is. A source that cannot be reached leaves the last stored value in place, and the screen shows its age.

A copy of a source's statistics keeps the credit and the notice that its terms ask for, and a modified value is labelled as modified. The rule for the ECB is the strictest: a statistic that is changed after retrieval, such as a cross rate, is a derived value, and the config says it is derived.

** What this replaces

The 2016 ORE sample vintage no longer calibrates any generator. The datasets that depend on it are replaced when the sources are in place. The uniform demo is not calibrated from any source: its exaggerated volatility is the point, and it keeps fixed levels.
