* Plan

The inventory lists 22 trading resource routes in four groups. All four land on
one branch and one pull request: the first group's branch was cut before it was
merged, so the rest follow it there.

| Group | Routes | BFF source |
|-------+--------+------------|
| The part and the trade reads | 3 | =trades.ts= |
| Booking | 5 | =booking.ts= |
| Structures | 8 | =structures.ts= |
| Books and book changes | 6 | =books.ts=, =book-changes.ts= |

The blotter and the book tree are composed views and belong to
[[id:350D91AF-A4E0-43B0-849E-8188345DA443][Serve the trading composed views]].

Decisions made in the first group:

- The part is stood up by the adapter recipe, copied from =ores.http.synthetic=:
  its model, its four CMake files, the composite's parts list, the umbrella
  header, the aggregate, the file lists, the diagram, and the server's link and
  registration. It links =ores.refdata.api= beside =ores.trading.api=, because
  a trade's rows are named by their book and counterparty.
- A page's rows are the summary rows with five members added: the identifier a
  desk quotes, the counterparty's and book's names, and the trade's versions.
  The members are added to the row's JSON, as the BFF spread them into the row,
  so a summary member the BFF passed on is not restated by hand.
- An enrichment read is skipped when the page names nothing for it, where the
  BFF sent an empty one-of list; an empty list's meaning is the service's to
  state, and skipping it reads nothing.
- The structures of a trade, its terms' issue and a structure's reference are
  supplementary: a read of them that is refused answers none, as the BFF's did.

Decisions made in the second group:

- The acting party is the caller's own, read from the token's =party_id:=
  claim, where the BFF read it from its session. A session that states none
  offers no books, and refuses a booking.
- A booking body keeps the BFF's own member names, so the route reads it with
  its own shape rather than a protocol message, and checks every member as the
  BFF's schema did: an id, a calendar date that exists, a plain decimal, and
  the lengths of the two texts.
- The identifier and the bond instrument are written after the anchor and the
  booking, each with the activity the booking answered, because the protocol's
  instrument batch refuses a kind with no writer yet. The three writes are not
  one transaction, as the BFF's were not.
- A link is recorded against the newest state's activity, which the route
  reads and decides for itself rather than trusting the reply's order.
- The identifier schemes fall back to the codes the tenant's trades already
  carry when the tenant declares none, so a tenant provisioned before the list
  was copied can still book.
- The unit's helpers live in a =booking= namespace. Every unit of a component
  builds into one translation unit, so a helper named =array_of= in two units
  is a redefinition, not two functions.

Decisions made in the third group:

- The tree a structure's page draws is assembled in the gateway, as the BFF
  assembled it: the store holds a parent as an id and a leg as a member row, so
  the tenant's structures and members are read once and the root is found by
  walking up.
- A page's party is read from the legs, because a structure holds none of its
  own and the database pins every leg to one party. Its name is empty: the
  caller's token names the party by id, and the page already draws the id when
  the name is empty.
- The leg write states its body before it reads anything, so a caller that
  cannot say which trade and why is refused for that and not for the state of
  the structure.
- The structure catalogue is read whole and joined here, as the BFF joined it:
  a type's name is the catalogue's when it has one, and its home market decides
  the asset class.

Decisions made in the fourth group:

- A book or portfolio write forwards the whole row the screen states, not the
  three members the route checks: the BFF's schema let every column through, so
  the route reads the body as the protocol's own write shape, leniently, and
  checks the id, the party and the name.
- The rights read joins the accounts that hold them, which is IAM's data, so
  the part links =ores.iam.api=. The route is read only: the server addresses a
  right by its code alone, so a grant would collide and a removal would reach
  whichever account's row it found first.
- A proposal line is read in the client's own member names and mapped to the
  protocol's line. The request, the line number and the provenance are the
  server's to state, so a client that names them is read as naming the entity
  again.
- A raise that the service refuses as invalid is an answer and not an error:
  the maker has something to correct, so the reason comes back with no request
  id.

* Result

The trading part is stood up and serves the trading area's 22 resource routes:
the three trade reads, the five booking routes, the eight structure screens and
the six book screens, at the BFF's own addresses and in its shapes. The two
routes the area still lacks are its composed views, =/api/blotter= and
=/api/books/tree=, which belong to [[id:350D91AF-A4E0-43B0-849E-8188345DA443][Serve the trading composed views]].

The part links =ores.trading.api=, =ores.refdata.api= and =ores.iam.api=: a
trade's rows are named by their book and counterparty, and a portfolio's rights
are joined to the accounts that hold them.
