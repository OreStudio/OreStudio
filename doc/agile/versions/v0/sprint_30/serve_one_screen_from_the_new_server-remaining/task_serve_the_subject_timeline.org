* Goal

Serve =GET /api/timeline= from the gateway. The route answers one subject's story
for three subjects, and each is its own composition: a request is the story the
part already serves, wrapped with its subject and no gaps; a person is the
account, its contact record, the roles it was given, the parties it works in and
its sign-ins; a party is its own versions, the versions of each part it holds and
the links it keeps. Every source a caller may not read is a gap in the answer
rather than a failure, which is why the answer carries a list of gaps at all.

* Plan

The work was sized while the workflow composed views were served, and it was left
to this task on purpose: a person's timeline that silently left out the sign-ins,
or a party's that left out the parts it holds, would look complete while being
short, which is the failure worth refusing.

Each subject is served as its own family, smallest first, and each states its
composition once:

| Subject | What it composes | Standing gaps |
|+--------+------------------+---------------|
| request | the story the part already answers, wrapped with its subject | none |
| person | the account's own versions, the roles it was given and the parties it works in read as link histories, its contact record, and its sign-ins | the sign-in log is a rolling window, so an older sign-in is not shown |
| party | its own versions, the versions of each part it holds, and the links it keeps | no read lists a part that was removed from the party, so its closing is not shown |

The entity keys are =ores.iam.account=, =ores.iam.account_role=,
=ores.iam.account_party=, =ores.iam.account_contact_information= and
=ores.iam.auth_event= for a person, and =ores.refdata.party= with its parts and
links for a party. Every one of them is read through the gateway's own history
read, reached by =history_subject_for(entity_type)=, so no route invents a subject.

Two rules the families share with the rest of the part. The events are ordered by
the one rule the request story already uses: newest first, and within one second
the later act first, because the timestamps on the wire carry whole seconds. And a
read that fails is a fault while a read the caller may not make is a gap: only a
refusal becomes a gap, so a service that is down is not reported as a permission
the person lacks.

The answer carries an ETag and stays fresh for 30 seconds, as the inventory
records for this view.

Each family is finished when its subject answers at the BFF's address with the
BFF's member names and status codes, its events carry the kinds and fields the
screen draws, its gaps are stated with the reason the screen shows, and its route
is paired in the inventory.

* Notes

The subject a stream is read for is a word and not an entity type. That is the
BFF's own split, and it is kept because it is the right one: which rows make up a
person's story is the server's business, and a browser that named the entity
types would be stating the composition and would then drift from it.

Three compositions, one of them new. A request's stream is the story the inbox
already answers, wrapped, so the two subjects cannot become two shapes. A
person's and a party's are composed here, from the reads their owning components
already served, and neither invents a subject: each entity type reaches its own
read through =history_subject_for=.

The gap list is the part worth reading twice. A person's is not symmetric with a
party's, because what the product cannot say is not symmetric. A sign-in log keeps
a window, and the stream reads the most recent 200, so an older sign-in is not
shown: that is a fact about the read and it is stated even for a person who has
never signed in. A part removed from a party is closed rather than deleted, and no
read lists a closed row, so the party's stream states that for each kind of part
instead of showing a story that looks complete.

A refusal becomes a gap; a failure does not. The BFF put both in the same list,
which read a service that was down as a permission the person lacked. Here only
the refusal the owning component states is a gap, and a transport failure is
thrown so the route answers that the service did not answer. The history step
states its three bad answers - no read for the type, no permission, a read that
failed - as an answer that did not come, and the composition turns each of them
into a gap with the sentence the screen shows.

The compositions are proved against a live deployment, not only by construction.
The database was recreated from this checkout, the demo tenant was provisioned, and
the gateway the systemd unit runs answered all three subjects over HTTP with the
cookie a sign-in sets. The run is =evidence/timeline_live_run.txt=: 20 events for
the person, 492 for the party, 3 for the request, and the address refusing what the
BFF refused. The schema and the tree agree, at fingerprint =cabc2ecdbf1f608e=.

A party's rows answer only to a session acting as that party, so the party the
stream is read for must be the party the session chose: the party's own record is
tenant-scoped and reads either way, while its parts and links are party-scoped and
read as empty for any other. This is the deployment's scoping and not this view's,
and the run chooses the party it reads.

What a live run does not prove. The gap for a source the caller may not read was
not exercised against a real refusal, because the demo tenant's administrator holds
every read this view asks for. The path is proved by the unit tests that state the
refusal and by the code that turns a refusal into a gap, not by a refused live
read.

The one instant formatter is not restated here. The entries carry the instants the
history layer stamped and the protocol's own text form writes them, as every other
answer in the part does.

* Result

request at the BFF's address, with its member names and status codes, so the
workflow area serves all 18 of the routes the inventory counts for it and 288 of the
BFF's 333 are paired.

A request's stream is the story the inbox already answers, wrapped, because the
entry and its one ordering left that route and became the part's own. A person's is
the account, its contact record, the roles it was given, the parties it works in and
its sign-ins. A party's is its own versions, the versions of each part it holds and
the opening and closing of each link it keeps. No route invents a subject: every
entity type reaches its read through the gateway's own history step, which is now
stated once and carries the service's answer whether or not it answered.

The gaps are the part worth reading twice, because what the product cannot say is
not the same for a person and a party. A person's sign-in log keeps a window, and
the stream reads the most recent 200. A part removed from a party is closed, and no
read lists a closed row, so the party's stream says so for each kind of part
instead of showing a story that looks complete. A source the caller may not read is
a gap; a source that fails is thrown and answered as the service not answering, so
a service that is down is never reported as a permission the person lacks. The one
thing the reply cannot distinguish - no read, no permission, a failed read, all
stated in one flag - is stated as what is true of all three, and the shared read
says why.
