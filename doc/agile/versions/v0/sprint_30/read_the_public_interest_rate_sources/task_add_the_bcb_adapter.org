* Goal

The annualised CDI is stored as fixings in the system tenant, with its history, read from the Banco Central do Brasil through the SGS API, =api.bcb.gov.br/dados/serie/bcdata.sgs.4389/dados=, in JSON. The adapter is specialised: it reads the Banco Central do Brasil alone, and it supplies only its fetcher to the shared half of the official adapter.

* Acceptance

- The adapter reads the Banco Central do Brasil alone, and it does not copy the announce, the demand, the backfill or the publish code.
- The Banco Central do Brasil is a supplier in the catalogue, its series maps to the BRL-CDI item, and a session and a feed scoped to that item are seeded in the system tenant.
- A fresh environment stores the history as fixings within minutes of provisioning, and the log names the source and the count it stored.
- Each fixing is the decimal of the published percent, with the credit "Source: B3, through the Banco Central do Brasil".
- The depth of the history is measured and written into the calibration sources note.
- Unit tests parse a recorded response, and a malformed response gives an error, not a fixing.

* Plan

- =bcb_fetcher= reads a series of the SGS API. Its key is the series number, such as =4389=, of one to nine digits.
- The API serves a daily series in windows of at most ten years, so the fetcher asks in parts of five years.
- The API answers both an empty window (a JSON 404, "Value(s) not found") and an unknown series (a text 404, "A serie nao existe!") with a 404. The fetcher uses =read_window=, with =/dados/ultimos/1= as the latest value, to tell them apart.
- Dates are =dd/MM/yyyy=, and values are percents as text, so =decimal_of_percent= keeps every published digit.
- The provider's terms point at B3's financial indicators page, because B3 computes the CDI, and the credit names both B3 and the BCB.
- The tests replay recorded answers through a stub transport. No test reads the API.
