* Acceptance

- ores_trading_swap_leg_amounts_tbl and ores_trading_swap_leg_rates_tbl exist, keyed by the trade, the leg ordinal and an entry ordinal.
- swap_leg no longer carries notional, fixed_rate or spread; the children carry them.
- map_leg reads every notional and every rate or spread, not the first of each.
- The import writes the children and the export reads them back, so a stepped leg round trips.
- The component regenerates drift-clean and builds, and the codegen suite passes.

* Plan

The leg's economics become rows of their own and the wire carries them, in
five parts.

1. Two child models, =swap_leg_amount= and =swap_leg_rate=, keyed by the trade,
   the leg ordinal and their own ordinal, with =rate_role= naming the arm that
   stated the rate (=fixed= or =spread=).
2. =swap_leg= loses =notional=, =fixed_rate= and =spread=: a single column
   cannot hold a schedule, and keeping both would state one fact twice.
3. =map_leg= stops taking the first of each list; =map_leg_amounts= and
   =map_leg_rates= read every entry, including the =Amortizations= arm when
   the document states a schedule instead of a list.
4. The carrier =swap_instrument_data= gains the two collections, and the
   hand-written payload codec carries them, so the export can rebuild the
   lists it reads.
5. The import writes the children after the legs, and the export reads them
   back through a batch read on each child.

The children are flat, like the =instrument_*= children beside them: no
identity field group and no audit group, so their domain class is one struct.

* Result

A leg's notionals and its rates are rows of their own, and the import keeps
every one of them.

- *Two child tables.* =swap_leg_amount= holds one notional per row, dated from
  the day it applies; =swap_leg_rate= holds one rate or spread, with
  =rate_role= naming the arm of the document that stated it. Both are keyed by
  the trade, the leg ordinal and their own ordinal.
- *=swap_leg= lost its economics columns.* =notional=, =fixed_rate= and
  =spread= are gone, because a single column cannot hold a schedule and
  keeping both would state the same fact twice.
- *The mapper keeps the whole list.* =map_leg= read
  =first_notional(...)=, =Rates.Rate.front()= and =Spreads.Spread.front()= and
  dropped the rest; the new =map_leg_amounts= and =map_leg_rates= read every
  entry, and read the =Amortizations= arm when the document states a schedule
  rather than a list. The amortisation's own type, end date and frequency are
  not carried yet, and the reason is that this row states a value and a start
  date.
- *The children cross the wire.* =swap_instrument_data= gained the two
  collections and the hand-written payload codec carries them, with a
  fallback tier for a payload written before the change, because the export
  rebuilds a leg from the payload and nothing else.
- *The import writes them and the export reads them back*, through a batch
  read on each child that mirrors the legs' own.

Verification: the component regenerates with no drift; the codegen suite is
1276 passed; the full preset build exits 0 with no errors and no compiler
warnings; and the ORE mapper round-trip suite is 614 cases and 482159
assertions, all passing, against a database recreated from this branch. That
last one is the proof that matters: the golden IR and swaption documents state
stepped legs, the export rebuilds them from the children, and the comparison
is byte-level.

Not run: the trading service's eventing integration tests. They need the

fleet.
