* Summary

ores.qt had a QA Validation Runner. A tester opened a =test_scenario= doc, saw
its steps, marked each PASS or FAIL with a note, and saved. The save wrote the
results back into the doc. The prototype brings that to the browser as a regular
module with two screens: the queue of scenarios waiting and done, and the runner
that works through one scenario. The story and the task that drive the scenario
sit beside its steps.

It is throwaway. It is not production code, it reads no service, and every
value on it is mock data.

Open the [[file:test-scenarios/][prototype]].

* Detail

** The verdict

Accepted on 2026-10-10: variant B, one step at a time. A Pass moves on to the
next step. A Fail stays on the step, so the tester can write the note first. The
rejected layouts stay in the prototype, switchable from the bar at the foot of
the page. The queue carries no heading of its own, because the Tests tab names it.

** The queue

One design, because the choice of runner layout does not change it. Waiting
scenarios come first, then done ones. A row shows the scenario, the story and
task behind it, its target, its progress and its state. A notification in the
bell and a row in the queue open the same scenario.

** The three runner variants

| Variant | Shape | What it settles | What it gives up |
|---------+-------+-----------------+------------------|
| A · Steps beside the detail | A list of steps on the left, the chosen step on the right | The tester sees the whole scenario and one whole step at once | It needs a wide window |
| B · One step at a time (accepted) | A progress bar, one step, and the verdict buttons. A Pass moves on and a Fail stays | It fits a narrow window beside the application under test | The tester sees the rest of the scenario only on request |
| C · The document | Every step in order with its controls under it | It reads like the org page, so a finished scenario is easy to review | A long scenario makes the tester scroll |

** The overall result is inferred

The tester marks steps. Any FAIL makes the scenario FAILED. All PASS makes it
PASSED. Anything else leaves it PENDING. The same rule ran in the Qt tool.

** The cases the bar switches

- *Queue* shows what waits and what is done.
- *Running* opens a scenario with one step marked.
- *Reopened* opens a passed scenario read-only, with a Retest button.
- *Failed step* opens a scenario with a failed step, a note and a screenshot.

** What the screens cannot do, and say

1. *Nothing reads or writes a scenario.* The BFF has no scenario store. ores.web
   cannot rely on compass, so the BFF must parse the docs compass creates and
   write results into them itself, behind an interface that a database store can
   replace later.
2. *No notification for a waiting test.* The inbox raises none. The queue is
   every scenario in state PENDING.
3. *A scenario has no tester.* The doc carries a target and an environment and
   no assignee.
4. *No image upload.* A browser cannot capture the screen, so a step takes a
   pasted or dropped image. No BFF route saves it beside the scenario.
5. *No org renderer in the web package.* The parser in =ores.org-js= is the one
   to port.
6. *A deployed server has no work tree.* Where scenarios and results live in
   production is open.
7. *A retest overwrites.* It rewrites the result in the org file and git keeps
   the history. Keeping each run is the alternative, and the prototype does not
   choose. A retest asks for confirmation first.

** A scenario closes when it is saved decided

Marking steps never closes a scenario. A save closes it when no step is pending,
and a retest opens it again. A scenario that arrives done opens closed and
read-only. A first version of the page derived this from the steps and locked a
scenario the moment a tester marked a step Fail. The review caught it.
