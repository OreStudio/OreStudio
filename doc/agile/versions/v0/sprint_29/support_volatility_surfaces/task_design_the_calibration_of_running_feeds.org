* Goal

The story states what happens after provisioning: a feed resumes from its own
last stored spot after any restart, takes a newly approved calibration at its
next market day roll without a restart, and is re-anchored to the public rates
only by an explicit, recorded operator act.

* Plan

Read what a restart does in the code and the journal, and where the GJR-GARCH
process holds its state, to see what a running feed needs to stay continuous and
where it can take a new calibration. Weigh three ways to keep the level near the
public rates, and record the chosen one, a free-running level with an explicit
re-anchor, with the two new decisions and three tasks.

* Result

The story's Decisions record two more entries: a running feed is continuous, and
takes a new calibration at its market day roll; and a running feed's level
follows its own path, and only an operator re-anchors it (option A of three). The
approval decision, the schedule task and the diagram now say a running feed
takes a new calibration at its market day roll, not at its next start.

Three tasks build it, placed after the release: resume a feed from its last
stored spot, by writing the config's level from it as a new version, so each
restart and its initial price show in the history; take a new calibration at the
market day roll; and re-anchor a class to the public rates. The size of a
restart's jump was not measured; the
decisions rest on what the code and the journal show, that a restart begins at
the fixing and reads no price the run had reached.
