#+title: ores.marketdata.series_identity_shape
#+description: The shape of a series family's identity: which family it is, and what it is for. Its entries state the fields it requires and forbids.
#+type: ores.codegen.entity
#+component: marketdata
#+filetags: :model:entity:marketdata:
#+entity_singular: series_identity_shape
#+entity_plural: series_identity_shapes
#+entity_title: Series Identity Shape
#+brief: The identity shape of one series family, named by a code and described in words.
#+created: 2026-11-15
#+updated: 2026-11-19

The shape of a market series identity, one row per family.

A family is the pair of codes the taxonomy already carries -- the asset class
and the series subclass -- narrowed, for an index series, by the index family.
=index_fixing= is not one shape but several: an IBOR fixing states a currency
and an index name, a swap fixing a currency and a tenor, an FX fixing a currency
and the currency it is quoted in. A shape keyed on the subclass alone would be
wrong about all but one of them, so the family carries the index family too.

A shape is named by a code, such as =ir.index_fixing.ibor=, so a person and a
screen can refer to it by one key, and it says in words what the family is for.
What the family makes of each field is stated by its entries,
=series_identity_shape_entries=: one row per field, =required= or =forbidden=.

The writer that projects an identity row from an oresmd URI reads the shapes
and their entries, and refuses a row that misses a required field or carries a
forbidden one, naming the family and the field. A family with no shape is not
enforced: the writer projects its series unguarded, and the backfill counts them
as unstated. The catalogue grows family by family, and refusing every family
nobody has stated yet would stop an ingest for a reason that is the catalogue's.

** code

:sortable:    true

:no_generator_suffix: true
:END:

The shape's code: the asset class, the series subclass and, for an index
series, the index family, joined by dots, such as =ir.index_fixing.ibor= or
=fx.spot=. The entries name their shape by it. A check holds the code to the
family's parts, so a code never names one family while the row states another.

A generated shape takes its index family from the generator's counter, and its
code from the same parts, so each one holds the check and a family of its own.

#+begin_src cpp :name generator
std::string("ir.index_fixing.g") + std::to_string(idx)
#+end_src

** asset_class
:PROPERTIES:
:type:        text
:cpp_type:    std::string
:nullable:    false
:sortable:    true
:END:

The family's asset class, as the oresmd authority spells it and as the identity
row carries it: =ir=, =fx=, =credit=. This is the codec's spelling and not
=ores.refdata.asset_class_code='s -- refdata calls the same class
=interest_rates= -- so the column is checked against the oresmd schema rather than
against that catalogue, and a family keyed by one spelling and looked up by the
other would never match.

#+begin_src cpp :name generator
std::string("ir")
#+end_src

** index_family
:PROPERTIES:
:type:        text
:cpp_type:    std::string
:nullable:    false
:default:     ''
:skip_empty_check: true
:sortable:    true
:END:

The index family the shape is for, as the identity's =index= column spells it:
=ibor=, =swap=, =fx=. A datum family has no index and holds the empty string.

=ir=/=index_fixing= is two shapes, not one: an IBOR fixing is named by its
index, a swap fixing by its tenor, and a shape that stated one of them for both
would be wrong about one of them. So the family carries the index family too.

std::string("g") + std::to_string(idx)
#+end_src
