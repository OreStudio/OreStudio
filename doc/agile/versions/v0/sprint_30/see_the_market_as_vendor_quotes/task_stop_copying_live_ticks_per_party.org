* Acceptance

- No code publishes or subscribes to the party live subjects.
- The ingest publishes each tick once, and its publish count no longer grows with the number of parties.
- For the same run, the delivery meter holds the same count for each party, provider and item before and after the copies go; a test compares the two.
- The docs name only the vendor subjects.

* Plan

1. Remove the per-party publish in =announce()=, and give each tick one feed
   publish whose outcome every route of the tick reads: sent, failed or late.
2. Count one delivery for each route of a tick whose feed publish was sent, as the
   copy for that route was counted before.
3. Remove =tick_target::subject=, =market_tick_subject()= and
   =market_tick_subject_all()=, and their tests.
4. Move the loop tests onto the feed subject, and show the meter's count for two
   parties of one tick, and that nothing is published under the tenant's old
   subtree.

* Result

- The ingest publishes each tick once, on its feed's subject, and no copy for any
  party. =market_tick_subject()=, =market_tick_subject_all()= and the target's
  subject are gone.
- The meter counts one delivery for each route of a tick whose feed publish went
  out. A loop test drains the meter after a tick of two routed parties and finds
  one delivery for each, under the provider, feed and item, as the copies made it.
  The same test sees one publish on the feed and none under the tenant's old
  subtree.
- Suites: market data API 1134 cases, market data service 317, shell application
  65, HTTP market data 34; all pass. The ticks smoke test passes. On the live
  broker, the Acme tenant's old subtree carried no tick in 20 seconds while the
  Bloomberg feed subject carried ticks.
- Hedgr used =market_tick_subject_all()= and the per-party subtree. It loses its
  live ticks and does not build against these headers until it moves; the
  product owner chose to remove the copies now. The move is noted on the Hedgr
  task.
