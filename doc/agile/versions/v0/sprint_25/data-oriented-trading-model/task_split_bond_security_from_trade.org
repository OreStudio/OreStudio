* Acceptance

- The shared bond entities are keyed by the ORE reference datum id, which is SecurityId for a bond and ContractName for a bond future.
- bond_future keeps only what ORE keeps on the trade and its contract terms move to a shared entity.
- bond_leg and its rate, amount and amortization tables hang off the security rather than the instrument.
- bond_issue gains the six fields ORE has and we lack, and loses bond_notional, which ORE holds on the trade.
- The fields duplicated against the leg tables — currency, coupon rate, coupon frequency, day count — are held once.
- A trade that states its bond inline rather than by reference still round-trips.
- The ORE round-trip stays at zero loss across the bond products and the trade envelope.

* Plan

ORE is the ground truth. The job is to apply data-oriented principles
to ORE's data model, not to invent one, and not to diverge without
recording why.

** What ORE's model says

=ReferenceData= is a list of =ReferenceDatum=. Each datum carries an
=id= attribute, an optional =validFrom=, a =Type= element, and one
payload from a closed choice of thirteen. So the key is the datum id,
not a business name: a bond's is its =SecurityId=, a bond future's is
its =ContractName=.

Three things follow that we already believe. =id= plus =Type= plus a
closed choice is the explicit-variation rule from
[[id:BBD43BF2-9FEE-42B0-A667-9468AF759DC2][data-oriented design]] — per-type tables against a closed variant, not
an inheritance web. =validFrom= means ORE already treats this data as
time-varying, which our bitemporal tables carry natively. And because
the key is a datum id, no entity needs the words "reference data" in
its name: we name the thing, as =bond_issue= already does, which also
keeps clear of =ores.refdata=.

** The relationship the schema states

=bondReferenceDatum= is exactly =bondData= minus =SecurityId=,
=BondNotional=, =Payer= and =CreditRisk=.

That is the split, stated by ORE itself. Everything else on a bond is
static data about the security. The four exceptions are the trade's
own: which security, how much, which side, and whether credit risk
applies.

=bondFutureData= says the same thing in the other direction: v17 cut
it to =ContractName=, =ContractNotional= and =LongShort=, plus the new
=ApplyConversionFactor= and =UseFuturePrice=, and moved the thirteen
contract terms into =bondFutureReferenceDatum=.

** Where our model disagrees

Every bond table hangs off =instrument_id=, and =bond_instrument=
carries =trade_id=. So all of it is per trade:

- =bond_future= holds twelve contract terms that belong to the
  contract. Ten trades on one Bund future repeat them ten times.
- =bond_leg= and its rate, amount and amortization tables hold the
  coupon schedule, currency and day count. In ORE these are =LegData=
  /inside/ =bondReferenceDatum=, so they belong to the security.
- =bond_issue= is the one entity already shared — no =trade_id=, keyed
  by =security_id= — and it is =bondReferenceDatum= under another
  name. It is missing =CreditGroup=, =VolatilityCurveId=,
  =PriceQuoteMethod=, =PriceQuoteBaseValue=, =SubType= and
  =PriceType=; it carries =bond_notional=, which ORE puts on the
  trade; and its =currency=, =coupon_rate=, =coupon_frequency_code=
  and =day_count_code= duplicate the leg tables.

So the pattern to follow already exists in our own model. =bond_issue=
is the shape; the rest of the bond family does not follow it.

** The decision this turns on

ORE lets a trade state its bond either way: =bondData= carries
=SecurityId= /and/ every static field, so a portfolio can be
self-contained or can lean on a reference datum. Our model has to
round-trip both, which is what the inline acceptance criterion is for.
Normalising on the security is right; the inline case then populates
the security rather than bypassing it.

** Sequence

Shared entities first, because the per-trade tables point at them.
Model, mapper and tests move together — no commit removes a column
before its replacement exists. The component audit runs before the
task closes.
