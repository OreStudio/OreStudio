* Goal

No free source publishes an interest rate volatility quote, exactly as none
publishes an FX one. Interest rates is the first class to run through the shared engine, so
this task adds the three parameters and no measurement of its own: the transform, the
coordinates and the smile model. The values are the platform's own arithmetic, produced by
=orestudio= and held internal.

* Acceptance

- The derivation calls the shared engine and adds no measurement of its own. A measurement it needs is added to the engine, and the FX values are unchanged by the addition.
- The transform is a difference of the rate, in basis points, because a rate may be negative and its change is not a ratio.
- The window is the pillar's own length in trading days and never shorter than the engine's minimum, and a pillar whose history does not cover its window is refused alone, with the returns it needs.
- The level is a realized volatility in basis points a year, times an authored multiplier, and the multiplier is data and not a constant in the code.
- The projection to an expiry and a term is the closed form of the short-rate model the currency's curve runs. The derivation names the model and refuses a process with no closed form instead of approximating it.
- The smile across strikes is a parametric model over the shape of the same returns, and the derivation names the model and its parameters.
- Every value carries the method and the version that computed it, and is stored under =orestudio= with internal standing: no non-system party reads it and no meter counts it.

* Notes

- The series: the overnight fixing history for the short end, and the history of the term's own rate for a term. A term the curves do not carry is refused, never interpolated from the short end.
- The pieces the engine reuses: =fx_derivation_window=, the moments and the shrink to the prior, =level_multiplier=, the reach, and the method and version a series is stamped with.
- The seeded curves carry a per-currency =kappa= and =sigma= calibrated to the public rates (=synthetic_ir_curve_generation_configs=). The derivation reads the history; the process is what carries the measurement across expiry and term.
- The smokehouse package carries 520 =GBP-LIBOR-12M= fixings, which is the kind of history a term reads, and the public overnight fixings give the short end.
