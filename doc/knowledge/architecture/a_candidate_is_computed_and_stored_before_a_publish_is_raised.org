#+title: A candidate is computed and stored before a publish is raised
#+description: Every kind of derived market object is computed by an operation of its own into a stored, versioned candidate with its lineage and checks, so a decider approves numbers that exist.
#+type: knowledge
#+level: cross
#+filetags: :architecture:marketdata:publish:
#+created: 2026-11-11
#+updated: 2026-11-11

* Summary

A publish is a decision about numbers, so the numbers exist before anyone is asked. Each kind has a compute operation that computes a candidate for a subject at an as-of and stores it, with its lineage, its inputs and its checks, as a version. The raise names that version, a check that blocks refuses the raise, the decider reads the rest, and the apply publishes the version. Nothing is computed at the raise or at the apply.

* Detail

** What a candidate is

The compute writes the candidate's points against the subject's output series at the as-of, each with a lineage record naming the series it read and the version of the recipe that produced it ([[id:573F9165-C20B-463C-B0B3-F324AFE4E81F][Curve point provenance]]). The output series is the subject's own, named by its oresmd identity ([[id:C3E053CA-0D4B-480B-9119-E11530160EC1][oresmd: ORE Studio Market Data URI]]). The candidate is traceable and it is not authoritative.

A candidate's points carry their candidate version. A read of the output series by as-of answers through the read rule ([[id:86158AAD-423A-421A-8BE2-CC5123109297][A reader takes the newest published set at or before its as-of]]), which takes only published sets, so no generic read, screen or export reaches a candidate. A candidate is read only by the candidate reads of its kind: the trader inspecting it, and the decider.

** One candidate per subject and as-of, versioned

A candidate is keyed by its subject, its as-of and its version. A compute whose recipe and inputs are unchanged converges on the existing version and writes nothing new, so a retried compute does not multiply versions. A compute with a changed recipe or changed inputs writes a new version and keeps the old one. A request names the version it was raised for, so a decision never covers numbers computed after it.

** The inputs

A candidate records the published sets it was computed from, such as the two curve publishes a forward set reads, with the state of each at the compute. The apply refuses a candidate whose inputs were withdrawn after the compute ([[id:86D8986F-5247-4138-85C7-F8899C029F24][The trader applies an approved publish, which supersedes the live set and tells its readers]]).

** The checks

The compute runs the kind's checks and stores their result with the version. Each check is blocking or advisory. A blocking check failed means the candidate cannot be published, under any rule: a surface with calendar or butterfly arbitrage, a curve whose bootstrap did not converge, a forward set whose input curve is withdrawn. An advisory check is a fact the decider weighs, such as a pillar error within tolerance. The raise refuses a candidate with a failed blocking check ([[id:BF14C932-D699-4224-98DA-0481E49FA394][A publish raise names one candidate version, in one transaction]]), so a request closed without a person never publishes numbers that failed one.

** Why before the raise

A decider approves what the trader saw. If the numbers were computed at the apply, the published set would be whatever the market held at that moment, and the approval would cover nothing in particular. If they were computed at the raise, a trader could not inspect them before asking.
