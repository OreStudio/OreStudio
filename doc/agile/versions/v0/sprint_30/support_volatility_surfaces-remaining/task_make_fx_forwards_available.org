* Goal

The system states the FX forward of any pair that has spot and published funding curves. A reader sees four measures for each tenor: the FX forward rate, the discount factors, the forward points and the swap points. A new screen shows them. The volatility surface calibration reads its forwards from the same code. No forward is sampled or typed: each one comes from the spot and the two published IR curves. See [[id:49687AD7-396A-4CD8-B96B-B3F6675C9779][Covered Interest Parity and FX Forward Pricing]].

* Acceptance

- A pure function in =ores.analytics.quant= takes a spot, a spot date, a pip factor, tenor dates and two discount curves. It returns the forward rate, both discount factors, the forward points and the swap points for each tenor.
- The forward at date T is S * (DF_base(T) / DF_base(spot date)) / (DF_quote(T) / DF_quote(spot date)), with S in quote currency per unit of base currency.
- Forward points are (F(T) - S) / pip factor. Swap points are (F(far) - F(near)) / pip factor, and the near leg defaults to spot.
- ON and TN state the swap of their own two dates, so their swap points differ from their forward points.
- The tests hold the parity by a round trip: money invested in the base currency and covered at the forward equals money invested in the quote currency.
- A read operation answers one pair at one as-of, and names the published set of each currency and the spot observation it used.
- A missing spot, a missing published funding curve, a missing pair convention and a tenor beyond a curve are each refused, and the refusal names what is missing.
- A screen shows one pair at one as-of, as a ladder of the four measures and a chart of the measure the user selects. A control sets the start of the ladder to today, tomorrow or spot, and marks the rows it moves. The screen shows spot and the pip factor.
- The screen labels the numbers as implied by the IR curves, with no cross-currency basis.

** Gaps

1. A fresh database holds no market values. It has 0 observations, 0 curve publishes, 0 =fx_spot= series and 0 rows in the published pair and pair convention tables. A live proof needs feeds, a bootstrap, a publish of both curves and a publish of the conventions.
2. The spot lag has no home on the pair. =ores_refdata_currency_pair_conventions_tbl= has no =spot_days= column. The ORE import reads =SpotDays= into =fx_convention=, and the currency table has its own =spot_days=. I did not trace what the import stores. The plan is to take the larger =spot_days= of the two currencies, and to refuse if either is absent.
3. Spot exists for a USD hub only: the seeds name 14 currencies, and curves exist for 20 different ones. NOK has spot and no curve. A cross such as EUR/GBP needs a triangulated spot. =CRM_DERIVATION= suggests a cross-rates source, which I did not trace.
4. The curves are single-currency OIS, so the result is pure parity with no cross-currency basis. ORE example configurations discount through curves such as =USD-IN-EUR=, built from FX forward and basis quotes. An ORE price under such a configuration will not match these forwards.

** Decisions proposed

- Compute on read and store nothing. A forward is a pure function of two published sets and one spot, so a stored copy can only go stale. Add stored =fx_fwd= observations only when a consumer needs them, such as an ORE export of =FXFWD= quotes.
- Work on dates and discount factors, never on year fractions. The curves mix A360 and A365F, and a ratio of discount factors does not depend on them.
- Forward points run from spot to the tenor. Swap points run from a near leg to a far leg. The two are equal for a spot-start swap, so a selector for the near leg is what makes swap points useful.
- Spot is the latest =fx_spot= observation at or before the as-of. The result names it, with its age.

** Ground truth

Do not port the expected value of =testFairForwardRate= from QuantLib. =DiscountingFxForwardEngine= computes S * D_target / D_source and documents S as target per source. With the test's own data (S = 1.35 SGD per USD, USD 5%, SGD 3.5%, six months), that gives 1.3602, and parity gives 1.3399. The covered round trip balances only at 1.3399. The test only compares the formula with itself. QuantExt agrees with parity: =FxRateQuote= converts spot to today with S * D_target / D_source, the inverse direction. The port uses this fixture with the round trip as its oracle.

=FXFWD= and =FXFWD/RATE= quotes in ORE are points, scaled by =PointsFactor=, so an outright must not be written under that key without the scaling.

The knowledge page on parity needs one correction: it says the basis is added on top of the forward, while ORE builds cross-currency curves from forward and basis quotes. Fix it when this task closes.
