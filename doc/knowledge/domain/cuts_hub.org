This structure note gathers the cuts cluster. It states the definition, the body
a cut must hold, the components the body divides into, and the order in which to
read the pages. The classification of kinds is a page of its own. Return to
[[id:7474935C-95D1-3034-B833-83DCD9FB3A09][Knowledge]].

* Summary

A *cut* fixes a body of data at one timepoint. The purpose of a cut in this
repository is single: to provide a valuation that is fully reproducible. The
body therefore holds everything the valuation reads, which includes the deals,
the market data, the reference data, and the versions of the engine and of the
code that computed the result. The kinds of cut record the occasion a cut is
taken for, and the components record the two halves of the body.

* The definition

A *cut* fixes a body of data at one timepoint. Three parts follow, and each
answers a separate question.

- The *label* names the cut. A code, a reference date or a version serves as the
  handle that a report, a deal or a book carries.
- The *timepoint* fixes the cut. It is the instant held still, with its timezone.
- The *object* is the body of data the cut fixes. It may be a deal population, a
  market data set, both, or anything else a valuation reads.

The label answers which cut is meant. The timepoint answers when it holds. The
object answers what it holds. A statement that omits one of the three leaves the
cut unidentified, and a claim about a cut carries the three together.

The sentence fixing the definition constrains two things. Something is fixed, and
one instant fixes it. Every further property of a cut is a fact about the kind:
how often the instant recurs, which location owns it, and which process takes it.
Those facts qualify a cut without entering the definition, and
[[id:33E879B7-496A-406D-B3F2-72637379AD02][Kinds of cut]] works out what
follows from that.

* The kinds

A kind records the occasion a cut is taken for. The purpose is single and fixes
the body; the kind fixes the instant, the recurrence and the centre, and the use
the result is put to. The classification, and the argument for it, is on
[[id:33E879B7-496A-406D-B3F2-72637379AD02][Kinds of cut]].

* The purpose and the body

Within ORE Studio the purpose of a cut is single: to provide a valuation that is
fully reproducible. An official report, a verification of prices, and a view for
a desk are uses a cut is put to, and every one of them is served by a valuation
only if that valuation can be reproduced. The single purpose contains the
others, which is why the body has one size and not several.

The size of the body follows from that purpose and from nothing else. The
argument has three steps, and a public statement supports each of them.

The first step fixes what reproducibility is. A computation is reproduced when
the same software runs on the same input data and returns the same result:

#+begin_quote
running the same software on the same input data and obtaining the same results
#+end_quote

The sentence is Claerbout's definition, quoted by Plesser
([[https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5778115/][Plesser, *Reproducibility
vs. Replicability*]]). It places the software inside the condition rather than
beside it.

The second step establishes that the software and its settings are inputs. A
change to a program can change its output, and the change need not be large:

#+begin_quote
Even the slightest change to a computer program can have large intended or
unintended consequences.
#+end_quote

That sentence is Sandve et al.'s fourth rule
([[https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1003285][Sandve
et al., *Ten Simple Rules for Reproducible Computational Research*]]). The same
paper states what must therefore be recorded:

#+begin_quote
If the step is performed by a computer program, the critical details include the
name and version of the program, as well as the exact parameters and inputs that
were used.
#+end_quote

and, for the version specifically:

#+begin_quote
In order to exactly reproduce a given result, it may be necessary to use programs
in the exact versions used originally.
#+end_quote

The third step establishes that the environment around the code enters as well.
The Turing Way states the requirement in one sentence:

#+begin_quote
In order for research to be reproducible, the computational environment that it
was conducted in must be captured in such a way that others can replicate it.
#+end_quote

and gives the mechanism with an example in which the same script returns two
answers:

#+begin_quote
Therefore this simple script returns different answers depending on the
computational environment in which it is run.
#+end_quote

The philosophical literature draws the same boundary around computation. The
Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy describes computational reproducibility as

#+begin_quote
reproducing particular analysis outcomes from the same data set using the same
code and software
#+end_quote

adding that this is the most common form of it. Plesser, whose paper is the
source of the Claerbout sentence, states the practical consequence in his own
words:

#+begin_quote
I consider it to be essential to preserve artifacts such as software, scripts,
and input data underlying computational science publications.
#+end_quote

The three steps share one conclusion. Data, code, settings and environment belong
to one class: each is an input whose variation changes the output. A cut holds
the valuation's inputs, so it holds that class in full. A cut therefore holds
five parts.

- The *deals*. The population the valuation values.
- The *market data*. Spots, curves, forward points, volatility surfaces,
  fixings, and the model parameters that are driven by the market.
- The *reference data*. The conventions, the calendars and holiday sets, the
  valuation and pricing settings, the curve configurations, and anything else
  the run reads to turn a deal and a quote into a number.
- The *engine and the code*. The version of the risk engine and the revision of
  the code that computed the result.
- The *identity and the authorisation*. The label, the timepoint, the signatory
  and the version.

The list admits a test. Take a result the cut claims to support and ask which
changes would make the number differ. Each of the five parts answers yes. A
proposed field that answers no is provenance about the run, and provenance is
worth keeping, but the body is the set of things whose variation moves the
number.

A cut that fixed the data and omitted the engine version would hold a body from
which its own result does not follow. The omission survives casual review
because the halves read as one variable and one constant: the market moves, and
the software waits. The software waits only until the next deployment.

The component pages below take the two data halves of this body. The settings,
the engine and the code have pages of their own, and this cluster holds them
because the cut records them.

* Components and their order

The body divides into two components, the deals and the market data, and the
division has consequences for the process that fixes them.

The components differ in owner, in review and in mode of failure. A deal is
reviewed by reading it and comparing it to what the desk booked, and it fails by
being absent or wrong. A rate is reviewed by comparing it to an external source,
and it fails by being stale or unapproved. A corrected deal moves no rate, and
an approved rate moves no deal. One act that fixed both would tie every trade
correction to a fresh approval of the day's rates, and would therefore slow the
correction the review exists to serve.

Our working convention follows from that. The two components are fixed by
separate acts, the deals first and the market data second. The deal cut is
retaken until the population is clean, because a person may still correct a deal
in production. The market data cut follows, since a provisional market data set
is needed to value the deals and confirm they are right before the day's rates are
committed.

* Reading order

The classification comes first, because purpose fixes the body and the instant a
kind contributes. The component pages then take the definition in the two
directions the body admits, and the last page takes the same definition into the
expiry sense of the word.

** The classification

[[id:33E879B7-496A-406D-B3F2-72637379AD02][Kinds of cut]] — what a cut is for,
placed under the four purposes in use, with what each kind fixes, the attributes
its purpose gives it, and how each relates to the components.

** The market data component

[[id:DD59B04C-DAD2-458C-B35E-3F6B0B3D6DC6][Market data cut]] — the values a
valuation reads at the timepoint: quotes, curves, surfaces and fixings, the
approved rates applied on top, the snapshot that fixes them, the published sets
and their provenance, and what our system holds today.

** The deal component

[[id:19630B5D-A163-42B5-B9C4-61A87BAE8F5F][Trade cut]] — the population a
valuation values at the timepoint: which deals, the state each holds, the book
each belongs to, the corrections applied, and what our system holds today.

** The expiry sense of the word

[[id:3B39B2E2-6E05-4DF8-B68B-6ACA12F2FCE9][The option-expiry cut code]] — the
definition applied to expiry: a label for the time of day at which an option
expires on its expiry date, fixing an instant rather than a population.

* What the sources establish

The purpose of a cut, and the size of the body that follows from it, rest on the
reproducible-research literature quoted in [[*The purpose and the body][The
purpose and the body]]: Claerbout's definition as reported by Plesser, Sandve et
al.'s rules on programs, versions and parameters, the Turing Way's account of the
computational environment, and the Stanford Encyclopedia's definition of
computational reproducibility. Each quotation is reproduced exactly and carries
its URL.

Three further claims in the cluster have a public source, and each is recorded on
the page that makes it. The admissible sources of an independent price
verification rest on a prudential standard
([[https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/101/chapter/I][EBA RTS (EU) 2016/101]], Article 3), and the stated open and close of a
centre's business day rests on one settlement system's published hours
([[https://www.federalreserve.gov/paymentsystems/fedfunds_about.htm][Fedwire Funds Service]]); both appear in
[[id:33E879B7-496A-406D-B3F2-72637379AD02][Kinds of cut]]. That an official rate
is published once per working day at a stated time rests on the central bank
reference rates
([[https://www.ecb.europa.eu/stats/policy_and_exchange_rates/euro_reference_exchange_rates/html/index.en.html][ECB euro foreign exchange reference rates]]), which appears in
[[id:DD59B04C-DAD2-458C-B35E-3F6B0B3D6DC6][Market data cut]].

Everything else is a definition of a term or our working convention. The order
of the two component acts, the existence of the flash and start of day kinds, the
numbered end of day sequence, one cut per book, the after-hours rule, the
arbitrary instant a process may name, and the times that cut codes name have no
public source we could verify. They are written here as the convention we work
to. Practice at a particular firm is a matter for that firm's own record.

* What this cluster does not cover

The pricing settings themselves, the engine's configuration documents, and the
reads served over stored market data belong to other clusters. This cluster
states what a cut must hold and why, and leaves the meaning of each setting to
the page that owns it.
