#+title: Task: Make the durable consumers durable
#+description: The durable push consumers are not durable. Stopping a service removes its consumer immediately, so a command published while it is down is dropped rather than held, which contradicts both the durable subscription pattern and the workflow engine's assumption that a step waits on a stream. Create the consumer with js_AddConsumer and a stable deliver subject and group, bind it with the jsSubOptions so the server keeps it across a service restart, and prove a command published while the service is down is delivered when it returns.

* Result

The durable push consumers are durable now. They are created explicitly with a
name, a delivery subject and a delivery group derived from the stream and the
service, and the subscription binds to that consumer instead of creating one of
its own. The server keeps the consumer and its position across a restart, so a
command published while the service is down waits for it.

The acceptance was the reproduction that failed before. With ores.compute.service
stopped, its consumer still existed and reported no interest, where before it
disappeared. A command published into that window left one unprocessed message on
the stream and none in flight. Starting the service delivered and acked it: ack
floor one, unprocessed zero, redelivered zero, and the stream drained.

A full provisioning pass then completed tenant_setup_workflow nine steps of nine
and nineteen bundle publishes, with all fifteen step-command streams drained to
zero messages and one acked consumer each. Two consumers predating the restart
were reused with their original creation time, which exercises the reuse path.
The suites are 96 of 96.

Not covered, and worth its own task: the down-window delivery has no automated
test, and MaxDeliver is ten with the default thirty second wait, so a service
down for more than a few minutes could exhaust delivery attempts for a held
command. The stream keeps the message for a day, but the delivery bound governs
push redelivery.
