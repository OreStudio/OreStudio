* Goal

Hold the fixing sources of [[id:5AF9CEEF-A878-443A-8C32-C6E9F6C26FC9][Fixing sources for option cuts and non-deliverable currencies]] as reference data: label, administrator, kind (published, keyed, derived), asset, time and zone, holiday calendar, rounding and status (active or ceased). Give the cut code its entity, as the cuts story designed it, and have each cut name its fixing source. Seed the WM/R cut sources, the central-bank FX references, the settlement rate options of the non-deliverable currencies and the interest rate indices.

* Acceptance

- The supported sources are seeded with their administrator and status; LIBOR is ceased.
- Each seeded cut code names a fixing source with its time and zone.
- A currency pair's default cut and fixing source resolve to seeded rows.

* Plan

Four refdata entities, each a =simple-lookup= model in =ores.refdata=, so the
codegen writes every layer:

1. =fixing_source_kind= (published, keyed, derived) and
   =fixing_source_status= (active, ceased).
2. =fixing_source=: code, name, description, kind, asset class, administrator,
   publication time and zone, calendar, rounding and status. The publication
   time is the first =:type: time= column.
3. =cut_code=: code, name, description, cut time and zone, and the fixing
   source that fixes the market at the cut.
4. =currency_pair.default_cut_code=, nullable, checked against the cuts by the
   insert trigger. The DQ publish function copies it, and the pair seed sets it
   by rule: a PLN pair takes =WAR1100=, a HUF pair =BUD1200=, a metal none,
   every other fiat pair =NYC1000=.

The seeds are written by a data script from the tables of the design page.

* Notes

The cut and publication times are time-of-day columns: PostgreSQL =time=
without time zone, the zone in its own IANA column, and
=ores::platform::time::time_of_day= in C++.

The calendar code is not checked against the calendar table. The calendars are
published from their DQ dataset after the populate scripts run, so a check at
seed time would refuse every row. Each seeded source names a calendar the
calendar dataset holds.

=TR20H= is not seeded: ORE does not document which source it stands for, and a
source row needs a publication time. Imported ORE examples that name it are
unaffected, because no FX index is checked against this table yet.

The publication times of the non-deliverable currencies' settlement rate
options, and of the Bank of Canada, IMF and CBRT rates, are market convention.
They have not been checked against Annex A or the publishers' own pages, and
need that check before a trade depends on them.

* Result

Four refdata entities hold the fixing sources and their cuts:
=fixing_source_kind=, =fixing_source_status=, =fixing_source= and =cut_code=,
each generated through every layer from its model. A currency pair names its
default cut in =default_cut_code=, which the pair's insert trigger checks
against the cuts and the DQ publish copies from the pair dataset.

  sources, LIBOR ceased. Met.
- Each seeded cut names a fixing source with its time and zone: 7 cuts, each
  the time and zone of its WM/R source. Met.
- A currency pair's default cut and fixing source resolve to seeded rows: 171
  pair artefacts name =NYC1000=, 2 =WAR1100=, 2 =BUD1200=, and the trigger
  refuses a cut that is not seeded. The 4 metals name none. Met.

The times of the non-deliverable currencies and of the BOC, IMF and TCMB rates
need checking against their publishers; see the notes.
