* Goal

The summary view =ores_trading_trade_summaries_vw= unions two shapes: a trade row with a book, a date and a state, and a structure row with none. That union is why the screen order, filter and count cannot be an index scan, and why a structure row drops out of any filter on book, status or date.

This task makes the view one shape. The structure branch goes. A trade whose instrument is a composite is one row, like any other trade: its book, date and status are its own, and its instrument type is the deal kind the composite states. Its components are marked as legs of that trade, the parent trade id names the row they sit under, and the parent count is the number of components.

The structures that exist are not lost when the branch goes, so this task converts them first: each structure becomes a composite trade with the booking, state and identifier its legs imply, which is the shared book and the earliest date, the state the legs share, and the structure reference as the trade identifier. Each member becomes a component leg. The conversion runs once, is idempotent, and is tested on the sixty structures the demonstration holds.

- A composite trade appears once, with its own book, date and status, and its components are marked as legs of it.
- The rail counts a composite as one row and its components as rows of their own.
- The sixty structures in the demonstration become composite trades before the branch is deleted, and no member trade loses its structure reference.
- The page and the rail answer the same rows for the same filter.

* Notes

- The union's second branch is why a package has no book, date or status on the
  screen. It reads =ores_trading_structures_tbl= joined to a lateral that counts
  members and gathers their identifiers; a filter on any column the branch leaves
  null drops the row, which is why a desk filtering by book never sees a package.
- The conversion must copy the structure's reference before the table goes:
  =ores_trading_structure_identifiers_tbl= holds it, and the screen's search
  finds a package by it. A trade identifier carries it after the conversion.
- The member's =role= and =sequence_number= move to the composite leg, which T1
  gives both columns.
- The summary's =structure_id= column becomes the parent trade id. The web blotter
  reads it through =trades.ts=; check that read before renaming, and keep the
  rail's dimensions unchanged.
- The conversion is idempotent: a structure already converted leaves a composite
  trade with the same id, so a second run writes nothing.
- The union is also what makes the page slow. An =EXPLAIN (ANALYZE, BUFFERS)= of
  the page statement at ten thousand trades shows the structure-member lateral
  scanning =ores_trading_structure_members_tbl= once for each of the ten thousand
  trades, and an identifier lateral searching the index ten thousand times:
  123,970 shared buffers for one page of a hundred rows.
