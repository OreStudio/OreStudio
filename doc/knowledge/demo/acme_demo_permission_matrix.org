#+title: A persona may act because of its role, and no other reason
#+description: The role each Acme persona holds, the permission matrix by role for market data and approvals, and how a decide permission reaches a person through the role a member of staff was given.

* Summary

An Acme person holds two roles: =Member=, which every account holds, and the one
job role the seeder gave the account when it loaded the staff. Everything the
person may do follows from those two rows and from the permissions they bundle.
No account is granted a permission directly, so the question "can this persona
decide a book change" has one answer: does its job role carry the code. That is
why adding a persona to the demonstration is two decisions and not one, and why
the seeder's =role= field is the only place a persona's authority is stated.

An account holds roles, a role bundles permissions, and an operation checks the
code it names. The chain has no shortcut, and the two exceptions are both
deliberate. =TenantAdmin= holds the single permission =*=, which satisfies every
check in the tenant, and a service account holds its component's wildcard, which
is why a service can write what no person may.
[[id:3CAA394F-E84C-3434-331B-6E664F7E84D2][Role-Based Access Control]]
states the model, and
[[id:AF46793A-FF6C-4066-A059-279CE9936420][Identity and Access]] orders the pages
that carry it.

Seven job roles across 73 staff, and one administrator account the profile
creates outside the staff datasets. A persona holds exactly one job role, so the
matrix below has one row per persona.

The empty cells are the point of the table, and three of them carry a decision.

- =Member= holds none of the inbox codes, so the approvals queue is not a screen
  every person sees. It is a screen for the functions that decide, and a member
  with no decide role reads an empty queue.
- The =Controller= and =Finance= roles hold no =marketdata= code. Deciding a book
  change does not require reading the party's market data feeds, and the
  demonstration keeps the two apart.
- The row for =iam::roles:assign= is empty for every job role. The only holder is
  =TenantAdmin=, through its =*=, so the decision on a role request stays with
  the administrator. [[id:2BA72C77-99DC-4538-8937-52A572CF6F53][Role requests]]
  states why that write is administrative.

** The two rules that make the matrix matter

A permission is necessary and not sufficient. Two further rules sit above the
codes, and both are enforced in the database rather than in a screen.

- *Four eyes.* A person never decides a request they raised. The decisions table
  refuses the row, and the queue cuts the person's own requests out of the page
  before it is paged, so the refusal is rarely reached.
- *One decision per person.* A request that needs two approvals, or several
  parts, takes one decision from each person whatever part they answered for, so
  two roles held by one account do not let it approve twice.

[[id:0FE6BDD9-EB24-4B07-8B0C-D816AAF1BEBF][Approval requests]] states both, and
[[id:8B0D92BD-7D15-45B7-80EA-07D111F73EB3][the approval model]] applies them to
the kinds the demonstration seeds.

** What a persona cannot do by holding a role

Three limits are worth stating because a reader will ask.

- A job role is per tenant. A person of the Acme tenant holds an Acme role and
  reads nothing of another tenant, whatever the role's name.
- A role does not select a party. The demonstration gives each person a default
  party, and a read that is party-scoped sees that party and its children, so a
  controller in London decides a London book for the same reason a London trader
  books into it.
- A role cannot change itself. Adding a permission to a role is an IAM write,
  held by the administrator, and granting a role to a person is the same write.
