#+title: The identity a feed runs as
#+description: Who may start a feed, why a feed runs as its owning tenant and not as the person who started it, and how a start and a restart obtain the same token.
#+type: knowledge
#+level: cross
#+filetags: :marketdata:feeds:identity:iam:
#+created: 2026-10-19
#+updated: 2026-10-19
#+startup: inlineimages

* Summary

A feed acts for the tenant that owns it. It never acts for the person who
pressed Start. Starting a feed also subscribes the caller's party to it, and
that subscription, the feed binding, is the caller's own act. Three questions
about the feed have three owners. The synthetic service checks
whether the caller may command the feed. The collection's run grant records that
a person consented to the feed acting in its tenant and party. A short-lived run
token, which the service exchanges for the grant at every start, carries the
authority of the feed. A Start by a person and a restore after a restart use the
same exchange, so a feed behaves the same either way.

* Detail

** Three questions, three owners

#+ATTR_HTML: :class hug-leading
| Question | Owner | When it is asked |
|----------+-------+------------------|
| May this caller command the feed? | the synthetic service, from the caller's token | at the Start and Stop commands only |
| Should the feed run? | the feed's =enabled= and =auto_start= flags | at each start and at each restore |
| What may the running feed read and write? | the run token, from the collection's run grant | at each start |

The caller's token answers the first question and nothing else. It does not
reach the feed. A feed that a restart starts has no caller, and it needs no
caller, because the other two questions do not depend on one.

** The consent

A collection of feeds belongs to one tenant and one party. A person's consent
that its feeds act in that tenant and party is a
[[id:78B82824-0BBB-4442-A9FA-E1F265DF57BD][run grant]]. The synthetic service
states these choices for it.

#+ATTR_HTML: :class hug-leading
| Field | Value |
|-------+-------|
| =resource= | =synthetic.market_data_generation_config/<collection id>= |
| =role= | =SyntheticFeedRun= |
| =audience= | =SyntheticService= |
| =max_runs=, =not_after= | empty: the grant is standing |

The =SyntheticFeedRun= role holds two permissions and no more:
=marketdata::market_series:read= and =marketdata::market_observations:read=.
They let a feed resolve the vintage price that it starts from. A run
token carries the role's permissions that the grantor holds in the party at the
moment of the exchange. IAM refuses the grant when the grantor does not hold
every permission of the role.

The collection stores the grant id in =run_grant_id=. The grant id is not a
secret. A feed with a fixed price reads no marketdata and needs no token. Only a
feed that starts from a vintage price holds one.

** When the grant is made, used and ended

- *Made.* A Start by a person on a collection that holds no grant gives the
  consent. The service creates the grant with the person's token and stores its
  id on the collection. The person must act in the collection's party.
- *Used.* Every start exchanges the grant for a run token, whether a person or a
  restore causes it. The run identifier is the feed config id. The service keeps
  tokens in a run token cache.
- *Ended.* A person who holds =iam::run_grants:revoke= revokes the grant, and
  IAM answers =grant_lapsed= from then on. Deleting a collection leaves its
  grant in place until someone revokes it.
- *Lapsed.* IAM answers =grant_lapsed= when the grantor no longer holds the
  role's permissions or is no longer active. The feed does not start. A restore
  logs the reason. A Start by a person who holds the permissions is a new consent
  that replaces the lapsed grant.

A running feed does not need the token. It publishes its ticks under the
service's own identity. A lapse therefore stops no running feed. It stops the
next start.

The permission check happens before anything else. A caller without the kind's
config permission and the run permission gets =forbidden=, and no grant is
created.

** The binding is the caller's subscription

A feed binding records that a party consumes a producer source. A Start by a
person subscribes that person's party, whether the feed was already running or
not, and a second party that starts the same feed gets a binding of its own.
The service creates it with the caller's token, in the caller's party. It is the
caller's act, so it does not use the run token. A restore subscribes no party,
because the stored bindings stand. A bound feed that a restore starts keeps
every binding it had.

** The restore

The synthetic service restores its feeds when it starts. The running set is not
stored. The =enabled= and =auto_start= flags of the configs are the stored form
of it.

The walk reads each tenant's configs in a context of that tenant, opened on the
tenant's root party. Row-level security shows a tenant context its own
collections. The party policy shows a party's collection only to a context whose
visible parties include that party, and the root party's subtree is every party
of the tenant. A system feed starts only in the pass of the system tenant. A
tenant that fails is logged and does not stop the others.

** What can go wrong

#+ATTR_HTML: :class hug-leading
| Condition | What the feed does | What the person reads |
|-----------+--------------------+-----------------------|
| The caller lacks a permission | does not start; no grant is made | =forbidden= |
| The grantor lacks a permission of the role | no grant is made | IAM's refusal, naming the permission |
| The grant lapsed or is revoked | does not start | the reason, in the reply or in the log |
| A vintage-priced feed finds no grant at a restore | is skipped | a log line that names the feed and says a Start gives the consent |
| IAM does not answer | does not start | the reason, in the reply or in the log |
| Another feed holds the series | does not start | the name of the holder |
