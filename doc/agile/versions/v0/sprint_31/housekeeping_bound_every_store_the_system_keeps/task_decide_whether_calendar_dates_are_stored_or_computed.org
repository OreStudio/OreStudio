* Notes

** The three options, costed

| Option | Stored | What it costs | What it risks |
|--------+--------+---------------+---------------|
| Materialise per tenant, as today | 283 MB a tenant: 486,540 rows at 610 bytes a row | nothing new; the materialisation service already exists | one copy of the same bytes per tenant, and adding a tenant adds a copy |
| One copy in the system tenant | 283 MB once, plus the 19 MB of exceptions every tenant already reads | a read policy, a repository that reads across tenants, and the materialisation pointed at the system tenant | a pricing read that assumes the caller's tenant must learn to ask for the shared one |
| Compute on demand, store nothing | none | the rule engine runs per process and per horizon, on the pricing path | a run that needs a horizon nobody stated computes it again; the work moves from a nightly job to every reader |

The horizon decides the volume in all three: 13,515 rows a calendar is 37 years
of days, and the default end year is 2050. A shorter horizon shrinks the copy
but not the ratio between options.

** The decision

One copy in the system tenant, kept as a derived cache and rebuilt on demand.
The reasoning is the classification's: a calendar's days follow from its rule
and its exceptions, both of which the platform authors and publishes once in the
=world= bundle, so the days are the same bytes in every tenant. A per-tenant copy
writes them once per tenant; computing them in every pricing run charges the run
for work the installation can do once and hand to everyone. The copy is
disposable, which is what makes keeping it safe: =calendar_materialisation_service=
rebuilds it from the rules, so the store is bounded by the horizon rather than by
how many tenants exist.

The reporting environment's 612 MB is consistent with this one: 283 MB for the
dates and 19 MB for the exceptions, held about twice over.

* Result

The three options are costed against the store as it is: 486,540 rows and 283 MB
for 36 calendars over 2024 to 2060, 610 bytes a row with its indexes, 13,515 rows
and about 8 MB a calendar, and 19 MB of exceptions that every tenant already
reads from the system tenant.

The decision: the dates are held once, in the system tenant, and the
materialisation service is kept as the way that copy is rebuilt from the rules.
Recomputation is not rejected — it is what makes the single copy disposable, and
it bounds the store by the horizon rather than by the number of tenants.
