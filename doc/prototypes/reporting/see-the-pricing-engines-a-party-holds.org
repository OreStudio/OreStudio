* Summary

This prototype draws the list of pricing engine configurations and the page of one, and nothing else. It invents no layout. The list is the standard list: breadcrumb, plural title, one sentence, Refresh, Workflows and Add, a search, two filters, the table, the pager and the four states. The page is the standard record page: the lead line, the access mark, Edit and Delete only for a person who may take them, and the tabs Details, Products, Parameters, Reports and History with Revert.

The two additions are the two things a market-risk owner asks that the standard does not answer. The Products tab opens with a *coverage strip*: how many of the product types the party trades have a row, and which do not. The Reports tab opens with the *reach*: a change reaches the next run of each report named, and a run already produced keeps what it ran with.

The page answers to the journey [[id:386DD47E-DA9E-4EA7-B925-852048EE47B7][See the pricing engines a party holds]]. It is throwaway: it reads no service, writes nothing to one, and states which parts of the interaction no operation supports. It follows the [[id:2BF7F03F-6BF4-45FF-8E26-4D3B4E2D943D][Record screen standard]] and the rules in [[file:../index.org][Prototypes]]: plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript, no framework, no build and no backend, with the design tokens of =projects/ores.web/packages/web/src/styles.css=.

* Detail

** The question the screen answers

"Which engine prices my swaptions, and which of my reports use it?" The screen answers it in two reads: the list, to find the configuration, and the page, to read what it prices with and who binds it. The coverage strip answers the third question, what is left unpriced.

** States and switches

- =?screen=list= and =?screen=record&id=c1|c2|c3&tab=...=. =c1= is the ACME group configuration as the seeder writes it: three products, one global setting, four reports. =c2= is a nine-product AMC configuration with four versions. =c3= is bound by no report.
- =?state=all|empty|error|loading= for the four list states.
- =?actor=viewer|analyst|admin=: a viewer reads, an analyst adds and edits, and only the administrator deletes. An action a person may not take is not drawn.
- Delete is refused where a report binds the configuration, and the page says that no service refuses it today.
- Revert is offered on any version older than the current one, where the fixture holds the version's values, and writes them back as a new version.

** What is fake

Every value. The product and report counts in the list are fixtures, because no operation returns a count for a list row. The traded product types are a fixture, because no operation returns them. The reports on the Reports tab are names, because no read lists the reports that bind one configuration. The versions of =c1= and =c3= keep no values, so the History tab says so. The product, model and engine rows of =c2= are real triples counted from the ORE example files, and the settings of the Bermudan swaption are the ones most example files carry.

** Where the code lives

=doc/prototypes/reporting/see-the-pricing-engines-a-party-holds/index.html=. One file, plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript, with the style and the script inline, no modules, no fetch and no build step.

** What it argues

- The list is drawn as the record screen standard draws it, without a variation.
- The coverage strip belongs on the Products tab, above the table, and states the products left unpriced by name.
- The Reports tab states the reach before it lists a report.

** The verdict

None yet. This page is on the branch that proposes it and waits for review.

** What it does not settle

- Whether the list columns product count and report count are worth a second read per row. The prototype draws them; a build can drop them.
- Whether the coverage strip should also appear on the list, as a count per row. A configuration that leaves a traded product unpriced is the one a person wants to find first.
