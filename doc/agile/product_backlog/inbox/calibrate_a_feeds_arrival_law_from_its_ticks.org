* What

Fit a feed's arrival law to a series of observed tick times. A Poisson law takes
the mean rate. A Hawkes law takes its baseline, branching ratio and decay by
maximum likelihood, which for an exponential kernel has a recursive form and
costs one pass over the ticks. The fit reports its goodness by the same
compensated gaps the Fit tab already plots against Exp(1), and the result can be
saved onto the feed's row.

* Why

Gemini's review called its selector "select and calibrate". Selecting exists:
each feed's law is chosen on its own Arrivals tab. Calibrating does not: a
person sets a branching ratio and a decay by hand and judges them by eye. A
desk that wants a synthetic feed to look like a live one needs the numbers
from the live one.
