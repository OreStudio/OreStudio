This page documents a [[id:F44766FA-1E9C-416F-849A-E131101B2ECA][user journey]]: A trader or quant defines how a volatility surface is parameterised and quoted: its expiry and strike axes, its grid, its quoting convention, its interpolation across both axes, its extrapolation and any parametric model.

A user journey describes what one person is trying to do, before anything is
built. It is a design document that outlives any sprint, so it names the
screens, the entity fields each screen shows, and the operations and messages
each step needs.

This journey is written against a model that does not exist yet. It records the
framework the desk needs, so that the surface model, when it is built, starts
from the screen the person works on rather than from a table.

* Goal

"I want the desk's surface to be quoted and interpolated the way we agreed, so
the calibrator reads the quotes we mean and not the ones it guesses." The
journey is done when the framework is saved and the grid preview shows the
expiry and strike labels the desk quotes, with the conventions the desk uses.

* Actor and trigger

The actor is a trader or a quant acting for the desk. The trigger is a new
surface, a change in how the desk quotes a delta, or a calibration that read a
label the wrong way. The journey starts at the surface list, for an asset class
and an underlying, and ends when the person leaves the framework. The framework
is not a published object: it is the recipe the calibration reads, and it
changes nothing until a surface is calibrated from it.

* Steps

1. *Find the surface.* The person opens *Market data*, then *Surfaces*, and
   chooses an asset class and an underlying. No surface entity exists, so the
   system shows the volatility series it can infer: the =market_series= rows
   whose subclass is =volatility=, grouped by their oresmd URI. The screen says
   that no framework is stored for any of them.
2. *Open or start a framework.* The person selects a series and the system
   shows the framework screen, empty. There is nothing to read from the server,
   because no surface record exists; the screen states the intended framework
   and reads the quoted cells it can find under the series.
3. *Set the two axes.* The person names the expiry axis as a list of tenors
   (=1M=, =3M=, =6M=, =1Y=, =2Y=, =5Y=, =10Y=, =30Y=) and the second axis as a
   strike or delta label axis (=ATM=, =25RR=, =25BF=, =10RR=, =10BF=). The label
   vocabulary does not exist; the screen shows the intended labels read-only and
   names the gap.
4. *Define the grid.* The person says which (=expiry=, =label=) cells the desk
   quotes, the quote type (=volatility=, =price=, =normal volatility=), the
   expiry dates (=IMM=, tenor dates) and the day count. The system previews the
   grid: how many cells the axes produce, which the series holds a point for,
   and which are missing.
5. *Set the quoting convention.* The person says whether a label is a strike, a
   delta or an ATM/risk-reversal/butterfly combination, then the delta
   convention (forward, spot, premium-adjusted) and the ATM type (forward,
   delta-neutral). Nothing stores these values; the screen lists them as the
   intended convention.
6. *Set the interpolation across both axes.* The person chooses the time
   interpolation (across expiries) and the strike interpolation (across the
   second axis), such as linear in variance over time and cubic across strikes.
7. *Set the extrapolation.* The person chooses how the surface extends past its
   last expiry and past its last strike: flat, linear or none.
8. *Choose the parametric model where one is used.* Where the desk fits a model
   rather than interpolating the grid, the person chooses it (=SABR=, =SVI=) and
   names its parameters. The screen shows the choice; there is no model to fit
   and no parameter store.
9. *Save the framework.* The person chooses *Save framework*. Nothing is
   written, because no surface entity exists. The screen keeps the framework in
   the page session and says so.
10. *Leave.* The person goes back to the surface list. No framework survives the
    screen; see /What is missing/.

The snapshot reads take an oresmd URI and return one observation per point, so
they read a surface's cells as a grid; they do not know that the points are a
surface, and they carry no axis order, no label vocabulary and no as-of in the
past.

* What is missing

1. *There is no surface model.* Nothing is keyed by a surface identity, and no
   subject names one. There is no =marketdata.v1.surfaces.*= or a surface
   config. The candidate is a surface entity with its axes, its grid and its
   quoting convention. Until then the screen shows the volatility series it
   can infer and states that no framework is stored.
2. *There is no axis or grid storage.* The expiries and the strike or delta
   labels are not rows, and a grid cell is only an =oresmd_uri= point under the
   series. The candidate is an axis table and a grid-cell table, or a shape
   table the cells resolve against. Until then the screen decodes the point
   URIs through =oresmd_uri_codec= and shows the distinct labels it finds; it
   cannot store them or fix their order.
3. *There is no strike-label vocabulary.* No code list or table names =ATM=,
   =25RR= or =25BF=, so a label is free text inside a URI. The candidate is a
   reference-data strike or delta label list. Until then the screen names a
   label as the text it decoded and cannot validate or order it.
4. *The quoting convention has no fields.* Nothing records whether a label is a
   strike or a delta, the delta convention, the ATM type or the premium
   adjustment. The candidate is fields on the surface. Until then the screen
   lists the intended values and writes none of them.
5. *The parametric model has no storage.* No model kind and no parameters are
   stored, so a fitted =SABR= or =SVI= surface cannot be re-read. The candidate
   is a model kind and a parameter set per expiry. Until then the screen shows
   the choice and no fit.
6. *No screen exists.* No web page, HTTP route or shell verb reaches any
   surface operation. The candidate is a page and a route. Until then the
   journey runs only from the shell.
7. *A volatility series is not a typed surface scope.* =market_series.list=
   filters generically; no operation lists surfaces by underlying, currency or
   asset class. The candidate is a typed scope. Until then the person picks a
   series from the series list.

An ORE volatility configuration family exists in =ores.refdata= (for example
=curve_volatility_config=, which carries ORE's own =StrikeSurface= and
=DeltaSurface= kinds). That is one ORE input document's settings, not a
market-data surface: it holds no market points, no identity in
=ores.marketdata= and no published object. This journey's screen does not read
or edit it.
