* Goal

Signed in as the super administrator, the deployment's own world is real and named: the administrator is greeted by a human name and shown a picture, and the system administration area lists the accounts the installation runs on — the platform's service accounts among them.

The analysis behind the story: of the super administrator's journeys, first run, new tenant, the roster, the tenant screen and acting inside a tenant are built, and retire or reset and the operations journeys are their own sprint 27 stories. The legacy Qt application kept the deployment's own accounts on a first-class screen, under Operations > User Accounts, and the web's system administration area holds only the roster. What remains is the super administrator's own sight of the deployment, and this story lands it.

* Acceptance

- The system administration area reads the deployment's own accounts, the service accounts among them, each shown as the account it is.
- The initial administrator and a provisioned tenant's administrator carry human names, and every surface that greets or names a person prefers the name and falls back to the username.
- The two administrator avatars are part of the seeded pictures: setting the ACME example up attaches them, and each administrator shows their picture wherever a picture is shown.

* Decisions

- The deployment's own accounts are read as the session's own tenant, the
  ordinary accounts read, with no cross-tenant read added. The session's
  tenant is the system tenant for a super administrator, so the read
  already answers with the deployment's accounts.
- The system administration menu gains the accounts entry, revisiting the
  accepted navigation prototype's Tenants-only menu.
- The first run keeps its three fields (username, email, password); the
  creation paths record the administrator's name with a default.
