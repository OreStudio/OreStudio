* Summary

The rates family is the first family after the bond pilot. It is nine
instrument models over nine tables, and it already carries more of the
target shape than the bond family did: every table is keyed by its trade,
carries the audit spine, and — with one exception — declares a
=trade_type_code= check generated from the trade-type catalogue
([[id:A8E9807F-1BC8-4DA5-9A66-720D2444DA1B][the catalogue]]). What it
lacks is the factoring the instrument table rule asks for: there is no
family header, the shared legs table keeps a surrogate key, the option
block's dates and collections are text, one product has no coverage at
all, and two of the trade types the catalogue files under =swap= are not
interest-rate products.

The larger problem is the boundary. The ORE mapping never writes the
family's legs to the database, overwrites a cross-currency swap's type to
=Swap=, leaves seven product columns that exist permanently empty, and
has no reverse mapper for three products. The schema and the mapper
between them lose what the instrument is.

This page inventories the family across its surfaces and proposes the
target model: one family header holding the codes and the fields every
product shares, product fact tables for the products that state fields of
their own, a re-keyed legs table with amount and rate children, and the
family's use of the shared option, schedule and strike shapes the bond
pilot built. It closes with the routing repairs the family needs, the
blotter's common-date view, and the staging.

The proposal follows the rule section 14 states, after the bond pilot:

| Level | Table | Holds |
|-------+-------+-------|
| Family header | one per family | the fields every product in the family shares; a =trade_type= check listing exactly the family's codes; keyed by the trade and pinned to the anchor |
| Product facts | one per product with fields of its own | only those fields; keyed by the trade, with a check on its own codes |
| Shared children | legs, schedules, option block, strikes, premiums | keyed by the trade, an ordinal and a role code; the role is data, not a table |
| Security | issue or contract | the terms the issuer fixes, keyed by the reference datum id and shared by every trade on it |

The rates family has no security level. A swap has no issuer and no ISIN:
every field of a rates trade belongs to that trade. That is the one place
the rule's four levels do not all apply, and it is why the proposal below
spends its length on the header, the legs and the shared children rather
than on a master entity.

* Detail

** Context: the bond pilot and the instrument table rule

The rates story exists to apply what the bond pilot proved. Three bodies
of prior work bound this one, and their conclusions are assumed.

- Section 14 of
  [[id:FDC35F65-CBF0-40A0-96D0-EA180F2237EE][Investigation: the trade shape]],
  which states the instrument table rule and names the families still to
  be reworked: "The rates, FX, equity, credit and commodity families are
  still to do."
- The clean-trading story
  [[id:0DC1BAC7-DDAE-4F64-B800-49FBF8D723C1][0DC1BAC7]] and its
  decisions, which set the shape the rates family must now join: the
  instrument's key is its trade (D14), the relationship the schema can
  hold is declared and enforced at the insert trigger rather than by a DDL
  foreign key (D15), the family header names the rows it owns and the
  generated delete rule closes them (D27), every instrument table declares
  the codes it accepts (D28), and the shared schedule table is the home
  for an observation schedule any family writes to (D29).
- The bond relational model deliverable
  [[id:9ABB181D-2582-4084-8FFB-A0CE4114B171][9ABB181D]] and its
  implementation, which is the shape the rates models are compared
  against: =bond_instruments= as the header, =bond_options= and its
  siblings as the product facts, =bond_legs= as the trade-owned legs, and
  the shared =instrument_*= children as the list shapes.

The rates analysis is deliberately narrower than the bond one. Bond asked
"where do the terms live?" because ten products share an ISIN and the
answer was a master entity. Rates has no such shared entity, so this page
asks a different question: *what does a rates family header hold when the
family has no security?*

** What counts as the rates family

The family boundary is the refdata product taxonomy, not the catalogue's
=product_type= column. That column is misnamed — the trade-model
investigation calls it defect 10 — and it carries the asset class:

#+begin_quote
Top-level product classification codes, shown on instrument_code and
asset_class_code.
#+end_quote

[[id:4043DE4C-5CDB-455E-B208-5AFF69AA76AE][ores.refdata.asset_class_catalogue]]
declares seven classes: =fx=, =interest_rates=, =credit=, =equity=,
=commodity=, =inflation= and =bond=. It defines =interest_rates= as
"deposits, forward rate agreements, interest rate swaps and swaptions,
caps/floors, and cross-currency swaps".

The domain notes agree with the taxonomy and disagree with the catalogue
on two codes:

|------+-------------+---------------------------+--------------------------|
| Swap | =vanilla_swap_instruments= | =rates= | swap |
| CrossCurrencySwap | =vanilla_swap_instruments= | =rates= | swap |
| FlexiSwap | =vanilla_swap_instruments= | =rates= | swap |
| ForwardRateAgreement | =fra_instruments= | =rates= | swap |
| CapFloor | =cap_floor_instruments= | =rates= | swap |
| Swaption | =swaption_instruments= | =rates= | swap |
| BalanceGuaranteedSwap | =balance_guaranteed_swap_instruments= | =rates= | swap |
| CallableSwap | =callable_swap_instruments= | =rates= | swap |
| KnockOutSwap | =knock_out_swap_instruments= | =rates= | swap |
| RateDigitalOption | none | =rates= | swap |
| SwaptionStraddle | none | =rates= | swap |
| ForwardVolatilityAgreement | none | rates-shaped, vol product | swap |
| RiskParticipationAgreement | =rpa_instruments= | *credit* | swap |
| InflationSwap | =inflation_swap_instruments= | *inflation* | swap |

The evidence for the two outliers is the knowledge base's own
identifier tables: =rpa.org= states =Asset class =credit=, and
=cpiswap.org= states =Asset class =inflation= with =ORE Studio product
code = none; not a product in its own right=. The catalogue's =swap=
for both is the taxonomy disagreement defect 10 describes.

*Decision proposed.* The rates family is the eleven =interest_rates=
codes. =RiskParticipationAgreement= leaves for the credit family and
=InflationSwap= for an inflation family that has no story yet;
=ForwardVolatilityAgreement= is recorded with them as a product the
family does not yet model. The three codes with no instrument cell keep
that state until the ORE importer writes them.

** The current model, surface by surface

*** The instrument tables

Nine tables, one per model, all keyed by =(tenant_id, trade_id)= with the
SCD2 spine and the audit columns. Their own columns, over the scaffolding
every entity carries:

| Table | Own columns |
|-------+-------------|
| =vanilla_swap_instruments= | start_date, maturity_date, settlement_lag, netting_set_id, description |
| =fra_instruments= | start_date, end_date, currency, rate_index, long_short, strike, notional, description |
| =cap_floor_instruments= | start_date, maturity_date, description |
| =swaption_instruments= | expiry_date, exercise_type, settlement_type, long_short, start_date, maturity_date, description |
| =balance_guaranteed_swap_instruments= | start_date, maturity_date, lockout_days, description |
| =callable_swap_instruments= | start_date, maturity_date, description |
| =knock_out_swap_instruments= | start_date, maturity_date, barrier_start_date, barrier_level, barrier_type, description |
| =inflation_swap_instruments= | start_date, maturity_date, inflation_index_code, base_cpi, lag_convention, description |
| =rpa_instruments= | start_date, maturity_date, reference_counterparty, participation_rate, protection_fee, description |

The three swaps — Swap, CrossCurrencySwap and FlexiSwap — share
=vanilla_swap_instruments=, so nine tables carry eleven
=interest_rates= codes.

Every table but =rpa_instruments= declares the codes it accepts. The
check is not written by hand: the model states
=:routed_by: ores.trading.trade_type_catalogue= and
=:routed_column: trade_type_code=, and the check is generated from the
catalogue so the lists are disjoint and a trade's instrument can sit in
one table only. =vanilla_swap_instruments= accepts the three swap codes,
=balance_guaranteed_swap_instruments= one, and so on.

*** The legs

=ores_trading_swap_legs_tbl= is the one shared legs table of the nine
families. Its row keeps a surrogate =id= and names the parent through
=trade_id=; =leg_number= is the 1-based ordinal and =leg_type_code= is the
discriminator (Fixed, Floating, CMS, CPI, OIS and the rest), so the leg
type is data and not a table — decision D10 of the clean-trading story
([[id:6F6B42BE-C8BB-4105-9ED3-7E87D67F9EB7][the model]]).

The leg carries one =notional=, one =fixed_rate= and one =spread=, with
=floating_index_code= null on a fixed leg and =fixed_rate= null on a
floating leg. It carries no amortisation row and no rate schedule, so a
leg whose notional amortises or whose rate steps is flattened at the
boundary. It validates five code columns against ores.refdata through
insert-trigger functions, and exposes one custom read,
=read_by_instruments_batch=, that the trade read path needs because no
generated read covers a fetch of many instruments' legs at once.

*** The shared children

The bond pilot built a family of list-shaped children under
=ores_trading_instrument_*=. Nine of them exist:

| Table | Key | Holds |
|-------+-----+-------|
| =instrument_schedules= | trade, owner_role, owner_number, schedule_role, sequence_number | a rule-based or date-based schedule |
| =instrument_schedule_dates= | the same, plus the entry ordinal | one date of a date-based schedule |
| =instrument_option= | trade | the option block |
| =instrument_option_premiums= | trade, sequence_number | one premium |
| =instrument_option_exercise_fees= | trade, sequence_number | one exercise fee |
| =instrument_option_payment_dates= | trade, sequence_number | one payment date |
| =instrument_strikes= | trade | the strike block |
| =callable_swap_call_dates= | trade, sequence_number | one call date of a callable swap |

The rates models do not reference any of them. The rates facts are flat
columns on the product table, the legs are in the legs table, and
=callable_swap_call_dates= is the one rates-owned child. The option block
and the schedule pair are used by bond alone today.

*** The ORE shape the tables have to hold

The upstream schema names one product data type per rates trade type, and
the family's own economics are defined there. The types that matter to
this family are =swapData=, =flexiSwapData=, =callableSwapData=,
=knockOutSwapData=, =bgSwapData=, =capFloorData=, =swaptionData=,
=swaptionStraddleData= and =forwardRateAgreementData=, in
=external/ore/xsd/instruments.xsd=.

Two properties of that schema shape the proposal below:

- Every swap-shaped product — =swapData=, =flexiSwapData=,
  =callableSwapData=, =knockOutSwapData=, =bgSwapData=, =capFloorData= and
  the =swaptionData='s underlying — holds its economics as a list of
  =LegData= elements. The leg list is the family's shared child, and the
  product data type is what distinguishes one product from another.
- =swaptionData= carries an inner =swapData=, so a swaption's underlying
  swap is the same structure as a standalone swap.

*** The ORE mapping

The importer and the exporter are hand-written end to end:
=swap_instrument_mapper.cpp= (1129 lines) holds a forward mapper for each
of the ten swap-family types and a reverse mapper for six;
=credit_instrument_mapper.cpp= (392 lines) holds RPA;
=trade_mapper.cpp= dispatches by trade type; =exporter.cpp:132-158=
routes the way back. The generated ORE binding, =domain.hpp= and
=domain.cpp=, is 13506 and 108648 lines and is not the problem.

Four things the mapping does to the family's data are worth stating
plainly, because they, not the schema, define what the target has to
repair.

- *The legs never reach the database.* =map_leg= fills a
  =swap_instrument_data::legs= vector for every swap-shaped product, and
  the import handler never reads it: the only =r.legs= use in
  =ore_import_execute_handler.cpp= is the scripted branch. No file under
  =projects/ores.ore/= sends a =put_swap_leg_request=. The export path
  reads legs back from the database, so a stored swap exports with no
  legs at all. The two legs of an IRS are the instrument.
- *CrossCurrencySwap and FlexiSwap are flattened onto the vanilla swap
  table, and the currency swap's type is lost.* =forward_swap= reads
  either =SwapData= or =CrossCurrencySwapData= and then writes
  =trade_type_code = "Swap"= for both; the reverse mapper always emits
  =<TradeType>Swap</TradeType>=. A cross-currency swap round trips as an
  ordinary swap.
- *Several products' own columns are never populated.*
  =swaption_instruments.long_short= and =.settlement_type=,
  =balance_guaranteed_swap_instruments.lockout_days=,
  =inflation_swap_instruments.inflation_index_code=, =.base_cpi= and
  =.lag_convention=, and =rpa_instruments.participation_rate= and
  =.protection_fee= all exist as columns and are never written by any
  mapper. FRA's =long_short= is hardcoded to =Long= in both directions,
  and CapFloor's is hardcoded on the reverse.
- *Leg direction has nowhere to go.* =legData/Payer= is required by the
  schema, and =ores_trading_swap_legs_tbl= has no payer column. A fixed
  leg and a floating leg are stored with the same direction. The leg's
  amortisations, payment dates, payment lag, last-period day counter and
  settlement data have no destination either.

The corpus that exercises this is large: 229 of the 1623 example
documents contain a rates trade, and across the whole examples tree the
trade counts are Swap 1352, Swaption 376, CapFloor 77, ForwardRateAgreement
60, FlexiSwap 12, InflationSwap 8, CrossCurrencySwap 6,
RiskParticipationAgreement 5, CallableSwap 4, BalanceGuaranteedSwap 3 and
KnockOutSwap 3. The three unrouted types have ORE types and elements but
no document at all.

*** The programming surface

The nine models carry =:has_batch_read: true= and generate their api,
core and service layers through the =trading-instrument= profile; each
entity is a fixed set of generated files (domain type, json and table io,
event, generator, protocol, handler, registrar, repository entity,
mapper, repository, service, history field mapper, service event
registrar and the SQL pair). The =rpa_instrument= model is the exception
in its SQL section: it declares no =:routed_by:= and no
=:routed_column:=, so no =trade_type_code= column and no coverage check
are generated for it.

The *family aggregation* around those generated leaves is hand-written,
and it is where the family's shape leaks:

- =registrar_rates.cpp:36-102= is the hand-written rates registrar. It
  calls the generated per-entity registrars, one line each, in a fixed
  order. =registrar_detail.hpp:45-48= declares it and
  =registrar.cpp:139= appends it to the composition root.
- The shared children are not registered there. =registrar_bond.cpp:130-169=
  registers =instrument_schedule=, =instrument_schedule_date=,
  =instrument_option=, =instrument_option_premium=,
  =instrument_option_exercise_fee=, =instrument_option_payment_date= and
  =instrument_strike= — the shapes the rates family is meant to share.
  =callable_swap_call_dates= is registered by the rates registrar instead.
- =instrument_payload.cpp= is a hand-written wire codec that duplicates
  what the generated per-entity protocols express: a leaf-name list, a
  =swap_leaf_payload= struct, and hand-written =encode_swap_leaf=,
  =decode_swap= and a rates branch of the instrument decoder. It sits
  beside =rates_instrument_variant.hpp=, =instrument.hpp= and
  =trade_instrument.hpp=, three hand-written variants.
- =trade_export_service.cpp= carries two hand-written copies of the
  routing the generator already produces: a switch that buckets trade ids
  per table (=:115-206=) and a per-rates-type service dispatch
  (=:331-377=), in a file that also calls the generated
  =instrument_table_for= at =:109=.
- The ORE side is hand-written throughout: =swap_instrument_mapper.cpp=
  holds a forward mapper per rates product, and =exporter.cpp:132-158=
  routes the reverse direction by trade type string.

- No generated eventing integration test exists for any of the nine

- No repository or service test exists for any rates entity. The

  =bond_leg_amount= and =trade_type= only.
- The wire codec's behaviour is covered by

** The proposed entity-relationship model

The model keeps the nine tables' trade-keyed identity and adds the one
level the rule asks for. The guiding decomposition:

- The fields every rates product shares become the *family header*. For
  this family the shared set is thin — =description= plus the dates the
  products that have them agree on — and the header's real work is to
  carry the family's coverage check and to own the family's rows for the
  delete cascade.
- The fields a product states of its own become that product's *fact
  table*, keyed by the same trade, with a check on its own codes.
- The rows a product states as a list — a leg, a schedule, a strike, an
  option — become rows in the *shared children*, keyed by the trade, an
  ordinal and a role code.
- The rates family has *no security level*. Nothing is keyed by a
  reference datum, because a swap has no issuer.

The relationships, stated as keys:

- Every rates row — the header row included — is keyed by =(tenant_id,
  trade_id)= and pins to the anchor with a composite foreign key on
  =(tenant_id, trade_id, party_id, trade_type)=. The anchor gains the
  unique index that key needs; that is a change to the trade model, not
  to this family.
- The product fact tables and the shared children name the same trade as
  the header. They are joined by the trade key, not by a declared foreign
  key, exactly as the bond family's facts join =bond_instruments=.
- The header declares the rows it owns in a =* Delete cascade= section,
  so the generated delete rule closes the family's legs, children and
  facts with it. This is finding R6's repair.
- =swap_leg= keeps =leg_number= as the ordinal and =leg_type_code= as the
  discriminator, and drops the surrogate =id=: the trade identifies the
  instrument and the ordinal identifies the leg, as =bond_legs= does with
  its =(trade_id, leg_role, leg_number)= key. This is finding R4's repair.
- =swap_leg_amount= and =swap_leg_rate= hang off the leg and carry the
  amortisation schedule and the rate steps the single columns cannot.
- The option block, schedules and strikes keep the shared shapes the bond
  pilot built. The rates family writes to them; it does not widen them.
  The one exception is =instrument_schedules.owner_role=, whose current
  check lists =trs_funding=, =repo=, =ascot_swap=, =option= and =trs=:
  the rates family's own owner roles are added to that list when the
  first rates schedule lands.

The alternatives, rejected:

- *Keep the nine tables as they are.* Each product table already carries
  its own check and its trade key, and the products share no economics,
  so a header looks redundant. It is rejected because nothing then owns
  the family: a delete leaves the legs and the children behind, and the
  blotter has no one row per rates trade to read. The header is a
  coverage and ownership device first, a field container second.
- *One table per distinct full shape.* It repeats the shared columns in
  every product table and still has no owner; it is the current design
  with the sparse columns moved.
- *A security level for rates.* There is no issuer and no ISIN. An
  =rate_issue= table would have no producer.
- *Fold the family into the trade anchor.* The anchor is immutable and
  its columns are fixed; the economics amend and the anchor must not.

** The blotter's common-date view

The rates story is where the blotter's common-date view lands (finding
R9). The view maps each product's date columns to one set of names —
trade date, start, expiry, maturity — so a deal grid reads one date
column across the family and each later family adds its own mapping.
The view is generated, not hand-written, because the mapping is a
property of the models.

** Mapping the current model onto the target

| Current | Target |
|---------+--------|
| =vanilla_swap_instruments= (header and facts in one) | the header's swap rows; =settlement_lag= to the header, =netting_set_id= out to the booking, no fact table |
| =fra_instruments= | header row + =fra_instruments= facts |
| =cap_floor_instruments= | header row + =cap_floor_instruments= facts + strikes and schedules as children |
| =swaption_instruments= | header row + =swaption_instruments= facts + the shared option block |
| =balance_guaranteed_swap_instruments= | header row + facts |
| =callable_swap_instruments= | header row + =callable_swap_call_dates= child |
| =knock_out_swap_instruments= | header row + facts |
| =inflation_swap_instruments= | leaves the family for inflation |
| =rpa_instruments= | leaves the family for credit |
| =swap_legs= (surrogate key) | re-keyed to (trade_id, leg_number) + amount and rate children |

The audit spine, the SCD2 overlap exclusion, the version uniqueness
index, the tenant isolation policy and the trade-activity foreign key
carry over unchanged.

** What each surface must change

- *Models.* Author the header model; re-key =swap_leg=; add
  =swap_leg_amount= and =swap_leg_rate=; give the header the
  =* Delete cascade= section; add =:routed_column:= and the
  =trade_type_code= column to the tables that lack it; retype the option
  block's text dates and collections; fix the three generator defaults;
  add the rates owner roles to =instrument_schedules=.
- *SQL.* Regenerated from the models. The migration script moves the rows
  of the nine tables into the header and the facts, which is the one
  hand-written artefact.
- *The catalogue.* Move =RiskParticipationAgreement= to
  =credit_instrument=, so the generated routing stops disagreeing with the
  importer and the credit table's check (finding R8). =InflationSwap=
  leaves for an inflation family when that family is scaffolded; until
  then its route stands and the reason is recorded. The three unrouted
  codes are recorded as products with no table.
- *The ORE mapping.* This is the family's largest repair, not a
  follow-up. Make the import write the legs it already builds (finding
  R15); keep a cross-currency swap's type instead of overwriting it to
  =Swap= (finding R16); populate the seven columns that exist and are
  never written, or drop them and record why (finding R17); give a leg
  its direction (finding R18); point each product's import at the header
  and its fact table; and add the missing reverse mappers for
  =InflationSwap=, =BalanceGuaranteedSwap= and =FlexiSwap= (finding R10).
- *The wire.* Replace the hand-written rates variant, carrier and codec
  with the generated per-entity protocols, or record why the carrier must
  stay hand-written — the three variants and =instrument_payload.cpp= are
  the family's largest hand-written surface (finding R13).
- *The composition roots.* Move the shared children out of
  =registrar_bond.cpp= into the root that owns them, and let the family
  registrars follow the generated shape rather than being hand-listed
  (findings R11, R12).
- *The service and the shell.* Regenerated for the header; the rates units
  follow the header's commands. The export service's hand-written switch
  and per-type dispatch collapse onto the generated routing it already
  calls (finding R12).
- *Tests.* Generate the eventing integration test for the nine
  instruments, and add repository and service coverage where the family
  has none (finding R14). Re-run the round trip over the family's corpus
  at the new shape.

** Staging

Each unit ends in a state a reviewer can verify on its own.

1. The routing repairs: RPA leaves for credit, the three generator
   defaults are fixed, and the coverage gap R1 closes. No table is
   reshaped. This is the smallest unit and it removes the two taxonomies'
   disagreement from the family's diff.
2. The legs: re-key =swap_legs=, give the leg its direction, add the
   amount and rate children, and make the import write the legs it
   already builds. This is the unit that makes a stored swap an
   instrument rather than a row (findings R4, R15, R18).
3. The header and the fact tables: author =rate_instruments=, move each
   product's shared fields to it, give it the cascade, populate the
   seven columns that exist and are empty, and stop overwriting the
   cross-currency type (findings R6, R16, R17).
4. The shared children: move the cap/floor strikes and schedules and the
   swaption option block onto the shapes the bond pilot built, and move
   their registration to the right root (finding R11).
5. The composite anchor key: add the anchor's unique index and the
   instruments' composite foreign key (finding R7).
6. The wire and the export path: the missing reverse mappers, and the
   hand-written codec's disposition (findings R10, R13).
7. The blotter's common-date view (finding R9).
8. The round trip over the family's corpus, the migration script, and the
   missing tests (findings R9, R14).

* Decisions

- *D1, the family boundary.* The family is the =interest_rates= asset
  class, not the catalogue's =product_type= value. =RiskParticipationAgreement=
  and =InflationSwap= are credit and inflation products and leave. Reason:
  the refdata taxonomy is the single source for the class
  ([[id:4043DE4C-5CDB-455E-B208-5AFF69AA76AE][asset_class_catalogue]]) and the two
  domain notes state the classes independently; =product_type= is defect
  10's misnamed column.
- *D2, the header's name.* =rate_instruments=, after =bond_instruments=.
  =swap_instruments= was rejected because the family's own legs table is
  already called =swap_legs= and the header is the family's, not the
  swap's.
- *D3, the header's content.* The header holds the coverage check, the
  trade-activity key, =description= and the dates the products agree on,
  and owns the family's cascade. It is a coverage and ownership device
  first; a field every product does not share does not move to it.
- *D4, the security level.* None. A rates trade's economics belong to the
  trade, so there is no master entity to normalise them onto.
- *D5, netting_set_id.* It leaves the instrument. =trade_booking= already
  holds =netting_set_id=, and holds it as a =uuid= foreign key into
  =ores_refdata_netting_sets_tbl=, where the instrument's copy is a bare
  =text= column checked against nothing. Keeping both would leave two
  columns for one fact with two types.

* Out of scope

- The credit and inflation families' own reworks, which own
  =rpa_instruments= and =inflation_swap_instruments= once they leave.
- The reference tables that turn the family's soft code references into
  real foreign keys. They are the shared reference-data work the
  trade-model investigation names, not this family's.
- The three unrouted codes' products, which need ORE importer support
  before a table can hold them.
