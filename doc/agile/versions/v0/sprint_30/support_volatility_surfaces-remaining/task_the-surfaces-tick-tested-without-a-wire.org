This page documents a [[id:31C868B9-306E-4282-BA8C-07E647021B34][task]] in the [[id:95541791-E25C-44A5-9625-C8A1E44976D7][Support volatility surfaces — the remaining work]] story. It captures the goal, current status, acceptance, and any notes or results.

* Goal

The review of PR #4686 found that the surface's tick is tested only through hand-built markets, so
four things it does are unproven: that it names a member the run has not published, that two ticks
against an unchanged curve emit identical quotes, that a spot feed's level reads back from the board
as the process's own value, and that the round trip goes *through* the board rather than around it.

=fx_vol_expiry_markets= was extracted for exactly this: it takes the three values and the periods and
answers one market per expiry, so a test can publish curves and a spot to a board, read them back
through the same views the feed reads, and assert both the messages and the markets without a wire.

Two layout questions ride with it, recorded as opportunities and not defects because nobody has
measured them: =run_market::drive= finds a series' drivers in a =std::map= per tick, and a board
member holds its points in a heap vector. Both are candidates for a flat arena once a run with a
thousand feeds is measured.

* Acceptance

- A surface ticked against a run that has published neither curve publishes nothing and answers =no market: curve:EUR=, naming the currency it could not read.
- The same tick against a run that has published the base curve names the quote currency, and against one that has published both curves names the pair's spot: =no market: spot:EUR/USD=.
- A curve published to the run's board reads back through the surface's own read as the same points, and the spot published under the pair's code reads back as the level that was published.
- Each expiry's market resolves at the value date the spot date plus that expiry's period gives, so the three expiries discount over three different horizons and no two markets are interchangeable.
- A market's forward is the ratio of the two curves' own factors at its value date, to =1e-12=, so the pair's two curves are both read and neither is assumed flat.
- Two consecutive ticks against an unchanged board hand the emitter identical markets and it emits identical quotes, so the board adds no drift of its own. A republication of the same values changes nothing either.
- The round trip goes through the board: curves and a spot are published to a board, read by the surface's read, turned into markets by =fx_vol_expiry_markets= and emitted, and then calibrated against those same markets, with no =fx_market= built by hand in that test. Its error is measured once and recorded in Measurements with its command and commit.
- The pair's spot member is named by one function at the writer and at the reader, so the spot a spot feed publishes is the spot a surface reads.
- A hidden benchmark states the cost of one driver lookup per tick and one board member read at a thousand members, with its command and commit. The budget it is read against is the sentence the tick's own body builds, which is the other thing the loop pays for on every tick: the layout of =drive= and of a member's points changes only if a lookup costs more than that sentence.

* Plan

Three questions the review left open, answered in this order, because each one's answer
decides what the next can even assert.

*Stage 1 — the seam.* The tick's gates are the two sentences the previous task shipped and
nothing tests. They are unreachable because they sit after a =nats::client&= in the
constructor and the publish loop. So the read comes out of =tick= into one function that
takes the board and the three member ids and answers either the three values or the member
it could not read. =tick= then keeps the publish loop and nothing else, and the message
stays byte-identical: the function names the member, the tick prefixes =no market: =. The
alternative -- constructing a feed with a default client -- would test the same sentence
through a class whose only remaining dependence is a broker, which is the wire this task
exists to avoid. The review showed the price of that: the =no market: = prefix belongs to
the tick, so no test of the read alone can assert the sentence an operator greps for. Every
gate answers before the publish, so a feed built on a client that is never used is not the
wire -- it is the sentence. The gate case therefore does what this stage argued against, and
the argument was wrong.

*Stage 2 — the values.* With the read in hand, the tests publish to a real board and read
back through the same views, under the name =fx_pair_member_code= builds. The curves are
stated points rather than ones =append_curve_points= derives from a process: that path has
its own tests, and stating the points is what lets the expected factor be a literal a reader
can check by hand.

*Stage 3 — the dates.* The value date is the claim the previous task made and never
observed: the three expiries must discount over three different horizons. A flat curve with
a point every tenor makes the expected factor =exp(-r t)= exactly, so the assertion is a
number rather than a direction, and a forward ratio off the two curves pins that both are
read.

*Stage 4 — no drift.* Two ticks, and a republish between them, compared point by point. The
emitter's processes are at their start and the drivers are zero, so the fixed point is
exact and any difference is the board's.

*Stage 5 — the round trip.* The existing round-trip test builds three identical =fx_market=s
by hand and hands each side the same one, which is why the review called it weak. It is
rewritten in place rather than joined by a second case: the case goes through the board end
to end and calibrates the quotes against the markets the read produced, and it keeps the fit
assertions it had, so the claim that the fit recovers the quotes is still measured -- on
three markets that differ, rather than on one stated three times. The plan said the old case
would stay; keeping it would have been a test that guarantees its own precondition.

*Stage 6 — the measurement.* =drive= and the board's layout were deferred because nobody
had measured them. A hidden =[.benchmark]= test, in the style of the pq layer benchmark,
states one lookup and one read at a thousand members; the numbers go in Measurements and
the decision follows from them, not from taste.

*Two leftovers found while reading for stage 1*, both one line: a comment above
=convention= that still describes the deleted =markets= field of the build input, and a
manual slash join in =make_fx_spot_feed= that the previous task's review recorded as fixed
by =fx_pair_member_code=. The second matters here because the spot member's name is what
the reader and the writer must agree on.

* Notes

** The layout questions are closed by numbers, not by taste

The two candidates the previous review deferred were =drive='s map lookup and a member's
points in their own heap vector. Measured at the scale the deferral named -- a thousand
series, a thousand members -- a lookup reads between 15.85 and 26.07 ns across three runs
and a member read between 6.39 and 8.09 ns across two, against 50.45 to 141.98 ns for a
sentence of the tick body's shape and a publish in the microseconds. The spread is the
machine's load, not the code's, which is why the table carries the range: at the worst
reading a lookup still costs a sixth of what the body's own message costs, so nothing on
the path is worth re-laying out. What a change would buy is 26 ns a tick, at ten thousand
ticks a second 0.26 ms of a second of run.

The second is worth one sentence of argument the number cannot make: a *reader* touches one
member, and that member's points are contiguous inside its own vector, so the locality a
flat arena would buy is locality the reader already has. A flat arena would help only a
sweep over every member, which nothing does today; the screen read that will does so off
the loop.

** Two things the tests corrected, which the code had wrong

- =fx_vol_point_tick::stale= is the noise model's stale-quote flag, not "this value repeats
  the last one". The no-drift test first asserted it and failed correctly: the clean
  contributor never reports a stale quote. The claim is carried by bit-for-bit equality.
- The round trip compared =fit.error=, which is signed, to an absolute value. It passed
  only because that fixture's residuals happened to be positive; with three different
  markets one is negative and the comparison caught itself.

* Result

The surface's tick is now testable without a wire, and what it does is tested. The read came
out of the body into =fx_vol_board_inputs=, which answers the pair's three values or the
member it could not read; the tick keeps the walk over the points and the publish, so the
sentence it logs is byte-identical to the one it logged before.

Four claims, each of which had none. The three gates, asserted as the whole sentence a
reader of the log sees -- =no market: curve:EUR=, =no market: curve:USD=,
=no market: spot:EUR/USD= -- by building the real feed and ticking it, with =publish_count()=
checked at zero after each, which is "publishes nothing" as a number rather than a hope. The
value date of each expiry: 16 December, 16 February and 16 May, at 30, 92 and 181 days, with
the discounts and forwards those dates carry stated as literals, so a wrong period addition
or a shared expression cannot agree with itself. Two ticks with the same values republished
between them quoting bit for bit, and stating the 1M ATM they quote rather than only
comparing it with its own next step. And a round trip that goes *through* the board --
curves and a spot published, read back through the surface's own read, and calibrated
against the markets that read produced -- costing 2.55e-09 at ATM and 6.56e-09 at the wings,
asserted at =1e-8= and =2e-8=.

The two layout questions the previous review deferred are closed by measurement rather than
by argument: a driver lookup reads 15.85 to 26.07 ns across three runs and a board read 6.39
to 8.09 ns, at the scale the deferral named, against 50.45 to 141.98 ns for a sentence of
the tick body's shape. Neither changed; the table carries the ranges because a single median
would claim more precision than the number has.

The review of this branch found that the first version of these tests asserted the member
name and never the sentence -- nothing called =tick= -- which is the finding this page's
Review table records first. It also found the expectations computing themselves, the
no-drift case comparing the emitter with itself, and the round trip asserting forty times
looser than it measured. All four are fixed in the commit the Measurements cite.

Two leftovers from that review table were real and are gone: the comment above the build
input's convention described a field deleted in the previous task, and =make_fx_spot_feed=
built the pair's member code with a slash join of its own -- the one string the spot's
writer and the surface's reader have to agree on, and now the one function that builds it.

What is not here: the two gates' *static* side, the start verb's refusal of a pair with no
curve feed. It is asserted where it lives, in the feed kind registry's tests, and this task
adds nothing to it.
