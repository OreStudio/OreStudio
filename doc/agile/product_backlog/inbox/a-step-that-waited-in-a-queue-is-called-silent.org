#+title: A step that waited in a queue is called silent
#+description: The engine's step deadline counts from dispatch, so a step queued behind a slow one is failed as silent; a real failure then leaves a tenant half-built, because a run's provisioning cannot be resumed.
#+type: capture
#+level: cross
#+filetags: 
#+created: 2026-10-27
#+updated: 2026-10-27

This page is a [[id:671F18E4-E09C-4B3B-BD24-D33DF8AE38A6][capture]] in the *inbox* bucket of the product backlog — a pre-sprint idea, not yet pulled into a sprint as a story.

* What

Count a workflow step's deadline from the moment a service starts the step,
not from the moment its command was dispatched, and let a run be resumed from
the step that failed without deleting what it already published.

* Why

A provisioning run on 2026-10-27 reached 7 of its 8 steps and then failed the
eighth as silent. The step had been dispatched together with two others, and
the service ran them one at a time: the step spent its whole two-minute budget
queued behind a password change and never started. The engine had already
stamped the step's start time at dispatch, so it could not tell a step that was
waiting from one that had begun and stopped reporting, and it failed a healthy
step.

What made the false failure expensive is the second half. The tenant stayed in
`bootstrapping`, the page then showed the setup wizard instead of the data, and
re-running `provision tenant` refused because the tenant existed. The only
supported repair is `workflow retry <instance> --step_name <name>`, which
requires knowing the instance id from the database. An operator does not.

The two belong together because either alone is a half-fix: a correct deadline
still leaves a run that a real failure strands, and a resumable run still fails
steps that were merely queued.
