#+title: A data-oriented data model for ores.trading
#+description: The code-review-data-model review of the whole ores.trading data model: regime classification, per-family decision and invariant checks, the findings with causes, anchored scores, and the proposed data-oriented target model — a normalised relational model of atomised entities with keys and foreign keys and no object graph, the domain and the wire as projections, composite and versioning semantics preserved as per-table facts and temporal joins, container messages at the boundary, with the service-layer changes and PR sequencing.
#+type: knowledge
#+level: cross
#+filetags: :knowledge:architecture:trading:data_model:data_oriented_design:codegen:
#+created: 2026-09-08
#+updated: 2026-09-08

* Summary

ores.trading is a Regime 2 (persisted domain data) and Regime 3 (wire)
component; it has no Regime 1 kernel of its own. Its data model is
half codegen-driven. The modeling orgs fully express 25 of the
instrument families, but the families that carry the most domain
structure — bond, commodity, composite, credit, scripted, the two legs
tables, and the whole =trade= domain — live in the tree by hand, as do
the wire consolidation for fourteen families and a set of orphan
generated-looking stacks that have no org and never had one. The Sep-8
regeneration oracle produced 47 modified and 65 untracked files, and
ores.trading is absent from the drift registry, so nothing in CI
catches the divergence. Regeneration today would silently flatten
nested domain groups, drop domain-only members, rename primary keys,
re-optionalize members, and re-emit superseded messaging files.

The review scores the component 2 out of 5 (Regime 2 and Regime 3),
with High debt. The findings trace to three root causes: codegen
grammar gaps (four known, from task [[id:2548EE95-7C62-4C48-BA11-2146604B2082][2548EE95]]), a tree that
evolved past its models (the trade domain and the legacy instrument
families), and a wire consolidation that happened tree-side instead of
model-side.

The proposed target is a proper data-oriented model in the
relational reading of the DOD book, not an incremental completion of
the current shape. No object graph is modelled: an entity is its
rows; relationships are primary and foreign keys; composition,
ownership and aggregate versioning do not exist as model concepts.
All trading data is normalised: tables are not sparse, no repeating
groups hide in columns, and optional or repeating structure is
declared as keyed child rows. Every persisted family gets an org; the
grammar grows (mid-groups for the C1202 layout, column mapping,
custom methods, wire emission control); and semantics survive as
per-table facts — the SCD2 temporal spine, optimistic versioning, soft
delete, keys and foreign keys, composite as-of reads as temporal
joins, trade lifecycle rows and the successor chain. Object-shaped
documents (a trade with its parts, a composite with its legs) exist
only at the boundary: the service assembles them from rows, and the
wire carries them as parts messages or as container messages composed
from the generated part types.

* Detail

** Scope, method, and evidence

This analysis applies the [[id:AE72D444-02B1-4428-B56C-062B849ED568][code-review-data-model]] skill to the
whole ores.trading data model, against the criteria of
[[id:BBD43BF2-9FEE-42B0-A667-9468AF759DC2][Data-Oriented Design in ORE Studio]]. It classifies the component
into regimes, walks the decision checks per entity family, spot-checks
the invariant checks, and reports findings, anchored scores, verdict
and debt. It then proposes the target data-oriented model and the
service-layer changes the model requires.

The review reads every modelling org under
=projects/ores.trading/modeling/=, the domain C++ under
=projects/ores.trading/api/include/ores.trading.api/domain/=, the
repository, service, and messaging headers under
=ores.trading.core=, and the SQL under
=projects/ores.sql/create/trading/=. It builds on prior analysis:
the codegen gap investigation (task [[id:2548EE95-7C62-4C48-BA11-2146604B2082][2548EE95]]), the bond probe
org [[id:BE4698B1-56B1-4686-AD68-C16D4611A1FD][BE4698B1]], the C1202 complexity investigation
(file:doc/investigations/msvc_c1202_rfl_complexity.org), the Sep-8
drift oracle it produced, the temporal and composite versioning design
[[id:BA159E88-74EB-4401-91CC-1DE7DF5AE50A][BA159E88]], and the Sep-6 entity classification and drift baseline
work (sprint 25 story =entity-classification-drift-rollout=). The
current task and story that drive this work are
[[id:40DA4D49-C608-4AC0-9F69-B7D3DF75E3C7][the analysis task]] in [[id:688959F0-A1AB-4021-A57E-1103AD92773A][the redesign story]].

No hot-path or performance claims are made anywhere in this review:
ores.trading is not a numerical kernel. Access-pattern statements cite
service and repository signatures (batch reads, at-version reads,
history) and SQL index definitions.

** Regime classification

| Regime | Part of the component | Applies |
|--------+-----------------------+---------|
| 1 — numerical kernels | None. Pricing, simulation and analytics live in marketdata, analytics and compute; trading consumes results. | No — unscored |
| 2 — persisted domain data | Every entity: instruments, legs, trades, identifiers, lookups, and their C++ domain types and SQL. | Yes |
| 3 — wire and messaging | NATS protocol/handler/registrar surface for instruments, trades and lookups. | Yes |

The regimes cover table-shaped data and its projections. Document
shaped payloads (FpML documents, ORE XML inputs) map to =trade= and
its instrument entities at the boundary; no entity model persists
document trees in trading, so the boundary note of the criteria page
does not change anything here.

*** Invariant surface — domain C++ (176 files)

Domain types live under
=ores.trading.api/domain/= as a quartet per entity: the domain header,
a JSON codec header, a table codec header, and a table-I/O header.

- Generated quartets exist for all 25 org-bound families.
- Hand-written quartets exist for the five legacy families (bond,
  commodity, composite, credit, scripted) and for =composite_leg=.
  They follow the generated shape closely: value semantics, group
  layout, no pointers. The composite quartets include the legs
  accessors and the read-only =composite_instruments.legs.list=
  subject support.
- Orphan stacks exist for =swap_leg= (domain, table codecs, SQL,
  repository/mapper — but no service and no org), for
  =activity_type= and =fpml_event_type= (full generated-style service,
  repository, domain and SQL stacks), and for =product_type= (domain
  header and the SQL type =product_type_t=). Git history shows no org
  ever existed, or was ever deleted, for these names. =swap_leg= is
  the shared legs table of all nine rates families; =activity_type=
  and =fpml_event_type= have no wire or UI surface today;
  =product_type= is bound as the C++ type of a =trade_type= lookup
  member and as the SQL type of the =trade.product_type= column.

Invariant spot checks: the generated types carry plain value
semantics with no owning-pointer members; the hand-written quartets
mimic them. The =instrument.hpp= concept layer glues the families.
The failure is not in the types that exist; it is in which types the
generator owns, and in the absence of any drift check to prove the
hand-written remainder stays aligned.

*** Persistence surface — SQL (90 files under =ores.sql=)

=projects/ores.sql/create/trading/= holds one =CREATE= and one
=NOTIFY TRIGGER= file per table, plus the aggregate =trading_create.sql=,
the trade-family function files, and the bond and credit extension
files.

- The bound 25 instrument tables, generated from the orgs.
- Hand-maintained tables for bond, commodity, composite instruments,
  composite legs, credit, scripted — and =swap_legs=, the shared legs
  table of the nine rates families.
- The trade domain: =trades= (a generated-style SCD2 table carrying
  hand-maintained trigger SQL, ten index statements and a delete rule
  on top of its template), =trade_identifiers=, =trade_party_roles=,
  plus the =_bu_= and =_functions= files.
- The four lookup tables and the phantom =activity_types=,
  =fpml_event_types= and =product_type= families.

Generated tables (the 25 and, where modelled, the lookups) carry the
standard SCD2 temporal spine: primary key =(tenant_id, id,
valid_from, valid_to)=, a =tstzrange= exclusion constraint, current
row partial unique indexes, an insert trigger that performs the
select-for-update version dance and raises =Version conflict:
expected version %, but current version %=, and soft delete that
closes the row. The hand-written tables replicate the pattern by
hand. The two legs tables add their own rules: =composite_legs=
requires =leg_sequence >= 1= and validates that =instrument_id=
exists among current =composite_instruments= rows; =swap_legs= is
written by the nine rates families' code with no owning table.

*** Wire surface — messaging (24 protocols, 59 handler/registrar files)

The wire organisation is not a projection of the orgs; it is a
patchwork of three shapes that grew in sequence.

1. Per-family generated protocols, handlers and registrars for the 16
   FX and equity families:
   =ores.trading.api/messaging/<family>_protocol.hpp= (16 files) and
   =ores.trading.core/messaging/<family>_instrument_handler.hpp= +
   =<family>_registrar.hpp= (32 files).
2. A hand-written consolidated =instrument_protocol.hpp= covering
   fourteen families — the five legacy families plus the nine rates
   families — with per-family =list/save/delete= subjects, =history=
   for most families, and the read-only
   =composite_instruments.legs.list= subject. Its
   includes span every instrument domain header, FX and equity
   included, which shows the consolidation intent; it was written by
   hand, tree-side. On the core side the consolidated
   =rates_instrument_handler.hpp= serves the same fourteen families
   for handler dispatch, and the five legacy families have their own
   standalone handler headers with no registrar at all.
3. Trade and lookup protocols (=trade_protocol.hpp=,
   =trade_identifier_protocol.hpp=, =party_role_type_protocol.hpp=,
   and the rest) with per-entity handlers and history-provider
   registrars. The trade registrar and trade event files were never
   committed; the trade handler is wired through a hand-composed
   service chain.

The org grammar has no way to suppress or steer the per-family
emission (gap (d) below). Regenerating the nine rates orgs re-emits
36 per-family messaging files that the tree deliberately superseded
with the consolidated protocol and handler.

*** Service surface (40 services under =ores.trading.core/service=)

The generated service convention is batch- and version-shaped:
=list(offset, limit)=, =count=, =get_<N>=, =get_<N>_at_version(id,
version)=, =get_<N>_batch(ids)=, =save(v)=, =save_batch(vs)=,
=delete_<N>(id)=, =delete_<N>_batch(ids)=, =history(id)=. The
at-version and history members cite the temporal composite
versioning design [[id:BA159E88-74EB-4401-91CC-1DE7DF5AE50A][BA159E88]] in their comments.

Beside that generated surface sit hand-added members that the model
cannot express. =fra_instrument_service= carries
=get_swap_legs(instrument_id)= and =get_swap_legs_batch(...)=, with a
comment that all rates families share the swap_legs table and that
this is the shared access point for any type. Its repository member is
the shared =swap_leg_repository=. The composite save path, the legs
aggregation, and the history reads that join parent and legs are
hand-composed. Sep-6 binding clobbered the fra legs accessors;
commit 2e75d95704 restored them by hand.

The repository layer holds 123 headers: per-entity
entity/mapper/repository triples, the shared =swap_leg_repository=
and =composite_leg_repository=, plus the orphan activity_type and
fpml_event_type triples.

*** Eventing surface

The 16 FX and equity orgs carry the inline =eventing-integration-test=
flag; their generated integration tests live under
=ores.trading.core/tests/= as
=<family>_eventing_integration_tests.cpp=. The nine rates orgs do not
carry the flag and generate no eventing tests. Trade eventing exists
only as hand-written tests under =ores.trading.api/tests/
eventing_trade_events_tests.cpp=.

** Decision checks (Regime 2), walked per entity family

Where the orgs run, the decision surface is sound:

| Check | Verdict | Evidence |
|-------+---------+----------|
| Identity by key | Pass on modelled families | Every family: instrument_id PK under tenant/workspace; trade FKs to books, portfolios, party, instruments by key, not by pointer or owning link. |
| Table-shaped children | Pass at entity level | Children are rows in their own tables: =trade_identifiers=, =trade_party_roles=, the legs tables. No nested object trees in SQL. |
| Core/cold split | Pass for the generated shape | Identity and audit group columns ride the SCD2 spine; economics stay in the row. History and versioning are table- and trigger-level, not in the row. |
| Batched collection access | Pass where generated | =get_<N>_batch(ids)=, =save_batch=, =delete_<N>_batch=, =list= on every generated service; the Sep-8 brace-style drift shows the 16 FX/equity services carry this surface. |
| Explicit variation | Pass by construction | A family of 30 instrument tables, one per type, plus hand groups for the richer ones — closed per-type variation, no inheritance web. |
| Normalised tables | Fail | Wide rows of optional economics members across the 25 orgs (=fx_asian_forward=: 12 of 12 economics optional). Under the relational rule, feature presence is row presence, so these split into keyed child rows (F11). |

Most failures below are failures of the decision surface as a whole:
the orgs cover 25 of roughly 40 persisted tables; the richest domain
structures are outside the model; and the model cannot express
several shapes the tree legitimately chose. Three of the four known
codegen gaps from task [[id:2548EE95-7C62-4C48-BA11-2146604B2082][2548EE95]] are decision-surface gaps. One
failure sits inside the modelled set: the sparse wide rows (F11), a
normalisation failure the relational target fixes.

(a) Nested mid-groups are inexpressible. The tree decomposes bond,
commodity, composite, credit, scripted and trade domain headers into
nested group layers (for example, a bond's =terms= and =details= under
its economic core). The org grammar models only flat members plus the
identity/audit pair, so these families could not be bound. The C1202
investigation (file:doc/investigations/msvc_c1202_rfl_complexity.org)
shows why the tree shape is not cosmetic: MSVC limits
=rfl::Literal= to nine fields, so plain nested sub-structs of at most
nine members are the mandated decomposition. The bond probe
[[id:BE4698B1-56B1-4686-AD68-C16D4611A1FD][BE4698B1]] regenerated a bond org in the current grammar and diffed it
against the tree: regeneration flattens the three mid-groups into one
fat row, drops =security_id= (a domain-only member that does not
persist), re-optionalizes =int= and =double= members that the tree
keeps as mandatory values with =value_or(0)= defaults, and renames
the primary key =id= to =instrument_id=. Every one of those changes
loses or relocates semantics, and the org grammar cannot express any
of it today. =rfl::Flatten= does not reduce the parent's field count,
so there is no codegen-side workaround.

(b) The column-name mapping key does not exist. The tree's legacy
tables use =id= as the primary key column while the identity group
names the member =instrument_id=; the mapper layer bridges this by
hand (for example, =scripted_instrument_mapper.cpp:35=). A
regeneration that follows the org strictly renames the column and
breaks every legacy reference.

(c) Custom service methods are inexpressible. The fra legs accessors
and the composite legs aggregation exist only in hand-written service
headers. The paste-block machinery that would let an org declare such
methods exists in the generator (=core.py=, line ~995) but is unused.

(d) Per-family messaging emission cannot be suppressed. The orgs
unconditionally emit the per-family protocol/handler/registrar
triple. That is correct for FX/equity, where the per-family wire is
the tree reality, and wrong for the nine rates families, where the
tree consolidated into =instrument_protocol.hpp= and
=rates_instrument_handler.hpp=. Regeneration of the nine rates orgs
re-emits 36 superseded files.

*** F1 — the unmodelled families carry the most structure

Bond, commodity, composite, credit, scripted, and both legs tables
exist only in the tree. Their domain quartets, mappers,
repositories, services, handlers and SQL are hand-maintained. Nothing
in CI compares them to a model; the component is not in the drift
registry. The bond probe ([[id:BE4698B1-56B1-4686-AD68-C16D4611A1FD][BE4698B1]]) proves what a regeneration
would do today: emit ~30 files that flatten and drop the tree's
semantics. The probe org is parked in the agile tree; it returns to
the modeling directory once the grammar can express the shape.

*** F2 — the grammar cannot express the tree's chosen domain shapes

The tree decomposed the rich families into nested mid-groups of at
most nine fields (the C1202 ceiling) precisely so the C++ would
compile on MSVC. That decomposition is the correct DOD shape: it
keeps the economics groups small and plain. The grammar cannot
receive it. Domain-only members like =security_id=, which live in the
domain header but not in the row, have no representation at all. The
probe's three divergences — flatten, drop, re-optionalize — are each
a silent semantic change that a drift gate would have to special-case
forever. The fix is grammar growth, not tree shrinkage.

*** F3 — the trade domain diverged from its org on every facet

The tree's =trade= is the five-group decomposition (=trade_identity=,
=trade_parties=, =trade_classification=, =trade_lifecycle=,
=trade_audit= — the field-group orgs exist and the domain headers use
them). The =trade= org is an 18-column flat list with no profile,
never bound, and no identity/audit groups. The deployed
=ores_trading_trades_tbl= is template-generated — its header names the
=sql_schema_domain_entity_create.mustache= template and its columns
match the org plus the spine — but the tree layers hand-maintained
semantics on top: an insert trigger that validates the book, the
portfolio (including the parent match), the successor self-FK, the
counterparty, the status, the asset class, the trade type, the
activity type and the change reason, and derives =party_id= from the
book; ten index statements (two current-row uniqueness indexes plus
eight access paths on tenant, book, portfolio, netting set, trade
type, instrument, asset class and workspace); and a soft-delete rule.
The Qt controllers carry hand-evolved features (openEdit, import, the
change-reason cache); the service chain is hand-composed; the trade
registrar and trade event files were never committed. A regeneration
from the org today would emit a flat 18-column domain and clobber the
trigger logic, the access indexes and the delete rule. Yet the
=trade_identifier=, =trade_party_role=, =lifecycle_event= and the four
lookup orgs are drift-clean — so the trade binding problem is
contained to =trade= itself, and the fix is a faithful org, not a new
architecture.

*** F4 — the wire is a second artefact, not a projection

The criteria page states the rule: the wire shape is a codegen
projection of the Regime 2 entity, not a second artefact. Trading
violates it twice. The consolidation (one protocol, one handler for
fourteen families) is exactly what a model should express — but it
was done by hand in the tree, so the orgs still emit their per-family
files and the consolidated files drift outside the model. And the
three shapes coexist without a statement of why: FX/equity per
family, fourteen families consolidated, the trade/lookup set
per-entity. The correct fix makes the wire organisation a model
decision: an org or profile key that selects the consolidated
projection for the families that ship it.

** Scores, verdict, debt

| Regime | Score | What it resembles |
|--------+-------+--------------------|
| 1 — numerical kernels | — | Not applicable; unscored. |
| 2 — persisted domain data | 2 | Several Important findings and one Critical (F1) in the main flow; the modelled subset is sound except its normalisation (F11). |
| 3 — wire and messaging | 2 | The wire is not a projection (F4) and the Regime 3 flat-value checks pass only on the 16 per-family projections. |

Verdict: the component's data model is a transition artifact. The
modelled subset (25 families) passes its checks except normalisation
(F11); the unmodelled subset (the legacy five, the legs tables,
trade) is where the domain lives, and it is invisible to every gate.
The score is 2, not 1,
because the divergence is latent: the deployed tree works, nothing in
the component regenerates in CI, and the failures would only manifest
when a regeneration ran. The moment the drift gate extends to
trading, the score drops to 1 unless the grammar gaps close first.

Debt: High (score band 1-2).

Two reviews of this component should land within one point. The
scores rest on countable artefacts: 38 orgs, 176 domain headers, 40
services, 24 protocols, the 47+65 Sep-8 oracle result, the probe
diff. If a second review disagreed, the disagreement would be in the
classification (whether the hand-written legacy stacks count as the
"core facet domain headers" the invariant walk covers), not in the
evidence.

** Root causes

1. The org grammar grew profile-first, not shape-first. Binding
   (FX/equity, then rates) proved the identity/audit spine, batch
   surface, and per-type tables. The rich families predate the
   profiles and could not be bound, so they stayed hand-written and
   the bind stories closed with vacuous zero-diff acceptances:
   trading was never in the drift registry that would have measured
   them.
2. The tree evolved past its models where the model stopped. The
   trade org (18 flat columns) dates from before the five-group
   decomposition; the decomposition, the indexes, the Qt features
   and the registration chain all happened tree-side afterward.
3. Consolidation happened by deletion, not by expression. The rates
   wire consolidation and the instrument_protocol merge removed
   per-family artefacts from the tree instead of switching them off
   in the model, so every regeneration resurrects them.

  47 modified, 65 untracked files across five groups — 16 FX/equity
  service headers (brace-style only), the fra service (hand-restored
  legs methods), 36 rates messaging files the orgs re-emit, and the
  trade family (Qt features, hand-maintained trigger and index SQL,
  event files, hand-composed chain). Clean groups: =trade_identifier=,
  =trade_party_role=, =lifecycle_event=, the four lookups.
- The bond probe [[id:BE4698B1-56B1-4686-AD68-C16D4611A1FD][BE4698B1]] is the concrete proof of F2; its
  verdict drives the grammar work in the sequencing below.
- The C1202 investigation sets the layout ceiling the mid-group
  grammar must honour: nested sub-structs of at most nine members,
  plain values, nested JSON on the wire.
- [[id:BA159E88-74EB-4401-91CC-1DE7DF5AE50A][BA159E88]] designs temporal composite entity versioning: the
  versioned child + parent version bump via a touch function, plus
  =find_as_of= and =list_by_as_of= reads. It was applied to refdata.
  The trading target follows the relational reading instead: each
  entity versions on its own spine, and the composite as-of read is a
  temporal join over keys. Option A's aggregate versioning is
  deliberately not adopted for trading; the semantics section below
  states the divergence.

** The target data-oriented model

The target follows the relational reading of DOP, taken from the DOD
book: section 5.4, =There is no entity=
(https://www.dataorienteddesign.com/dodmain/node5.html), and the
relational-model chapter
(https://www.dataorienteddesign.com/dodmain/node11.html). Its
principle, stated once: no object graph is modelled. =trade=,
=instrument=, =leg=, =identifier= and =lookup= are sets of atomised
rows. Each row has a primary key; relationships are foreign keys and
joins. There is no parent that owns children, and no aggregate that
versions as a whole. A composite instrument is =composite_instruments=
rows and =composite_legs= rows joined by keys; a trade document is
=trades=, =trade_identifiers= and =trade_party_roles= rows. The
document exists only when a read or a message needs it: the service
assembles it from rows, and the wire carries it. Data is normalised:
tables are not sparse, and optional or repeating structure is keyed
child rows, not nullable feature columns. Versioning is a per-table
property of the SCD2 spine. Semantics survive because the facts and
their keys survive — identity, temporal validity, referential
integrity, ordering as keyed data. Nothing survives because a trigger
or a hand-written aggregate keeps it consistent; invariants that need
write-time maintenance are keys, constraints and generated rules, or
they are gone. This reading corrects the first draft of this
analysis, which had adopted refdata's aggregate versioning (option A
of [[id:BA159E88-74EB-4401-91CC-1DE7DF5AE50A][BA159E88]]) for the composite; that is object-graph thinking,
and this model does not adopt it.

*** Entity shape: atomised entities, keys, one spine

- Every persisted family gets an entity org: the five legacy
  families, =composite_leg= and =swap_leg= first, then =trade=.
  Coverage is the acceptance test: no domain class, SQL table, or
  wire artefact lives only in the tree.
- All instrument orgs share the =trading-instrument= profile, the
  =instrument_identity= domain-identity group, the =ores.dq=
  audit-record group, and =has_batch_read=. The identity group's
  columns are declared once, in the group org, and inherited — the
  per-org repetition of F8 disappears. The =rpa= three-column variance
  is resolved in the group's favour.
- An entity declares its mandatory single-valued facts as members.
  Optional or repeating structure is declared as child entities with
  keys and foreign keys, never as nullable columns (the Normalisation
  section states the rule).
- Every table's keys and foreign keys are declared in the org and
  emitted as constraints: =composite_legs= references
  =composite_instruments= by key, the trade children reference the
  =trade= row, and =successor_trade_id= is a self foreign key. Index
  keys and validation rules are declared in the org, not recreated by
  hand in SQL.
- The audit group, version columns and the SCD2 spine are generated
  from the profile, not repeated in every create file.

*** Normalisation

The relational chapter of the DOD book sets the storage rules this
target adopts (https://www.dataorienteddesign.com/dodmain/node11.html):

- Tables are not sparse. Optional members and repeating groups are
  not columns in the entity's row; they are child rows keyed by the
  entity's key. Stored this way, every present fact is a row and no
  column waits NULL for a feature the type may not have.
- Every table has a key, and relationships embed the referenced row's
  key. Compound keys express ordered membership: a leg is keyed by
  its instrument and its =leg_sequence=.
- Facts that derive from other stored facts are not stored; a derived
  member is a query or a join, decided when a read needs one.

Current trading data violates the rule on both surfaces. The orgs
declare wide rows of optional economics: =fx_asian_forward= has 12
economics members, all optional; =fx_digital_option= has 7 of 12;
=equity_accumulator= has 5 of 13 (F11). The =trade= org is one wide
18-column row over five domain groups (F3). The one deliberate
exception is a foreign key that references an optional relationship —
an unlinked instrument, a trade with no successor: absence of a
relationship is not absence of a feature, and that FK stays nullable.

The target normalises family by family in the conversion PRs (the 25
bound families in PR 4, the hand-written families and legs in PR 5):
optional economics move to keyed child tables, and regeneration
migrates the trading SQL in the same pass. Mid-groups are not
affected: the C1202 groups structure the columns that travel together
inside one entity's C++ type and JSON; they become tables only when
the group is optional or repeating.

*** Grammar growth (closes gaps (a), (b), (c), (d))

1. Mid-groups with domain-only members (gap (a)). The org grammar
   gains a nested group key: a member may declare =group= with a
   nested entity group that has its own =* Fields= (including
   =domain_only= members that never persist), its own =table_column=
   prefix or absence, and its own flattening contract — emit the group
   as a plain nested sub-struct of at most nine fields (the C1202
   ceiling), never flatten into the parent, never re-optionalize a
   member whose tree shape is mandatory, and keep domain-only members
   out of the JSON/table projections. The bond probe org
   [[id:BE4698B1-56B1-4686-AD68-C16D4611A1FD][BE4698B1]] becomes the regression test: the grammar work is done
   when the probe regenerates drift-clean against the tree.
2. Column-name mapping (gap (b)). An org key overrides the emitted
   SQL column name for a member (=id= where the tree legacy tables
   say =id=, the group default =instrument_id= elsewhere). The mapper
   bridge becomes generated, not hand-patched.
3. Custom methods (gap (c)). The paste-block machinery is wired to
   the orgs: a service section in the org declares the multi-table
   operations the family needs (fra legs accessors, transactional
   composite or trade saves, legs batch reads). Binding no longer
   clobbers them, and the hand-restore commits (2e75d95704 and
   successors) become unnecessary.
4. Wire emission control (gap (d)). A per-entity key (or a profile
   attribute, see F9) selects the wire organisation: per-family
   projection for the 16 FX/equity families; consolidated projection
   for the fourteen families that ship =instrument_protocol= and
   =rates_instrument_handler=; suppressed where a family's legs have
   no standalone subjects beyond the read-only legs list. The
   consolidated protocol and handler become
   generated projections of the families that select them, so the
   tree-side merge stops being a deletion that regenerations undo.
5. Eventing (F9). The =trading-instrument= profile gains the
   eventing-integration-test switch; the per-org flags retire and all
   25 families — nine rates included — generate eventing tests.

*** Semantics preservation under atomisation

Atomising the domain raises the question the redesign must answer:
what semantics must survive the split into rows, and where does the
model state them? The list below is the contract; every item becomes
an explicit model or generated-SQL element, never hand SQL in the
tree.

1. Versioned temporal spine. Each persisted table carries the SCD2
   spine: primary key =(tenant_id, id, valid_from, valid_to)=, the
   =tstzrange= exclusion constraint, current-row partial indexes, and
   soft delete by closing the row. Optimistic versioning: the insert
   trigger selects the current row for update, bumps =version=, and
   raises =Version conflict= on a mismatch. The trade tables, the
   legs tables and the legacy five follow the same generated spine.
2. Audit attribution. Every row change records the =ores.dq=
   audit-record columns (who, when, which workspace, and the change
   reason where the UI supplies one). The change-reason cache that
   the trade Qt controllers hand-evolve is a cold annex of the audit
   group, not a controller feature.
3. Composite relation, no composition invariant. =composite_instruments=
   and =composite_legs= are two entities. A leg row carries the
   =composite_instruments= key it belongs to as a foreign key, and
   =leg_sequence>=1 is keyed data: the ordered set of legs is rows and
   keys, not a container. Each entity versions independently on the
   SCD2 spine; optimistic concurrency is per table. The composite as
   of a date is a temporal join: the instrument rows whose validity
   window contains the date, joined to the leg rows whose windows
   contain it. There is no parent version bump and no aggregate
   version. This is a deliberate divergence from refdata, where
   option A of [[id:BA159E88-74EB-4401-91CC-1DE7DF5AE50A][BA159E88]] models aggregate versioning with a touch
   function; trading follows the relational reading. If a
   whole-composite version becomes a business need, it is a query or
   a reporting snapshot, decided when the need appears.
4. Trade lifecycle and the successor chain. =trade= keeps one row per
   lifecycle event, with =status_id= into the =dq= FSM states,
   =successor_trade_id= a self foreign key that chains an amendment
   or exercise to its successor, and =activity_type_code= into the
   activity types. The current trade is the open row; history is the
   closed rows. The tree SQL's hand-maintained insert trigger
   (soft-FK validations, party derivation from the book), its ten
   index statements and its delete rule are declared in the org and
   generated: foreign keys as keys, access paths as index keys,
   validations as org rules.
5. The swap_legs question. The nine rates families write one shared
   legs table today. The relational default is one legs entity per
   family with a real foreign key to its instrument; PR 5 settles
   whether the shared table survives as a discriminator-carrying
   entity with per-family validation keys, and the fra accessor is
   replaced by per-family generated legs access.

*** The wire: projections, and parts or container messages

The wire stays a projection of atomised rows (Regime 3): flat value
messages per entity, per-family or consolidated per the families'
choice:

- Per-family: the 16 FX/equity families keep their per-family
  protocol/handler/registrar, generated as today.
- Consolidated: the fourteen families that ship
  =instrument_protocol.hpp= and =rates_instrument_handler.hpp= select
  the consolidated projection; those two files become generated.
- Trade and lookup messaging stays per-entity, generated from the
  trade and lookup orgs, with the registrar and event files committed
  from the first regeneration — no never-tracked files.

Saving or reading a multi-entity shape — a trade with its identifiers
and party roles, a composite with its legs — travels one of two ways,
chosen per operation:

- Parts messages. The client sends one message per constituent row
  and the service applies them in one transaction. This path needs no
  message type beyond the entity projections.
- Container messages. The client sends one message that contains the
  parts, and the service splits it into rows. The container is a
  boundary type: it is not an entity, it is not persisted, and it
  models no ownership or composition. It composes the generated value
  types of the parts.

Containers are handcrafted first. rfl cannot be assumed to express an
arbitrary container of variant-bearing parts, so the first container
for an operation is written by hand over the generated rfl types of
its parts; when the grammar can declare it, codegen emits it. Either
way, the parts inside a container are the generated rfl types: one
implementation of a part, two ways to carry it.

The acceptance test: regenerate trading and diff; the wire surface
must be identical to the tree's by construction, because the tree's
organisation is now the model's organisation.

*** Service-layer shape

The generated service surface (list, count, get, at-version, batch,
save, delete, history) already is the batch shape the atomised model
needs; it extends to the new families. Beside it, the orgs declare
the multi-table operations (gap (c) closes):

- Legs batch access per family: =get_<family>_legs= and the batch
  variant, generated from the legs entity keys. The shared fra
  accessor disappears with the shared-table decision (PR 5).
- Transactional saves across tables: save a trade or a composite from
  parts messages or from one container message; the service writes
  the rows of all tables in one transaction. The method is declared
  in the org; the rows stay normalised.
- Temporal as-of reads: an as-of read joins the rows of a family
  across its versioned tables and returns the assembled document.
  The join is a read; nothing stores the assembly.
- History reads that join parent and children are generated per table
  and joined at the service, not hand-composed chains.

*** Lookups and orphans

- The four lookups stay lookup entities under their profiles.
- =product_type= becomes a real org (it is the type of the
  =trade.product_type= column and of a =trade_type= member) or is
  folded into the trade_type lookup cleanly; =activity_type= and
  =fpml_event_type= are either given orgs and a stated purpose or
  deleted. Phantom stacks with no org and no wire surface have no
  place in the target.
- =ores.trading.module.org= and the knowledge index are refreshed in
  the same pass (F12).

** Out of scope and open questions

- The numerical-kernel regimes of marketdata, analytics and compute
  are out of scope; trading only consumes their results.
- Qt presentation features are recorded as drift evidence but not
  re-implemented here; the openEdit, import and change-reason-cache
  features keep working through the converted model.
- The =ores.dq= audit group and =ores.refdata= soft-FK parents (book,
  portfolio) are other components' models; trading only gains the org
  keys needed to express them.
- Resolved: the composite as-of question. Trading does not adopt the
  refdata aggregate versioning of option A of
  [[id:BA159E88-74EB-4401-91CC-1DE7DF5AE50A][BA159E88]]; each entity versions on its own spine, and the composite
  as-of read is a temporal join (see Semantics preservation). The
  divergence is recorded so the refdata work is not re-litigated per
  PR.
- Open question: swap legs — one legs entity per rates family with
  real foreign keys (the relational default), or the shared table
  kept as a discriminator-carrying entity. PR 5 settles it on the
  probe.
- Open question: which optional members of the 25 families are
  feature absences (child rows) and which are relationship absences
  (nullable foreign key). PR 4 resolves them family by family against
  the wire and UI uses.
- Open question: parts or container messages per operation, and which
  containers rfl can express. PRs 5-7 settle each operation as its
  container is handcrafted.
- Open question: the fate of =activity_type= and =fpml_event_type=
  (model with purpose, or delete). Decide in PR 9.
