* Summary

A member works in one or more parties. The journey answers two questions on one
screen: where am I acting, and where can I be. The prototype puts the identity,
the party the session is acting for, and the list of the account's parties on
one page, and compares three arrangements of those panels.

It is throwaway. It is not production code, it reads no service, and every value
on it is mock data.

Open the [[file:where-i-work/][prototype]].

* Detail

** The verdict

Accepted on 2026-10-09: variant A, one card per party, with the two actions a
member takes — act for it, or make it the default — on the card they change.
The rejected shapes stay in the prototype, switchable from the bar at the foot
of the page.

** The three variants

| Variant | Shape | What it settles | What it gives up |
|---------+-------+-----------------+------------------|
| A · One place each | A card per party, each carrying "Switch to this party" and "Set as default" | The two actions sit with the place they change, so nothing depends on a selection made elsewhere | It is the tallest of the three when a member works in many parties |
| B · One list, one action bar | A table of parties and a bar of actions under it | Compact for many parties, and one place where an action happens | The member must hold a selection in mind while reading the bar |
| C · Two columns | "You are acting for" and the identity on the left, the list on the right | Best when a member switches often; the acting party stays in view | It repeats the acting party in both columns |

** The acting party is not the default party

The screen separates two things a person can easily run together. The party the
session *acts for* decides what the screens read now, and it changes when the
member switches. The *default* decides what quick sign-in picks next time, and
it changes when the member sets it. The prototype draws both states on every
party, so the difference is visible rather than explained.

** The cases the bar switches

- *Three parties* is the ordinary case: one acting, one default, one other.
- *One party* is the empty state of the journey. The screen says there is
  nothing to switch rather than drawing a disabled control, and it states the
  rule it obeys: an account with no party is refused at sign-in, so the list is
  never empty.

** What the screen cannot do, and says

1. *The parties cannot be named.* The association read answers with =party_id=
   and no read this screen may call turns an id into a name. The names on the
   page are mock.
2. *Set as default has no path.* =iam.v1.ops.set_my_default_party= exists on
   the server and no BFF route, client method or control reaches it.
3. *Clearing the default cannot be stated.* =set_my_default_party_request=
   carries a party id and no "none", so the build draws no *Clear default*.
4. *The switch is wired and no screen offers it.* =POST
   /api/session/switch-party=, =api/client.ts= and =SessionProvider.switchParty=
   exist end to end, and the new-party journey is their only caller.
5. *The association read has no client wrapper.*
   =account_parties.list_by_account_id= is generated and nothing in the BFF or
   the wire client speaks it.

** Document claims the tree contradicts

- The journey document says "nothing in the web client switches party". The call
  is wired from the BFF route through the client and the session provider, and
  the new-party journey calls it. What is missing is the member's screen and the
  account-menu entry point. The correction belongs to the extraction pass, which
  owns the document.
- The journey document names =TopBar.tsx= as the place that leaves party
  switching with the account. No =TopBar.tsx= is in the tree; the shell is
  =AppRoutes.tsx= and the session provider.
