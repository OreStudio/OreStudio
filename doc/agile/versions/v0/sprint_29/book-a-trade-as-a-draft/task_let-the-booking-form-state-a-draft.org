* Acceptance

- The booking form takes a draft reason from the lookup, and books live when none is chosen.
- A draft booking writes the =draft_capture= activity and the reason on the trade.
- The reasons are read through a route of their own, so the form offers what the store holds.

* Plan

Three small pieces. A route that reads the draft reasons, because a form must
offer what the store holds rather than a list written into the screen. A field on
the booking body, and the trade's own column behind it. And the activity: a
booking that states a reason is captured with =draft_capture=, which is the
transition that stops the state machine at draft.

The rule lives where it already lived. The trading service refuses a
=draft_capture= with no reason and refuses a reason on a trade that goes live, so
the form states a reason or nothing, and the service decides.

* Result

A booking states whether the deal it captures is a draft, and why it waits.

| Piece | Where |
|-------|-------|
| The reasons | =GET /api/draft-reasons= reads the lookup: the code, what it waits on, and the desk |
| The body | =draftReasonCode=, absent means the trade goes live at once |
| The activity | =draft_capture= when a reason is stated, =new_booking= otherwise |
| The trade | the reason on the anchor, so a report of aged drafts reads it beside the deal |
| The cases | a draft booking writes the draft activity and the reason; a booking with no reason still writes =new_booking= and none |

The rule is the trading service's, where it already lived: a =draft_capture= with
no reason is refused, and a reason on a trade that goes live is refused.

What this unblocks: the completion executes drafts, and until now no screen could
make one, so the path it exists for — capture two deals as drafts, group them,
and watch each one ask Operations — had no way in.
