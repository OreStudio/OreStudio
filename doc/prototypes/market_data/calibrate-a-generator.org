* Summary

A synthetic feed takes its first value from a ranked public fixing, and the
size of its moves from the spread of that fixing's returns. The prototype
argues what a person sees and edits about that decision: the ranked items, the
item and provider the resolver takes, the value and time taken, the measured
volatility, and the fixed level as a stated fallback. It carries three
variants of one screen on a static page. Vintage is retired and the screen
says so; there is no vintage control to judge.

* The question

The runner already resolves the start: it walks the ranked items, skips an item
whose provider's terms are not accepted or whose fixing is missing or after
the clock, takes the first usable one, reads it as =1/x= when the item quotes
the pair the other way round, and scales the mixture by the measured spread. A
person authoring the generator cannot see any of that. Which of those facts
belongs on the screen, and in what shape, is the question.

* The variants

** A — Resolution first

The start card sits at the top: the level a run will take, the item and
provider behind it, the time received, the inversion, the measured volatility
and the scale. Under it is the ranked table with policy, window and reorder
controls. The resolution trail and the fixed-level card sit beside it. This
variant argues that the resolved start is the answer a person came for, and the
items are its working.

** B — Ladder

One card per item in rank order, each with its own state and reason: chosen,
terms not accepted, no fixing, after the clock, or a higher rank took it. The
resolution trail is under the ladder and the start card is beside it. This
variant argues that a person reads the *why* of an unused item locally, beside
the item, not in a separate trail.

** C — Flow

The items as chips, an arrow into the resolver, an arrow into the process and
feed. An inspector edits the selected item, and the last runs say which item
each start took. This variant argues that the start is a pipeline whose parts
are inspected one at a time, and that the run's own record is the evidence.

Switch with the bottom bar, or with =?variant= (=a=, =b= or =c=; the default is
=a=).

* What it settles

- **The fixed level is a fallback card, never a source.** Every variant states
  when it is used, and why no item applies. No variant offers a second kind of
  source beside the fixings.
- **The resolver's trail is first-class.** A skipped item carries its reason
  where a person edits it. A run that fell through to the fixed level can say
  which item failed which test.
- **The inversion is stated where the value is.** The value is read as =1/x=
  and the screen says so; the source value is not changed, and its credit stays
  with the value.
- **The volatility is a measured number, not a hidden parameter.** The screen
  shows the daily spread, the hourly it implies, the window it was measured over
  and the factor applied to the configured mixture.
- **Vintage is absent.** The screen has no vintage control, field or wording
  that offers one. A fixed level is the only alternative, and it is a fallback.
- **The provider's credit travels with the value**, because the ECB's terms ask
  for citation and a computed cross is itself a change to the source.

* What it does not settle

- Where the calibration lives in the product: a tab of the feed record, a step
  of the feed editor, or a screen of its own. The prototype draws it as a
  screen beside the generator; [[id:2159FD0C-714C-45CF-BC8A-9ABFB98D3254][Build the calibration screen]] puts it on the
  feed page.
- Whether the volatility window is per item or per generator. The prototype
  keeps it per item, because the model does.
- The exact scale formula beyond the daily log returns over the window and the
  hourly conversion shown. The build reads the runner's own formula.

* The mock data

One synthetic EUR/USD generator, five ranked items, and a market clock of
2026-10-31: the ECB EUR/USD item with a fixing on 30 October, an IMF item whose
fixing is a week old, an ECB USD/EUR item that must be read as =1/x=, a Bank of
Canada item whose terms are not accepted, and a TCMB item with no fixing. Two
catalogue items wait behind *Add from the catalogue*. The resolver mirrors the
runner's order and tests; the volatility comes from a deterministic return
series, so the same edits always give the same numbers. The state panel prints
the whole state after every action.
