rises, an FX feed's mixture moves weight toward its wide components, and the shape

* Acceptance

- The weights move with =A= by a stated rule, renormalised to sum to one.
- The reweighting is variance-preserving: the stdevs are rescaled after the
  weights move, so the loading remains the only channel to sigma and the
  calibration test of the previous task still holds.
- The mean of the series is unchanged.
- The kurtosis of returns rises with activity; a test states the window and the
  sampling interval it measures.
- A feed with the tail channel off behaves exactly as it does today.

* Plan

- Deviation from the acceptance: the weights do not move. A variance-preserving
  move of weight toward the wide components can lower the kurtosis (0.8/0.2 on
  stdevs one to four: 9.75 falls to about 4.4), so the rule spreads the stdevs
  instead: =stdev ^ (1 + tilt)=, rescaled to the variance, with
  =tilt = tail_loading * ln pace=. The kurtosis then rises with activity for any
  mixture whose stdevs differ, and the weights and the mean hold.
- The switch is one market-wide =tail_loading=, default zero, set with the
  market's other numbers. A per-feed switch would need a column on the
  generation configs; it waits until a feed needs a different loading.
- The scheduler passes the tilt on the tick context, and the FX feed sets it on
  the process before each step. Both mixture processes take it.

1. The reweighting function on the mixture the FX feeds carry, with its
   renormalisation and the variance-preserving rescale.
2. The test: the same seed under calm and under a pinned active market, kurtosis
   and mean compared.
3. A switch per feed, so the channel can be turned off while it is being proved.

* Result

An FX feed's mixture now leans toward its tails as the market gets busier. At
each tick the tilt is =tail_loading * ln pace=. Each stdev becomes
=stdev ^ (1 + tilt)=, rescaled so the variance holds. The weights and the means
do not move, so the mean holds too. =tail_loading= is a market-wide number. It
defaults to zero, and the system tenant sets it beside the market's other
numbers (shell, BFF and the tab).

This deviates from the acceptance, which asked the weights to move. A
variance-preserving move of weight can lower the kurtosis: weights of 0.8/0.2 on
stdevs in the ratio one to four fall from 9.75 to about 4.4. Spreading the
stdevs raises the kurtosis for any mixture whose stdevs differ. The market
volatility channel note records the reason.

Evidence:
- The analytic test over tilts from -1 to 2 checks that the weights and means
  are fixed, the mean and variance hold to 1e-12, and the kurtosis rises
  strictly.
- The sampled test takes 200,000 one-tick log-returns from one seed, untilted
  and at 2 ln 2 (a pinned active market at loading 2). The kurtosis rises by
  more than 2 and matches the analytic value within 10%. The variance holds
  within 3%, and the mean holds within 4 standard errors.
- A process that is never tilted, or is tilted by zero, draws the same path
  bit for bit.
- The scheduler passes 2 ln 2 at a pinned active pace, and zero once the
  loading is set back to zero. A negative loading is refused.
- Live: =tail_loading=2= was set and the market pinned active while the feeds
  ticked. The loading was then restored, and =-1= was refused naming the
  number. The harness spec passes 9 of 9.

A per-feed switch is not built. It needs a column on the generation configs,
and it waits until a feed needs a loading of its own.
