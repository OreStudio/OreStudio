* Acceptance

- A surface is calibrated from its quotes, checked for arbitrage and written as a candidate with lineage.
- A second person publishes a candidate, and the published surface and its slices can be read and seen on a screen.
- A report binds to a published surface, and ORE prices an FX option from the exported grid.
- A synthetic generator publishes contributor quotes that a blueprint can calibrate from.
- The synthetic feeds start once from their calibrated levels, take a new calibration at the market day roll, and recalibrate on a schedule.

* Decisions

- *A latent volatility surface implies a latent yield curve, and the run shares the one it already has* (2026-11-13). Context: a surface's quotes are volatilities at delta labels, which become strikes only against the forward of their expiry; the feed quotes against a unit market, so the calibration reads its quotes back against a different forward than the one they were solved in. The system tenant is the vendor and publishes raw quotes only, so a curve cannot be published for it to read. Decision: the surface is struck against the forward of the run's own latent curves -- each IR curve feed already steps a short-rate model whose discount factors are analytic at any horizon and prices its pillars off that state -- read from a market board the run holds, with no curve published. Rejected: a party's published forward set, which inverts the dependency; a system-published curve, which contradicts the ownership decision; and re-bootstrapping the vendor's quotes per tick. The design is [[id:675E89FC-B5AA-4250-8B86-78062F0F22BC][The forward a synthetic surface is struck against]], reached by [[id:4825DEE0-D3E4-4E38-B46F-0C5EA838F1E0][the investigation]] and grounded in constraint 3 of [[id:BB2B7C4C-FD4E-4DCA-BF1D-00F929622B87][the approach note]] and in the forward-relative constructions of [[id:84E7CEA8-9654-431C-A8D1-C8B97E35B6D5][Kienitz (2024)]] and [[id:EC220F01-1844-4EED-A61D-BCF9F01EE00C][van den Berg (2026a)]], [[id:3F849D36-022B-49CC-AD71-791481F36581][(2026b)]]. The four choices it settles -- curves only on the board, pillar dates per currency, strikes re-solved every tick, and a round-trip tolerance stated beside the bootstrap -- are recorded there with their alternatives, and the owner accepted them on 2026-11-13, each reversible as that page records. Consequences: the emitter takes its markets per step rather than at construction; a surface feed refuses a pair whose currencies the run does not carry; and a party's surface differs from the vendor's by its own bootstrap, which a test states as a tolerance.

- *The synthetic market correlates through shared Brownian factors* (2026-11-12). Context: every synthetic feed drew its noise alone, so the FX volatility surface could not lean with spot, and OD-1 of the approach note was open. Decision: the owner chose Path F of [[id:8D5466B2-74B5-4C0D-B6DC-528CD466A47F][the joint market investigation]], formalised in the owner's technical report: shared Brownian factors in simulated time, block factors first and global factors in Phase 5, static loadings before regime loadings, and the surface's forward from the published spot and curves. The design is [[id:25C467BE-07A3-432C-8CB8-6B194E748570][Shared Brownian factors give every synthetic series a signed correlation]]; the build is [[id:9331995E-79ED-40B3-923D-524975949FB5][Build the latent market of shared Brownian factors]].
- *The publish lifecycle closes every gap the review of its notes found* (2026-11-11). Context: the review of PR #4606 found contradictions and steps a kind could not meet. Decision: a read by as-of takes the newest published, unwithdrawn set at or before the as-of, the last published at an equal as-of, so a withdrawal falls back to the set it had superseded; a live or a superseded set may be withdrawn, and a binding still reads its set by publish record; provisioning holds an expiring grant per run for the kinds it raises and never changes the tenant's mode, and withdrawals are never granted; a request a rule closes is applied in the raise's transaction, and one a person approved lapses after the kind's apply window; a candidate with a failed blocking check cannot be raised, and the apply refuses a candidate whose inputs were withdrawn; candidates are versioned, an unchanged compute converges on its version, and reads by as-of never return one; an equal as-of is a correction; the notice is an outbox row. Consequences: the notes and the task state these; they await the owner's confirmation before the PR merges.
- *Every derived market object publishes through one lifecycle* (2026-11-11). Context: curves, forward sets and surfaces each made a candidate authoritative in a different way, and a surface publish waited although the tenant approved without a person. Decision: the owner signed off the recommendations of [[id:44E630B8-3CA4-42C5-BA94-75618F121327][The publish of derived market data]]: one lifecycle for every kind; the candidate is stored before the raise; the trader applies a publish and a withdrawal; an older candidate than the live set is refused; a context bound to a transaction refuses a change of tenant or party; auto-approval is documented in full; a failed provisioning turns auto-approval off. The target is [[id:B74CEF18-B533-45A1-B65E-F7DF836988D6][Publishing derived market data]]. Consequences: [[id:A171C0C0-634A-4ADF-8772-6EBBBDF94166][Publish every derived market object through one lifecycle]] brings the system to it.
- *Provisioning leaves each party ready to price from its own surfaces* (2026-11-09). The owner chose: every operational party has funding curves in all 20 currencies, forward curves for USD pairs and for the crosses with a convention, and calibrated surfaces. A cross spot derives from its two USD legs. The published surfaces use each party's forward sets, so Calibrate returns to the backlog until those exist.
- *The system owns raw market data, and parties own what derives from it* (2026-11-09). The owner's model: the system tenant plays the vendor, as Bloomberg would, and subscriptions and derived data such as curves and forwards are party concerns. A funding curve is therefore bootstrapped per party.
- *Provisioning runs under a visible auto-approval* (2026-11-09). A recorded per-tenant switch closes approvals during provisioning. Afterwards regular users publish through maker-checker, unless the profile keeps the switch on for a demonstration. The switch must be obvious to anyone troubleshooting.
- *IR feeds are calibrated from public rates before provisioning publishes forwards* (2026-11-09). Context: the FX spot feeds start from the public Frankfurter fixings and the FX variance is fitted to their history, but the IR feeds start from fixed template levels. The system stores no public IR rate. Forwards built from those curves carry rate differentials with no tie to the market. Decision: the owner chose to finish IR calibration before the forwards are complete. The forward records, their reads and their screen can be built first. [[id:30A638A1-D5EF-4DDF-BAB9-4121CDC1B488][Publish FX forward curves in provisioning]] waits on [[id:C0BA69C1-6E68-4AD2-896C-0F6283BD3BAF][Read the public overnight rates through an adapter]] and [[id:619CE2BF-C972-47CF-8900-AC2742AC2CD9][Calibrate the synthetic IR feeds from the public rates]], in the story Build the IR curves from public data. Consequences: another environment takes the two IR tasks in parallel with the FX work here, and the forwards step in provisioning starts once both are merged.
- *A cross settles on its two calendars, and its spot comes from the USD legs* (2026-11-10). Context: the acceptance asked for the convention on a USD holiday. Decision: follow ORE, whose cross conventions advance on the two currencies' calendars only, so a USD holiday does not move a cross's spot date; QuantLib confirms the dates. The spot is the ratio of the party's two USD legs, each read in the market's direction. Consequences: a cross forward equals the ratio of its USD-leg forwards on the same dates.
- *Raw quotes are delivered into each party's own series* (2026-11-09). Context: the system tenant plays the vendor, and two readings were possible: one shared copy that parties read, or delivery through a subscription into each party's own series. Decision: the owner chose delivery, which the sprint 29 routes already do, with metering per party. London and New York keep their own vendors and also take Bloomberg, because only Bloomberg quotes the whole funding set. Consequences: no reorder of the tasks is needed. The feeds already run, so the release task is not a prerequisite of the party curves.
- *The EUR/USD blueprint asks for the expiries its source quotes* (2026-11-11). Context: the first live calibration was refused, because no 2Y quote existed. The derived source lists 2Y but quotes it only from two years of fixings, which the seeded history does not reach. Two fixes were possible: seed the expiries the source quotes today, or let a calibration drop an expiry that has no quotes. Decision: seed 1W to 1Y. A calibration that dropped an expiry would change the published grid from one day to the next. Consequences: EURUSD_SSVI has 35 pillars and a 1W to 1Y grid. 2Y returns with [[id:1226D830-9285-4C45-9F5C-72F72AFC7352][Seed the public fixings history from a DQ snapshot]].
- *An end-of-day quote is aged by its source* (2026-11-10). Context: no volatility quote of the derived source ever reached a party. The ingest dropped every quote older than 5 seconds, and a derived quote is an end-of-day value stamped at its day's close. Two fixes were possible: age a quote by the service of its source, or deliver derived quotes as dated observations, as fixings are. Decision: the owner chose the first. A quote of an =end_of_day= source stays current for 5 days, which covers Easter: a Thursday close is still current on the Tuesday morning, and every other source keeps 5 seconds. Consequences: volatility quotes take the same subscription path as IR quotes and spots, and no second delivery path exists.
- *Market vols show on the synthetic screen, and surfaces on the Evolution page* (2026-11-10). Context: no screen showed any volatility. The Evolution page has a surface panel, but it needs a series that declares two axes, and no vol series does. Decision: the owner placed the raw quotes, which the system publishes as a vendor would, on the synthetic screen, and a party's published surface on the Evolution page. Consequences: a new task shows the market vols. The calibrate task declares the output series' axes, and the screens task shows the surface on the Evolution page. The order of the work does not change.
- *FX forward curves are published records* (2026-11-09). The owner reversed computing forwards on read. A forward set is written to the database with its provenance and passes through maker-checker, as an IR curve does. Provisioning publishes them in a separate step after the funding curves.

- *The FX forward ladder journey and prototype are signed off together* (2026-11-09). The owner chose one sign off for both. The screen starts from USD pairs, its ladder start moves near dates only, and its chart spaces tenors evenly.
- Split from [[id:3D2A2BD4-4126-4E54-9046-A9E00966D898][Support volatility surfaces]] on 2026-11-08 at sprint 29 close. The acceptance above is copied from that story; its done tasks are recorded there.
- Reviewed on 2026-11-08 at sprint 30 start. Goal rewritten, because the architecture it named is done. The tasks are two arcs: the surface and the calibration of the synthetic feeds.
- *The tasks run in dependency order, and the surface path reaches pricing* (2026-11-09). Context: three surface tasks had no goal or acceptance, and the construction note's path from a calibrated surface to a report had no tasks for the publish, the ORE export or the screens. No blueprint was seeded. Decision: the task table is in working order, the surface arc first. The arc adds the engine, split out of the calibrate task so unit tests can prove it alone, a seeded blueprint for EUR/USD, the publish, the ORE export and the screens. Consequences: the story closes only when pricing reads a surface; the first surface calibrates from the derived quotes, so the generator can follow it.
- *The calibration fits gamma, as QuantExt does* (2026-11-09). Context: the decision of 2026-11-03 set SSVI with gamma = 1/2 for the synthetic surface. QuantExt calibrates a power-law SSVI with gamma in (0, 1), and its bounds on real-shaped smiles are the ground truth this engine ports (=QuantExt/test/sviparametricvolatility.cpp= and =QuantExt/test/bfrrvolsurface.cpp= in ORE 1.8.17.0). [[https://riskhub.org/blogs/ssvi-and-surface-wide-parameterization][SSVI and Surface-Wide Parameterization]] (riskhub.org) found a power-law gamma of 0.28 on a Heston surface. Decision: the calibration engine fits rho, eta and gamma, with phi(theta) = eta * theta^(-gamma). Consequences: the no-arbitrage conditions are checked expiry by expiry, because the curvature condition depends on theta once gamma differs from 1/2. The generator's own choice of gamma is taken up in its task.
