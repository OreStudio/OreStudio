A point-in-time record, written while the story that commissioned it was
open, of an [[id:A7F8D9E2-3C4B-4A5D-B67C-8E9F0A1B2C3D][investigation]] into what a realistic trade population for ACME
requires. It covers whether bond support is complete, what population
machinery exists, how DQ and synthetic generation compare, what the
vendored ORE engine offers, and what the ACME tenant holds today.

It is a snapshot, not a description of the system. Whatever it concludes
that the code must keep doing belongs on the page that owns the subject.
Cite that page from code, recipes and models, not this one.

* Context

The ACME tenant holds =0= trades. The report package built for it is
structurally valid and substantively empty, because the trading write path
has never been exercised by anything but integration tests. A report over
an empty population proves nothing about the report.

The story must populate the ACME books so that the reports have something
to compute. The population has to satisfy four properties at once, and
they are what made this an investigation rather than a task:

- the dates must sit relative to the present, so a population provisioned
  today is still plausible when it is re-provisioned next month;
- the references must be real, so every trade names a party, a book, a
  netting set and a counterparty the tenant actually holds;
- a second provision must not duplicate a trade and must not move its
  version, because the system is bitemporal and every spurious version is
  permanent history;
- the population must price, which means it has to reach the vendored ORE
  engine through the export that already exists.

The question the story asked was which mechanism produces such a
population, and whether the machinery to produce it already exists.

* Paths taken

Seven paths were explored. Two were design alternatives for date
relativity and are recorded with their verdicts. Two were audits that
supply the generator's inputs. Two were reuse ideas, the DQ staging table
and the ORE sample corpus, and neither survives its own details. One was
a salvage of the DQ idea that stages the parameters rather than the
trades.

** The coverage audit of the bond family

The story starts with bonds, so the first path was to establish whether
bond support is complete and whether it follows the data-oriented design.
"What complete" means here is stated by
=doc/knowledge/architecture/data_oriented_design.org=, which classifies
=ores.trading= as Regime 2 (persisted domain data) plus Regime 3 (wire)
at lines 14-16 and 87-99. Regime 1 does not apply. Completeness for a
family is therefore three things together:

- the Regime 2 decision surface is fully modelled, so nothing lives only
  in the tree. Lines 212-227 name the artefacts a review reads.
- the entity's facts split by change reason, multiplicity or reader under
  the composite pattern, with the parent version bump. That is
  =codegen_patterns.org= lines 60-73.
- the wire is a flat projection of those rows, with no nested objects and
  no polymorphic payload fields. Lines 280-286.

A passing build is not the test, and neither is coverage alone. The
criteria page states the distinction at lines 244-258. The decision
surface can fail, and codegen does not rescue it.

The audit found the family broad and codegen-bound. =ls
projects/ores.trading/modeling/ores.trading.bond*.org= returns 42 orgs.
Nineteen are =ores.codegen.entity= and 23 are =ores.codegen.field_group=.
The nineteen entities are =bond_instrument=, =bond_issue=,
=bond_issue_call_date=, =bond_issue_conversion_target=, =bond_issue_leg=
and its five children =bond_issue_leg_amount=, =bond_issue_leg_rate=,
=bond_issue_leg_amortization=, =bond_issue_leg_schedule= and
=bond_issue_leg_schedule_date=, =bond_leg= and its three children
=bond_leg_amount=, =bond_leg_rate= and =bond_leg_amortization=,
=bond_forward=, =bond_future=, =bond_option=, =bond_repo= and =bond_trs=.
The separate =ores.trading.ascot.org= carries the eleventh code.

Coverage is declared once, in
=projects/ores.trading/modeling/ores.trading.trade_type_catalogue.org=.
Parsing its =* Trade types= table at lines 47-172 gives 121 trade types.
Thirty have an empty =instrument= cell and are therefore routable
nowhere. The bond family is nearly whole. Ten of the eleven bond-typed
codes route to =bond_instrument=:

#+begin_src
Bond ForwardBond BondFuture BondOption BondRepo
BondTRS BondPosition CallableBond ConvertibleBond Ascot
#+end_src

The eleventh, =BondFutureOption=, is unrouted. The catalogue states the
consequence at lines 24-27. An empty =instrument= means nothing imports
it, so nothing can export it. The routed column check in the generated
schema agrees.
=grep '^    check ("trade_type_code" in' projects/ores.sql/create/trading/trading_bond_instruments_create.sql=
returns exactly the ten codes.

The export carries the whole family through one carrier.
=bond_instrument_data=, modelled at
=ores.trading.bond_instrument_data_field_group.org=, holds 28 members.
They are the header, the issue, the issue's call dates and conversion
targets, the product's fact row, the bond's legs, and the document
residue the tables cannot hold. The catalogue's =** Instrument types=
table at lines 201-209 is the one row that names it, and the generator
emits the container from the model rather than by hand.

The composite pattern is half-built, and that is the central finding of
this path. The pattern requires a child foreign key declaring
=:bump_parent_version:=, =:list_by:=, =:list_by_uuid:= and
=:list_by_as_of:= (=codegen_patterns.org= lines 60-73). It states what
the bump buys at lines 67-73. A write to any part bumps the parent's
version in the same transaction, so the parent's windows partition time
into spans in which the parts are stable, and a read assembles the entity
from the timeline with no link table.

Measured on the bond orgs:

#+begin_src
cd projects/ores.trading/modeling
grep -c '^:bump_parent_version:' ores.trading.bond_leg.org          -> 0
grep -c '^:bump_parent_version:' ores.trading.bond_instrument.org   -> 0
grep -c '^:list_by: *true'       ores.trading.bond_leg.org          -> 1
grep -rl '^:bump_parent_version:' *.org -> structure_member, trade_additional_field,
    trade_booking, trade_identifier, trade_party_role, trade_portfolio, trade_state
grep -rl '^:list_by_uuid:' *.org -> (none)
grep -rl '^:list_by_as_of:' *.org -> (none)
#+end_src

The bond children have the list-by-parent read and none of the bump. The
two as-of flags exist nowhere in the component. The trade anchor's own
parts do declare the bump, which shows the pattern is expressible and was
applied to one family and not the other. The consequence is concrete.
=bond_leg=, =bond_leg_amount=, =bond_leg_rate= and =bond_leg_amortization=
can each be rewritten while =bond_instrument.version= stands still. An
as-of read of the family therefore cannot say which legs were current
when.

Three smaller gaps follow from the same audit.

- *The declared foreign keys are not database foreign keys.* Both
  =bond_instrument.org= at lines 145-210 and the generated create script
  describe the trade, activity and issue keys.
  =./compass.sh db sql -- -At -c "SELECT count(*) FROM pg_constraint WHERE contype='f';"=
  returns 25, and every one is a TimescaleDB catalogue constraint. The
  bond keys are trigger checks instead. That is correct for a temporal
  parent and wrong for the immutable lookups the same models describe as
  database foreign keys.
- *=bond_instrument.org= line 36 records its own coverage limit.* It says
  the real foreign key to =ores_trading_trade_types_tbl= is PR 4's defect
  7. It has not landed.
- *The issue sits outside the economic digest.*
  =trade_type_catalogue.org= =** Instrument components= at lines 269-318
  lists =bond_instrument= as its own only component, and =bond_issue=
  appears in no digest table. The issue row holds the bond's terms. An
  amendment to those terms therefore moves the trades' economics without
  moving their =economic_digest= or their =external_version=
  (=trade.org= lines 153-195). Whether that is intended is a decision
  rather than a defect, and nothing in the models states it.

*** What the audit found conforming, and what it did not

The evidence separates cleanly. It compiles and it is largely modelled,
and it does not reach the target shape.

Conforming:

- The normalisation failure of September 2025 is gone for bonds. The
  terms moved off the wide instrument row onto =bond_issue=, which
  =bond_instrument.org= lines 17-29 describes as the change-reason split,
  and each optional feature is a keyed child row rather than a nullable
  column. That is the relational rule of =data_oriented_design.org= lines
  109-116.
- Money is exact. =notional= and =face_value= are =numeric(38, 12)= in
  SQL and =ores::utility::decimal::decimal= in C++, and the schema checks
  =face_value > 0=. That matches the exact-money-at-rest check at
  =data_oriented_design.org= line 269.
- The wire carries typed arrays rather than one tagged variant.
  =grep -rn AddTagsToVariants projects/ --include=*.hpp --include=*.cpp=
  returns nothing, and =instrument_batch.hpp= is one array per entity with
  the element type named by the catalogue. That is the no-polymorphic-
  payload-fields rule, and it retires the October defect recorded in
  =doc/agile/product_backlog/inbox/msvc_c1202_bond_export_carrier.org=.

Not conforming:

- *The nested aggregate is still the C++ shape.* =bond_instrument.org=
  lines 340-348 bind =domain_identity_group= and =domain_audit_group=, so
  the domain class holds =instrument_identity identity;= and
  =audit_record audit;=. =data_oriented_design.org= lines 69-90 rules that
  out and calls it a known codegen defect rather than a licence. Every
  bond entity carries it.
- *=bond_instrument_data= is a 28-member container on the wire.*
  =codegen_patterns.org= lines 110-117 permit a container at the
  boundary, and the ORE XML import is exactly that boundary, so this is
  defensible. It is still an aggregate type whose members are other
  reflected aggregates, and it is carried inside the =trade_instrument=
  variant at =trade_instrument.hpp= lines 43-52. Nothing in the models
  states which of the two readings applies.
- *No bond entity beyond =bond_instrument= declares =has_batch_read=.*
  =grep -c has_batch_read ores.trading.bond_leg.org= returns 0 against
  =bond_instrument.org= line 355. The batch read the export needs exists
  as =read_legs_by_trade_ids= in =ores.trading.core/src/service/=, which
  is the custom-repository-method escape hatch rather than a modelled
  read.
- *Party isolation is absent.* =codegen_patterns.org= line 89 names
  =:rls_tenant_isolation:= and =:rls_party_isolation:= for every entity.
  =grep -rl '^:rls_tenant_isolation:' projects/ores.trading/modeling/*.org=
  returns 77 files and =grep -rl '^:rls_party_isolation:'= returns 1
  (=ores.trading.structure.org=), against 111 entity orgs. In the live
  database
  =./compass.sh db sql -- -At -c "SELECT count(*) FROM pg_policies WHERE policyname LIKE '%party_isolation%';"=
  returns 156, so the mechanism is real and trading mostly does not use
  it.

** The DQ staging path, as it stands

The second path was to stage the population as a DQ artefact and publish
it. It fails on three grounds, each of which survives a redesign.

=doc/knowledge/architecture/dq_artefacts.org= states the purpose in its
first sentence at lines 14-16. An artefact is a staging table that holds
an entity's rows by dataset until a publication writes them into the
service that owns the entity. Line 210 makes the ownership explicit. It
is the owning component's data in transit. Its guarantees are the
publication contract at lines 99-130, which are idempotence, three
accepted modes (=upsert=, =insert_only=, =replace_all=), a return of one
row per action taken, =clock_timestamp()= for row closure, and refusal of
any target but the system tenant for installation data.

A dataset is registered in =ores_dq_datasets_tbl=, whose columns are =id,
tenant_id, version, code, catalog_name, subject_area_name, domain_name,
..., as_of_date, ingestion_timestamp, ..., artefact_type, valid_from,
valid_to=. The model of time is stated at
=doc/knowledge/domain/data_quality.org= lines 70-71, which names business
time (=as_of_date=) and system time (=ingestion_timestamp=) and calls the
pair bi-temporal reasoning.

A vintage is therefore the pair of business date and ingestion instant
that a dataset row carries. What it is not is a date inside the staged
rows. Nothing in the artefact pipeline rewrites a staged value relative
to =as_of_date=. Publishing is a copy, optionally preceded by a close. A
vintage says when the data was true of the world, not how to make it true
of today.

Live confirmation.
=SELECT as_of_date, count(*) FROM ores_dq_datasets_tbl WHERE code LIKE 'acme%' GROUP BY 1=
returns one row, =2026-10-15 00:00:00+01|72=. All 72 ACME dataset rows
carry the provisioning date and nothing else. Their codes are such as
=acme.acme_group.books= and =acme.acme_hk.portfolios=, from
=SELECT d.code, d.as_of_date, d.version FROM ores_dq_datasets_tbl d=. A
re-provision at a later date either leaves the old vintage or mints a new
one. It never moves a trade's dates.

Why the path fails for trades whose dates sit relative to today:

1. *There is nothing to stage.* No trade artefact table exists, and
   =dq_artefacts.org= lines 44-47 require the owning model to opt in.
   Building one means generating a staging table for every instrument
   family the population touches, plus a publish function per family
   against the contract at lines 99-130.
2. *A vintage is the wrong axis.* The requirement is that the population
   sits relative to today. A dataset vintage is a fixed business date. To
   make the dates relative you must either regenerate the staged rows at
   each provision, which is generation wearing DQ's clothes, or shift them
   after publication, which is mechanism B and carries its costs below.
3. *The publish function cannot be the place the arithmetic lives.* It is
   a data movement function with an idempotence contract. Putting date
   arithmetic in it makes idempotence depend on the arithmetic, which the
   contract does not contemplate.

Measured on the tenant, the DQ path also stages nothing relevant.

- *DQ carries no trade artefact table.*
  =./compass.sh db sql -- -At -c "SELECT table_name FROM information_schema.tables WHERE table_schema='public' AND table_name LIKE 'ores_dq%';"=
  lists 156 tables and not one of them stages a trade, a bond instrument
  or an instrument leg. The nearest thing is
  =./compass.sh db sql -- -At -c "SELECT DISTINCT code FROM ores_dq_datasets_tbl WHERE code ILIKE '%trade%' OR code ILIKE '%bond%' OR code ILIKE '%instrument%';"=,
  which returns one row, =synthetic.feeds.tradeweb=, a market data feed.
- *No seed writes a trade.* =ls projects/ores.sql/populate/trading/= shows
  23 files, all reference data. They are =trading_trade_types_populate.sql=,
  =trading_activity_types_populate.sql=, the type vocabularies and
  =trading_structure_reference_data_populate.sql=.
  =find projects/ores.sql -path '*populate*' -iname '*trade*'= finds only
  those lookups.
- *The ACME seeder dataset stages no trades.*
  =projects/ores.seeder/datasets/acme_corporation/dataset_overview.org=
  lines 33-60 describe the dataset's files, and
  =ls projects/ores.seeder/datasets/acme_corporation/*.json= returns 36 of
  them. They are companies, LEIs, business units, portfolios, books,
  accounts, netting agreements and sets, CSAs, report definitions, report
  configurations, risk report configs and today's market configuration. No
  trade file exists, and no artefact table stages one.
- *No trade set exists.*
  =./compass.sh db sql -- -At -c "SELECT table_name FROM information_schema.tables WHERE table_schema='public' AND table_name LIKE '%trade_set%';"=
  returns nothing, and the task that would create the partition,
  =D054BDD3-351F-42D3-9638-7D3BAEEAD332= "Partition a run's trade
  population into trade sets", is in the backlog.

** Mechanism A, generation relative at an anchor

The third path, and the one recommended. The generator takes an as-of
date as an input, and every date it emits is an offset from that anchor.

The machinery is present in outline.
=ores.utility/generation/generation_context.hpp= constructs from a seed at
line 43 or randomly at line 48, and =generation_engine.hpp= offers
=past_timepoint(int years_back = 3)=, which returns a point relative to
=std::chrono::system_clock::now()= (=generation_engine.cpp= lines 76-80).
What is missing is an anchor parameter rather than a hard read of the
clock, and offset arithmetic per date field.

Date relativity is exactly what this buys, and it is what the tree
already reaches for. It is also the only one of the two mechanisms that
is coherent for a schedule stored as a rule.

The existing generator machinery is real and its output today is test
fixtures. Every model declares =generator_facet_name: generators=, and
=ores.trading.api/src/generators/= holds a =generate_synthetic_<entity>=
per entity. The only consumer of these in the tree is the =parent_seed=
paste block, and
=grep -rln parent_seed_snippet projects/ores.codegen/library/templates/=
returns exactly one template, =cpp_nats_integration_test.cpp.mustache=.
So the machinery seeds an integration test's foreign key chain and nothing
else. =grep -rn "generate_synthetic_trade\b" projects/ --include=*.cpp=
finds no caller outside its own definition.

** Mechanism B, shift the dates after insert

The fourth path. Take a static population from DQ or from the ORE sample
corpus, insert it, then update the dates by a delta. This was tested on
its merits rather than dismissed, and it loses.

*** What a trade's dates are, and which are derived

Almost all of them are stored, in a small number of shapes.
=SELECT table_name||'.'||column_name||' : '||data_type FROM information_schema.columns WHERE table_schema='public' AND (column_name LIKE '%date%') AND table_name LIKE 'ores_trading%'=
returns 97 columns across 50 tables. Three shapes matter.

- *Stored anchors as =date= columns.*
  =ores_trading_trade_bookings_tbl.trade_date=, =bond_issues.issue_date=,
  =rate_instruments.start_date= and =.maturity_date=,
  =equity_*_instruments.expiry_date=, =commodity_instruments.maturity_date=,
  =cds_instruments.protection_start_date=,
  =bond_issue_call_dates.call_date= and
  =instrument_option_payment_dates.payment_date=.
- *Stored schedules as rules, not as dates.*
  =ores_trading_instrument_schedules_tbl= holds =schedule_kind, start_date,
  end_date, tenor, calendar, convention, term_convention, rule,
  end_of_month, first_date, last_date, remove_first_date,
  remove_last_date=. =bond_issue_leg_schedules= holds the same set.
  =bond_schedule_rules_field_group.org= lines 14-18 states the intent, "a
  start date and a tenor, expanded against a calendar under a date rule".
- *Stored schedules as explicit date lists.* The other arm is
  =instrument_schedule_dates.schedule_date= and
  =bond_issue_leg_schedule_dates.schedule_date=, selected by
  =check ("schedule_kind" in ('rules', 'dates'))=.

What is derived is the expansion. The instrument header's =start_date= and
=maturity_date= are copies taken from the schedule at import, not
independent facts.
=projects/ores.ore/core/src/domain/swap_instrument_mapper.cpp= lines
89-113 define =start_date_from_schedule= and =end_date_from_schedule=,
which read =Rules.front().StartDate= and =Rules.front().EndDate=, or the
first and last entry of an explicit list, and lines 1315-1316 assign them
onto the header. The views confirm it.
=projects/ores.sql/create/trading/trading_common_dates_vw_create.sql=
joins =rate_instruments= and =trade_bookings= to present =trade_date,
start_date, expiry_date, maturity_date= as one row.

A delta shift therefore has two cases, and the first is the awkward one.
For a rule-based schedule, shifting =start_date= and leaving =tenor= alone
makes ORE re-expand a coherent schedule from the new anchor, which is
mechanism A with an extra database write. For a date-list schedule, every
entry must be shifted individually, and the header copy with it. Either
way you must also walk the other date-bearing columns. They are
=call_date=, =schedule_date=, =payment_date=, =pay_date=,
=exercise_date=, =value_date=, =protection_start_date=, =upfront_date=,
=averaging_start_date=, =barrier_start_date= and =settlement_fixing_date=.
The derived payment, fixing and accrual dates cannot be shifted at all,
because they are not stored. They are re-derived by the engine from what
you did store.

The corpus advantage therefore does not survive intact. To shift an ORE
sample coherently you must rewrite its schedule rules and its date lists,
at which point the output is a re-anchored sample rather than a verbatim
one. It stays recognisable as the sample only if you keep the tenor and
the rule and move the anchor, which is mechanism A.

*** Whether the constraints survive

The schema enforces little about dates and nothing about calendars.
=grep -h '^    check (' projects/ores.sql/create/trading/*.sql | sort -u=
gives 162 check expressions. The date-relevant ones are:

| Constraint | Table |
|---|---|
| =check ("end_date" > "start_date")= | =fx_variance_swap_instruments= |
| =check ("maturity_date" is null or "start_date" is null or "maturity_date" > "start_date")= | several |
| =check ("maturity_date" is null or "start_date" is null or "maturity_date" >= "start_date")= | several |
| =check ("valid_from" < "valid_to")= | every generated table |

No check requires a payment date to be a business day, and none could.
Calendars are reference data held elsewhere, and ORE applies them at
pricing time. A shift that moves a Saturday payment onto a Sunday passes
every check the database has.

The order checks do survive a uniform delta, because a positive delta
preserves order. They fail the moment you clamp, snap or cap any single
date, which is exactly what a plausible-looking population invites.

*** What a post-insert update does to the change history

The system is bitemporal and the update is visible. The generated insert
trigger, for example
=projects/ores.sql/create/trading/trading_bond_issues_create.sql= lines
100-160, selects the current row =for update=, sets =NEW.version =
current_version + 1=, closes the old row with =valid_to =
clock_timestamp()=, and inserts the new version with =valid_to =
ores_utility_infinity_timestamp_fn()=. The comment at line 155 gives the
reason =clock_timestamp()= is used rather than =current_timestamp()=.

Three consequences for mechanism B.

- *The population reads as created, then corrected.* Every shifted row
  carries two versions instead of one, and the first version's window is
  however long the seed and the shift were apart.
- *The correction is visible to a reviewer.*
  =doc/knowledge/architecture/history_diff_architecture.org= lines 14-30
  describe one generic history request served by codegen'd per-entity
  handlers, with =ores.diff= computing field and intra-value spans, and a
  surviving browser renderer at
  =projects/ores.shell/api/src/app/commands/history_diff_renderer.cpp=.
  The shift is not hidden. It is the first thing the history shows.
- *An as-of read sees the pre-shift dates.* The versions sub-resource and
  the as-of reads exist precisely to answer what this looked like on date
  D, and the trade set carries an as-of. The task
  =8BC0E84C-9B4B-4A12-8E3F-E86EF2A8A807= "Export a trade set as an
  archive" says at its =* Goal=, "The as-of travels on the set". A
  population whose dates were written wrong and then corrected answers the
  wrong thing for every date before the correction.

A further cost is semantic rather than historical. The trade anchor's
=external_version= "moves when the economics the trade holds change, and
never for an internal reason" (=ores.trading.trade.org= lines 162-169),
and =economic_digest= is stored rather than recomputed. Schedule dates are
declared economic.
=grep -A 6 '^\*\* start_date' projects/ores.trading/modeling/ores.trading.instrument_schedule.org=
shows =:economic: true= on the rule's start date. So a shift moves the
economic digest of every trade it touches, and
=doc/knowledge/domain/trade_versioning.org= lines 27-28 gives the rule
that follows, "a trade changes version only if the agreement with the
customer has changed". A date of performance is a term of the agreement,
so the external version should move with it, and every trade then reads
as amended by agreement with the customer. It was not. By contrast
=trade_booking.trade_date= at lines 125-131 is =filterable= and not
economic, so the agreement date is exempt while the performance dates are
not.

*** Whether it is idempotent

Nothing in the mechanism is idempotent on its own. A relative shift by
=+N= days applied twice gives =+2N=. Idempotence needs an absolute
target, which means reading the current anchor, comparing it to the
as-of, and writing only when they differ. That is a convergence loop
rather than a shift, and it is the shape
=principle-make-operations-idempotent= asks for.

There is a mechanism that would make a double-run detectable. The audit
columns carry =change_reason_code=, validated against
=ores_dq_change_reasons_tbl=, and that table already holds
=system.admin_reset= "Data reset by system administrator for
re-provisioning" and =system.initial_load=, from
=SELECT DISTINCT code FROM ores_dq_change_reasons_tbl WHERE code LIKE 'system%';=.
A shift could claim =system.admin_reset= and a later run could refuse to
shift a row already carrying it. That is a marker rather than a
guarantee, and it costs one more write per row.

*** The honest version of B's one advantage

The advantage is real but narrow. A source corpus stays recognisable, so
the population would be the ORE samples' trades rather than lookalikes.
It survives for exactly one case, the rule-based schedule shifted by
moving the anchor and keeping the tenor, which produces the same dates
the generator would have produced. It does not survive the date-list
schedules, where a shift is a rewrite, and it does not survive the
calendar-free check surface, where nothing tells you the shifted dates
are business days.

** DQ for the parameters, not the trades

The fifth path, and the one salvageable version of the DQ idea. Stage the
parameters a generator reads, not the trades.

An artefact table holding the population shape (which books, which
counterparties, which products, which tenors, which as-of) publishes
through the ordinary pipeline and gives DQ's idempotence and vintage
story for free. The generator then runs at population time and the dates
are relative because the generator is the thing that emits them. The
artefact is small, diffable and stable, and the dates are never staged.

Called that way, DQ is a good fit for the inputs and a poor fit for the
trades.

** The mechanisms side by side

| Property | DQ, as it stands | DQ, parameters only | Mechanism A, relative at generation | Mechanism B, shift after insert |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Stability across re-provisioning | Dataset vintage is stable; rows never move | Stable, because the parameters are | Exact for a fixed (seed, as-of); different as-of moves the population deliberately | Stable only if the shift is absolute and guarded |
| Date relativity | None. A vintage is a fixed date | Full, by the generator | Full | Full, but by rewriting stored dates |
| Referential integrity to parties and counterparties | Not addressed by the pipeline | Not addressed by the pipeline | Guaranteed if the generator picks from the tenant's real rows | Guaranteed, because the corpus already references real rows |
| Reviewability of a diff | Good. Generated SQL and JSON diff cleanly | Good. The parameter set is small | Good. Regenerate and diff | Poor. The diff is version 1 against version 2 of every shifted row |
| Business-day correctness | Not applicable | Not applicable | Guaranteed if the generator snaps to a calendar | Not guaranteed by anything in the schema |
| Cost to build | High. A staging table and a publish function per family | Low | Medium. Anchor plus offset arithmetic per field | Medium to high. A per-field date walk across the 50 date-bearing tables |

* Conclusions

Bond support is complete in coverage and incomplete in the composite
pattern. All ten bond trade codes route to a real table, the tables
exist, the export walks them, and the family is codegen-bound. The parent
version bump the composite pattern requires is absent from every bond
child, so the parent's windows do not partition time into spans in which
the parts are stable. The single highest-value bond gap is that bump,
because it is the one that makes an as-of read wrong rather than merely
inelegant.

Trade generation is not a DQ problem and not a formality. DQ carries no
trade artefact table at all, and its versioning is a dataset vintage
rather than a date. Synthetic generation already has the machinery, the
weighting model and a seeded context, and its output today is test
fixtures. The context's determinism is broken in three places in the
tree.

The vendored ORE engine ships a trade generator that cannot be run from
this tree, because the SWIG module it imports is not vendored. It is
useful as a coverage list and not as code.

The conclusion of the comparison is mechanism A, generation relative at
an anchor date, inside =ores.trading=, seeded, with a
regenerate-and-diff review.

** Why mechanism A and why mechanism B lost

Mechanism B was tested rather than dismissed, and it loses on three
grounds, not one.

1. *Its derived dates are already relative, so B does not add what A
   lacks.* =swap_instrument_mapper.cpp= lines 89-113 show the header dates
   are copied from the schedule, and =bond_schedule_rules_field_group.org=
   lines 14-18 show the schedule is a rule. Shifting the anchor and
   keeping the tenor is exactly what A does, with a database write and a
   version row added.
2. *Its constraints do not check what matters.* The 162 check expressions
   in the trading schema constrain order and the spine. None can constrain
   a business day, because calendars are not in the schema. A shifted
   population is unverified where it most needs verifying.
3. *Its history makes the population read as corrected.* The trigger
   writes a second version, the history diff renders it, and the as-of
   read the trade set already carries answers for the pre-shift state.
   Separately, schedule dates are =:economic: true=, so a shift moves the
   =economic_digest=, and by the rule in =trade_versioning.org= lines
   27-28 the =external_version= follows, which reports every trade as
   amended by agreement.

B's one advantage, keeping the ORE corpus recognisable, does not survive
the date-list schedules or the calendar gap. A corpus that has been
partially rewritten is neither generated nor verbatim.

Mechanism A keeps the property the requirement actually asks for. The
population is a function of (seed, as-of), and moving the as-of moves it
coherently.

The user's reasoning is right, and for a narrower reason than stated.
Relative dates are not a synthetic-versus-DQ question. They are an
*anchor* question, and the anchor belongs in the generator's signature.

** What makes a seed deterministic

The seed is necessary and it is not sufficient today. Three things must
hold.

1. *The seed must reach the engine.* =generation_context.hpp= line 43
   constructs from a =uint64_t seed=, so the parameter exists and the
   population must pass it explicitly rather than take the random
   constructor at line 48.
2. *The identifier minting must stop reading the clock.*
   =generation_engine.cpp= lines 42-53 build a UUID v7 whose first 48 bits
   are =std::chrono::system_clock::now()= in milliseconds. Two runs with
   the same seed produce different ids. Either the timestamp is derived
   from the seed and the anchor, or the population uses deterministic keys
   it computes itself.
3. *The text generation must be seeded too.* The generated entity
   generators call =faker::lorem::sentence()=, =faker::number::integer()=
   and similar, for example
   =ores.trading.api/src/generators/activity_category_generator.cpp= line
   50 and =bond_forward_generator.cpp= line 50.
   =grep -rn "faker::seed" projects/= returns nothing, so faker-cxx draws
   from its own unseeded engine. A seeded context alone does not pin a
   single description string.

With those three closed, "same seed and same anchor produce the same
rows" is true and testable. Note what determinism does not mean. The same
seed at a later anchor must produce a different population, moved forward
coherently. Determinism is over the pair, not over the seed.

** How the population stays relative to the present

The as-of is an input to the generator, never a read of the clock inside
a field generator.

- The context carries =(seed, as_of)=.
- Every date field is emitted as =as_of + offset(tenor)=, where the tenor
  comes from the product's own terms, so a swap's schedule rules keep
  their tenors and move their anchor.
- The generator emits schedule rules wherever the schema offers the
  choice, because a rule is relative and a date list is not, and
  =check ("schedule_kind" in ('rules', 'dates'))= lets it say so.
- Business-day snapping runs in the generator against the same calendars
  ORE will use, so the emitted dates are the dates the engine would
  compute. Nothing in the schema can check this, so the generator is the
  only place it can be guaranteed.
- A re-provision at a later as-of re-runs the generator. The population
  moves and nothing is shifted.

** How referential integrity is guaranteed rather than hoped for

The database already enforces most of it, so the work is to feed it real
keys.

- *Party.* Set =app.current_party_id= per write, because
  =ores_trading_bond_instruments_insert_fn= overwrites the column from
  session state. Generate one party's population in one pass, then the
  next.
- *Counterparty.* Pick from the 38 links in
  =ores_refdata_party_counterparties_tbl= for the party being written,
  not from the 2454 counterparties. Nothing checks this, so the generator
  must be the check, and the weighting page should carry the per-party
  list.
- *Book and netting set.* Pick the book from the trade's party and the
  netting set from that party and the chosen counterparty, because the
  booking trigger refuses anything else.
- *Staff.* Set =modified_by= and =performed_by= to a real username, and
  pick it from the =Trading=-role holders in
  =ores_iam_account_parties_tbl= for the party being written. This is the
  one piece with no model behind it, so it is a decision to make and
  record rather than a lookup to perform.
- *The prerequisite to close first.* Give staff accounts a
  =default_party_id=, or declare the =account_parties= rows as the
  authoritative mapping. Today 2 of 61 current accounts have one, and the
  other 59 have no party of their own while their =account_parties= rows
  are many-to-many, so the question of which person entered a trade cannot
  be answered from the data.

** How a reviewer sees what changed

Two layers, and the first is the one that matters for a seed.

- *Regenerate and diff.* Because the population is a function of
  (seed, as-of), a review is a rebuild at a stated pair and a diff of the
  emitted SQL or fixtures. This is the review the seeder already imagines
  for its datasets, and it is the one that catches an accidental change to
  the shape. It needs the emitted population to be committed or written
  where a diff can see it.
- *Entity history.* The generic history request, the per-entity handlers
  and =ores.diff= (=doc/knowledge/architecture/history_diff_architecture.org=
  lines 14-30) already render version-to-version changes. Under mechanism
  A the first version is the only version, and history stays quiet. That
  quiet is the signal.

* Recommendations

1. *Build the trade generator and the seeded population inside
   =ores.trading=, using mechanism A, and review it by regenerating and
   diffing.*
2. *Put the generator in the =generators= facet.* Every trading model
   already declares =generator_facet_name: generators=, and
   =ores.trading.api/src/generators/= is where the machinery sits. The
   population generator extends that facet rather than starting a new
   component, because it must read the flight of entities the models own.
3. *Put the arithmetic in =ores.utility/generation=.*
   =generation_context= and =generation_engine= are already the seam, and
   =past_timepoint= already reaches for the clock. Add an anchor to the
   context and an offset helper to the engine. This is one change that
   serves every component's generator.
4. *Put the trigger at the boundary, not in the model.* A shell command
   or a service operation calls =trading.v1.ops.book_trade= for the anchor
   and the booking, and the generated repositories for the instrument
   family. Do not add a populate script per table. =dq_artefacts.org=
   lines 44-47 already say the owning model states the staging shape, and
   the same principle puts the write behind the owning service.
5. *Keep the corpus and the weights as data.*
   =doc/knowledge/domain/trade_population_weights.org= already states a
   weighted distribution over 86 ORE products, with the rule at lines
   57-69 that every product carries a non-zero weight so each pricing path
   is exercised. That page is written to be a data input. Keep it one.
6. *Close the seed's three determinism gaps first.* The anchor on
   =generation_context=, seed-derived ids, and a seeded faker are phase 0.
   Without them a re-provision is not repeatable and the convergence
   acceptance cannot be written.
7. *Decide the person-to-party mapping and record it.* This is a
   prerequisite and not a task. Give staff accounts a =default_party_id=,
   or declare the =account_parties= rows authoritative.
8. *Allow DQ only for the parameters.* A staged artefact holding the
   population shape is a good fit. A staged artefact holding trades is
   not.
9. *Do not build a trade artefact table in DQ, a date-shift pass, or a
   populate script per table.* These are the three designs the
   investigation rules out.

** What has to be built, and what is reused

Reused:

- the =generators= facet, =generation_context=, =generation_engine= and
  =parent_seed= blocks, as the skeleton;
- =trading.v1.ops.book_trade= and the generated repositories, as the write
  path;
- the trigger-enforced integrity for party, book, netting set and
  counterparty identifier, as the guarantee;
- the export walk, =ores::ore::xml::exporter::export_portfolio=, as the
  bridge to the engine;
- =trade_population_weights.org=, as the weighting data;
- =ores.diff= and the history renderer, as the review surface;
- the seeder's dataset convention, for the parameters if a staged form is
  wanted later.

Built:

- the as-of anchor and its offset arithmetic in =ores.utility/generation=;
- a seeded id scheme that does not read the clock;
- a faker seed call, or generated text that does not use faker;
- the bond population generator and its per-party counterparty and staff
  selection;
- a shell command or service operation that runs the generation for a
  party and a tenant;
- the person-to-party decision, which is a prerequisite and not a task.

Not built, and deliberately:

- no trade artefact table in DQ;
- no date-shift pass;
- no new populate script per table.
