* Summary

The design is
[[id:785C68A0-DD94-404A-BF16-57814554DA12][Operational Authorisation Queue]], and this prototype
is the first drawing of it as a screen. The thing to settle is not the
content — the page states that — but *where the two divisions live*: a deal
belongs to a class (flow or non-flow) and it sits in a state (awaiting, held
over, amendment, authorised, denied), and a person works those two at once.

Two answers are drawn side by side:

- *Rail*: class and state as one tree in the rail, the list beside it, and the
  open deal in a fixed pane on the right.
- *Tabs*: state as tabs over a full-width table, class as a segmented control
  above them, and the open deal in a drawer that covers the list while it is
  read.

It is one page of mock HTML, CSS and JavaScript. It calls no service, needs no
build and no session, and both variants open from the same file with
=variant=rail= or =variant=tabs=.

* What both variants have to do

** The queue is a queue, not a list

Ordering is by recency, with one override: a deal not exercised and close to
expiry is pinned at the top in red, with a banner naming it. An option that
expires while it waits in a queue is a loss the queue caused, so urgency
outranks arrival.

** A decision always carries a comment

Authorise, hold over and deny are disabled until the comment has text,
authorisation included. A hold also needs a reason from the fixed list — and
that list is mostly handover markers between regional teams, so the field is
drawn as a select with the handover entries last, not as a free-text box.

** A denial cannot become an authorisation

The denied deal opens with its reason, the Authorise button is not drawn, and
the notice says why. The one action it offers is the undo: un-authorise the
denial with a reason, which returns it to unseen and leaves a new version to be
authorised if it is right. A keyboard attempt at the transition raises the
exception rather than quietly succeeding.

** An amendment says what changed

The fields that changed are marked in the fact list, with the previous values
underneath, and an amendment adds its reason code, its department, and the
operational error flag — the flag that separates a deal booked wrongly from one
that moved for a business reason.

** A new version does not lock the row

The row carries "updated 2 min ago by …" and the pane says the row is not
locked: the operator may read it now or come back. Nothing in the prototype
takes a lease on a deal.

** A structure authorises as a whole

The package's legs are listed inside the open deal, and the actions are the
package's own.

* What the two variants disagree about

| Question | Rail | Tabs |
|----------+------+------|
| How many things am I filtering at once? | Two, visible together | One at a time, the class set first |
| Where does the deal go when I open it? | Beside the list, always | Over the list, while it is read |
| What does the list read as when nothing is chosen? | The class's whole state group | The state tab's rows across classes |
| Cost at 1200px | The list loses a third of its width | The table keeps its width, the deal covers it |

The rail answers "what is in this class, in each state" without a click. The
tabs answer "what is awaiting authorisation" faster, but hiding the class
behind a control means a person can read the queue they did not mean.

* What this does not decide

- Whether the queue is its own area screen or a tab of the Operations area.
  The crumb reads =Operations › Operational authorisation queue= in both.
- How the amend diff is drawn when many fields changed; one row is marked here.
- The reports the design names — time held, and what stayed overnight — are
  absent.
