* Summary

The GMM components prototype argues what the component screen should show before
any of it is built. It carries three variants of the screen and three mock weight
states, so the layout is judged against numbers, and it argues the row the group
page records: a mixture is browsed across every feed at once, and the weights are
normalised on save, so the screen must show the raw sum as well as the normalised
weights. The typed weight and the stored weight are two different numbers, and a
person editing a mixture has to see both before the save decides.

It is throwaway, with no service and no build step. Open the
[[file:manage-gmm-components/][component screen]].

* Detail

** The question

A component is one weighted Gaussian of a feed's mixture: =component_index=, a
description, a mean, a standard deviation and a weight. The weights are per feed,
not per screen. The stored rows need not sum to one, and the save divides every
weight of a feed by its raw sum, so the typed w is not the w the mixture draws from.

The open question is how the screen shows both numbers without making either one
look like a fault. A raw sum of 0.90 is not a refusal, and a screen that hides it
hides the only evidence that the typed numbers changed.

** The variants

Switch with the bar at the bottom, or =?variant=. The default is =a=. The
variants disagree about which question the screen answers.

| Variant | Name | The structural argument |
|---------+------+-------------------------|
| =A= | components across the FX feeds | One row per component, gathered under its feed, with the per-feed weight sum repeated once, the audit trail of the row, and Edit, Delete and New Component. The mixture is browsed across every feed at once. |
| =B= | density preview | The selected feed's mixture drawn as f(x) = Σ w_i·N(x; μ_i, σ_i) over log-return percent, the components faint under the bold mixture, and the moments the components imply. |
| =C= | normalisation and validation | Each feed's raw sum, the normalised weight per component, a flag when a raw sum misses 1, and bars of the normalised weights. The save's arithmetic drawn as data. |

The variants disagree about structure, not colour: *A* spends the screen on the whole catalogue of mixtures, *B* on one feed's shape, *C* on the typed weights against the stored ones.

** The states

Switch with the bar at the bottom, or =?state=. The default is =normal=.

| State | What it shows |
|-------+---------------|
| =normal= | Every feed's raw weight sum is 1, so normalisation is the identity. |
| =unnormalised= | GBP/USD's raw weights sum to 0.90 and is flagged, while USD/JPY and AUD/USD still sum to 1. |
| =single= | USD/JPY has one component, a plain Gaussian whose σ alone sets the width and whose w stays 1.00. |

The unnormalised state is the point of the prototype. It makes the two numbers
distinguishable by a person who is not reading the code: the raw sum sits at 0.90
with a warning, the normalised weights still draw a valid mixture, and the save
goes through.

** Driving it

The bottom bar switches variant and state, and both are query parameters too, so
=?variant=c&state=unnormalised= opens one screen directly. In variant *A* the feed
buttons choose the feed a new component joins, and the table is live: *Edit* opens
the mock =GmmComponentDetailDialog=, *Save* writes the edit back, *Cancel* drops it,
*Delete* removes and renumbers a row, and *New Component* appends one at w = 0.00.

Every control is prototype chrome, drawn to look unlike the design being judged.

** What is fake

Everything on the page is mock data. The three feeds, their identifiers, their
=oresmd://= URLs, the components, the actors, the timestamps and the versions are
hard-coded literals, and a saved row is attributed to =you (mocked)= at =now=. There
is no server, no session and no build step: a reload returns the page to its seed.

The arithmetic is real, and the page does it: the density, the moments, the raw
sums and the normalised weights are computed from the mock rows as the page draws
them.

** Where the code lives

=doc/prototypes/market_data/manage-gmm-components/=. One file, =index.html=, with
its CSS and its JavaScript inline: no framework, no =prototype.css=, no
=prototype.js= and no build step. The page opens by double-clicking it. The site
build copies the directory through =ores-deploy-web-app= as it stands, adding nothing.

** The verdict

The group accepted the argument and recorded it as the row this note answers to: a
mixture is browsed across every feed at once, and the weights are normalised on
save, so the screen must show the raw sum as well as the normalised weights. What
the group took is that pairing: the cross-feed list of *A* and the table of *C*
answer it, while the density preview of *B* stayed on the page because it reads one
feed's shape and the decided question is what the save does to every feed's numbers.

The three variants and the three states stayed on the page as the record, and a
panel from any of them may still be borrowed.

** What it does not settle

The typed weight. The page says the save divides each weight by its feed's raw sum,
and the prototype keeps the typed value in the editor. Whether the stored row is
rewritten is open; [[id:7493D798-DD0D-43A5-8DAB-6E63377253DC][The GMM component]] records
that nothing rewrites the stored rows and that the draw normalises them itself.

The refusal. The page warns when a raw sum is not 1 and lets the save through.
Whether the shell refuses a sum that misses 1 by more than a tolerance is open.

The all-zero set. The page can delete every component of a feed, which no save
would accept: the one rule a stored set must satisfy is a sum greater than zero,
and the service and the client check it elsewhere.

The single component. With K = 1 the mixture is a plain Gaussian and σ alone sets
the width. Whether the screen keeps a component that carries the whole mixture,
and whether the density preview belongs on this screen or the feed editor, is
open.

The shell. The footer names the =gmm_components= resource operations. The page
asserts them; no service answers.
