#+title: Role-Based Access Control
#+description: The RBAC model — roles compose permissions, users are assigned roles — as applied in ORE Studio's IAM subsystem.
#+type: knowledge
#+level: cross
#+filetags: :knowledge:security:iam:rbac:
#+created: 2024-06-15
#+updated: 2026-10-06

/Role-Based Access Control (RBAC)/ restricts system access to
authorised users based on their job roles, rather than individual
user permissions. By grouping permissions into roles (e.g. =admin=,
=user=), the project simplifies access management, enforces least
privilege, and lowers the blast radius of any one compromised
account. RBAC is the model that backs [[id:8AEB87D6-0CE2-1434-E71B-60BEAE20ADF0][Identity and Access
Management]] in ORE Studio; the concrete role/permission entities and
their assignments live in [[id:D00BEA0D-E501-C534-A013-E6F40C1A6097][ores.iam.core]]. Return to [[id:7474935C-95D1-3034-B833-83DCD9FB3A09][Knowledge]].

* Key Components of RBAC

- *Roles*: collections of permissions defining job functions
  (/e.g./, "Nurse", "Manager").
- *Permissions*: specific rights to view, create, or modify data.
- *Users*: individuals assigned one or more roles.

* Core Principles

- *Role-Based Assignment*: users are assigned roles, and
  permissions are attached to those roles, not the user directly.
- *Least Privilege*: users receive only the minimum permissions
  necessary for their role.
- *Separation of Duties (SoD)*: As described in [[https://www.ibm.com/br-pt/think/topics/rbac][IBM's research]], SoD
  prevents conflicts of interest by requiring multiple people to
  complete high-risk tasks.

* Roles in ORE Studio

A role holds explicit permission codes. There is no read wildcard such as
=refdata::*:read=: a wildcard grants resources that do not exist yet, so an
entity added later would be readable by every role that holds it without
anybody deciding so. =*= and =component::*= exist, for the administrator roles
only.

Every person account holds *Member*. Member holds the reads every person's
screens need and nothing more: the reference data vocabulary, and the
furniture every screen uses, such as badges, change reasons, feature flags and
pictures. A job role, such as Trading or Operations, holds only what its job
adds. An account without Member sees empty screens; that fails closed, and an
administrator gives Member back.

A read is classified before it is granted:

| Class | Examples | Granted to |
|-------+----------+------------|
| Vocabulary | Currencies, countries, calendars, day counts, tenors, the status and type lookups | Member |
| Screen furniture | Badge definitions, change reasons, feature flags, pictures | Member |
| Organisation | Names, job titles, pictures and who reports to whom, for the people in your parties and under you | Member, through =iam::organisation:read= |
| Business fact | Parties, counterparties, books, business units, trades | The job roles that need it |
| Personal data | Contact information, sign-ins, sessions | The person, through a self read; administrators |

Which reads are open to every member without any role is decided separately,
in [[id:804C7048-DBBF-4B39-8737-BFB4949884C4][Authorised reads]]. Member is not
an exception to that rule: it is a role, stored on the account, that an
administrator can see and withhold.

* Benefits of RBAC

- *Simplified Management*: reduces the overhead of managing
  permissions for thousands of users.
- *Increased Security*: according to the [[https://www.cloudflare.com/en-gb/learning/access-management/role-based-access-control-rbac/][Cloudflare write-up]], it
  limits unauthorised access and reduces the risk of data leaks.
- *Compliance*: facilitates adherence to data-protection
  regulations.
