* Acceptance

- The derived source reads a pair's levels through get_pair_levels and differences them into returns.
- fixing_provider, ecb_fixing_item and ecb_legs_of are gone, with their tests.
- A pair whose source stopped fixing one of its sides is refused, naming the provider and the
  day it stopped, instead of being read from its last fixing. (The first draft read "USD/CLP and
  USD/COP are back in the catalogue"; measured, neither can be: see the Result.)
- A fresh tenant provisions through every step with the derived source on the new read.

* Plan

Slices 3 and 4 of [[id:C330DD6F-258B-4D41-930D-AE00FC60BF2E][the derive task]], in one change so the move and the deletion land
together.

- The feed's reader becomes =levels(pair, to)= and =pair_log_returns= takes one series.
- The adapter answers it with =get_pair_levels=; the ECB constants and their test go.
- Bring USD/CLP and USD/COP back, which the IMF quotes and the ECB does not.

What the provisioning run of the first draft changed:

- *USD/COP.* The IMF last fixed COP on 2022-02-28 (1213 fixings), so the pair was read from a
  four-year-old rate and quoted as today's. The read now refuses a side whose last fixing is
  more than a week before the window's end; the pair stays out of the catalogue.
- *USD/CLP.* 2345 IMF fixings, short of the 2520 returns a 10Y window needs, so its surface
  lacks 10Y and is refused whole. Sizing the grid to the sample is the sizing story's task,
  "Compute how far a pair's surface reaches"; the pair stays out until then.
- *The first party.* The blueprint step waited three minutes per party in turn, so the first
  party's wait ended while the derived source was still on its first pass: Acme Corporation Plc
  held NZD/JPY from 09:18:34 and the step, which ran from 09:15 to 09:27, never saw it. The
  wait is now over every party together and lasts while blueprints keep becoming ready.
- *The late routes.* The second run gave 105 blueprints none of their quotes. The marketdata
  listener thread ran each subscription event's demand refresh itself, and the usage recorder in
  it wrote 2707 and then 8568 demand intervals, 3 and 4.3 minutes each. The US, UK and HK routes
  arrived at 11:15, 7.5 minutes late; the derived quotes had published at 11:07:46 into a
  snapshot that held only Plc's route, and the derived feed does not send them again. The event
  handlers now ask the supervisor to refresh, and its monitor loop does the work.
- *Not the cause.* A pair's level read takes 40 to 60 ms (2555 levels, measured at debug level
  in the synthetic service), so the per-request read of the fixing sources is not a cost worth
  a cache.

Also in this change, at the person's request: the setup rail opens each step to show its times,
its log and, for a failed step, its error one piece of work to a line; the run states how many
steps are done, with a bar and its elapsed time; a running step states its elapsed time. A
failed step turns red and keeps its error behind its disclosure. Each step records what it did,
one line per piece of work, so its log is no longer empty.

Also at the person's request, after the third run failed on two curves of one party: a setup
whose market data, trades or reports are incomplete still gives a usable tenant, so those steps
no longer stop it.

- *Essential steps.* Each step kind states whether the tenant needs its work. A step that is
  not essential and fails, or outlives its deadline, ends with warnings: the engine puts its
  error in its log and the run goes on. The flag is carried on the run's plan rows, beside the
  deadline. The essential kinds are the system data, reference data, legal entities, parties,
  staff and passwords, auto-approval, calendars, the hand-over and the finish.
- *The curve wait.* The party curve step waited per party in turn, like the blueprint step did,
  and Plc's SEK and ZAR overnight series arrived 20 seconds after its three minutes. It now
  waits over every party together while curves keep becoming ready.
- *The report.* The shell names the steps that ended with warnings when a run finishes. The
  login answer now states the account's previous login, so the shell prints a summary on the
  tenant administrator's first login after the setup finished, and prints the warnings in full
  with =setup-report=. The web shows a notice on every screen until the administrator dismisses it,
  and a =/setup-report= page that lists each step's warnings above the whole run.

* Result

The derived volatility source reads each pair's levels through =get_pair_levels= and differences
them into returns; the ECB helpers and their tests are gone. A pair whose fixing stopped more
than a week before the window's end is refused with =fixing_stopped=, naming the provider and
the day. USD/CLP and USD/COP stay out of the catalogue, for the reasons in the Plan.

A fresh Acme tenant provisions through all 17 steps. The blueprint and curve steps wait over
every party together, and the marketdata listener no longer runs the demand refresh itself.

The setup is resilient: a step that is not essential and fails, or runs out of time, ends with
warnings and the run goes on. The shell names those steps when a run finishes, prints a summary
on the tenant administrator's first login after the setup, and prints the full warnings with
=setup-report=. The web shows a dismissible notice and a =/setup-report= page. The live run had
no warned step, so the yellow path was proven by the two engine tests and by one step marked
warned in the local database.

Open gap against the story: the derived source still writes its quotes under the system party,
so "each party derives its own volatility quotes" is not yet met. The story stays STARTED.
