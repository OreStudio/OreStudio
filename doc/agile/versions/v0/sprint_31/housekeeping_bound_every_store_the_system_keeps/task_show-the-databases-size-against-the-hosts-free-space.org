* Goal

A person can answer one question from the home: is the database about to fill the disk? The answer is two series that move together and the time the free space lasts at the rate the database grows.

* Acceptance

- The installation measures the database's total size and the host's free space on a schedule, and keeps both as a series
- Every measurement states the database and the host it describes, because a size without its host states nothing
- A read serves both series over a horizon, so a person reads a trend and not only today's number
- The system administrator's home states the database's size, the host's free space, and the time the free space lasts at the current growth rate, or says why it cannot be stated
- The tenant administrator's home states the size its own rows hold and the change over the horizon, so a tenant reads the same two numbers for its own slice
- The figures lead on the dashboards and are not only on the housekeeping screen, because the disk fills whether or not somebody opened housekeeping
- The panel warns at a stated threshold before the disk fills, rather than when it has

* Plan

The circle is the design. No SQL can ask a filesystem how much room it has, and
no host can state the size of a database it does not hold. So the row is written
in one place: =ores_scheduler_record_capacity_fn= reads the database's own size
and takes the free space from its caller, and the caller is a scheduler action
that runs on the host and measures a named path. A caller can therefore state the
free space and never the size.

The series lives in =ores_scheduler_capacity_samples_tbl=, a hypertable chunked
on the day, holding the system tenant's rows so every session reads them and only
the installation writes them. The read is =scheduler.v1.capacity.list=, which
answers the window it covered beside the points, like the store's series.

The host cannot always be measured. A path that is not there answers null rather
than zero, the row records the database's size and a note, and the panel states
the note instead of a verdict: an unreadable disk and a full one are different
facts.

The measurement is the platform's: =ores.platform= gained =get_filesystem_space=
and =get_host_name=, each with a POSIX and a Windows branch, because a scheduler
has no business holding either.

On the screen the figures read as the free space's share of the filesystem, so
the verdict says the same thing at 500 GB free of 2 TB and 50 GB of 200 GB. The
thresholds are stated where the code states them: warn below 20% free, alarm
below 10%.

* Result

The installation measures itself: every hour a scheduler action asks the host for
the free space of the filesystem it names and the database for its own size, and
writes both beside the database and host they describe. The system
administrator's home states the database's size, the free space, the share it is
of the filesystem, and the days the free space lasts at the rate it is falling,
with the trend beneath.

Proved on this environment: the reads answer 200 over the three horizons, the
sampler fired once a minute while its cadence was a minute, and the rows hold
real figures (1.94 GB database, 529 GB free of 972 GB, host =Wiener=). A fresh
recreate grants the scheduler service its tables by prefix, which covers the new
one.

Two things the work did not do. The tenant's own slice is
[[id:166B2434-D3DF-4DED-B59E-1AF449EE3D03][its own task]], because a tenant is a
share of every store and not a store. And the series has no bound of its own: the
catalogue does not name it, so it grows for ever until the task that accounts for
every relation puts it there — the capture states the gap.
