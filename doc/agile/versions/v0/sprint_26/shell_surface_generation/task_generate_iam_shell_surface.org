* Goal

Every shell command iam answers is produced by a generated unit, or is recorded as a hand-craft with a reason. iam carries the largest hand-written surface in the clean set: accounts_commands, rbac_commands, tenants_commands and provision_commands register verbs beside 23 generated units. Seven of those verbs send a request the generated units already send, and one refuses every call.

* Plan

Read every hand-written iam verb's request and compare it with the request each
generated unit sends. Retire the verb when a generated unit sends the same
request, which covers seven of them, and retire the one that refuses every call.
Keep the rest with a reason.

Two readings made the outcome larger than the count suggests. The generated
=account_operations= unit already carries eleven verbs, so four of the
hand-written account verbs send exactly what it sends. And =accounts
sessions-for= is dead: it answers every call with a refusal.

The retirements take their recipes and scripts with them, and the provisioning
smoke recipe is migrated to the generated names because it calls two of the
retired verbs.

* Notes

Eight verbs are retired, and each sent a request a generated unit already sends.

| Retired | Request | Generated replacement |
|---------+---------+-----------------------|
| =accounts create= | =save_account_request= | =account_operations save-account= |
| =accounts lock= | =lock_account_request= | =account_operations lock-account= |
| =accounts unlock= | =unlock_account_request= | =account_operations unlock-account= |
| =accounts list-logins= | =list_login_info_request= | =login_info list= |
| =accounts sessions= | =list_sessions_request= | =sessions list= |
| =accounts sessions-for= | none: it refuses every call | =sessions list= |
| =accounts active-sessions= | =get_active_sessions_request= | =session_operations get-active-sessions= |
| =tenants history= | =list_tenant_versions_request= | =tenants versions= |

=sessions-for= was the surprise. It answered every call with "Listing sessions by
account is not served", so it was a verb that could only fail, and the page it
pointed at is the generated =sessions list=.

The verbs that stay:

- =accounts login= and =accounts logout=, with their root aliases, change this
  client's own session. The =login= operation renders no unit because a unit
  that sends a request and prints the reply cannot express that. The
  =provision= porcelain calls =process_login=, so the handler stays too.
- =accounts history <username>= uses the generic history protocol and the diff
  renderer, keyed by username where the generated =accounts versions= is keyed
  by id.
- =accounts info <username>= resolves a username and composes two generated
  reads, the account and its roles.
- =accounts set-default-party= accepts a party id or a full name, so it resolves
  the name first. The generated =set-my-default-party= takes the id.
- =permissions suggest= accepts a hostname or a tenant id in one argument where
  the generated =suggest-role-commands= takes three separate ones, and the
  provisioning recipes call it in that form.
- =provision tenant=, =provision party= and =bootstrap setup= dispatch a
  workflow and can block on it. The =tenant_provisioning= and =bootstrap=
  operations state the requests; the wait is what the hand-written verbs add.

Three iam operations render no unit: =login=, =password_policy= and
=tenant_provisioning=. The first and third are the sign-offs above.
=password_policy= has no shell surface at all, and the shell was never given one.

The retirements took their hand-written recipes and scripts with them:
=accounts/lock=, =accounts/unlock= and =accounts/list_login_info= are deleted, and
the generated =account_operations=, =login_info=, =sessions=,
=session_operations= and =tenants= recipes and scripts already cover the
replacements.

The provisioning smoke recipe called two of the retired verbs, and it carried two
more lines that no longer matched any command: =bootstrap <principal> <password>
<email>= where the generated command is =bootstrap create-initial-admin=, and a
=tenants add= line missing its =is_registration_default= argument. It is migrated
to the current shapes, so the recipe matches the surface it documents.

* Result

iam's hand-written surface is eight verbs smaller and six reasons longer. The
generated =account_operations= unit turned out to carry four of the verbs
=accounts_commands= still registered, =login_info=, =sessions= and
=session_operations= carried three more, and =tenants versions= carried the
eighth. =accounts sessions-for= was the ninth finding: a verb that refused every
call.

The four units that registered those verbs are two now. =tenants_commands= is
gone entirely, because the history verb was all it carried. The two that remain
are signed off verb by verb: session-changing sign-in and sign-out, a generic
history renderer, a username-addressed info view, a name-resolving default-party
setter, a role-suggestion read with a different argument shape, and the three
verbs that dispatch a workflow and wait on it.

The suite passes 821 assertions in 312 cases and the application suite 53 in 22.
The accounts menu answers ten verbs at the runtime, down from seventeen, and the
shell registers its 227 menus with no collision.

Migrating the recipes turned up two more stale command lines in the provisioning
smoke recipe, which are fixed with the retirements.
