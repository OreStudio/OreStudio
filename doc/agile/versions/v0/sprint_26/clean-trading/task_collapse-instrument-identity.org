* Goal

Give the component two identities where it carries three, and give the
database the relationships the schema can hold. This is item M05 and
the identity half of M07, and it implements decision D2: the trade id
identifies the trade and its instrument, and the security issue is a
separate reference datum.

The rule is already written down.
=doc/knowledge/architecture/trade_modeling.org= says that the economic
terms belong to one agreement, that the trade and instrument
relationship is one to one, and that "the trade id identifies both".
=doc/knowledge/domain/trade.org= names the four entities: the product
type, the security issue where one exists, the trade, and the
instrument carrying the economic terms.

The code disagrees. =instrument_identity_field_group.org= declares
=instrument_id= as a key and =trade_id= as an optional soft
back-reference, and 26 models inherit the group. =trade= adds a second
nullable pointer back at the instrument. The declaration carries 91
=instrument_id= columns and no foreign key targets an instrument table,
so the two directions of a one-to-one relationship are two nullable
pointers that nothing can make agree.

This re-opens two tasks the sprint 25 story abandoned:
=trading_model_cleanup= task =7B23DB0F=, collapse the instrument
identity into the trade, and task =0C525D24=, split the bond security
from the trade. The order is the order the abandoned split task gave:
collapse first, because the reverse reshapes the same tables twice.

* Plan

** 1. Model the collapse

=instrument_identity_field_group= is the leverage point. It declares
=instrument_id=, =trade_type_code=, =party_id= and =trade_id=, and 26
models inherit it. Removing =instrument_id= there and making =trade_id=
the non-nullable key carries most of the change. The remaining entities
keyed on =instrument_id= are the per-product and child tables. The
sprint 25 analysis counted 47 of them; re-count at this head, because
PR 3 adds more.

=trade.instrument_id= goes with it. The link becomes the child's
=trade_id=, and a real foreign key replaces the soft one.

** 2. Sequence so nothing breaks in the middle

Model first, then regenerate, then the hand-written callers, then the
SQL. No commit removes a column before its replacement exists. The
component audit runs before the task closes.

** 3. Split the security, after the collapse

*Moved out of this task by decision D17.* The split reshapes the bond
family around a security identity and is the largest part of the diff,
so it is =task_split-bond-security-from-trade.org= and PR 5. What
follows is the plan it inherits.

Apply the split plan from the abandoned task, which is written against
ORE's schema rather than invented:

- =bondReferenceDatum= is exactly =bondData= minus =SecurityId=,
  =BondNotional=, =Payer= and =CreditRisk=. Everything else on a bond is
  static data about the security, and the four exceptions are the
  trade's own: which security, how much, which side, and whether credit
  risk applies.
- The key is the datum id, so a bond's is its =SecurityId= and a bond
  future's is its =ContractName=. No entity needs the words "reference
  data" in its name; =bond_issue= is already the shape to follow.
- =bond_issue= gains the six fields ORE has and we lack, and loses
  =bond_notional=.
- A trade may state its bond inline or by reference. Normalising on the
  security is right, and the inline case then populates the security
  rather than bypassing it.

** 4. Regenerate and repair the callers

Regenerate the component and the SQL, then repair every hand-written
caller: the mappers, the readers, the import path and the shell
commands. The ORE mapper and its container are hand-written and will
need a pass.

** 5. Prove

Run the ORE round trip through the shell over the bond documents and
require zero loss. Then the whole-tree build, the four suites and the
drift dry run.

** 6. Record

Update the story's checklist rows for M05 and M07, and record the
closure of defects 7, 9, 10 and 13.

| Change | Count |

The shape of the change in the models:

- =instrument_identity_field_group= drops =instrument_id= and makes
  =trade_id= the =boost::uuids::uuid= the group exists for; the two leg
  identity groups gain the same member, which is what the domain struct
  reads (the first build failed on =no member named 'trade_id' in
  composite_leg_identity=).
- Each of the 30 instrument families drops its =instrument_id= column and
  makes =trade_id= the primary key, and declares
  =* Foreign keys ** trade_id= against =ores_trading_trades_tbl= with
  =:nullable: false=. The redundant =(tenant_id, trade_id)= index the old
  soft link carried is gone: the generated primary-key index covers it.
- The 28 fact and child tables rename their column; the four that already
  declared a foreign key (composite_leg, swap_leg and the two leg groups)
  retarget it at the trades table. =swap_leg= loses =:skip_check:=, which
  existed only because its parent column had no single instrument table to
  point at.
- =trade= drops =instrument_id=, its index and its generator block;
  =trade_classification= drops the field.

The hand-written callers, in the order the sequence fix required:

- =stamp_ids= now takes one id, the trade's: the instrument's key is the
  trade, so there is no second id to stamp. The import planner mints the
  trade id and stamps it; the ORE import handler writes the trade's and the
  instrument's =trade_id= from it and no longer writes
  =trade.classification.instrument_id=.
- The export join in the trade handler reads the instrument by the trade's
  own id instead of by a separate instrument pointer, and the child
  collections (legs, call dates, constituents, underlyings) are keyed by
  it.
- The bond reader and the parent-scoped queries rename their parameter and
  their column; the shell's commodity verb takes the trade as its first
  argument instead of minting an instrument id, and the equity position
  child verb takes it in place of the instrument id.

** Two defects the gates caught, and the fixes

1. The DB recreate refused the first schema: =too many parameters
   specified for RAISE= compiling =ores_trading_swap_legs_insert_fn=. The
   =swap_leg= foreign-key block was rewritten without
   =:error_message:=, so the generated check raised an empty message and
   the column became a parameter. Fixed in the model, regenerated, and the
   recreate then exited 0. The whole set of foreign-key entries was swept
   for the same omission; this was the only one.
2. The first regenerated eventing tests did not compile. The seed plan
   wrote a parent row's columns flat, which was wrong for the trade: it
   groups its columns into identity, parties, classification, lifecycle
   and audit. The codegen fix is its own commit and carries five new
   cases; the collapse is the change that exposed it.
