#+title: Store ticks at one hundred thousand feeds
#+description: At 100,000 feeds the ticks hypertable takes about 145,000 rows a second, about 12.5 billion a day, and four times that in an active market. PostgreSQL and TimescaleDB must ingest, keep, drop and query that volume.
#+type: capture

This page is a [[id:671F18E4-E09C-4B3B-BD24-D33DF8AE38A6][capture]] in the *next* bucket of the product backlog — a pre-sprint idea, not yet pulled into a sprint as a story.

* What

=ores_marketdata_ticks_tbl= must take the stored side of the volume in
[[id:B1B486EC-E42B-43CF-82AE-D9F2E70DAA79][Run the market data path at one hundred thousand feeds]], keep it for as long as it is needed, drop it after, and answer queries
over it, on one PostgreSQL instance that ten environments share.

| Feeds   | Source ticks/s | Stored rows/s (5 routes) | Rows a day     | Rows/s, active (x4) |
|---------+----------------+--------------------------+----------------+---------------------|
| 110     | about 32       | about 160                | about 14 million | about 640         |
| 10,000  | about 2,900    | about 14,500             | about 1.25 billion | about 58,000    |
| 100,000 | about 29,000   | about 145,000            | about 12.5 billion | about 580,000   |

The figures scale today's configuration: 110 feeds configure about 115,000
ticks an hour, about 0.29 a second for each feed, and each source tick is stored
once for each of five party routes. A different mix of feeds or routes moves
them, so treat them as the order of magnitude to design for, not a forecast.

Each of these holds at today's volume and does not at 100,000 feeds:

- Ingest is one COPY transaction per batch from one worker. At 145,000 rows a
  second the batch size, the number of writers, the WAL rate and checkpoints all
  decide whether the store keeps up.
- The table is not compressed, because row-level security does not work on
  compressed chunks. A row holds four UUIDs, a timestamp, a smallint and a
  double, about 82 bytes, and with the row header and the index entry it costs
  about 150 bytes on disk. At about 12.5 billion rows a day that is about 1.9
  terabytes a day, an estimate from the row layout, not a measurement.
- Nothing drops old chunks: the Apache edition has no retention job ([[id:CB2F97F2-5D7F-4986-A95F-2426FBECEA0E][The ticks hypertable grows without bound on the Apache edition]]).
- Chunks are one day wide, so one chunk would hold billions of rows and its
  index on series and time would not fit in memory.
- The Apache edition has no continuous aggregates, so every count or rate is a
  scan of raw rows. [[id:8C251770-BD8F-4CD6-9B7F-FE7004EC1A8E][Drill into stored ticks by series for a window]] must stay a narrow, bounded query, and anything
  per-minute must come from samples, not from this table.
- PostgreSQL 18.6 runs =io_method = worker=. Its io_uring method speeds up large
  reads; benchmark it once scans at this volume exist, since the setting applies
  to every environment on the instance.

* Why

The volume is set by the feed count, not by a choice in the database layer, so
the store must be designed for it rather than tuned after. The decisions are
coupled: compression against row-level security, the TimescaleDB edition
against retention and aggregates, chunk width against index size, and one
shared instance against ten environments.
