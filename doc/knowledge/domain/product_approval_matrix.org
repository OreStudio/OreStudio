* Summary

A product approval matrix states which product types a firm permits a part of
itself to trade. This page argues that the matrix has three levels — the legal
entity, the desk and the book — that each level may state a set equal to or
narrower than the one above it and never wider, and that the portfolio tree is
not a level of it. The argument rests on where authority sits in a trading firm:
a mandate is granted to a legal entity and to a desk, while a portfolio is a
grouping of books for aggregation and access that moves without an approval.

* Detail

** The three levels

| Level | The row that states it | What it says |
|-------+------------------------+--------------|
| Legal entity | =party_product_types=, with =parties.restricts_product_types= | The product types the entity may trade at all |
| Desk | =business_unit_product_types=, with =business_units.restricts_product_types= | The subset its desks may trade |
| Book | =book_product_types=, with =books.restricts_product_types= | The subset one booking unit may carry |

Each level has two states and not one. A level whose flag is false states no set
and permits every product type the catalogue holds, which is what a level that
has never been restricted does. A level whose flag is true states its set, and an
empty set is then a decision: it permits nothing. The distinction matters because
the same absence of rows means opposite things on either side of the flag, and a
screen must say which of the two it draws.

The three levels form a chain. A business unit's set is a subset of its party's,
and a book's set is a subset of its business unit's, so a book's set is a subset
of both. The chain is enforced where the sets meet, so no caller can break it: a
row may not name a type its parent does not permit, a parent may not drop a type
a child still carries, and a level may not start restricting while a child
carries a type outside the set it would state.

** Why the chain, and why each level is equal or narrower

A firm's permission to trade something is granted once, to a legal entity, and
delegated downwards as the organisation takes structure: a desk is chartered for
part of what its entity may trade, and a book is opened for part of what its desk
is chartered for. Delegation only narrows; a level that widened would grant a
permission its grantor does not hold, which no approval process allows.

Equality is permitted at every link, and it is the common case. A party with one
desk that trades everything the party may trade states no desk set, and the desk
inherits. A book that carries everything its desk carries states no set of its
own. A level therefore states a set only where it differs from its parent, and
the resolution reads the chain from the leaf upwards until it finds a stated set.

** Why the portfolio tree is not a level

A portfolio is the folder a book sits in. It aggregates books for reporting, it
nests, its rights decide who may see the books under it, and a book is moved
between portfolios as an administrative act — the [[id:130A3261-E788-4222-9BD7-371AD92982FB][Book]] hub and
[[id:A08424B7-E6D7-4993-955C-53AEC241ABA5][Books and portfolios]] state both. Three consequences keep it out of the
matrix:

1. *A move would change a permission.* If a book's set followed its portfolio, a
   reorganisation that moved the book would change what it may trade, with
   nobody approving anything. An approval record a view can move is not a
   control.
2. *The nesting has no settled meaning.* A portfolio tree can be several deep.
   Whether a child portfolio inherits, intersects or overrides its parent is a
   question with several defensible answers and no obvious one, and a control
   cannot rest on an ambiguity.
3. *Its rights answer a different question.* Portfolio rights decide visibility,
   as [[id:C84899BD-5EC8-4AA7-88D7-CE7ED19BC573][book access and permissions]] argues. Visibility is who may look; the
   matrix is what may be traded. A firm grants them in different processes to
   different people.

The desk is the level a firm actually grants, and the model already names it: a
book carries an optional owner business unit, constrained to the units its
portfolio names. The desk is therefore reached without a new link, which is the
other reason it is the right level and the portfolio is not.

** The rules the chain rests on

| Rule | Where it holds |
|------+----------------|
| A level states a set, or states none | The flag on the level's own row |
| A child's set is a subset of its parent's | One trigger per link, on the child's set |
| A parent may not start restricting while a child exceeds the set | A trigger on the parent's flag |
| A type a parent drops must not be carried by a child | A trigger on the parent's set |
| A level that states no set permits every type the catalogue holds | The resolution, which answers no set rather than an empty one |

The catalogue of product types belongs to the trading component, so the
resolution states "every type the catalogue holds" as an absence rather than by
reading the catalogue: a null answer means every type, and an empty array means
none. That keeps the reference data from reading the trades.

** Where the sets come from

A provisioned tenant states its sets as data quality data, beside the records it
stages. The book's set is a junction artefact of the books dataset, and it is the
only level curated: the publish states a desk's set as the union of its books' and
the entity's as the union of its own books', which is the smallest set each level's
children accept and keeps the affinity stated once. The affinity a book's name
implies — cable options in the book that trades cable — is then read both by the
published set and by the generator that fills the book, so the two cannot drift.
