* Goal

A party that subscribes to a provider is routed to exactly the asset classes the
feed it reaches actually serves, so a subscription step never asks for a route the
store refuses and finishes without a warning.

* Acceptance

- The subscription step asks for a route only for an asset class the chosen feed
  serves, and the run records no warning.
- A provider that serves interest rates through one feed and FX through another
  gets both routes, each on the feed that serves it.
- A test covers the case: a provider whose catalogue holds two classes, reached
  through a feed whose scope holds one.

* Plan

1. *Reproduce.* Provision the smokehouse tenant. The step ends
   =completed_with_warnings= and the warning is the store's own refusal:
   =Invalid route: feed afd34349-1c6c-56b2-a686-97b623ee051a serves no ir scope=.
2. *Read the cause.* The step takes the asset class from the authority of every
   =oresmd= address the provider maps. A provider's catalogue is not a feed's
   scope: =derived= maps the FX volatility quotes and, since the term indices
   became ours, interest rate fixing items too, while the feed =derived-fx-vol=
   serves FX alone.
3. *Fix.* The route's class comes from the scopes of the feed the route names,
   intersected with what the provider maps. The feed is already resolved before
   the route is written.
4. *Prove.* Re-provision and watch the step end without a warning; add the test in
   step 3 of the acceptance.

* Notes

- The warning costs the tenant nothing today: the party does not read the derived
  term indices, and its surface published from the FX route. It is still a defect,
  because every future provision records it and a reader cannot tell a harmless
  warning from a load-bearing one.
- The route table enforces the rule, so the store is right and the step is wrong.
  The refusal is on =ores_marketdata_routes_insert_fn=.
