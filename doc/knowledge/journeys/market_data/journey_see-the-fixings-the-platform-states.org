This page documents a [[id:F44766FA-1E9C-416F-849A-E131101B2ECA][user journey]]: A system administrator reads every FX and IR fixing series the platform holds, one row each, to see what it states, where the values came from, and how far each series reaches; a row opens the series page, and a series that holds no day is shown rather than hidden.

A user journey describes what one person is trying to do, before anything is
built. It is a design document that outlives any sprint, so it names the
screens, the entity fields each screen shows, and the operations and messages
each step needs.

* Goal

"What does the platform state, and how current is each part of it?" The journey
is done when the person has seen every fixing series the platform holds, can say
which of them stopped and which never started, and can open the series behind a
row to read its days.

The journey only reads. The platform states what it observed, and the screen
holds no authorisation: a day the platform never stated is a party's to enter,
with an explanation, in its own fixings manager.

*This is the system view, the screen called Fixing observations, not the fixings manager.* The two read the same values
and share nothing else: the system view states what the sources published,
missing days included, and offers no act, while the fixings manager is where a
party requires, adopts, overrides and authorises the fixings its trades use. The
four surfaces and the question each answers are set out in
[[id:5D2A8C41-6E3B-47F0-9A28-1C4B7E9D3F62][The fixing screens and what each one is for]]. A screen that offers to fill a
missing day is the manager wearing this screen's title.

* Actor and trigger

The actor is the super administrator, the person who answers for the
installation rather than for a party. The trigger is a party reporting a fixing
it cannot get, a check that a feed is stating what it should, or the morning
question of whether the overnight curves ran. The journey starts at *Market
data / Fixing observations* and ends when the person leaves the page.

* Steps

1. *Open the fixings.* The person opens *Market data / Fixing observations*. The system
   states every FX and IR fixing series the store holds, one row each: what the
   series fixes, its class, the underlying or pair, the source its values came
   from, the provider that owns it, the value of its latest day, that day, and
   how many days it holds.
2. *Read a row.* The person reads one series: =USD SOFR 1M=, stated by
   =derived-a= under =derived=, at 3.94% on 26 November, 520 days held. A series
   that ended states the last day it reached and the days it holds, and carries
   its last value; nothing is carried forward to a day nobody observed.
3. *Meet a series that holds nothing.* A series the platform holds and never
   stated is a row, and it says =no day= with a count of zero rather than
   disappearing. It is the state the person is often looking for.
4. *Narrow to a class.* The person asks for the IR series alone, or the FX
   series alone, and the list answers that class.
5. *Narrow to a currency.* The person asks for one currency. An FX series is
   listed under either of the two currencies it names: =EUR/USD= answers both
   =EUR= and =USD=.
6. *Narrow to a source.* The person asks for the series one source states, such
   as =ecb-frankfurter= or =derived-a=, and reads how many series it fills and
   how far each reaches.
7. *Search.* The person types part of a pair, an index, a source or a provider,
   and the list narrows to the rows that name it.
8. *Open the series.* The person opens a row, and the fixing series page shows
   that series by the name the row gave it, under the same trail: *Home › Market
   data › Fixing observations › USD SOFR 1M*. It shows the days, the values and
   the feeds that carry it, and its way back is the list. The list is the way
   in; it builds no second reading.
9. *Leave.* The address holds the filters, so the person can send the exact view
   to someone else.

* Screens and wireframes

One read-only list. The filters come first, then the rows, then a legend that
says what the two states mean, so a person never reads a row without knowing
what an empty one is.

The steps keep no state across a reply, so no workflow is needed.

The owner signed off this journey and its prototype on 2026-11-26, subject
to two changes made the same day: the screen is called *Fixing observations*,
and the series page draws the values of the series it opens. The sign-off
followed the reading of
[[id:5D2A8C41-6E3B-47F0-9A28-1C4B7E9D3F62][The fixing screens and what each one is for]], which put this screen apart from
the fixings manager.

* Operations and messages

#+ATTR_HTML: :class hug-leading
| Step | Operation | Subject | Status |
|------+-----------+---------+--------|
| Open the fixings | the series with their latest day, count, source and value | =marketdata.v1.ops.list_fixing_series_summaries= | exists |
| Open the series | one item's series, its values and the feeds that carry it | =GET /api/marketdata/fixing-series?uri=...= | exists |
| Narrow to a class, a currency, a source | the same read, filtered | the same operation, with =asset_class=, =currency= and =source= | exists |

The read is the one the task built: the aggregate is the database's, and a
series that holds no value is one of its rows with a count of zero.

* What is missing

- A rhythm per series, so the screen could say a series is *late* rather than
  only *last stated on a day*. The prototype leaves that out and states the
  latest day, which a person judges against the series' own rhythm.
- The count of days a series is missing since the day it should have stated,
  which follows from the same rhythm.
