* Acceptance

- Every synthetic protocol header has a committed TypeScript twin: 12 protocol
  modules under =packages/wire-protocol/src/generated/synthetic/protocol/=
  and the 9 domain modules they import.
- Every one of those twins is generated from an org model. The four non-entity
  operation surfaces are operation models, so no hand-written protocol header
  remains in the component.
- =check_protocol_twin_coverage.py= passes with synthetic among the covered
  components.
- =check_component_drift.py --component synthetic= regenerates every address
  and reports no drift, so a twin cannot go stale silently.

* Plan

Three steps, in the order the gates forced them.

** Generate the entity twins

The nine entity models produce 9 protocol twins and, because each protocol twin
imports its entity's interface, the =ores.ts.domain= facet produces the 9 domain
twins they name. The protocol twin alone does not compile: the domain facet is
part of the same job.

** Model the four operation surfaces

Committing the twins is what makes the twin gate cover the component, and the
gate then demands a twin for =every= =*_protocol.hpp=, not only the entity
ones. Four hand-written headers had none:
=feed_config_protocol.hpp=, =simulate_fx_spot_paths_protocol.hpp=,
=simulate_ir_curve_paths_protocol.hpp= and =preview_ir_curve_shape_protocol.hpp=.
They are the operation surfaces the clean standard's P04 recorded as staying
hand-written. The estate has two precedents, and =iam= is the other one: its
non-entity operation surfaces are operation models
(=ores.iam.*_operations.org=, =ores.iam.*_messages.org=) whose C++ header and
TypeScript twin both generate.

We took the iam precedent. Modelling them is the only way both sides generate
from one source, and a hand-written TypeScript twin would have mirrored a
hand-written C++ header with nothing keeping the two in step. This supersedes
the P04 exception for this component.

=parameter_spec= is carried by both the simulate and the preview request, and a
payload type is resolved inside its own protocol, so it cannot be declared in
two models without either duplicating the C++ struct or leaving the TypeScript
side naming a type no module exports. The two IR operations therefore share one
model, =ores.synthetic.ir_curve_operations=, and one generated header.

The batch bounds stay out of the protocol. =max_simulate_num_ticks=,
=max_simulate_num_paths= and =max_preview_curve_entries= are the service's
clamps, not wire fields, so =simulate_paths_common.hpp= remains a hand-written
header and the request structs no longer alias two of them as unused members.

**Migrate the consumers and regenerate

Eight consumer files re-point at the merged header, =component_files.cmake= is
regenerated from the tree scan, and the two superseded headers are deleted.

* Notes

- The scaffold's acceptance was wrong twice, and both corrections are worth
  keeping. It said nine twins, but the request that proved it named only the
  =ores.ts.protocol= address: the domain facet is required too, because a
  protocol twin imports its entity's interface.
- The scaffold also named the wrong blocker: it said the models needed the
  =* Physical space= address mechanism before their TypeScript could be
  generated. They do not, and a targeted run created all nine entity twins from
  the models as they stood.
- The drift gate needed no change. =_committed_ts_facets= scopes a TypeScript
  facet by whether its output directory exists, so committing the twins puts
  =ores.ts.domain=, =ores.ts.protocol= and =ores.ts.trade_type= into the address
  set on their own.
- An earlier claim that the drift gate cannot see untracked generated files was
  wrong. It diffs the tree with =git diff=, but it also records the untracked set
  before regenerating and fails on anything regeneration materialises
  (check_component_drift.py, "untracked file(s) materialized by regeneration").
  The gate that let M06 hide is the twin gate's scoping, not this one: both
  gates agree there is nothing to check until a component's first twin lands.
- =component_registry.py= carried a stale paragraph saying synthetic "is not
  listed yet" while the tuple lists it. Corrected here to record the M06
  closure and the four items still open.
- clever_dijkstra owns the clean-standard record, =task_clean_synthetic.org=,
  and its M06, P04, V05 and V06 rows still read as open or held. The file is on
  main, so the rows were struck here rather than handed over in a message:
  M06 is closed, P04 records that the operation surfaces are modelled now, and
  V05 and V06 drop the twin from their open lists. This is the one file this
  branch edits that another environment owns.
- V06's row claimed =COMPONENTS_UNDER_TEST= stays =("iam",)=, which is false:
  the tuple carries =synthetic=. Corrected to say that the registry lists a
  component with four items still open. That disagreement is a gate question in
  its own right -- the registry asserts every item passes while the record says
  four do not, and nothing cross-checks the two -- but it is out of scope here.
- Three gate failures surfaced on the merged tree and none was synthetic's.
  =format= was the one exception: the new line in =ir_curve_preview_handler.hpp=
  needed clang-format. The other two came from =79e1123446= ("Serve the
  sub-periods coupon type list"), which added a refdata entity but left the
  hand-written composition points behind: the service event registrar and the
  shell refdata composite each needed one include and one call. That left
  =codegen-tests= red on two cases, and the same refdata work had left
  =ores_schema.{refdata,trading}.puml= and the index stale. All three were fixed
  on this branch, because the story cannot land through a red gate and each fix
  is the one the check names. The diagrams were regenerated without the
  timestamp-only churn the generator writes into all 22 files, and the SVG
  redraws for diagrams whose source had not changed were left alone because the
  render is not byte-stable.

* Result

Synthetic speaks TypeScript. 21 generated modules are committed: the 9 domain
interfaces and the 12 protocol modules, each protocol module the twin of a
generated C++ header. The four hand-written operation headers are gone, replaced
by three operation models from which both sides generate, and the two IR
operation surfaces share one model so =parameter_spec= is declared once. The
drift gate regenerates every address and reports no drift, and the twin gate now
covers the component. The M06 item on the clean standard is closed; P04 is
superseded for synthetic.

Verified on the merged tree: the whole tree builds, =compass test run --
-R synthetic= passes 4 of 4 (=ores.synthetic.api=, =.core=, =.service= and
=ores.shell.synthetic=), =check_protocol_twin_coverage.py= reports 418 headers
in 18 components with no opt-out, and =check_component_drift.py --component
synthetic= reports no drift. Three gate failures on the merged tree were fixed
here; two were trunk's from =79e1123446=, and the third was a clang-format
tweak.
