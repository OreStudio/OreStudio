#+title: The IR curve template and its parameters
#+description: The tenor and instrument ladder, the pillar ticks the resolver derives from it, and the named parameter values resolved against the definition catalogue.
#+type: knowledge
#+level: cross
#+filetags: :synthetic:config:ir:process:
#+created: 2026-10-06
#+updated: 2026-10-06

* Summary

An =ir_curve_generation_config= carries two sets of child rows. The
instrument ladder is held as =ir_curve_template_entry= rows, declared in
=ores.synthetic.api/domain/ir_curve_template_entry.hpp=. The short-rate
parameters are held as
=ir_curve_generation_config_process_parameter_value= rows, declared in
=ores.synthetic.api/domain/ir_curve_generation_config_process_parameter_value.hpp=,
against the vocabulary in
=ores.synthetic.api/domain/yield_curve_process_parameter_definition.hpp=.

* Detail

** The template entry

One row is one point of the grid the curve publishes. It holds a
=sequence_index= running from the short end to the long end, a
=start_tenor_code=, an =end_tenor_code= and an =instrument_code= that names
how the point is priced.

Every entry is modelled as a genuine half-open period rather than a single
maturity label.

- A point instrument, which is a deposit or a swap, sets =start_tenor_code=
  to ="SPOT"=. That is a real zero-duration tenor in the catalogue that
  resolves to the spot date, and not a null or a sentinel.
- An interval instrument, which is a forward rate agreement, sets
  =start_tenor_code= to its own front tenor. A three by six FRA therefore
  carries ="3M"= and ="6M"=.

The symmetric shape is what lets =validate_curve_template= in
=ores.synthetic.api/src/domain/curve_template_validation.cpp= detect a real
period overlap with a plain =windows_overlap= test. That helper comes from
=ores::refdata::domain= in =tenor_resolution.hpp=. A point-versus-interval
special case would otherwise be needed.

** From the ladder to pillar ticks

=resolve()= in
=ores.synthetic.api/feeds/ir_curve_template_resolver.cpp= turns each entry
into an =ir_curve_resolved_entry=. That record carries the entry's start and
end as calendar dates, the count of ticks ahead of the horizon for each, an
accrual year fraction, and, for a swap, the fixed-leg schedule of payment
dates and accrual fractions the par-rate solver needs.

The refdata inputs are gathered once by =build_ir_curve_refdata_context=.
That call reads the tenor, instrument code, payment frequency and tenor
convention resolution catalogues, and it additionally reads the event store
when the convention is the FOMC schedule walk. The tick loop never touches
the catalogues again, which is the same resolve-once-tick-many shape the FX
feed uses for its process and its subject.

The resolver's simplifications are deliberate and they are the facts a reader
needs. One tick is one calendar day. Spot equals the horizon. Year fractions
use actual over 365 fixed. There is no business-day calendar and no
currency-specific spot lag.

** The parameter value and its definition

A parameter is split across two rows.

- The value row holds =config_id=, =parameter_definition_id= and
  =parameter_value=. The pair of the config and the definition is unique, so
  a parameter appears at most once per config. A hand-written trigger
  enforces that the definition's =process_type_code= matches the config's
  =process_type=, which the codegen single-argument mechanism cannot express.
- The definition row holds =process_type_code=, =parameter_name=,
  =display_name=, an optional =symbol=, =short_label=, a rich =description=,
  =data_type=, =default_value=, optional =min_value= and =max_value=, and a
  =display_order=. Definitions are system-tenant reference data and tenant
  users never edit them.

=map_parameters_to_yield_curve_process= in
=ores.synthetic.api/src/domain/yield_curve_process_parameter_mapping.cpp=
joins the two vectors and constructs the typed process. It rejects a value
row whose definition belongs to another process type, a duplicate parameter, a
missing required parameter, an unexpected parameter and a value outside its
definition's bounds.

** The facts the code shape does not show

The template rows carry no rate and no noise. Every published rate is derived
from the parent process's =discount_factor()= at the entry's maturity, so a
published batch is by construction a slice of one internally consistent
curve. Nothing in the grid can be independently wiggled.

=default_value= is not a fallback. The mapping layer throws on a missing
parameter rather than substituting the default, and the default exists only
to pre-fill a new row in the editor.
