* Goal

No feed the platform reads offers a volatility quote, for any asset class. The vendor
feeds serve rates, spots and fixings; the only volatility the store holds is what the
simulator writes itself, and the derived quotes the platform computes. The swap and cap books
are the sharpest case, because ORE already names their volatility blocks as components of
today's market and nothing has ever written them.

So the platform measures the volatility it needs. A class derives it from a history the
platform holds: a window as long as the pillar, the moments of a return series, a level
multiplier, a shape shrunk to a prior, and the reach the sample supports. That measurement
is the same work in every asset class, and only three things differ: the transform that
turns the series into a return, the coordinates a quote is named by, and the smile model.
They are parameters of one engine, and the engine is the first deliverable here.

Interest rates is the first class to run the whole chain, because it is the class whose
trades the seed holds and whose volatility block ORE waits for. The story derives a
currency's normal volatilities from its rate history, simulates a latent cube with vendor
views of it, calibrates a party's surface from those views, publishes the set through the
lifecycle, and prices a swaption book from the result.

The story is also the test of two claims the system makes: that a kind is a row in a
table and not a branch in the code, and that a derivation is a transform, a coordinate set
and a smile model. The FX surface was the first instance of the first claim. Interest rates
is the second instance of both.

* Acceptance

- One engine derives volatility. The only inputs that differ by asset class are the
  transform, the coordinates a quote is named by and the smile model, and no other part of
  the engine names an asset class.
- A class with no history is refused by name, and the refusal states the series it lacks
  and the returns a pillar needs. The inventory says which classes can be served now.
- The FX quotes the derivation produced before the lift are the ones it produces after it,
  cell for cell, over the same history.
- A currency and an as-of answer the normal volatility of every expiry and term, and the
  answer names the series, the window, the multiplier and the model it read.
- A derived value is stored under =orestudio= with internal standing: no non-system party
  reads it and no meter counts it. It states the method and the version that computed it.
- A latent cube and each vendor's view of it exist for a currency, and the difference
  between a view and the cube is the noise its contributor added.
- A party calibrates a surface from the views it holds, and the fit states its errors, its
  arbitrage checks and the pillars no contributor quoted.
- The publish and the withdrawal of the surface take the steps the FX surface takes, no
  step branches on the asset class, and the kind is a row in the kind table.
- A book of swaptions or caps prices from the published surface, and a report states the
  trades it priced and the market it lacked.
- Every part the audit names generic is used unchanged, and every part it names FX only is
  parameterised or stated as a limitation.

* Decisions

- *No feed offers a volatility quote, for any asset class.* The only volatility the store
  holds is what the simulator wrote itself, and the derived FX quotes the platform computes.
  This is the premise of the story: a volatility is measured here or not at all, so the
  derivation is a platform capability and not an interest rate feature.
- *The same problem has one answer, for every class.* The measurement is shared and the
  class supplies three parameters: the transform (a log return for a series that stays
  positive, a difference for a rate that may be negative), the coordinates a quote is named
  by (a pair with an expiry and a delta label; or a currency with an expiry, a term and a
  strike), and the smile model. The window rule, the moments, the multiplier, the shrink to
  a prior and the reach belong to the engine and name no class. The method and its version
  belong to the class's computation, because a different computation is a different method;
  the stamping of a series with them is the store's and is generic.
- *Every derived value explains itself.* A derivation method is data: its code, its version, the
  assumptions it rests on, what it fits and what it does not. The note is shown beside the value on
  the page that shows the value, versioned with the method, so a reader learns the limits of a
  simulated surface from the page and not from a review of the code. A method with no note cannot
  write a value.
- *A class's derivation is a descriptor and a module, one shape for every class.* The method and its
  version, the transform, the coordinates, the smile model, the projection and the note are declared
  in one place, and the emitter, the batch runner and the page read that declaration. They never
  branch on the asset class, and adding a class adds no place where a class is named.
- *A derivation runs over sets, not one series.* The platform quotes every interest rate series to
  build every surface, so the batch takes many series and returns flat contiguous outputs, with no
  mutable state shared between series and a measured cost over the whole seed. A per-series call
  with a vector of cells is the wrong shape for the job, and the shape is a requirement rather than
  an optimisation to do later.
- *The batched pass is kept, and pinned.* One pass over a grid writes the same rules a second
  time over prefix sums, because a day of a grid must cost the same whether its window is a
  week or ten years. The test that the grid equals the day-at-a-time answer is what holds the
  two together, and folding the pass into the engine would trade that test for a slower read.
- *The lift comes before any interest rate value exists.* A second copy of the measurement
  is the fault this story exists to avoid, so the FX derivation is lifted first, and its
  values must come out as they were. The lift is also the cheaper half: the FX derivation
  already holds the window rule, the multiplier, the shape prior and the reach.
- *Interest rates is the first class through the whole chain, and the others follow.* The
  seed holds 10 books of swaptions, 10 of caps and 2 of straddles, and ORE's today's market
  names =SwaptionVolatilities= and =CapFloorVolatilities= as components nothing writes. The
  inventory names what equity, credit, commodity and inflation need; this story builds none
  of them, and a class is added later as three parameters and a series.
- *The first interest rate value is measured from a rate history, not from a process
  parameter.* The generated curve of a currency is already calibrated to the public rates,
  and its short-rate process carries the shape the measurement is projected through. The
  derivation reads the history directly, so a stored value states the series it came from.
- *The quote layer already serves this asset class.* The datum schema reads an ORE
  =SWAPTION= and =CAPFLOOR= key, in ATM and Smile form, with normal, lognormal and
  shifted-lognormal quote types and the shift that goes with them. The classification rules
  place both in =interest_rates= as volatility, and the ingest stores them through the same
  key grammar as every other datum. The story adds the derived object and the simulator; it
  does not rebuild the quote layer.
- *The cube starts where the curve is.* Its ATM level at the as-of is the derived
  volatility, so the cube and the curve agree at the start of a run and the smile is the
  only part the cube adds. A cube whose level drifts away from the curve it prices against
  is a fault the audit must be able to see.
- *A swaption surface is a cube, not a sheet.* A quote states an expiry and a term, and its
  smile adds a strike. The FX page's expiry-by-delta heatmap is one slice of this object, so
  the page renders the slices the quotes declare, and the class adds no page.
- *The production page stops at the quotes, and the party's fit has its own screen.* The
  calibration records its errors, its checks and its missing pillars, and the party's
  screens read them from the record: a person learns to calibrate by taking quotes as an
  input, whichever source they came from. A calibration debugger inside the generator would
  serve neither the platform developer nor the party.
- *Normal volatility first, and the volatility type stays a column.* The swaption market
  quotes basis-point volatility and ORE reads either type, so the first surface states
  normal volatility, and a shifted-lognormal surface is another row of the same kind rather
  than another code path.
- *The screen comes last, and it must not need a change when the class changes.* The
  production is watchable in the store without a page, and the page needs the FX page to
  exist first. When it is pointed at interest rates, the test is that only the data
  changes: the axes and their labels come from the surface's description, and the page
  names no asset class. A branch the page needs for a class is a fault in the description
  or in the read, and the fix belongs there.

* Out of scope

- Volatility for equity, credit, commodity and inflation. The inventory says what each one
  needs; the story builds none of them.
- The choice of smile model, which the calibration task settles, and the choice of
  volatility type, which the audit settles before a quote is derived.
- Re-filing the derived FX quotes under an owner.
- New pricing engines. The story prices with the configuration the engines already name.
