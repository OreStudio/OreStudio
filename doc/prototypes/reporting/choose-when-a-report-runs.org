* Summary

This prototype argues where scheduling lives: on the report, in a schedule list,
or on a calendar. It carries three structural variants switched with =?variant=,
and it answers to
[[file:../../knowledge/journeys/reporting/journey_choose_when_a_report_runs.org][Choose when a report runs]].

It is throwaway. It reads no service, and the page says plainly which parts of a
scheduler it cannot show.

* Detail

** The question

A schedule is one field on a report definition, but "what runs tomorrow morning"
is a question about the whole set. Those two readings want different screens, and
the open question is which one earns the space.

** The three variants

Switch with the bottom bar, or with =?variant=.

| Variant | Name | The structural argument |
|---------+------+-------------------------|
| =A= | On the report | Scheduling is a field group on the report: a cadence and a time, edited in place, with the next fire times under it. |
| =B= | A schedule list | One row per schedule, scanned and edited together, so the whole week is answerable in one view. |
| =C= | A calendar | A weekly grid of when reports fire, each report in its slots, so clashes and quiet nights are visible before they happen. |

** What the page refuses to invent

Two operations exist, =reporting.v1.ops.schedule_report_definitions= and
=reporting.v1.ops.unschedule_report_definitions=, and a definition carries a
schedule expression. **There is no operation to read or write a scheduler job
from this screen, and there is no time zone on the schedule.** The calendar
variant shows an installation clock and says the times are derived from the
expression, and the note says a built screen could not show a job's state, its
last firing or its next due time. A scheduler dashboard drawn as though the
server served it would be a lie a reviewer could not see.

** What a schedule does and does not reach

It sets when the run starts. It does not change the report's inputs, its
portfolio or its outputs. Only a tenant administrator may schedule, and only for
reports their own party owns; every other role reads the cadence and nothing more.
The page states the party constraint rather than hiding it behind a disabled
button.

** What is fake

The cadences, the next fire times and the reports are mock. Nothing reads a
server, and the application has no reporting UI today. The next fire times are
computed from the expression in the page, which is what a person is actually
judging when they set a cadence.

** Where the code lives

=doc/prototypes/reporting/choose-when-a-report-runs/=. Three files, plain HTML,
CSS and JavaScript, no framework and no build step.

** The verdict

None yet. This page is on the branch that proposes it and waits for review.

** What it does not settle

The time zone, which the schedule does not carry at all. Until it does, a
calendar that draws a local week is drawing a promise the server cannot keep.
