* Goal

A risk or reference data administrator reads and changes the product approval matrix from the web, at the party and at the book. The set a party permits and the set a book narrows are legible together, an attempt to widen a book past its party is refused in the person's own words, and the screen draws what the standard already draws rather than a shape of its own.

* Decisions

Settled on 2026-11-25, when the story was formed.

1. *The arc is the screen pipeline.* The four steps are the four tasks, in the
   order of [[id:61C6B0A5-03A5-4424-8EA3-EE7E587629FD][Take a topic through the screen pipeline]]: extract and judge
   the journeys, prototype, build, test and sign off. Each step ends in a
   document the owner signs off before the next begins.
2. *This is not a reference data topic.* The topics of
   [[id:C0D1A833-3E57-489A-A4D3-8C44266D3D6D][The screen pipeline topics]] are the Qt entities the web has yet to
   carry. The approval matrix is new: its sets and the rule between them
   arrived with [[id:F77263EF-6F2D-4129-BA60-CA6C2F2D6153][this story]]'s
   predecessor, so it takes the pipeline's steps without a row in that table.
3. *Two subjects, one reading.* The party's set is the maximum and the book's
   set narrows it, so the journeys must let a person see both at once or move
   between them without losing the comparison. Whether that is a tab of the
   party, a panel of the book, or a screen of its own is the journeys' to
   decide, and the prototype's job is to show the decision.
4. *The refusal is the screen's to state.* The database already refuses a book
   outside its party, with the party's set in the message. The screen must not
   let the attempt leave the page in the first place, and must read the
   database's refusal when it arrives.

* Decided at the sign off

The owner signed the journey off on 2026-11-25. What that settles:

1. *The journey is accepted as written.* One journey over the party's tab and
   the book's panel, with the nine steps and the judgement table it carries.
2. *The four placements stay open for the prototype.* Where the set lives, how
   the two sets read together, what a party that states nothing looks like, and
   where a refusal stands are the prototype's to draw and the owner's to
   refuse or accept.
3. *Two gaps are the server's and not the screens'.* No operation answers the
   set a book may carry, and a refused write does not carry the party's set in
   its words. Both are captures in the product backlog, and the build task
   reads them rather than working around them.

5. *Both surfaces answer the screens.* =ores.http= is replacing the BFF, and
   until it does, every resource the screens read or write is answered by both,
   as [[id:34CB899A-D114-4B54-9A0E-E3BEE5FF49F2][the route inventory]] measures. The approval
   matrix is no exception: the two junctions take the =http-route= facet the
   other junctions take, and the build task's acceptance names the parity.

6. *Portfolios are not a level of the matrix.* A portfolio is the folder a
   book sits in: it aggregates, it nests, and its rights decide who may see the
   books under it, not what those books may trade. It is also mutable, since a
   book can be moved to another portfolio, so a mandate hanging off it would
   change what a book may trade on a reorganisation without anybody approving
   anything. The sets therefore sit at the legal entity and at the book, which
   is where authority sits in a trading firm, and the matrix ignores the
   portfolio tree.
   The idiomatic finer grant, if one is wanted later, is the *business unit*,
   not the portfolio: a book already names an optional owner unit, constrained
   to the units its portfolio names, and desks rather than folders are what a
   firm grants a mandate to. That addition is one junction and one resolution
   step when a desk needs its own set; it needs no change to what exists.
