* Summary

A security identifier names one tradeable instrument: a specific bond
issue, a specific listed share. Three schemes carry most of the
traffic. ISIN is the international standard, CUSIP covers the United
States and Canada, and SEDOL covers the United Kingdom and Ireland.

These codes identify the security, which is one of the three scopes in
[[id:AB71AFF8-0B08-4610-9F12-C5A84D8AB892][modelling a trade]]. They say nothing about a deal, and
[[id:E080E32B-5E53-4AB7-903F-145D070C045C][trade identifiers]] answer that separate question.

* ISIN

ISO 6166 defines the International Securities Identification Number.
An ISIN is twelve characters: a two-letter country code from ISO
3166-1, a nine-character national security identifier, and one check
digit.

A National Numbering Agency assigns ISINs for its own jurisdiction,
and the Association of National Numbering Agencies coordinates the
agencies. The country prefix therefore records which agency issued the
code rather than where the security trades. Securities issued into the
international market through Euroclear or Clearstream carry the
prefix =XS=, which belongs to no single country.

An ISIN identifies the security and not the listing. The same ISIN
appears on every venue where the security trades, so an ISIN alone
cannot say which venue a price came from.
[[id:F3010EA7-6A0E-405C-81FA-1EF051749B5E][External market data identifiers]] covers the schemes that answer that
question.

* CUSIP

The Committee on Uniform Security Identification Procedures gives the
scheme its name. A CUSIP is nine characters: six identifying the
issuer, two identifying the issue, and one check digit. It covers the
United States and Canada, and CUSIP Global Services assigns codes on
behalf of the American Bankers Association.

A CUSIP is the national security identifier inside a United States
ISIN, so the two codes carry the same nine characters, and the ISIN
adds the country prefix and its own check digit. The CINS extension
applies the same format to securities outside North America.

* SEDOL

The Stock Exchange Daily Official List gives the scheme its name. A
SEDOL is seven alphanumeric characters, the last of which is a check
digit. The London Stock Exchange assigns SEDOLs and acts as the
National Numbering Agency for the United Kingdom and Ireland, so a
SEDOL forms the national security identifier inside a =GB= ISIN.

Unlike an ISIN, a SEDOL is assigned per listing rather than per
security, so one security listed on several venues has several SEDOLs.

* Which code a bond carries

A bond issue is the case where these codes do the most work. An issuer
sells a tranche of debt under fixed terms, and the ISIN is what the
market quotes and settles against. [[id:9754A325-F6FB-4F44-AB56-660D980A3F11][Bond issuance]] sets out the
mechanics of the issue itself.

Issuers re-open existing lines rather than always creating new ones. A
re-opened tranche carries the ISIN of the line it joins, which is why
an ISIN identifies a security rather than a single sale event.

* What the model holds

ORE names a security through =ReferenceData=. A bond trade points at
its reference datum through =BondData/SecurityId=, and the value there
is the security identifier.

We do not yet hold a coding scheme registry for security identifiers.
The data quality coding scheme table registers party identifiers, such
as LEI, BIC and MIC, and the securities schemes on this page have no
equivalent entry.

* Sources

The three schemes are described from their defining standards and from
the assigning bodies' published material: ISO 6166 for ISIN, CUSIP
Global Services for CUSIP, and the London Stock Exchange for SEDOL.
This page cites no edition or clause for any of them.
