* Goal

A clickable prototype of the registration door that the design can be judged against, on a throwaway page with mock data. It renders the five states the target state defines and argues three structural answers to the one question that matters: how a person learns, before they submit, that their new account may not be usable until an administrator acts.

* Acceptance

- The door renders all five states: closed, the form, a refusal, a usable account and a waiting account.
- Three variants disagree about structure rather than colour or copy.
- The difference between a usable account and a waiting one is visible before the person submits, on every variant.
- Every variant is reachable by URL, so a variant can be linked to a reviewer.
- The prototype is published beside its description and the other prototypes, and it reads no service.

* Plan

1. Take the five states from the target state, and the mock policy from its policy-read table.
2. Choose three structurally different shapes, not three skins: one panel, a form beside a live status column, and a step rail.
3. Build it as a static page under =doc/prototypes/registration-door/=, which is how the tree keeps throwaway prototypes: plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript, no framework and no build step.
4. Publish it with the site build so a reviewer can open it beside the trade entry prototype.
5. Hand it over for the verdict, and record which variant won and what was taken from the others.

* Result

The prototype is [[id:6E98150F-FED9-42ED-B51F-8C7A099C7273][Registration door prototype]], at =doc/prototypes/registration-door/= and published on the site.

Three variants: *A* one panel, with the destination below the form and the outcome replacing it; *B* the form beside a live status column that states the tenant, the party, the role and whether the account will work at once; *C* the step rail the bootstrap screens use, with the write deferred to a review step. Five states: closed, the form, a refusal, a usable account and a waiting one, with a nomination toggle that turns the same submission into either outcome.

The verdict is not in yet. Until it is, the task stays open and no variant is treated as chosen.
