* Summary

The reporting line is one field on the account: =reports_to_account_id=, a
nullable soft self-reference. The shape only appears when somebody holds it in
their head, and the journey is the screen that draws it and lets an
administrator correct it.

The prototype reads the ACME demo dataset — 58 accounts, their job titles and
their managers — lays it out three ways, and states every gap the journey
records.

It is throwaway. It is not production code, it reads no service, and every value
on it is mock data.

Open the [[file:reporting-lines/][prototype]].

* Detail

** The verdict

*Not yet signed off.* The recommendation is variant A, an indented tree with the
selected person beside it.

** The three variants

| Variant | Shape | What it settles | What it gives up |
|---------+-------+-----------------+------------------|
| A · Tree | An indented tree with expand and collapse, and the selected person in a panel beside it | Depth and branches are plain, the whole tenant stays on one screen, and the controls have a fixed place | It is not an organisation chart to the eye |
| B · Chart | Boxes joined by connector lines | It reads as an org chart, which is what an administrator often means by "the shape" | It stops working at a few hundred people and on a narrow window |
| C · Table | One row per person, indented by depth, with a manager column | It is the easiest to search and to read a manager from | The shape has to be inferred from the indentation |

** The declared gaps the screen states

1. *There is no tree read.* The server answers one flat page of accounts, so
   the page nests the flat list in the browser. A large tenant cannot page a
   branch, and the whole roster must arrive first.
2. *The server accepts a loop.* Nothing refuses =A reports to B= when =B=
   already reports to =A=. The manager picker leaves out every person who
   reports to the selected one, directly or through others, and the panel says
   that the screen is doing the check the server does not.
3. *There is no reporting-line-only write.* One field means the whole
   =update_account_request=, so a concurrent change to another field is
   overwritten by the copy the page read.
4. *No office can be named.* The card shows the party a person works in, and
   the association read carries =party_id= alone. Office values are marked in
   amber on the page.
5. *The line has no history here.* The account is versioned and the screen
   states none of it.
6. *There is no approval.* The journey makes a change a proposal that a
   hierarchical superior or a tenant administrator answers, and nothing holds
   one: no request, no queue, no notification and no decision. The *A proposal*
   case draws who decides and states that the line does not move.

** The cases the bar switches

- *Clean tree* is the ordinary case.
- *No lines* is the empty state: nobody reports to anyone, every person is a
  root, and each is offered as a manager.
- *A proposal* is the waiting case: one person asked to move, and the platform
  cannot hold the answer.

** Why the tree is drawn from a flat read

The journey document says the tree is assembled by the client from a flat read,
not fetched as a tree. This prototype does exactly that, so the cost is visible:
the whole roster arrives before anything is drawn, and a tenant the size of a
large bank would send every account to draw one branch. The tree read the
journey proposes is the fix, and the prototype argues for it rather than hiding
the problem.
