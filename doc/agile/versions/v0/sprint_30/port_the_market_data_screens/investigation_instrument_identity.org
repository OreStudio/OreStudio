A point-in-time record, written while the story that commissioned it was open, of an [[id:A7F8D9E2-3C4B-4A5D-B67C-8E9F0A1B2C3D][investigation]] into how the five screens' instrument kinds map onto ORE Studio's datum schema, identity fields, coordinate fields and the subject, and what that means for the instrument catalogue, the ladder and the surface contracts.

It is a snapshot, not a description of the system. Whatever it concludes that the code must keep doing belongs on the page that owns the subject; cite that page from code, recipes and models, not this one.

* Context

Hedgr's specification leaves the catalogue's grouping rule as its first open
decision: derive a grouping rule, or keep a server-side derivation table. The
page also assumes a provider's ladder is *several series*, one per forward start
and tenor, which a client must join.

The port needs to know which is true in ORE Studio before anyone writes the
catalogue, the ladder or the surface read. A wrong grouping rule produces a
catalogue that invents instruments, which is the failure the screens exist to
prevent.

* Methodology

Read =projects/ores.marketdata/api/include/ores.marketdata.api/datum/schema.hpp=
(the per-instrument-type field lists, their roles and each row's subject) and
=datum/market_datum.hpp= (the datum, its series and its coordinates). Then
checked the result against the running deployment's own rows:

#+begin_src sql
select provider_code, count(*) from ores_marketdata_market_series_tbl
 where valid_to = ores_utility_infinity_timestamp_fn()
 group by provider_code;
#+end_src

* Paths taken

** The schema is already the derivation table

=schema.hpp= states, per instrument type, an ordered list of =field_spec { name,
role, may_be_none }= where the role is =identity= or =coordinate=, plus the
row's =subject= and =asset_class=. A datum's series is exactly its identity
fields; a datum's point is its identity fields plus its coordinates
(=datum::series_of= and =datum::datum_at_point= enforce this).

So the grouping rule, the axes and their order are not decisions to invent: they
are =schema_of(type).fields= filtered by role. The specification's "DECIDE" is
answered by the schema, and a second derivation table would be a copy that
drifts.

** A ladder is one series, not several

The =ir_swap= row is:

: identity:   ccy, index_name (may be none), fwd_start, tenor
: coordinate: term

Read against the deployment, the series is spelled
=oresmd://ir/AUD?type=series&instrument=ir_swap&quote=rate&fwd_start=0D&tenor=1D=
and =term= is the rung. So one series holds the whole ladder across ON, 1W,
1M … 30Y, and the rungs are coordinates of that one series.

That contradicts the specification's assumption that a provider's ladder is
several series that a client joins. In ORE Studio the join does not exist: a
ladder is one series read across its =term= axis. The provider dimension is a
different thing entirely (below).

** The provider is on the series row, not in the datum

=market_series= carries =provider_code=; the datum does not. So two providers'
quotes of one ladder are two series rows with the same URI and different
=provider_code=, and the "one column per provider" of the prototype is a join on
that column, not a difference in identity fields.

** The party is the dimension that multiplies rows

The deployment holds five current rows for one URI and provider:

#+begin_src sql
select oresmd_uri, provider_code, count(*) rows, count(distinct party_id) parties
  from ores_marketdata_market_series_tbl
 where valid_to = ores_utility_infinity_timestamp_fn()
   and oresmd_uri like '%instrument=fx_spot%ccy=USD'
 group by 1, 2;
-- oresmd://fx/AUD?...&ccy=USD | bloomberg | 4 rows | 4 parties
-- oresmd://fx/AUD?...&ccy=USD | ore       | 5 rows | 5 parties
#+end_src

That is the "five AUD-AONIA copies" hedgr reported: five parties, one series
each. Party scoping is therefore not a nice-to-have filter; without it the
catalogue shows five of everything.

** What each kind is, in schema terms

#+ATTR_HTML: :class hug-leading
| Kind | Instrument type | Coordinates | Subject | Note |
|------+-----------------+-------------+---------+------|
| pair | =fx_spot= | none | =unit_ccy= (base) | =ccy= is the quote side, so both sides are in the identity |
| curve | =discount=, =zero= | =term= | =ccy= | =curve_id= names it (AUD-AONIA) |
| ladder | =ir_swap=, =mm=, =fra=, =basis_swap= | =term= | =ccy= | =fwd_start= and =tenor= are identity, so they distinguish peers |
| surface | =fx_option= | =expiry=, =strike_label= | =unit_ccy= | pair in the identity, smile in the coordinates |
| surface (published) | =fx_vol_surface= | =expiry=, =strike= | =unit_ccy= | =surface_id= names it |
| fixing | not a datum | — | — | =ores_marketdata_market_fixings_tbl=, a separate table |

The declaration order of a type's coordinates is the draw order, which is what
the screens need for =axes[].values=.

* Conclusions

1. *The grouping rule is the schema.* An instrument is one series identity for
   one party: the type, the quote type and the identity fields the schema
   declares. Its axes are the schema's coordinate fields, in declaration order.
   Its sources are the =market_series= rows carrying that series, one per
   provider. Nothing needs to be derived, and no derivation table should be
   written.
2. *hedgr's ladder assumption does not hold here.* A ladder is one series
   sliced by =term=, so =get_rate_ladder= does not join series across rungs. It
   reads the provider's series and states the rungs; the provider columns are
   several *series rows* of one identity.
3. *The party is the whole scoping story.* One series URI exists once per party;
   a read that omits the party multiplies the catalogue.
4. *A pair needs no axes and a surface needs two*, and both come from the same
   rule, so the catalogue has one algorithm rather than five.
5. *Fixings are not datums.* The fifth kind in the specification's list is not
   in this schema, so the catalogue either reads =market_fixings= separately or
   drops the kind. That is a real decision, not a detail.

* Recommendations

1. Write the catalogue against =schema_of(type)= rather than a grouping rule of
   its own. Name it from the subject plus the identity fields that distinguish
   peers (=IR AUD IR_SWAP 0D 3M=, =AUD-AONIA=), and mint =instrument_id= from the
   canonical series URI plus the party, so it is stable across restarts and
   distinct across parties.
2. State =kind= as a function of the type and its coordinates, in one place in
   the protocol header, so the catalogue, the ladder and the surface read agree
   on what a surface is.
3. Correct the ladder contract in the light of finding 2: its columns are
   provider *series rows*, and its rungs are the =term= coordinates of one of
   them. hedgr's spec should be amended where it assumes several series.
4. Decide the fixing kind before the catalogue ships: either read the fixings
   table, or remove =fixing= from the kind vocabulary and from the search
   screen's badge list.
5. Take the party from the session and never from the request, as the
   specification already says; the row counts above show why.
