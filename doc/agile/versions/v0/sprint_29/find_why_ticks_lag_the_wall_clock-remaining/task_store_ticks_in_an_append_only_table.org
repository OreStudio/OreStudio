* Goal

The observation table stops at about 8,300 rows per second for one connection because its insert trigger runs for every row. An append-only table with one index reaches about 187,000 at 162 bytes for each row. Move ticks to it, and give the readers of the observation table a view over both.

* Acceptance

- A hypertable for ticks with no trigger, no valid_to and one index on the series and the time, with a retention policy for each class and no compression.
- The ingest writes ticks to it, and a batch needs no dedupe against a trigger.
- A view joins it with the observation table, and the readers keep their results: snapshots, evolution, slices, ORE export, curve pillars, lineage and the feed binding activity.
- The ingest drains a burst at more than 100,000 rows per second on one connection, measured with the tick injector. Met for the store path of the loop (median 117,000). Not met with margin for the whole path through NATS in this task (median 97,600 on a loaded machine). Met by the task "Overlap the copy with the next batch" (median 128,100).

* Plan

(Implementation strategy. Written when work starts; key decisions
are distilled into the parent story's =* Decisions= at close, but the
plan itself stays — it is the historical record of what we did.)

Decision of the owner, 2026-10-21: this task keeps one row for each series. A series is one
observation with its provenance, owned by a tenant and a party, so ticks are not shared and one
row for each source does not apply.

The design is in the section on tenant and party isolation of the tick ingest pipeline note.
In short: one append-only hypertable for each retention class, with =tenant_id=, =party_id=,
=series_id=, =ts=, =coord= and =value=. The reads run under the tenant policy and the party
policy, in the scalar subquery form. A writer role with =BYPASSRLS= and =INSERT= only writes
with =COPY=. The tests of write rate, read cost and isolation are done and recorded in the
note. The table is not compressed, because row-level security is not supported on compressed
chunks (Timescale documentation). Retention drops whole chunks. The order of work:

1. Provision the writer role in the role script. Done: =ores_<env>_marketdata_writer= has
   =BYPASSRLS=, is not in the service role and holds no grant. The =INSERT= grant on the tick
   table comes with the table in step 2, and the role never gets =SELECT=. The =.env= carries
   =ORES_MARKETDATA_SERVICE_WRITER_DB_USER= and =ORES_MARKETDATA_SERVICE_WRITER_DB_PASSWORD=, from =.env=
   format version 33.
2. Write the table, its index, its two policies and its retention class, with the notification
   trigger off. Done in =create/marketdata/marketdata_ticks_create.sql=, as =ores_marketdata_ticks_tbl=,
   with one-day chunks, a seven-day retention policy where the licence allows it, and =INSERT= for
   the writer role. The script is hand-written, because the entity template makes a trigger and a
   =valid_to=. A scratch database ran it twice, and the checks of isolation passed: a session with no
   settings read 0 rows, one tenant read its own 2 rows, one party read 1 row, a tenant with another
   tenant's party read 0, and the writer was refused =SELECT=.
3. Move the ingest to =COPY= with the owner copied from the cached series, and give the service
   a second pool for the writer role. Done as a copy next to the observation write, so the
   readers of the observation table keep working until step 4 moves them. =tick_writer= holds
   one libpq connection, not a pool, because the ingest worker is its only caller, and it writes
   each batch with one =COPY=. The loop copies the tick of every stored item with the owner of
   its binding, which is the owner of its series. A failed copy is logged once, counted, and does
   not change the store or the republish. A datum with a coordinate is not copied, because the
   table has no coordinate dictionary yet, and the loop warns once for each such series. The role
   comes from =ORES_MARKETDATA_SERVICE_WRITER_DB_USER= and =..._PASSWORD=, and an empty user turns
   the copy off. The observation write goes when step 4 ends. Tests: the row format, the write to
   a database that is not there, the parser, and a loop test that copies a tick as the writer and
   reads it back as the marketdata service, which has no =BYPASSRLS=: 1 row for the party of the
   series and 0 for another party.
4. Point the readers at the table, one at a time, with a test for each. A reader of a party or a
   tenant lists its series first. Done so far:
   - The coordinate dictionary, =ores_marketdata_tick_coords_tbl=. The ingest gives each datum
     of a series a number the first time it sees it, and the copy stores that number in =coord=.
     Coordinate 0 is the datum of a series without coordinates. The loop now copies datums with
     coordinates, so the warning of step 3 is gone. The table has the tenant policy only,
     because the ingest context names no party.
   - Reader 1, the as-of snapshot (=read_as_of_records=, which =read_as_of= calls). It serves the
     snapshot, the curve pillars and the curve republish. It reads the latest tick of each
     datum with one index probe, and it reads the observation table as before. On a tie the
     observation row wins.
   - Readers 2 and 3, the evolution buckets and the slice instants, in the same way.
   - Every datum has a row in the dictionary, including the datum of a series without
     coordinates. The row holds the URI that the feed sent, which is the URI of the
     observation rows. The first version used the URI of the series for that datum, and the
     export could not read it.
   - Reader 4, the ORE export. Decision of the owner, 2026-10-21: the ingest no longer writes
     the second row under the previous close. The export takes an optional valuation date.
     It then keeps the history of the observation table, and replaces the lines of that date
     by one line for each datum with the value at the close of that day, from both tables. The
     history stays because analytics such as historical simulation and P&L explain read more
     than one date. The report run passes the date of its run document, and the shell takes
     =--date=. Without a date, the export is the observation table as before, and the ticks
     of a live feed are not in it.
   - The dated export carries a value forward only up to an age limit, 5 days by default, which
     covers a weekend and a holiday. The request and the shell (=--max-age-days=) can change
     it. A datum with an older value has no line at the date, and the answer counts these
     datums and the carried ones. The report step logs a warning when any are left out. This
     keeps a run from taking a stale quote for a current one.
   - Review of PR 3615 found two faults, both fixed on this branch. The first export with a
     date dropped the history. The assignment of coordinate numbers and the write of the
     activity row opened one database connection for each datum and each binding. Each now
     takes a few statements for a batch. The smaller findings are in seven backlog tasks of the
     story.
   - Reader 5, the activity of a feed binding. Decision of the owner, 2026-10-21: the tick has
     no source column. The ingest writes one row for each binding in
     =ores_marketdata_feed_binding_activity_tbl=, with the series and the time of its newest
     tick, at most every five seconds. The reader reads that row. The row can trail the newest
     tick by five seconds.
   - The ingest stores a tick only in the tick table, with one =COPY= for each batch. The
     observation write, the close row, the split-and-retry of a failed batch and
     =keep_last_per_key= are gone. A store that does not answer leaves the batch unstored and
     republished, with the backoff of before. Two ticks of one datum and time in a batch make
     one row, and the later tick wins. Without a writer role, nothing is stored.
   Not covered by this step: the list and count of observations of a series, which the shell
   and the screens use, read the observation table only, so a live tick is not in them. The
   plan did not list that reader.
5. Measure a burst with the tick injector. Done, and the result is mixed. The hidden tests of
   =app_feed_ingest_loop_tests.cpp= (tag =[burst]=) send 60,000 ticks over 200 datums to one
   binding and time three paths: a bare =COPY= of the tick writer, the store path of the loop
   with no NATS, and the drain through a real NATS subscription and the worker thread. A C++
   publisher takes the place of the Python injector, because the Python injector needs a
   running service and bindings for its source, and it cannot send fast enough to test a drain
   of more than 100,000 rows each second. The last tick of a burst is the last row to appear,
   so one index probe of that row finds the end of the drain. The tests delete their rows.
   - The first measurement gave about 65,000 rows each second through NATS, and about 70,000
     for the store path. A bare =COPY= gave about 225,000, so the loop added about 10 microseconds
     for each tick.
   - A profile with callgrind showed why. Reading and writing the URI of each tick took about
     41,000 instructions in =plan_tick=, and the loop wrote the series URI again for every tick.
     Each tick also copied its bindings and built its republish subject.
   - The loop now names each distinct URI once and builds the targets once for each source and
     datum. They live in the bindings snapshot, so a refresh leaves none that is stale. The
     series, coordinate and stats lookups no longer build a string for each tick, and a row
     holds views of its ids.
   - After that, the store path gives about 117,000 rows each second and the drain through NATS
     about 98,000, on a machine at 11 to 16 load on 8 CPUs, which other environments loaded.
     The drain through NATS does not reliably pass 100,000 rows each second. The acceptance
     criterion is met for the store path and not met with margin for the whole path. The task
     "Overlap the copy with the next batch" followed, and it raises the drain through NATS to a
     median of 128,100 rows each second over ten runs.
   - What is left is spread thin: allocation, the CSV time stamp, the TLS of the republish, and
     locks. A bare =COPY= takes about 0.26 s of the 0.55 s for 60,000 rows, and the loop takes the
     rest, one after the other in one worker. Overlapping the =COPY= of one batch with the
     preparation of the next is the one large step left, and it needs its own task.
6. Open a separate task for the session functions of the policies, which cost seven times on an
   indexed read. Done: "Remove the session functions from the policies". The step 5 result also
   has a task, "Overlap the copy with the next batch".
