* Goal

A journey is the design document a prototype answers to, written before anything is built. This one states the person's goal on the Official rates screen, every step with the shape the standard already draws and the judgement where it does not, the wireframe, the prototype that draws it, the entities and operations each step needs, and what the server does not provide yet. Every gap names the task that owns it.

* Acceptance

- A user journey exists under doc/knowledge/journeys/market_data with every section its contract requires.
- Every step is judged against the record screen standard, with the steps no rule draws named.
- A wireframe is drawn beside it and embedded, and the prototype section links the prototype and lists its states.
- Every missing operation is listed with the task that owns it, and the journey is wired into the market data journeys index.

* Plan

- Write the journey from the screen's two jobs, not from the existing screen:
  governance, which the screen does, and operations, which it does not.
- Judge every step against the Record screen standard and against the
  operations the server answers today.
- Draw the row and the page as one wireframe: the list with terms, freshness and
  adapter state, and one provider's fixings and comparison.
- Name every gap with the task that owns it: the health read, the pause
  decision, the backfill design, and the deletion an accepted withdrawal needs.

* Result

The journey is written at
=doc/knowledge/journeys/market_data/journey_run-the-public-sources.org= and wired
into the market data journeys index. It states the goal in the person's words,
twelve steps, the eleven-row judgement table, the wireframe, the prototype and
its states, the entities and the operations with their status.

Four gaps are named with the task that owns each: freshness and the three
failure states (the health task), the pause and the deletion a withdrawal needs
(the pause decision), and the chosen backfill range (the backfill design).

The journey and its wireframe ride PR #4032, which also carries the prototype
the journey answers to and the close-out of both tasks.
