#+title: Tick-to-tick returns understate a correlation that holds exactly
#+description: The Epps effect: two series sampled at their own times show a correlation of tick returns below their true instantaneous correlation; the Hayashi-Yoshida estimator measures it without bias.
#+type: knowledge
#+level: cross
#+filetags: :correlation:statistics:market_data:synthetic:knowledge:
#+created: 2026-11-12
#+updated: 2026-11-12

* Summary

Epps (1979) observed that the correlation of stock returns falls as the
sampling interval shrinks. Much of the effect is a property of the measurement:
two series that tick at their own times have returns over intervals that only
partly overlap, and the part that does not overlap adds variance but no
covariance. So a correlation computed from tick returns, or from returns aligned
to the last tick before a grid point, is biased toward zero. Hayashi and Yoshida
(2005) gave an estimator of the covariance that sums the products of every pair
of overlapping returns and is unbiased for any sampling times independent of
the processes. It is the right way to state that a synthetic market's
correlation holds under irregular sampling, and the wrong test is the one that
looks natural.

* Detail

** The bias

Let $X$ and $Y$ be Brownian motions with instantaneous correlation $\rho$, and
take one return of each over the intervals $I$ and $J$. Then

\[
\operatorname{Corr}(\Delta X, \Delta Y) = \rho\, \frac{|I \cap J|}{\sqrt{|I|\,|J|}}.
\]

The ratio is one only when the intervals coincide. For two series with
independent Poisson clocks the expected ratio falls as the clocks get faster
relative to the window, which is the Epps effect in its simplest form. Market
microstructure noise and lead-lag add to it in real data; a synthetic market
without them shows the sampling part alone.

** The Hayashi-Yoshida estimator

For returns $\Delta X_m$ over intervals $I_m$ and $\Delta Y_n$ over $J_n$,
covering $[0, T]$,

\[
\widehat{\langle X, Y \rangle}_T = \sum_{m,n} \Delta X_m\, \Delta Y_n\; \mathbf{1}\{I_m \cap J_n \neq \emptyset\}.
\]

Every pair of returns whose intervals overlap contributes; no data are
discarded or interpolated. For Brownian motions sampled at times independent of
them it is unbiased for $\rho T$ and consistent as the mesh shrinks (Hayashi and
Yoshida, 2005). Divided by the square root of the two realised variances, it
estimates $\rho$.

** In ORE Studio

The synthetic market states correlations as the inner products of factor
loadings
([[id:25C467BE-07A3-432C-8CB8-6B194E748570][Shared Brownian factors give every synthetic series a signed correlation]]).
A test of that statement samples two drivers at their own irregular times and
estimates their correlation with the Hayashi-Yoshida estimator. A test that
correlates tick returns directly would measure the overlap ratio as well as the
loadings, and would fail a correct implementation. The arrival work already
measures count correlation; this measures signed correlation of the noise.
