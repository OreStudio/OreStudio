* Acceptance

- Each daily Frankfurter provider with a terms page has its terms read and recorded on the sources page, and those that allow storage are providers with a source. (Amended on 2026-10-28: the first catalogue takes the four whose terms allow storage among the eleven read; the rest wait for their terms pages, see Notes.)
- Each provider's currencies are vendor instruments mapped to FX fixing items of the form FX-SOURCE-CCY1-CCY2.
- The system party subscribes and routes to each public provider.
- EUR/USD, GBP/USD and USD/JPY are each covered by at least three providers.
- Each provider records the time of day it publishes, in its own zone, so the adapter asks for a day's fixings after they are out. The store keeps the date of a fixing alone.

* Notes

- The terms were read on 2026-10-28. Kept, as they allow storage: the ECB, the IMF,
  the Bank of Canada and the Central Bank of the Republic of Türkiye. Left out: the
  Bank of England, whose spot rates are LSEG's; FRED, whose terms forbid storing or
  caching; and Norges Bank, the Czech National Bank, the Riksbank and Banxico, whose
  terms pages could not be read. The IMF and TCMB ask permission for commercial use,
  which the administrator weighs when accepting.
- A source name takes no dot, so the sources are =<code>-frankfurter=.
- The first live run stored the IMF, Bank of Canada and TCMB rates under the wrong
  items: Frankfurter rebases every provider to the euro unless asked for a base, and
  the fetcher had assumed the provider's own pivot. The fetcher now names the pivot
  and refuses a row in another base; the run was repeated on a fresh database.
- Acceptance needed its writer before any data could flow, so the server-side
  operation =marketdata.v1.ops.accept_provider_terms=, with its shell command, is
  built here rather than with the first-run screen.

* Result

The official rates catalogue is seeded by a generator,
=projects/ores.sql/utility/generate_official_catalogue.py=, from Frankfurter's
provider list: four providers whose terms allow storage (the ECB, the IMF, the Bank of
Canada and the Central Bank of the Republic of Türkiye), each with its terms and credit,
a =<code>-frankfurter= source, its currencies as =iso4217= vendor instruments mapped to
the FX fixings =FX-<KEY>-<PIVOT>-<CCY>= (140 in all), and the system party's subscription
and route. The terms are accepted through =marketdata.v1.ops.accept_provider_terms=, the
one writer of an acceptance, which takes the account from the session and the time from
the service; the shell command =provider_terms_operations accept-provider-terms= calls it.
The official rates adapter is registered as a deployment-only process with the four
sources, with its own NATS certificate.

Evidence, live on a fresh database: the four sources announced 140 ids with none unknown;
their demand was refused as =terms_not_accepted= until the super administrator accepted
the terms, and Bloomberg, which states none, was refused acceptance; the backfill then
stored 29,331 fixings (ECB 8,190, IMF 8,974, Bank of Canada 6,378, TCMB 5,789) from about
400 days back to the last published day, with =fixings_failed=0=. EUR/USD is covered by
the four, each on its own pivot, and they agree: ECB 1.1150, IMF 1/0.89686 = 1.1150, Bank
of Canada 0.71235/0.63926 = 1.1143, TCMB 0.020103/0.018026 = 1.1152. GBP and JPY are quoted
by all four too. The first run had filed three providers' rates under the wrong items,
which this check caught; see Notes.
