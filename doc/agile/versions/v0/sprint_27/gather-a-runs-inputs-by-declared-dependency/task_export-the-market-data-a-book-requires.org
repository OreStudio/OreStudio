* Goal

=marketdata.v1.ops.export_market_data_to_storage= takes no book and no date.
Its own documentation states the stance: an ORE market file "is a snapshot of
everything a run needs, not a slice of one series, and an export that could
name a subset would produce a file that does not reproduce anything". That was
true of a whole-tenant run. A run that gathers per book needs the export
narrowed to the requirements that book resolves and read as of the run's date,
so two runs of one book on different dates read different rows.

* Acceptance

- The request names a book and an as-of date, and the export writes only the
  series that book's resolution requires.
- The rows are read as of the date, not as of now.
- A requirement that no series answers fails the step and names the missing
  data, because ORE cannot price a curve whose quotes are quietly absent.
- The market and fixings bodies remain what ORE parses, as they are today.

* Plan

The serializer already exists and is proven: =ore_export_service::write_all=
walks the series, observations and fixings and renders the files ORE reads.
What changes is the selection it walks and the date it reads at, so this task
is mostly about the resolution task's output reaching it.

The documented stance above has to be reversed deliberately rather than
quietly: the comment argues that a subset does not reproduce anything, and the
answer is that the *resolution* is what makes a subset reproducible — the set
is derived from the book's own trades at a stated date, not chosen by a caller
who might choose badly. When the stance changes, that reasoning belongs in the
same comment.

Fixings are read the same way and stay a separate file, because the engine
reads them from one of its own.
