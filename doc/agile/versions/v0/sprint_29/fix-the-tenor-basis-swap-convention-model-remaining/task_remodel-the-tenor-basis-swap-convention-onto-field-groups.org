- The "market-data annex" is =oresmd_uri=, which the atomisation left on the

* Plan

** Follow the ORE parameter contract the engine settings and conventions already share

The work is one application of a standard that landed on main on 2026-10-25
and 2026-10-26, not a modelling idea of its own.

- Every value ORE reads from a document is a /leaf/, and every leaf has one
  documentation entry in the ORE parameter contract
  (=ores.reporting.ore_parameter=: schema, XPath, label, help, description,
  default, format, value domain). A screen restates none of it.
- The element type a leaf belongs to is data, not code.
  =ores.reporting.engine_settings_group= with
  =engine_settings_group_setting= is the engine settings' shape, and
  =ores.refdata.convention_kind= with =convention_kind_term= is the
  conventions' shape: the owner decided on 2026-10-25 that a convention kind
  is modelled as the engine run settings model their groups.
- A term names its leaf by schema and XPath. The row for the tenor basis swap
  is already seeded: seventeen =TENOR_BASIS_SWAP= terms spanning
  =convention.name=, =tenor_basis_swap_pay_receive.*= and
  =tenor_basis_swap_long_short.*=, each with its position, in
  =refdata_convention_kind_terms_populate.sql=.
- Screens draw a leaf by =doc/knowledge/ui/ore_element_and_leaf_screen_standard.org=
  (rules =OL-*=), applied first to conventions.

** What this means for the field groups

- The field groups are a *reflection-width* change, not a schema change. The
  table names, the column names and the seventeen term rows stay as they are,
  so the contract keeps matching the tree. Grouping must preserve every column
  name and its entity.
- The groups attach inside the entities main already has: the central
  =convention= row (identity and audit), =tenor_basis_swap_pay_receive= and
  =tenor_basis_swap_long_short= (each form's own members). Nothing is
  restated in a codegen model that the contract already carries, so a group
  contributes columns only: no label, help, default or value set.
- The precedent in the tree is the bond family's field groups
  (=ores.trading.bond_*=_field_group.org=), which is what the codegen support
  this branch keeps was written for.

** Order of work

1. Group the central row's identity and audit, then each part's members, and
   regenerate.
2. Prove the contract still matches: every =convention_kind_term= row's
   (entity, column) must still name a column that exists.
3. Run the refdata and ORE convention suites, and the drift check.
4. Leave the screen work to the story that owns it
   ([[id:3D6ACCDB-6209-47BC-86F5-864A65C6389B][Draw every ORE leaf the same way, starting with conventions]]),
   which already draws a convention's terms with the shared leaf parts.

* Notes

** 2026-10-26: the family service refuses a grouped member

The grouping was attempted on =tenor_basis_swap_pay_receive= first, with four
field groups -- identity, legs, coupon, and the =ores.dq.audit_record= tail --
and the refdata regeneration refused it before writing any C++:

#+begin_example
ValueError: tenor_basis_swap_convention: the part 'pay_receive'
'tenor_basis_swap_pay_receive' is a grouped entity; the composite family
service writes flat domain objects
#+end_example

The refusal is deliberate and sits in =_family_entity_view()= in
=projects/ores.codegen/src/codegen/core.py=. The family service addresses a
row by one key column, builds flat domain objects from the write records and
reads a part by its parent, so a nested domain object is outside the shape it
can generate. It refuses the central row as well as a part, so no member of a
convention family can be grouped without teaching the family service to build
and read grouped domain objects.

Measured on main at =dd4af2b9ca=, from the generated headers:

| Struct                            | Members |
|-----------------------------------+---------|
| =convention=                      |      13 |
| =tenor_basis_swap_pay_receive=    |      20 |
| =tenor_basis_swap_long_short=     |      18 |

The atomisation of 2026-10-15 already cut the one 24-member literal into
three, and made each leg form a table of its own. What remains is the width of
each part, and the family protocol that carries the central row and a part in
one message.

Three ways forward, and the choice is the owner's:

1. Teach the family service to build and read grouped domain objects, then
   group the parts. It is the shape the acceptance asks for. It touches the
   family service template, and the family protocol's JSON becomes nested,
   which is a contract change -- the decomposition section of
   =data_oriented_design.org= warns that grouping is not a wire decision.
2. Leave the family flat and treat the atomisation as the decomposition the
   page asks for: the per-literal width is now 20 at its widest, and the
   question left is the accumulation path, which is the sibling task "Split the
   configuration document protocol by document family".
3. Group only where a member is not part of a family, which does not apply to
   this convention.

** 2026-10-26: the family service now writes grouped members

The three ways forward recorded above were put to the owner, who chose the
first: teach the family service to build and read grouped domain objects.
That is done, and the remodel is applied.

- The family view resolves a member's domain groups before it is built and
  gives every column the prefix of the member that carries it, so the key,
  the party, the version and the commentary are named by their path. The
  generator, the repository mapper, the history field mapper and the table
  display were the templates that still wrote a flat path.
- The central row is grouped into identity, payload and the
  =ores.dq.audit_record= tail. Each part is grouped into the identity it
  shares with its sibling, one group per leg, its coupon treatment and the
  same tail. Every group holds at most five members: identity five, the
  paying and receiving legs three each, the long leg two, the short leg
  three, the coupon groups three and two, the audit tail five.
- The tables, the columns and the seventeen =TENOR_BASIS_SWAP= term rows are
  unchanged. The write records and the wire shape stayed flat: only the
  domain struct nests, so the contract still names a real column.
- =ores.dq.api= is now a public dependency of =ores.refdata.api=, declared in
  the component's own =component_overview.org= rather than in the generated
  CMakeLists, because the generated headers include the audit record.

** 2026-10-26: the stamp walks into a grouped row's members

A grouped row carries =tenant_id= and =party_id= inside its identity member,
and the write refuses a row whose party is the nil uuid. =stamp_document()=
only stamped a row that had =modified_by= on the struct itself, so it walked
past a grouped row's identity and the party stayed nil: the first database
run of the convention bundle tests failed with "Invalid party_id".

Stamp every member of an aggregate as well as walking into it. =stamp()= is
guarded field by field, so it does nothing for a member that carries none of
the security fields.
