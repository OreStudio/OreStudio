This page is a [[id:671F18E4-E09C-4B3B-BD24-D33DF8AE38A6][capture]] in the *inbox* bucket of the product backlog — a pre-sprint idea, not yet pulled into a sprint as a story.

* What

A report is owned by a *team*, not by a person. The decision is reached and not
implemented: no column holds it, no read returns it, and no cell draws it. What a
team is in this system is still open, and the open questions are listed below so
the future work starts from them and not from a guess.

* Why

=ores_reporting_report_definitions_tbl= records no owner. Its
=projects/ores.sql/create/reporting/reporting_report_definitions_create.sql= states
the columns, and none of them answers "who is answerable for this report":

- =modified_by= and =performed_by= are audit. The insert trigger fills
  =performed_by= from the service or =current_user= and validates =modified_by=
  against an IAM account username. They name who wrote the row and who ran the
  write, and audit records what happened, not who answers for the outcome.
- =party_id= names a legal entity. A legal entity is not a group of people. It
  cannot be asked a question and it cannot approve a change.

So a failed run has no addressee, and a change to a definition has no approver.
The Owner cell on the reports-a-party-runs screen has no source, which is why the
gap stayed open rather than being filled with a second-best column.

* Why a team, and not an individual

An individual is the cheap option, because a nullable =owner_account_id= records
one. It fails on three counts:

1. It answers "who last touched this", not "who answers for this". A person who
   last edited a definition is not thereby the person a failed run goes to.
2. It breaks when the person moves. A resigning account leaves every definition
   it owns unowned, and the handover is a write per row.
3. Reports, in practice, are run and repaired by a rota. No one person holds the
   report; the shift holds it.

A team answers for the report, absorbs people leaving without a write to the
definition, and can be empty only by a real omission — a definition nobody's team
claims — rather than by someone resigning.

* What a team is, left open

These are questions, not answers. The future work settles them before a column
exists.

- What is a team in this system? IAM roles already exist, and a party already
  exists, so "team" names a thing that overlaps two things already modelled.
- Is a team a role, a new entity, or a group of accounts? Each has a different
  cost to read and a different meaning for the cell.
- Is ownership one team or several, with one that is primary? The screen draws one
  Owner cell, and the model may hold more.
- Does ownership need history, as the bitemporal entities carry? A change of owning
  team is a fact with a time, and the rest of the tree records such facts.
- How does ownership meet the request-and-approve path? That path needs a second
  person, and so a second team. Whether the approver is drawn from the owning team
  or from another is undecided.

* Why it is deferred

It is deferred for one reason and it is not urgency. It needs the answer to what
a team is before a column can be designed, and the column is the first artefact.
Phase 3 of the reporting gaps does not depend on it: the definition id on a run's
event, the health word of a cancelled and a skipped run, the on-demand word and
the party code in the session all proceed without an owner.
