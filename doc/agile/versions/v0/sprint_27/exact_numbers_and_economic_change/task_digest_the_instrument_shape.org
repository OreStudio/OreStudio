* Acceptance

- The digest covers declared economic fields only and ignores identity and audit.
- A component write recomputes the parent digest and bumps the external version only when it differs.

* Plan

(Implementation strategy. Written when work starts; key decisions
are distilled into the parent story's =* Decisions= at close, but the
plan itself stays — it is the historical record of what we did.)

*What has landed.* `economic_digest` and `trade_economic_digest` in
`ores.trading.api/domain`, with the exclusion rule, the framing and the
canonical rendering; the `economic_digest` text column and the
`external_version` integer on the trade; a generic write-observer trait in
`ores.database` with the trading specialisations in `trade_write_observation.hpp`
reached through `export.hpp`; and a DB-backed test binary,
`ores.trading.core.digest.tests`, registered by its own `CMakeLists.txt`.

*A behaviour change to expect, and not a defect.* The refresh ends by writing
the trade row (`trade_repository{}.write(ctx, trade)`), and a write of a
versioned entity inserts a new version. So every write of an observed child now
also creates a **new version of the parent trade**, in the same transaction.
That is the design — the digest is stored on the trade, so a component change
must touch the trade, and the internal version is supposed to move on every
recorded change, of which this is one. Two consequences worth knowing before
reading a test failure as a regression:

- Checked, so the warning is bounded: the three suites that write an observed
  child — `trade_booking_eventing_integration_tests`,
  `trade_state_eventing_integration_tests` and
  `service_trade_operations_service_tests` — assert on the *child's* history
  (`history(id).size() == versions.size()`), not on the trade's version, so they
  do not break on the extra parent write. The general risk stands for any future
  test that counts a trade's versions.
- Storage grows by one trade version per component write. Acceptable for an
  amendment; worth a thought for a bulk import, which is another reason the
  boundary in item 2 is the right place to make the version decision.

*What is outstanding, as a work order.* Three corrections, in this order,
because the last two depend on the first.

1. **The component set is wrong in both directions.** The fold observes seven
   entities — `trade_state`, `trade_booking`, `trade_identifier`,
   `trade_additional_field`, `trade_portfolio`, `trade_party_role`,
   `structure_member` — which is the *internal*-version set, not the economic
   one. It includes a free-text comment, a lifecycle state and organisational
   links, and it misses every instrument. The rule: a component drives the
   digest when its fields state terms the customer agreed to. Read the
   instrument by `instrument_table_for(trade.trade_type)` from
   `trade_type_routing.hpp`, following `trade_export_service.cpp:96`, together
   with its legs and amounts; drop the seven. The switch is 29 arms and its
   skeleton is generated in `evidence/instrument_digest_skeleton.cpp`, one arm
   per enum value. Verified against the tree: all 29 enum values have both a
   `<family>_repository.hpp` and a `<family>.hpp`, so the skeleton names are
   real and none is missing. Two signatures to note: an instrument repository is keyed by
   the trade, so `read_latest(ctx, trade_id)` works; a leg table is keyed by
   its own id and needs `read_latest_by_trade_id(...)`.
2. **The version bump needs a boundary, not a write.** (Item 1 is done: the fold observes the 29 instrument families and reads the instrument and its children; the five non-economic children are out.) The condition is two
   comparisons: the digest differs, *and* the change crosses a customer-visible
   boundary. The idiom already exists in
   `trading.v1.ops.put_composite_instrument_with_legs` — one request that
   carries an instrument and its legs. Refresh and bump at the end of the
   shape-carrying handler; leave the per-component observer refreshing the
   digest only. Do not enable the bump until (1) is done *and* the stored
   digests are reset in the same change, or the correction itself will look
   like an economic change to every trade.
**The child set already has readers, and enumerating them is not guesswork.**
`projects/ores.trading/core/include/ores.trading.core/repository/parent_scoped_queries.hpp`
exports twenty trade- or issue-scoped readers, and they are the legs and
children the digest must fold:

    read_legs_by_trade_ids            read_leg_amounts_by_trade_ids
    read_leg_rates_by_trade_ids       read_leg_amortizations_by_trade_ids
    read_forwards_by_trade_ids        read_strikes_by_trade_ids
    read_options_by_trade_ids         read_option_exercise_fees_by_trade_ids
    read_option_payment_dates_by_trade_ids   read_option_premiums_by_trade_ids
    read_schedules_by_trade_ids       read_schedule_dates_by_trade_ids
    read_issue_legs_by_issue_ids      read_issue_leg_amounts_by_issue_ids
    read_issue_leg_rates_by_issue_ids read_issue_leg_amortizations_by_issue_ids
    read_issue_schedules_by_issue_ids read_issue_schedule_dates_by_issue_ids
    read_call_dates_by_issue_ids      read_conversion_targets_by_issue_ids

The issue-scoped ones take issue ids rather than trade ids, and a bond issue is
reached from the trade's instrument, so the read is two hops for that family.
`swap_leg` and `composite_leg` also expose trade-scoped readers of their own.

That turns correction (1) from "find fifty tables" into: one 29-arm switch for
the instrument (skeleton generated beside this task), plus these twenty calls,
plus the two leg repositories. Everything it needs already exists and is
exported.

**Will it compile?** Folding instrument rows instantiates `canonical_value`
for every included field of every family, and that function ends in a
`static_assert` on a type it cannot render — so a type it cannot handle is a
build failure and not a silent skip. Checked, because "it will compile" is the
claim this capability keeps disproving:

- `boost::uuids::uuid` carries `using value_type = std::uint8_t;`, so the
  sequence branch renders it as a deterministic 16-byte sequence.
- An excluded field's type is never instantiated at all, because
  `canonical_struct` guards the call with
  `if constexpr (!is_identity_or_audit(...))`. So the `*_id` fields cannot
  break the build whatever their type.
- `security_id` is the one identifier in the allow-list and therefore *not*
  excluded — and it is a `std::string`.
- No trading domain type has a `std::map`, `std::unordered_map` or `std::set`
  member, which matters because `std::pair` is not an aggregate and the
  container branch would hand it to the `static_assert`.

And it is already demonstrated rather than only argued:
`economic_digest_ignores_identity_and_audit_fields` in
`ores.trading.api/tests/domain_economic_digest_tests.cpp:144` digests a real
`bond_instrument` (via its `make_bond_instrument` fixture), changes its
identity, audit, `issue_id` and a comment, and asserts the digest is unchanged.
That is also the coverage a reader might expect to be missing — the positive
case for exclusion, as against the mutation proof that removing an exclusion
breaks the suite. Both exist.

3. **The tests move with the set, and they depend on it more than the first
   read suggests.** All three cases in `trade_economic_digest_write_tests.cpp`
   drive the mechanism through `trade_identifier`, which is leaving the fold.
   But the *fixture* depends on it too: `prepare()` books the trade through
   `book_trade_request`, and it is the **booking** — one of the seven — that
   first triggers the fold and leaves a non-empty digest. `before.front()
   .economic_digest.empty()` is asserted false at line 149 on that basis. Drop
   the booking from the fold and every one of the three cases fails at its
   fixture, not just the two that name `trade_identifier`. So the tests cannot
   be patched in place; they need an instrument write in `prepare()` before
   they assert anything. A red suite during the move is expected.

   Two facts that make the move mechanical. Generators exist for every family
   (`ores.trading.api/generators/*_instrument_generator.hpp`, 99 headers in
   all), so the fixture does not need hand-built rows. And the family is not a
   free choice: `generate_synthetic_trade` sets `trade_type = "Swap"`
   (`trade_generator.cpp:49`), which routes to `vanilla_swap_instrument` through
   `instrument_table_for`, so the fixture must write **that** family with the
   anchor's id as its `trade_id`. Any other instrument would be read by no
   switch arm and the digest would hold still — the same silent under-read the
   correction exists to remove.

Until all three land, the stored digest is provisional and nothing acts on it.
The comments marking that are on the fold and on the test file.
