#+description: A cross or composite fixing is computed from two parent fixings, which may be published at different times.

* Summary

A *derived fixing source* computes its observation from the fixings of other
sources instead of observing a market directly. The common case is a cross
rate: an AUD/CAD fixing derived from an AUD/USD and a USD/CAD fixing of the
same administrator. A non-deliverable trade between two non-deliverable
currencies needs a *composite* source against a common settleable currency,
and the parent fixings of a composite may be published at different times.

* Detail

** Crosses

A cross fixing divides or multiplies two parent fixings through a common
currency, usually USD. The derivation must state the parents, their quote side,
the order of operations and the rounding, because the result depends on all
four. A mid cross is normally computed from mid parents. Some confirmations
name both parent sources; others name only the published, already crossed and
rounded result. The arithmetic of crosses through a common currency is the
subject of the [[id:48F6FD8D-56C7-4FF7-A369-CD13D08A6057][Cross-Rates Matrix]].

** Composites for non-deliverable pairs

A non-deliverable forward or option between two non-deliverable currencies,
such as EUR/KRW settled in USD, needs a composite source that combines a fixing
of each currency against the settlement currency. The two parents may fix at
different times and on different calendars, so the composite is available only
once the later parent is published. See
[[id:CD3E82C6-AE56-4D06-B949-0B3A5CE7A5FC][Deliverability and non-deliverable instruments]] and
[[id:2F7AD024-2CDF-4B31-9845-0879179B0D1A][Settlement Currency]].

** Presentation

A view of required fixings should show, for a derived source, the parent
sources it was computed from, so that an authoriser can check each parent. See
[[id:B5CA0D8F-D9C5-4F09-ABD7-7ED6F0FEF1A5][Fixing management and its controls]].
