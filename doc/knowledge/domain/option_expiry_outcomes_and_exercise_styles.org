#+title: Option expiry outcomes and exercise styles
#+description: Expiry ends an option's risk: it is exercised into a trade or cash, expires worthless, or settles from a fixing.
#+type: knowledge
#+audience: user
#+level: cross

* Summary

An option stops being a risk position at its expiry, on its expiry date and
cut. It then ends in one of three ways: it is /exercised/, producing a trade or
a cash settlement; it /expires/ worthless; or it is /settled from a fixing/,
when its terms make the outcome follow from an agreed observation. Expiry is
distinct from maturity, which is the end of a contract that has passed its
final date. An exercise can be partial, and its result can be an FX trade or a
cash settlement.

* Detail

** Outcomes

| Term | Meaning |
|------+---------|
| Exercised | The option ended in the money and was exercised. |
| Expired | The option ended out of the money and lapsed. |
| Settled from a fixing | The outcome followed from a fixing agreed at inception. See [[id:0CBDBE85-17DF-4A77-A231-FFB2101FC0CC][Fixing-determined and holder-determined exercise]]. |
| Matured | The contract has passed its final date. This is not an expiry. |
| Exercise trade | The trade booked by an exercise: an FX trade for a vanilla, sometimes a further derivative for an exotic. |
| Unexpire, unexercise | The reversal of an expiry or an exercise made in error. See [[id:0E638BE0-E3DA-4D05-A2FC-0F52FC1D24FC][Reversal of expiry events and post-expiry option states]]. |

** Exercise styles

FpML defines three exercise types, and each states when the holder may act:

- *European.* Only at expiry. The terms give the expiration date and time, the
  earliest exercise time, whether partial exercise is allowed with its minimum
  notional, and any exercise fee.
- *American.* At any time up to expiry. The terms add a commencement date and a
  latest exercise time, after which a notice counts as given on the next
  business day, and whether multiple exercise is allowed, with minimum and
  maximum notionals.
- *Bermudan.* On a list of dates, the last of which is the expiration date.

The instant an option expires is its expiry date at its cut;
[[id:3B39B2E2-6E05-4DF8-B68B-6ACA12F2FCE9][The option-expiry cut code]] explains why an expiry needs an
instant and how the cut code names it.

** How an exercise settles

1. *An FX trade.* The common case for a European vanilla: a trade that looks
   like a spot trade, for the option's notional at the strike, delivered on
   the option's delivery date, linked to the option as its exercise trade.
2. *A cash settlement.* The two currency flows are netted into one payment, as
   for non-deliverable options; see
   [[id:CD3E82C6-AE56-4D06-B949-0B3A5CE7A5FC][Deliverability and non-deliverable instruments]].
3. *Two cash flows.* The exercise books two cash flows instead of a trade, which
   suits a product whose client does not see the underlying option, such as a
   dual currency deposit.

Exercise trades are labelled as exercises, so that they can be grouped,
reported and reversed together.

** Partial exercise

The holder of an American option can exercise part of the notional, as many
times as the remaining notional allows. Each partial exercise reduces the
option's notional, which is an amendment that needs authorisation, and creates
a linked exercise trade. At expiry the same applies to a European option, and
any remaining notional expires.
