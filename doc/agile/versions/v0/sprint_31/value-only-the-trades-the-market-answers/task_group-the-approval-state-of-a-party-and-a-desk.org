* Goal

The approval chain gave a party and a desk one more field each, and the rule that
keeps C++ types small allows a listed type to shrink but never to grow: =party=
stands at 20 against a recorded 19, =party_entity= at 21 against 20,
=business_unit_entity= at 17 against 16, and =business_unit= crossed fifteen and
may not join the list at all. The flag belongs with the other facts of an
approval, so the fix is to give it a type of its own in the models and let
generation carry it, which returns all four to what their rows state.

* Acceptance

The premise was re-cut on 2026-11-26, after the measurement below showed that
a group member alone cannot bring the two row structs back. What is accepted
now:

- The change audit — the four columns =modified_by=, =performed_by=,
  =change_reason_code= and =change_commentary= — is one member, =audit=, in
  both the domain type and the row struct of a party and of a business unit.
  The model states the block once, as a field group, and codegen writes each
  layer from it.
- The row struct can group, which it could not before: every other template
  already honours the audit group, and the entity template was the one that
  did not. The capability is the task's deliverable, not a party-only edit.
- =party=, =party_entity=, =business_unit= and =business_unit_entity= hold no
  more fields than their =* Ceilings= rows state, and =business_unit= is at or
  under fifteen so it joins no row. The three rows that exist come down to the
  counts these types then have.
- No ceiling in =compass/checks/wide_types.org= rises: every row is the count
  its type has, and no row is added.
- The approval chain's behaviour does not move: the two approval pgTAP files,
  the refdata C++ suites, the wire roundtrip tests and the web typecheck pass.
  The party's and the desk's wire shape changes only in that the audit columns
  nest, which every generated type on the path mirrors.

* Plan

The plan's shape was settled by the measurement; the mechanism it records is
the one the counts left open.

1. Count the four types with the check's own command and record the numbers.
   *Done*: =compass-ng check --codec wide_types= at =0c91ae4d65= reports
   =party= 20 against a row of 19, =party_entity= 21 against 20,
   =business_unit= 16 with no row, and =business_unit_entity= 17 against 16.
   All four are exactly one field over, and all four crossed on
   =restricts_product_types=.
2. Choose the mechanism by what the counts say. A field group composes into the
   domain type alone — =convention_entity= is flat although =convention=
   declares three groups — so a group member cannot bring a row struct back.
   The two mechanisms left are moving the flag out of both tables, which
   rewrites the product approval SQL, or teaching the row struct to group the
   columns the model already groups. The owner chose the second on 2026-11-26:
   no schema change, the flag stays where it belongs, and the saving applies to
   every entity, not to these four.
3. Add the shared row type the group needs: the four change-audit columns as
   one struct in =ores.database=, beside =db_timestamp=, because every entity's
   audit block holds the same four columns in the same layer.
4. Teach the entity template to use it. =has_audit_group= is already in the
   model context and every other template that reads the audit block honours
   it; the entity template states the four columns flat and binds them one by
   one. It emits one member, =audit=, when the model declares the group, and
   stays flat when it does not, so no other entity's generated code moves.
5. Point the mapper at the nested member on the entity side. The domain side
   already goes through =audit_prefix=; the entity side is written flat in four
   places.
6. Declare =| audit | ores.dq.audit_record |= in =ores.refdata.party.org= and
   =ores.refdata.business_unit.org=, so both layers group from one statement in
   the model, and let codegen write the domain type, the row struct, the
   protocol, the schema documentation and the web types.
7. Lower the three ceiling rows to the counts their types then have, and leave
   =business_unit= off the list. No row rises and none is added.
8. Run the two approval pgTAP files, the refdata C++ suites, the wire roundtrip
   tests and the web typecheck and format: the chain's rules must not move, only
   the shape that carries them. The flag's two states stay distinct — false
   permits everything, true with no junction rows permits nothing — because
   that distinction is the chain's whole point.
9. Record the four counts before and after, with the command that measured them.

* Notes

The ceilings the check records today, read from =compass/checks/wide_types.org=
before any change: =party= 19, =party_entity= 20, =business_unit_entity= 16, and
=business_unit= carries no row at all, which is the rule's way of saying it must
come to fifteen or under rather than join the list.

The measurement the plan's first step asked for is in =* Measurements=. It
shows that the task's premise was wrong in one place and incomplete in another.
Wrong, because a group member cannot bring a row struct back: the entity
template states one member per column by design, and =convention_entity= is
flat although its model declares three groups, so the group reaches the domain
type alone. Incomplete, because the four types were not four different
problems: each was exactly one field over its row, and all four crossed on the
same boolean.

So the saving had to be one the row struct could take, and the audit block is
the only one it holds that the model can state as a group: four of
=party_entity='s 21 members and four of =business_unit_entity='s 17. Making the
row struct group it takes =party= to 16, =party_entity= to 18, =business_unit=
to 12 and =business_unit_entity= to 14 — every one under its row, and
=business_unit= well clear of the fifteen it may not exceed.

The alternative was to move =restricts_product_types= out of both tables into a
table of its own, which also lands all four on their rows. It lost on cost and
on reach: it rewrites the product approval SQL, the junction triggers that
maintain the permitted sets, the publish path that restricts a book, the shell
recipes and both pgTAP files, inside a story another environment is working,
and it leaves the next wide entity exactly as stuck as before.
