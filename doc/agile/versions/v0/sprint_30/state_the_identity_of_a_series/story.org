* Goal

market_series_identity is one wide table of nullable columns with one shape per instrument class, and nothing states which fields a class requires: the requirement lives in the code that parses an oresmd URI, so a new series can be written sparse and nothing refuses it until a consumer far downstream says it has no series. Declare the shape per class as data beside the classification rules, enforce it where identity is written, and name the class and the field on refusal. Note that identity_kind covers both FX and IBOR fixings: the family is asset_class, not key_kind.

* Acceptance

- Each series class declares, as data, the identity fields it requires and those it forbids.
- The writer refuses an identity row that misses a required field or carries a forbidden one, naming the class and the field.
- The FX classes require ccy and unit_ccy; the IBOR fixings require ccy and their index, and require no unit currency.

* Decisions

- *A shape is keyed by family, not by instrument* (2026-11-20). A family is the
  asset class, the series subclass and, for an index, the index family, which
  is the key the shapes table carries. A family no shape names is not enforced
  but counted as unstated, because the catalogue grows and refusing every
  family nobody has stated yet would stop an ingest for the catalogue's reason.
- *An index series must carry the index subclass before its shape is looked
  up* (2026-11-20). Otherwise a fixing that states another subclass is held to
  no shape and the guard cannot see the lie.
- *The writer fails closed; the backfill counts* (2026-11-20). A write through
  the repository refuses the batch. A backfill is a repair, so it counts the
  rows a shape refuses, warns with each, and projects the rest. It projects
  only the series of the parties its caller can see.
- *The CRM's own refusal stays* (2026-11-21). =read_fixing_sources= refuses a
  party whose FX fixing lacks =ccy= or =unit_ccy= even though the writer
  refuses such a row, because a row can still arrive by SQL or a restore.

* Out of scope

- A series written in SQL is not projected until something runs the
  projection. Captured as [[id:20D96191-9FBE-455C-B2EA-529C31867A76][A published curve's series has no identity until something else projects it]].

* Result

Each series family states as data the identity fields it requires and those it
forbids, in =ores_marketdata_series_identity_shapes_tbl= and its entries. The
identity writer refuses a row that misses a required field or carries a
forbidden one, naming the series, the family and the field; the backfill counts
such rows and leaves them unprojected. The FX families require =ccy= and
=unit_ccy=; the IBOR fixings require =ccy=, =index= and =index_name= and forbid
a unit currency; the CRM refuses a party whose FX fixing is sparse. On a freshly
provisioned database every one of the 1,611 series held to a shape conforms.
