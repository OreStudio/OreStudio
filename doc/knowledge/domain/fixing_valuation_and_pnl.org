#+title: A fixing replaces a projected rate with an observed one, which moves value, risk and P&L
#+description: Before its fixing a coupon is projected; after, it is observed. The switch moves value, makes delta jump and has its own P&L.
#+type: knowledge
#+audience: user
#+level: cross
#+filetags: :domain:fixing:valuation:pnl:
#+created: 2026-11-22
#+updated: 2026-11-22

* Summary

Before its fixing date, a floating coupon is valued from a projection of the
rate; after it, from the observed fixing; once its settlement trade exists, it
leaves the trade's value altogether. Each switch changes the value, and a
fixing makes the delta of many trades jump: a non-deliverable forward has FX
delta until it fixes and none after, and a volatility swap's sensitivity resets
towards zero as its fixings accumulate. P&L attribution therefore treats
fixings as an activity of their own, and the replacement of a projected rate by
the observed one is the /rate reset/ P&L.

* Detail

** Projected and observed

The value of a swap is the sum of the cash delivered, the cash settled and not
yet delivered, the past coupons whose fixings are not yet authorised, and the
future coupons. A future coupon uses a projection from the source's curve; a
past coupon without a settlement trade uses the fixing; a coupon whose
settlement trade exists has left the swap. Projections are computed by the
pricing library for valuation and for rolling a portfolio through time; they
are never stored as observations. See
[[id:C6A619F3-B2B6-42D5-9C41-5269A84BC96C][An observation is stored once per source and never stamped on the trades it fixes]].

A non-deliverable forward is discounted from its settlement date, and its
settlement amount stops depending on spot once it has fixed; see
[[id:B60031AD-CE57-5F9D-885C-1DAF504E6004][An NDF pays a cash difference on its settlement date]].

** Jumps in risk

A fixing removes uncertainty at once, so sensitivities jump:

- a non-deliverable forward has FX delta before it fixes and none after;
- an in-the-money non-deliverable option behaves the same way;
- volatility, variance and correlation swaps lose most of their sensitivity as
  their fixings accumulate;
- exotic options can jump in either direction.

A report of every trade that will fix soon lets traders anticipate the jumps;
the delta jump screen of the [[id:3976695F-F526-4C2A-80ED-89B54854CF6D][Trade Blotter]] is one.

** Rolling through a fixing date

When a portfolio is rolled forward through a fixing date, the trades must be
fixed. The time component of P&L uses the projection for rates and the forward
for FX, so that it measures the passage of time and not the fixing; the
activity component uses the actual fixing. A rolled non-deliverable trade is
fixed with market data that has already been rolled.

** In P&L attribution

- *Rate reset.* The difference made by the actual fixing replacing the
  projection. In FX it is simply called a fixing.
- *The fixings activity.* The trades that fixed during the day. Fixing a swap
  coupon moves value from the swap to the new cash trade, so the two together
  should show no P&L; a difference points to a trade fixed at the wrong value.
- *Order of activities.* Fixings are attributed after exercises and expiries,
  because a trade that has fixed and then expired no longer depends on the
  fixing.
- *Pairs.* Fixing a non-deliverable forward creates a fixing trade and amends
  the forward. The two net to zero but each looks like a break on its own.

The attribution identity and its activity components are in
[[id:B50355E0-F016-4CA1-9932-404DAF83EA7A][P&L Attribution]]; this note adds only what fixings contribute.
