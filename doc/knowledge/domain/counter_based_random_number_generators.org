#+title: Counter-based random number generators
#+description: A generator that is a keyed bijection of a counter, such as Philox or Threefry: any draw is addressed by key and index, with no state carried from the draws before it.
#+type: knowledge
#+level: cross
#+filetags: :random_numbers:stochastic_processes:knowledge:
#+created: 2026-11-12
#+updated: 2026-11-12

* Summary

A conventional generator, such as the Mersenne Twister, carries a state and
steps it once a draw, so the $n$-th draw is reachable only by the $n - 1$ draws
before it. A counter-based generator is a function $f(\text{key}, \text{counter})$
that maps each counter to random bits through a keyed bijection. The $n$-th draw
is $f(\text{key}, n)$, computed directly. Two consequences follow. Streams are
independent by construction, one per key or per range of counters, with no
seeding procedure to get wrong. And a draw can be /addressed/: its value
depends on its coordinates, not on how many draws came before. ORE Studio's
synthetic market uses the first property for every feed and needs the second for
its shared factors.

* Detail

** The generators

Salmon, Moraes, Dror and Shaw (2011) introduced three families. /Threefry/
reduces the rounds of the Threefish block cipher. /ARS/ uses the AES round
instructions. /Philox/ is a new design built from multiplications whose high and
low halves are mixed, chosen for speed on CPUs and GPUs. The authors report that
all pass the BigCrush battery of TestU01 with margin. The designs borrow from
cryptography, but the reduced-round generators make no claim of cryptographic
security; they are statistical generators.

Philox4x32-10 maps a 128-bit counter, four 32-bit words, under a 64-bit key in
ten rounds, and yields four 32-bit words a block.

** In ORE Studio

=rng_stream= (=ores.analytics.quant=, =math/rng_stream.hpp=) is Philox4x32-10. The
run seed is the key. The series and the =draw_purpose= (=arrival=, =price=,
=market=) hash to a 64-bit stream id in the high half of the counter, and the
draw index fills the low half. So a stream's draws depend on its key alone, not
on the thread, the order the streams were made in or how many exist. A stream
reads its draws in order; the block function =philox4x32_10= is a free function,
so a caller that needs a draw at an index computes that block directly. The
keyed Brownian path does this
([[id:14C79EA2-4E36-4EF2-A0DB-0EB932655029][A keyed Brownian path is a pure function of seed and time]]).

** Other implementations

Thijs van den Berg's Threefry engine for C++11 is distributed in the =sitmo=
package for R, which carries his header (MIT licence, 2014-2015) with the
=sitmo= and =vandercorput= engines (Balamuta, van den Berg and Stubner, 2021).
