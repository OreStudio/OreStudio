* Goal

Report management gets a UX of its own, and its front door is the list of
reports a party runs. The person opens reporting and lands on one row per report
definition their party owns. That row is where the arc of a report becomes
visible to a person: what exists, when it last ran, whether it is healthy, and
when it runs next. Everything else in the group is reached from a row.

The arc is the whole argument of the screen. A definition exists (its name, its
type, its party). It ran (the start and the duration of its latest run). It is
healthy or it is not (one of five health words, derived from that run and never
from the definition). And it will run again, or it waits for a person (its
schedule, and, where the server can answer, when it fires next). A report that
has never run appears on the same list with a word that says so, because nothing
ran and nothing was checked.

Complex inputs keep their own surfaces. A report does not author its
conventions, and this story does not author a party's analytics. Report
management chooses and links out; the authoring UX is somebody else's front
door.

* Acceptance

- Opening reporting lands on the list of reports the party runs, and not on a
  choice of areas.
- One row per definition the party owns, and each row states its name, its
  report type, its schedule, when its latest run started, how long it took, its
  health word, and the errors that run recorded.
- The health word is one of =failed=, =completed with errors=, =running=,
  =never run= and =completed clean=, and it is derived from the definition's
  latest run.
- A row that completed while carrying errors is drawn apart from a row that
  completed clean, without the person opening anything, and the errors cell
  separates errors from warnings.
- A definition that has never run does not read as clean.
- A party that owns no definition is stated as such, in words, and not shown as
  an empty table.
- Choosing the conventions a report binds reaches a list of named convention
  sets and one link out to authoring. Report management does not author a set.
- Authoring a party's analytics is reached the same way: a link out, and not a
  surface on this screen.
- Every read the screen makes is party-scoped from the session, and a person who
  may not read a row is shown no action that would be refused after they press
  it.

* Decisions

- *The front door is the list of definitions, not the ledger of runs.* The
  person arrives with no identifier, so the screen answers from the party alone.
  What is scheduled is the thing a trading desk checks before the first number
  is quoted. The prototype's ledger-first and attention-first variants remain
  readings of the same facts, and they are filters and histories behind this
  list, not the landing page.
- *Health comes from the latest run, never from the definition.* A definition
  can be perfectly healthy while its last run failed. The latest run is the run
  with the greatest start among the runs of that definition.
- *Complex inputs get their own screens.* Report management chooses a named set
  and links out. The pattern is
  [[id:DE6C0BDA-D12A-4E31-AA86-C323F5A7E016][Name the convention sets a report
  chooses]]: a short list, a choice, and one link. This story borrows that
  pattern for analytics rather than inventing a second one, and that story is
  the place the pattern is argued.
- *The analytics half is not authoring.* Analytics need the same treatment
  conventions received, so report management does not carry the analytic
  authoring story. Authoring analytics is its own UX, and it is deferred.
- *A gap is a deliverable.* Where the server cannot answer a column, the answer
  is a recorded gap, not a change to the design. The first task carries the
  initial list.

* What this story does not cover

The rest of the group is already written down, and it is not this story:

- Reading a run's outputs is
  [[id:969C14D2-4280-4D7C-8F51-1D395B521C83][Read what a run produced]].
- Diagnosing a failure, down to the trade, the analytic or the curve, is
  [[id:F58B0FB5-3FD8-4BEB-982E-E54D284C0539][Find out why a run went wrong]].
- The schedule editor, which chooses when a report runs, is
  [[id:933649DD-1F86-4B40-99FD-1D4316D39A46][Choose when a report runs]].
- Running one report now and watching it finish is
  [[id:76431668-8656-4276-9F9B-F292FE45B2CA][Run a report and watch it
  finish]].

This story owns the front door those four are reached from. It does not restate
them, and it does not change them.

* Out of scope

- *The implementation itself.* The first task is a specification and a
  server-side gap analysis. The screen, its endpoint and the reads it needs are
  the work that follows.
- *Authoring conventions.* A person chooses a named set here and follows one
  link out. Creating a set, naming it and binding its kinds belongs to
  conventions.
- *Authoring, editing or publishing a party's analytics.* Report management
  links out to that surface when it exists. No analytic is created, edited or
  published from this story.
- *Any change to the prototype.* The prototype is the design intent. Where it
  shows something the server cannot supply, the gap is recorded, and the
  prototype is not rewritten to hide it.
- *Any schema, service or seeder change to close a gap.* Each gap the first task
  records becomes the task that closes it, and those are their own work.
- *The other six reporting journeys.* Their screens, their writes and their
  states are not designed here.
