* Summary

A feed is a market data producer that ORE Studio runs as a live source.
Every producer, whatever its asset class, implements the one interface
=IFeed=, declared in =ores.marketdata.api/domain/i_feed.hpp=. The
interface lives in =ores.marketdata.api= rather than in the generation
service, so =ores.synthetic= and any future calibration service see the
same type without creating a dependency cycle.

* Detail

** The identity triple

A feed carries three names and each answers a different question.

- =source_name()= is the producer's unique identity within a tenant, for
  example "eur_usd_gbm_1". The feed controller keys its map of running
  feeds by this name.
- =qualifier()= is the published market data key that consumers look up,
  for example "EUR/USD" for an FX spot feed or "USD/SOFR" for an interest
  rate curve family.
- =role()= is the series role the feed publishes under, which is oresmd's
  =curve_role= for a curve. It is empty for a feed without roles, such as
  FX spot.

=feed_conflict_key= joins the qualifier and the role with a unit separator
and defines the identity two feeds must not share while both run. The
controller compares the qualifier and the role directly rather than the key
string, and that matters for one case. A feed whose ORE key does not parse
has an empty qualifier, and an empty qualifier conflicts with nothing even
though its key string is not empty. A feed with an empty qualifier has no
published market data key to protect.

** The lifecycle contract

=start()= blocks the calling thread until =stop()= is called from a
different thread, so a caller always runs a feed on a thread it owns.
=stop()= is thread safe, may be called from any thread, returns
immediately, and lets the tick loop exit asynchronously. Every other method
is single threaded. =publish_count()= returns the number of ticks published
since start.

A feed publishes its own ticks to NATS and the loop invokes no handler on
it, so the publishing path sits inside the producer rather than in the
framework around it.

** The remaining two members

=nats_subject()= is the subject the feed publishes on, derived from its
source name and its binding mode. =kind()= is the factory kind string the
producer registers under, and it is fixed for the producer's lifetime.
