* Goal

The four [[id:CEE25F6D-FE57-43AE-9770-DAD0C1576520][Access]] journeys run in
=ores.web= against the real server:

- [[id:3CA48F40-B601-497B-BA78-9822DBEBA8E5][Know what I may do]] — the
  member reads the roles they hold and the permissions those roles carry.
- [[id:3C753578-BC0E-433C-92B5-38031F050ADC][Change someone's access]] — the
  tenant administrator grants and revokes a colleague's roles.
- [[id:08169F5D-29B0-4758-968A-3C1875203A8A][Shape the role catalogue]] — the
  tenant administrator defines roles and the permissions each one bundles.
- [[id:DF0FBED2-59C1-43DE-945F-D8839884C38D][Ask for more access]] — the member
  asks for a role beyond the one registration gave them, and the tenant
  administrator answers.

This is the fifth group of
[[id:961BE2F3-3FE2-4140-BB18-EBDC89DF0913][Implement the user journeys in ores.web]].
It reuses the journey runtime the Setup group landed and adds no journey
entity. The journey documents stay with the extraction pass, except where
this group's survey proves one wrong.

Three of the four documents were written on 2026-09-23, before the IAM
clean standard and the security hardening, and two of the gaps they record
have already closed: =iam.v1.roles.assign= checks =iam::roles:assign= now,
and the account's credential fields are =:no_wire:=, so no read returns
them. What is left is a shorter and sharper list, and the build is sized
from the survey rather than from the documents as they stand.

* Acceptance

- Every claim in the four journey documents is checked against the tree, and
  every gap is either closed with its evidence or still open with the subject
  that would close it.
- A member reads only their own access: the role reads are self-scoped or
  permission-gated, and a test proves that another account's access is
  refused.
- A role's permission bundle can be written, so the catalogue journey can save
  what it composes rather than reading it back unchanged.
- An assignment read exists, so who granted a role, when, and why is visible
  to the member and to the administrator from the same answer.
- The four journeys run in =ores.web= against the real server, and the shell
  reaches the same subjects for the operations it carries today.
- The role request's gaps are either closed or stated on the screen, and the
  waiting state it needs in =ores.workflow= is recorded as its own decision
  rather than assumed.

* Decisions

- *The survey precedes the build.* Three of the four documents predate the
  clean standard that changed the code they describe, and two of their gaps
  have closed without anybody saying so. A build sized from the documents as
  they stand would re-do work that is done and miss what is left.
- *The reads are closed before any screen shows them.* A read that proves only
  a session and takes an arbitrary account id lets a member read a colleague's
  access. That is a defect with a screen in front of it, so it is fixed at the
  subject rather than guarded in the browser.
- *The catalogue's write is one subject, not a screen.* The journey's whole gap
  is that a role's permissions can be read and not written, so the unit is the
  write and its permission, and the screen is the group's own screen task.
- *A cross-sprint group closes DONE and gets a successor.* The programme's rule
  applies here: if the role request's waiting state does not land in sprint 26,
  what lands is recorded and the rest continues in a successor story.
- *The role request's waiting state belongs to =ores.workflow=.* The journey
  says so: a request that waits on a person keeps its state as a workflow
  instance, and the engine has no state that means "a person decides" yet. That
  is a workflow decision with a workflow owner, recorded here and not assumed.
- *Both clients reach one operation.* =ores.web= and =ores.shell= call the same
  subjects, so the group carries the shell side of its journeys and does not
  move the disagreement to the other client.
- *A journey a person cannot reach from a screen is not finished.* The four
  documents each carry a * Related journeys list, and each entry is a link on
  the screen. A screen that omits them has not implemented the journey.
- *The screens follow the accepted contracts.* The
  [[id:166B2D77-6D04-47D5-A651-5431E1F2E082][UX language]], the
  [[id:6743F6E3-578C-4E4B-9DD6-D5A0DF6734C8][UI design principles]] and the
  [[id:CDE625E2-96AE-4644-9921-282339925F3E][TypeScript Clean Standard]] bind
  the work. The [[id:67E68899-3D29-4861-B4F8-B0C9536EC4C1][entity specification]]
  is void and binds no screen.

* Out of scope

- The journey extraction and the journey documents, which
  [[id:28844DEF-E9AD-45B9-A6E0-CFF51CA1A064][Extract the user journeys that replace the ores.qt screens]]
  owns, except where this group's survey proves one wrong.
- The notification that tells an administrator a request is waiting, and that
  tells the member the answer. The journey records it as a gap of its own.
- The approval workflow behind =system.signup_requires_authorization=, which is
  a workflow in =ores.workflow= and the Entry group's recorded gap.
- Two-factor authentication, and the credential journeys' screens, which are the
  Credentials group's.
