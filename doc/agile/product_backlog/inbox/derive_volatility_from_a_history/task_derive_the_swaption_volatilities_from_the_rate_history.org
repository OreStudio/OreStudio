* Goal

No free source publishes an interest rate volatility quote, exactly as none
publishes an FX one. Interest rates is the first class to run through the shared engine, so
this task adds the three parameters and no measurement of its own: the transform, the
coordinates and the smile model. The values are the platform's own arithmetic, produced by
=orestudio= and held internal.

* Acceptance

- The derivation calls the shared engine and adds no measurement of its own. A measurement it needs is added to the engine, and the FX values are unchanged by the addition.
- The transform is a difference of the rate, in basis points, because a rate may be negative and its change is not a ratio.
- The window is the pillar's own length in trading days and never shorter than the engine's minimum, and a pillar whose history does not cover its window is refused alone, with the returns it needs.
- The level is a realized volatility in basis points a year, times an authored multiplier, and the multiplier is data and not a constant in the code.
- The projection to an expiry and a term is the closed form of the short-rate model the currency's curve runs. The derivation names the model and refuses a process with no closed form instead of approximating it.
- The smile across strikes is a parametric model over the shape of the same returns, and the derivation names the model and its parameters.
- Every value carries the method and the version that computed it, and is stored under =orestudio= with internal standing: no non-system party reads it and no meter counts it.

* Notes

- The projection reads the model's mean reversion and the pillar's two lengths, and nothing else. The
  level is what the measurement read, so the model's volatility and the history's level are one
  number stated twice.
- The driftless limit is =sqrt(T)=. A curve whose mean reversion is below 1e-8 reads the limit, which
  keeps the expression finite without a branch a reader has to trust.
- A pillar the sample does not cover is refused alone and states that the sample is shorter than the
  window; the rest of the ladder stands.
- The matrix is in whatever unit the caller keeps its changes in, so the store's basis points are the
  caller's to state. The engine does not convert between units.
- =latent_market_tests.cpp= carried an undeclared =math= alias that another file in its unity batch
  happened to provide. The new test file changed the batching and the alias went missing, so the
  file now states it.

- The series: the overnight fixing history for the short end, and the history of the term's own rate for a term. A term the curves do not carry is refused, never interpolated from the short end.
- The pieces the engine reuses: =fx_derivation_window=, the moments and the shrink to the prior, =level_multiplier=, the reach, and the method and version a series is stamped with.
- The seeded curves carry a per-currency =kappa= and =sigma= calibrated to the public rates (=synthetic_ir_curve_generation_configs=). The derivation reads the history; the process is what carries the measurement across expiry and term.
- The smokehouse package carries 520 =GBP-LIBOR-12M= fixings, which is the kind of history a term reads, and the public overnight fixings give the short end.

* Plan

The task is two deliverables, and it lands in two pull requests. The first is the derivation: a
rate history and a model's pillars to ATM normal volatilities and a smile, with no store and no
feed. The second is the emission: the adapter that reads a currency's term histories, writes the
quotes under the producer, stamps the method and the version, and holds them internal.

The derivation is cut where the measurement stops. =ir_vol_derivation= holds three things, and none
of them is a measurement: the projection, the smile, and the matrix that reads each expiry through
the engine. The projection is a product of two closed-form factors -- the variance a one-factor
Gaussian short rate accumulates over the option's life, and the damping a swap's tenor imposes by
averaging that rate over its own length -- and it is read through its driftless limit rather than
refused when a curve runs no mean reversion. A process this formula is not (Cox-Ingersoll-Ross, a
two-factor Gaussian, Black-Karasinski) is refused by name, because approximating it with the wrong
formula would produce a number nobody could audit.

The smile claims only what the shape supports: the first-order Gram-Charlier slope, the skewness
over six, in the standardised strike. It fits no curvature, and the header says so, because a smile
that pretends to a convexity it does not fit is worse than a line that admits it is one.

* Result

*Part one, the derivation, is committed at =ff4e275f73=.* =ir_vol_derivation= projects a term's
measured level to a swaption's ATM normal volatility, states the smile the return shape supports,
and reads a term's matrix over an expiry ladder through the measurement engine.

What part two still owes: the adapter that stores the quotes under the producer =orestudio=, stamps
the method and the version, and keeps them internal, so that no non-system party reads them and no
meter counts them. The task stays STARTED until it lands.
