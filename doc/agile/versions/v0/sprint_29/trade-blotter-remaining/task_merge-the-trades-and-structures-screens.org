* Goal

Settle whether a structure is a row in the trade blotter or a screen beside it,
and design the one screen that reads trades and structures together from more
than one perspective: the portfolio and book tree, the counterparty, and the
book.

Today the arc has two screens with no shared surface. The trade blotter lists
trades and cannot show a deal; the structures list shows deals and cannot show a
trade's economics; the deal's page repeats the legs that the blotter already
holds. A person who wants "everything we did with this counterparty today" has to
read two lists and join them by eye.

The deliverable is a decision, not a screen: which shape the screens take, what
each perspective asks, and what the read that serves them states. A prototype
follows the decision, and the screen follows the prototype.

** What already says the answer

Two pages in the tree state the target, and both contradict the two screens this
arc built.

[[id:3976695F-F526-4C2A-80ED-89B54854CF6D][Trade Blotter]] gives the deal grid a
*Trade ID*, a *Structure ID* ("Parent structure identifier; blank for standalone
trades") and a *Strategy* column ("Named structure: RR, Butterfly, Strangle,
Spread, DNT"), and it draws the *Structure view* as a mini-blotter of the
selected deal's legs, tabbed by product type. That is one screen, with the deal
as a row and the legs read inside it.

[[id:FC31FC9C-7A6E-4EFD-8A62-6DE4137F7160][Trade Structures and Deal Composition]]
states the same as a requirement: "the blotter needs to show the deal as one row
and the legs as children of it", and notes that front-office screens offer a
condensed and an expanded presentation of the same tree.

The data layer already agrees. The trade summary view emits rows of two kinds —
=trade= and =structure= — and the blotter's own read filters on =row_kind=. The
screen beside the blotter is a screen that reads the same rows with a different
filter.

** Prior art: the industry does not separate them

| System | Where the structure lives | What it implies for us |
+--------+--------------------------+------------------------+
| FpML =strategy= | Inside the trade: it substitutes for the abstract =product= and holds the legs as =product+ | A same-family structure is a trade whose product is composed, not a record beside the trade |
| FpML =tradePackage= | Around trades: "a bundle of trades collected together into a single unit for reporting", with a header, the trades, allocations and approvals | The untyped container is a reporting and allocation unit over trades, with no economics of its own |
| OpenGamma Strata | Inside the product: a =Swap= composes =SwapLeg= and resolves to =ResolvedSwapLeg= per leg | Keep the trade shape and the pricing shape apart; the screen reads the trade shape |
| Murex MX.3 | Inside the catalogue: "instruments can be combined to design structured products ... processed and risk managed like any native instruments" | A structured product is a first-class instrument, and the same blotter reads it |
| Nasdaq Calypso | Named topics: "1010 - Structured Flows" and "Calypso Trade Blotter" | Structured flows and the blotter are one continuous workflow, not two products |

** The candidate shapes

- *A. One blotter, structures folded in it.* The list reads the summary rows of
  both kinds; a structure row carries a disclosure triangle and its legs unfold
  under it; the columns state the deal for a structure and the leg for a trade.
  The deal page survives as the record a row opens.
- *B. One blotter with a perspective switch.* Shape A, plus a rail or a switch
  that changes the grouping and the columns: portfolio and book tree,
  counterparty, book, product family. Every perspective is the same read with a
  different filter and order.
- *C. Two screens, joined by links.* What exists today, with the blotter gaining
  a structure column and the structures list gaining the trade's economics. The
  cheapest change and the one that keeps the duplication.

The question the task answers is which of A, B or C the next screens take, and
whether the deal page is the record a row opens or a tab inside the blotter.

** The perspectives, and what each asks

| Perspective | The question | What it groups by | Evidence it needs |
|-------------+--------------+-------------------+-------------------|
| Portfolio and book tree | What does this portfolio hold, down to the trade? | The portfolio, book and desk hierarchy | The portfolio of each book, and a roll-up per node |
| Counterparty | What have we done with this counterparty, and when? | Counterparty, then trade date | Counterparty on every row, which the summary carries |
| Book | What is in this book today? | Book, then product family | Book on every row, which the summary carries |
| Deal | What does this structure hold? | The structure, then its legs | The structure rows and the member rows the summary view already emits |
| My day | What did I book or change today? | The trader and the day | The trader and the audit fields |

Two questions follow, and the task answers them rather than assuming: whether a
perspective is a saved view of one list or a different screen, and whether the
rows a perspective shows are structures, legs, or both with the choice folded.

** The read

The blotter's read is one page of =trade_summaries= with a filter. A structure
row already arrives from it. What the screen needs beyond today's read is: the
row kinds it may ask for (trade, structure, or both), the grouping the view
states (portfolio, book, counterparty), and the roll-up a group row carries. The
decision states which of those join the existing read and which need a new one;
it does not add a route per perspective.

** The prototype

A clickable prototype beside =doc/prototypes/trades/= with the chosen shape: the
list with its row kinds, the disclosure of a structure, the perspective switch,
and the trade page and deal page reached from a row. The prototype is the
artefact the screen is built from, as with the pages this arc already built.

** What this task does not do

It does not build the screen, move the routes, or change the read. It decides,
prototypes, and leaves the build to the tasks it files.

* Measurements

This task claims no number yet. The one that matters — how many structures a
party holds against how many trades — is read from the provisioned database when
the screen is designed, with its command and commit recorded then.

* Notes

- The question came from the owner on 2026-10-29, after the structures screens
  were reviewed: "we created a complete separation between trades and structures
  but to me that is not how a real trading system behaves", and "we need a screen
  that combines the portfolio tree and the trades display, possibly from
  different perspectives (e.g. counterparty perspective rather than portfolio
  tree and so on)". The task exists to answer that with evidence rather than
  preference.
- The two screens that exist today were built in
  [[id:2D3C5B23-E6DB-46D5-8732-ACF54987A33A][Build Book a trade]] and its
  structure screens; their shapes follow the prototypes, and the prototypes did
  not answer this question.
- The summary view's two row kinds are also why the blotter's search finds a deal
  by its own reference: a structure is already a row there.
- The counterparty rule is a separate, open question: the store enforces the party
  of a structure's legs and not the counterparty, while the screens state both.
  Whichever shape this task picks has to state what it can enforce.
- What is not prior art: no public Murex or Calypso documentation describes their
  internal trade-package model. The vendor pages are cited for the shape of the
  answer, and the schema documentation for the data model.
