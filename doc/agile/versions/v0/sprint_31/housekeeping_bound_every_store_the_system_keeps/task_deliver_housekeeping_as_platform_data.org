* Goal

The housekeeping configuration reaches an installation the way every other
platform datum does: as a dataset published into the system tenant, so it exists
in every environment, can be re-published, and appears in the DQ screens beside
the platform's other data. The installation's own decisions survive a
republish.

* Acceptance

- The sweep job and the retention policies are artefact types, staged from their own models, with a publish function each
- Both datasets are members of =system_core=, so provisioning publishes them into the system tenant
- A publish reconciles the contract and leaves an operator's period, cadence and state alone
- A republish of unchanged data writes nothing
- The retention policy table carries the system tenant rather than no tenant at all
- No populate script seeds a job definition or a retention policy
- The publish is covered by a pgTAP test

* Analysis

Three kinds of thing travel with this work, and the classification page sorts
them by the question that decides every dataset: who is the author.

| Thing | Author | Level | What follows |
|-------+--------+-------+--------------|
| The sweep job, and the policies' contract | the platform declares it in order to function | platform | published into the system tenant, one copy, never per tenant |
| The installed period, cadence and state | the operator decides | platform row, operator value | must survive a republish |
| The run ledger | produced by use | none | never published |

The third row is why the ledger stays out. It also means the ledger needs a
retention policy of its own: it grows by one row per policy per sweep, which is
the class of growth this story exists to bound.

Two defects surfaced while weighing this, and both are fixed here because the
dataset cannot be written correctly over them:

- *The policy table has no tenant.* Platform data is held in the system tenant
  and read through the shared-reference policy, which is what the job table now
  does. A policy modelled as a tenant-less lookup, like a change reason, is
  readable by any caller and publishes nowhere in particular.
- *Two cadences, neither owned.* The job's cron decides when the sweep fires and
  the policy's =cadence= column is read by nothing. The sweep becomes the tick
  and the policy's cadence becomes its own due check, so a store swept monthly
  is not swept daily and the column means what it says.

* Plan

The route is the one =from_an_entity_model_to_a_published_row= states, and
nothing about it is invented here:

1. Give =ores.scheduler.retention_policy= a system-tenant =tenant_id= and align
   its reads. This precedes publishing, because a publisher writes platform
   rows into the system tenant and a tenant-less table has no such place.
2. Settle the cadence: the job's cron is the tick, and each policy states when
   it is next due. The sweep applies only the policies that are due.
3. Opt =ores.scheduler.job_definition= and =ores.scheduler.retention_policy=
   into the artefact, regenerate the staging tables, and wire their create and
   drop scripts by hand.
4. Register the two artefact types, the two datasets and their =system_core=
   membership. No dependency between them: a policy names a table, not a job.
5. Write the publish functions. The contract fields upsert; the period, the
   cadence and the state are written only when the row does not exist, which
   the contract's =insert_only= mode already provides.
6. Add the scheduler's =publish_from_dq_handler= and register its subjects.
7. Delete the populate seeds, and test the publish with pgTAP: a first publish
   writes the rows, a second writes nothing, and a republish after an operator
   changes a period leaves it changed.
