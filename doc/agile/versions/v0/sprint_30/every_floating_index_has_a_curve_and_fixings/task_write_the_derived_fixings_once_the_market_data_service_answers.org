* Goal

The fixing writer in =ores.synthetic.service= runs as a reconciler. It waits
until the market data service answers, compares the series each curve states
with what the market holds, and writes what is missing. It runs again when the
market data service returns after a loss, and at a regular interval, so a
fresh environment holds the same fixings as a restarted one. The writer
already skips the days a series holds, so a run is safe to repeat.

* Acceptance

- On a fresh environment, without a restart, the synthetic log has no
  =have no series: NATS request failed= line, and the derived fixing series
  equal those after a restart (41 on the reading of this story).
- Stop the market data service while the synthetic service runs, start it
  again, and the writer completes the series it could not write, with no
  restart of the synthetic service.
- The service registry declares that the synthetic service depends on the
  market data service.

* Plan

1. Add a generic =reconciler= to =ores.service=: a pass on a thread of its
   own that runs again, after a wait that doubles to a cap, while it leaves
   work; a kick runs it at once. This is the core of the task Run a service's
   startup work as reconcilers, in the story A service waits for its
   dependencies and recovers when they return, so the writer is its first
   user rather than a writer with a retry of its own.
2. Make the fixing writer keep each plan until it is written, keyed by its
   index, and give the reconciler a pass over the plans not yet written. A
   failure is a reason to try again. A history the plan cannot state is logged
   and dropped, because a second attempt states the same nothing.
3. Prove it on a fresh environment, and by stopping the market data service
   under a running synthetic service.

* Notes

- The writer is =fixing_history_writer= in =ores.synthetic.service=. It ran
  at 03:17:04, while =compass services start= was still waiting for the
  fleet; the start took 486 s and timed out on the market data official
  adapter.
- The pattern this task applies, which the story
  [[id:1DB97F87-9AE4-4848-94CC-3CC9D0B46430][A service waits for its dependencies and recovers when they return]]
  gives every service: a service states its dependencies as data in the service
  registry (=:depends_on:=, today one value); the runner holds a waiting
  state and reports ready to systemd only when each dependency answers; it
  watches each dependency's heartbeat and marks itself degraded when one is
  lost; and its startup work runs as reconcilers, idempotent, that run when
  it becomes ready and again each time it leaves the degraded state.
- The pieces exist: =Type=notify= and =READY=1= in =systemd_notify.cpp=, the
  =heartbeat_publisher=, the telemetry =service_state= with =running= and
  =lost=, and the client credentials provider that already retries the
  start race with a backoff. See
  =doc/knowledge/architecture/service_bootstrap_phases.org=.

* Result

The fixing writer no longer loses a plan to start order. It keeps each plan
until the plan is written, and a reconciler in =ores.service= runs the plans
again, with a wait that doubles from 1 s to 60 s, while the market data
service does not answer. New plans run at once.

- =ores.service/service/reconciler.hpp=: =reconciler=, =reconciler_options=,
  =reconcile_result= and =next_reconcile_delay=, with six unit tests. A
  reason is logged as a warning when it changes and as debug when it repeats.
- =fixing_history_writer= keeps its plans in a map by index; a later plan for
  the same index replaces the earlier one, and a pass removes only the plan it
  wrote.

Acceptance:

- Met: on a fresh environment, without a restart, the tenant holds 41 derived
  interest rate fixing series and 21,320 fixings, the count of a restarted
  one, and the log has no =are not written= line. That start had no race,
  because the market data service answered before the curves started.
- Met: with market data stopped as the curves started, the writer left 3
  plans, said why, and finished 25 s after market data returned, with no
  restart of the synthetic service. The series already held their days, so
  this pass wrote no new fixings; it went through the failure and the retry.
- Not met here: the service registry cannot yet declare that the synthetic
