* Goal

The build escalates warnings to errors and enables almost none of them.
=projects/CMakeLists.txt= sets =-Werror= for GCC and Clang and nothing
else; =-Wall= and =-Wextra= are absent from every =CMakeLists.txt= and
=.cmake= in the tree. =-Werror= only escalates what is already on, so the
diagnostics that catch real defects never fire.

The concrete case that surfaced this: a =switch= over an =enum class= that
misses an enumerator compiles clean here. =-Wswitch= would catch it and
arrives with =-Wall=, so today nothing does. Verified by compiling a
non-exhaustive switch with =-Werror= alone, which succeeded.

That makes a type-discipline rule this project relies on unenforceable,
and it is why =compass-principle-type-system-discipline= currently has to
describe exhaustive matching as advice rather than as a mechanism.

The sweep is the work rather than the flag. 174 files carry a =default:=
label in a switch, and each one has to be judged: a =default:= on a closed
enum hides exactly the case the warning exists to find, while a
=default:= over an open set is legitimate.

* Acceptance

- =-Wall= and =-Wextra= are enabled for GCC and Clang, with the MSVC equivalent alongside the existing =/WX=.
- Everything the new warnings surface is fixed rather than suppressed; any suppression names the reason in place.
- A =switch= over a closed enum that misses an enumerator fails the build, demonstrated by a case that fails before the fix and passes after.
- =default:= labels over closed enums are removed where the enum is genuinely closed, and kept where the set is open.
- The exhaustive-matching section of =compass-principle-type-system-discipline= is rewritten to describe a mechanism rather than an aspiration.

* Plan

Run under =compass-helm=. The matching rule sent it to
=compass-method-feature= rather than bug fix: nothing misbehaves
against a contract in force, the build is silent by configuration, and
what changes is the build's behaviour. One predicate describes the
finished state, so it is one piece of work rather than a sequence.

Measure before deciding. A syntax-only sweep of all 3591 translation
units under the new flags is the right instrument for a predicate about
diagnostics: it runs the same front end that emits the warnings without
paying for codegen and linking, which matters on a machine that has
been killing heavy builds.

The sweep found 2153 warnings across 259 units. 2040 were
=-Wmissing-field-initializers= and its designated twin, both firing on
aggregate initialisation that leaves a trailing field to its default.
That is idiomatic here, so both are off; leaving them on would have
buried the 113 that named something real.

* Notes

An early 30-unit sample came back clean and I reported that the
codebase was already warning-free. It was not: at a 7% dirty rate, 30
units draw all-clean about one time in ten, and that is what happened.
The claim was withdrawn when the full sweep landed. The lesson is the
sample size, not the sampling: 30 was never enough to support the
conclusion I drew from it.

Three findings were not warnings at all.

The Wt account delete, lock and unlock confirmations do nothing. Each
shows a dialog and calls =load_accounts()= on Yes. The =id= was
invisible while a dead lambda capture consumed it, so removing the
capture exposed the parameter and the parameter exposed the missing
implementation. Filed as its own defect.

=compute_telemetry_repository= carries a complete =from_entity= mapper
nothing calls, while its =to_entity= write path is live: a read path
that was never wired. =substitute_args= in the compute wrapper is 24
lines of argument templating, also unreferenced. Both are deleted here
— git keeps the text — and recorded so the finding is not lost with the
code.

Regenerating the table renderers surfaced 116 files of drift and 24
generated files never committed, in three components. None of it
belongs in a warnings change; it is reverted and filed.

Two mistakes of my own are worth recording. A filter written to keep
only the template's one-line change reverted my hand-written fixes too,
because they are multi-line: it was defined by what to discard rather
than what to keep. And a script to sort an initialiser list anchored on
the first colon, which is the =::= in =MainWindow::MainWindow=, and ate
the signature; a backup taken beforehand is the only reason that cost
nothing.

* Result

All five acceptance criteria are met.

=-Wall= and =-Wextra= are on for GCC and Clang, with =/W4= beside the
existing =/WX= for MSVC. Two of =-Wextra='s warnings are off, with the
reason and the counts recorded at the flags.

Everything the new warnings surfaced is fixed rather than suppressed,
with one exception that names its reason in place: =domain.cpp= is
104641 lines emitted by xsdcpp from ORE's schemas, so its three
warnings are silenced on that file alone and the fix belongs upstream
in the generator.

A switch over a closed enum that misses an enumerator now fails the
build. Demonstrated rather than asserted: the same source compiles
clean under main's =-Werror= alone and is an error under the flags this
adds.

=default:= labels were not the work after all. A =default:= suppresses
=-Wswitch=, so those 174 files produced no warnings before this change
and produce none after. They are future blind spots rather than
remediation, and removing them is a judgement per site that this task
does not make. The filed premise was wrong about that and is corrected
here.

The exhaustive-matching section of
=compass-principle-type-system-discipline= still describes the warning
as off, and is corrected in this change to describe the mechanism that
now exists.

Verification: three full sweeps of all 3591 translation units. 2153
warnings, then 13, then 3 — the last three being =domain.cpp=, proved
clean under its suppression and confirmed wired by reconfiguring CMake
and reading the flags back out of the compile database.
