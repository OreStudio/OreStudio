* Acceptance

- The corpus test walks the eight credit documents and pins eight documents and eight trades.
- A closed set of known differences is empty or names each loss with its reason.

* Plan

1. *Port the rates proof.* The credit proof walks =Example_Trades=, imports each
   credit document, exports each trade as a document of its own, imports it
   again and compares the family rows of the two imports. The rows are every
   table the family writes: the header, the six facts, the basket, the legs and
   their amounts and rates, the option rows and their children, the schedules and
   the strikes.
2. *Pin the corpus.* A document that stops importing drops out of the walk, so
   the proof pins eight documents and eight trades.
3. *Close the set of known differences.* The set is empty and fails in both
   directions: a name that differs fails the suite, and a name listed that stops
   differing fails it too.
4. *Show it can fail.* Each trade must render a header row and a leg row, and a
   control changes one stored field and expects the rendering to change.

* Notes

- The proof passed on its first run, so it carries a control. Each trade has to
  render a header row and a leg row, and a second case changes the recovery rate
  of a credit default swap and expects the rendering to change.
- The proof compares the family rows of two imports. It does not compare the
  document text, so a spelling the table does not keep, such as =false= for a
  flag, is not a difference.
- The treasury lock of an agreement's underlying is not in the corpus, so the
  proof does not cover it. The unit 7 task records the limit.
- The proof needs a database only for the rest of the suite; it reads the
  corpus and the mappers.
