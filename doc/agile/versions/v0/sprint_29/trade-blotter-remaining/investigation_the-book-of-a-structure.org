A point-in-time record, written while the story that commissioned it was open, of an [[id:A7F8D9E2-3C4B-4A5D-B67C-8E9F0A1B2C3D][investigation]] into Must the trades under a structure share one book? What the model, the standards and the vendor systems say, and what the blotter tree should do when they do not..

It is a snapshot, not a description of the system. Whatever it concludes that the code must keep doing belongs on the page that owns the subject; cite that page from code, recipes and models, not this one.

* Context

The merged blotter draws a portfolio tree on the left and a trade list beside it,
and its rows are the top-level trades and the structures. A row must sit under the
book its trades belong to. A trade has a book; a structure does not. The
[[id:5057B9A6-66F0-4E4E-967F-DF915809DF3E][merge task]] therefore asked whether
all trades under a structure must share a book.

The question has two halves. The first is whether the rule is true of real
systems, which decides whether we are matching the market or inventing an
invariant. The second is what the tree does when the rule does not hold, which
decides whether the screen can rely on the book at all.

* Methodology

Three bodies of evidence:

- *Our own model.* What the trading tables enforce today, and what the domain
  pages already say about a trade's book, a book move, and nested trade sets.
- *The standards.* FpML, which models the commercial act and the account but
  never a book, and the composition ladder this repository already follows.
- *The vendor systems.* What Murex and Nasdaq Calypso publish about where a
  structured product sits and what a book is for.

The population was measured on the provisioned environment with the queries in
*What the data does*, which also states what the store does not enforce.

* Paths taken

** What our own model says

The trading tables enforce the party of a structure's legs and nothing else about
them. A trade's book lives on =trade_bookings=, which is a part of the trade, and
a structure holds no book column at all.

Three pages in the tree already state the rule from the other side:

- [[id:80760020-5896-4C1C-805D-87B93982F0C0][Nested trade sets]]: "A trade is in one
  book, faces one counterparty, sits in one netting set, and belongs to at most
  one structure." The book is part of the trade's address; the structure is a set
  the trade belongs to. Nothing there says two legs of one structure share an
  address.
- [[id:6CB4879F-3F4B-48CE-A01E-E1B9AEE866B0][Book moves]]: a trade moves between
  books when the two books share a legal entity, a branch, a regulatory book and
  an accounting classification. The boundaries that forbid a move are entity,
  branch, regulatory book and accounting classification. The structure is not one
  of them, so a single leg of a structure can move to another book without the
  structure being involved.
- [[id:FC31FC9C-7A6E-4EFD-8A62-6DE4137F7160][Trade Structures and Deal Composition]]:
  the structure is the customer's commercial unit; the ledger and the netting
  engine "need the leaves, not the container".

** What the data does

Every structure in the provisioned population has its legs in one book, and
nothing required it:

#+begin_src sql
-- Structures, and how many books their legs span.
with books as (
  select s.id, count(distinct b.book_id) as books
  from ores_trading_structures_tbl s
  join ores_trading_structure_members_tbl m
    on m.structure_id = s.id and m.valid_to = ores_utility_infinity_timestamp_fn()
  join ores_trading_trade_bookings_tbl b
    on b.trade_id = m.trade_id and b.valid_to = ores_utility_infinity_timestamp_fn()
  where s.valid_to = ores_utility_infinity_timestamp_fn()
  group by s.id)
select books, count(*) from books group by books order by books;
#+end_src

The answer is one row: 22 structures, each spanning exactly one book. The
population generator books a structure's legs together, so the data cannot tell a
rule from a habit.

** What the standards say

FpML, which is the standard this repository follows for the composition ladder,
has no concept of a book. It carries a =trade=, its party identifiers, its product
and its allocations. The book is an internal management and accounting dimension,
so a market-wide rule about a structure's book cannot come from the standard at
all.

Its two composition mechanisms answer the neighbouring question:

- [[https://cdn.fpml.org/spec/fpml-5-12-4-rec-1/html/confirmation/schemaDocumentation/schemas/fpml-doc-5-12_xsd/elements/strategy.html][=strategy=]] is a *product*: it substitutes for =product= and holds the legs as
  =product+=. The legs of a strategy are not separate trades with their own books;
  they are parts of one product inside one trade.
- [[https://schemas.liquid-technologies.com/FpML/5.10/tradepackage4.html][=tradePackage=]] is "a bundle of trades collected together into a single unit for
  reporting", and it carries =allocations=: "the key block economics are stated
  once within the trade structure, and the allocation data is contained in this
  allocations structure". A package is therefore *designed* to be split across
  accounts after execution.

That is the strongest evidence against the rule as an invariant. The container
FpML defines for a bundle of trades has an allocation as a first-class child, and
an allocation is exactly the act of putting the same economics into several
accounts or books.

* Conclusions

** The rule is a booking convention, not an invariant

In a front-office system a structure is normally booked into one book at
execution, because the book is who owns the P&L and the trader's risk, and the
package is one commercial act. That is a convention about how desks work, not a
property the economics require, and the systems that support allocation are
built to break it: a block trade is executed once and allocated to several
accounts, and FpML puts that allocation inside the package itself.

What the industry does enforce, and what our model already enforces, is the legal
entity. A trade sits on one entity's balance sheet, and a move across entities is
a close-out and a rebook. The counterparty and the netting set are the next
strongest, because credit exposure is measured per counterparty and per netting
agreement, not per book.

A book is the *accounting and management* unit: [[id:6CB4879F-3F4B-48CE-A01E-E1B9AEE866B0][Book moves]] shows that the only
things that pin a trade to a book are the entity, the branch, the regulatory book
classification and the accounting classification. A structure spans no such
boundary by construction, because its legs share a party today.

** So the tree cannot rely on the book to place a structure**

If the blotter's tree must place each structure row under one book, it needs a
rule. There are three, and only the third is honest:

1. *Enforce one book per structure.* It makes the tree simple and it is wrong: it
   forbids allocation, it forbids moving one leg, and it states a rule the
   economics do not have. It is also a booking rule, so it belongs in the store
   and not in a screen, which would make the same mistake as the party-only
   counterparty rule in reverse.
2. *Put the structure in the book of its first leg.* It hides the disagreement
   and puts a structure in a book that may hold none of its risk.
3. *Place the structure at the lowest common ancestor of its legs' books.* One
   book places it under that book, which is the common case and needs no special
   drawing. Books under one desk place it under that desk. Anything wider places
   it under the portfolio. The structure row states that it spans, so a reader
   knows the placement is a summary and not a fact about one book.

The flat option is unaffected: it draws leaves, and every leaf has one book.

* Recommendations

- *Do not enforce one book per structure.* It is not a rule of the market, it is
  not a rule of our model, and it would forbid allocation and book moves that the
  rest of the tree already allows.
- *Place a structure at the lowest common ancestor of its legs' books*, and state
  on the row that it spans books when it does. The common case is one book and
  draws exactly as it does today.
- *Keep the flat option*, which removes the question for a reader who is reading
  leaves.
- *Decide the invariant that does matter.* The party is enforced; the counterparty
  and the netting set are stated but not enforced. If a rule is wanted, it is one
  of those, and it belongs in the store with a refusal that names it, not in a
  screen.
- *Record the durable conclusion* on
