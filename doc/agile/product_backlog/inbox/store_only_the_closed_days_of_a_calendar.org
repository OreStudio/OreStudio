This page is a [[id:671F18E4-E09C-4B3B-BD24-D33DF8AE38A6][capture]] in the *inbox* bucket of the product backlog — a pre-sprint idea, not yet pulled into a sprint as a story.

* What

Decide what a materialised calendar must store. Today =ores_refdata_calendar_dates_tbl= holds a row for every day from two years ago to 2060, for each calendar a currency or a pair convention names, in each tenant: 35 calendars and 473,025 rows for Acme. Each row is a full bitemporal entity, with the insert trigger's checks, a GiST exclusion constraint and history. The two readers, the tenor resolution service and the FX forward ladder, keep only the closed days, which are 148,720 rows, or 31%. Weekends are 135,000 of these, and the calendar's weekend mask already gives them.

Options, from the smallest change:
- Store only the closed days, with a watermark for each calendar that says how far it is built.
- Store nothing, and build each calendar's closed days from its rules, exceptions and base calendar when it is read, with a cache in the service.

* Why

On 2026-11-10 one calendar's 13,515 days, inserted set-based and rolled back, cost 2.66 s through the trigger, 1.03 s with the trigger off, and 0.16 s with the trigger off and the exclusion constraint dropped. The machine was busy with a build, so these are ratios, not baselines. Storing only the closed days writes about a third of the rows, and storing none writes no rows. The calendar dates are shown in the UI, with their history, so this is a product decision.
