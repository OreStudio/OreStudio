This page documents a [[id:F44766FA-1E9C-416F-849A-E131101B2ECA][user journey]]: A trader undoes or supersedes a published curve after an error, with the reason kept and the approver's decision where the kind asks for one.

A user journey describes what one person is trying to do, before anything is
built. It is a design document that outlives any sprint, so it names the
screens, the entity fields each screen shows, and the operations and messages
each step needs.

* Goal

"That curve is published and wrong. I want it taken back, or a corrected one in
its place, and I want the record to say why." The journey is done when the
published curve is withdrawn or superseded, the reason is kept against the act,
and the consumers that read it have been told.

* Actor and trigger

The actor is the trader who owns the published curve. Where the kind's
=approvals_required= is one or more, an approver holding the kind's
=decide_permission_code= is a second actor on the same act, exactly as in
[[id:A85B6C4E-0F93-4410-BF2E-7167722849ED][Decide a publish]]. The trigger is
an error found after publication: a stale pillar, a wrong quote, a recipe
changed after the fact. The journey starts at the published curve and ends when
the withdrawal or the replacement is live. It undoes what
[[id:53F31919-86FA-4E35-8594-9E72197C4A3B][Publish the approved set]] did.

Withdrawal and replacement are two answers to one problem. Withdrawal takes the
curve out of use; replacement puts a corrected version in its place. The
approval count for a withdrawal is configurable like a publish's: the kind
carries =approvals_required=, so a tenant may let a trader withdraw alone
(count zero) or require one or more approvals. A refusal is final and an
approval is reversed rather than edited, as for every kind.

* Steps

1. *Find the published curve.* The trader opens the curve by its =oresmd_uri= or
   finds it in the curve list. The system shows the series and its latest
   points. The read that serves the curve states whether it is published, and
   an unpublished curve is the one to look at twice. No read lists the tenant's
   published curves; see /What is missing/.
2. *Read what is published.* The trader reads the points the system is using.
   The snapshot read states the instant a person published the set, the account
   that published it, the as-of the set was built at, and the request that
   authorised it, so the trader reads the act and not only the values.
3. *Read the trail.* The trader opens the curve's history and reads every
   version with the field-level difference from the one before, so the error
   and the version that introduced it are both visible.
4. *State the reason and choose.* The trader writes why the curve must be
   undone and chooses *Withdraw* or *Replace*. For a replacement the trader
   names the corrected set or its as-of.
5. *Raise the request.* The trader chooses *Raise withdrawal request* or
   *Raise replacement request*. The system raises an approval request for the
   signed-in trader, waiting, under the withdrawal kind. No market-data
   withdrawal kind is seeded; see /What is missing/.
6. *Watch for the decision, or publish at once.* Where the kind's
   =approvals_required= is zero the withdrawal is immediate; where it is one or
   more, an approver decides in the queue. An approver who is also the asker is
   refused.
7. *Apply the withdrawal or the replacement.* Once approved, the system makes
   the withdrawal or the replacement live: the withdrawn curve stops being the
   one pricing and reporting use, or the replacement becomes it. No such
   operation exists; see /What is missing/.
8. *See the consumers change.* The consumers that read the curve pick up the
   change at their next read. The system tells them a published curve changed.
   No notification kind exists for this; see /What is missing/.
9. *Confirm the audit.* The trader re-reads the history and sees the withdrawal
   or the replacement as a new version, with the reason and the decision
   attached. A withdrawal is not a deletion: the published version stays in the
   history.
10. *Leave.* The trader goes back to the curve list.

Steps 5 to 7 are the ones that carry state: the request waits on a person
between the ask and the act, and the act is applied by the component that owns
the kind. Nothing here overwrites history; the withdrawal is a new version.

* What is missing

1. *There is no withdrawal or replacement operation.* A published curve cannot
   be taken out of use or superseded by a corrected one. The candidates are
   =marketdata.v1.curve_bootstrap.withdraw= and
   =marketdata.v1.curve_bootstrap.replace=. Until they exist the action
   controls are disabled, and the only write,
   =marketdata.v1.ops.republish_curve=, would silently write a new set
   with nothing withdrawn and nothing linked.
2. *There is no withdrawn state.* A publish records the instant, the publisher,
   the as-of and the request, so a reader knows which set is live. Nothing marks
   a set withdrawn or superseded, so a withdrawal has no column to write and
   there is no read that excludes a withdrawn set. The candidate is a withdrawn
   instant and actor on the publish record, or a withdrawing record of its own.
   Until it exists a withdrawal would be a new publish, and the record would
   read as two live sets rather than one replaced by the other. The decision
   *Withdrawal and supersession* below states the state the record holds.
3. *There is no market-data withdrawal kind.* One kind is seeded,
   =iam.role_grant=, and it does not withdraw a curve. The candidate is a kind,
   =marketdata.curve_withdrawal=, with =decide_permission_code=,
   =comment_on_approve= and a configurable =approvals_required=, so a tenant
   may allow a trader to withdraw alone or require approvals. Until then the
   raise cannot be given a kind and the controls are disabled. The decision
   below names the kind, its count and who may withdraw.
4. *Nothing links a replacement to the version it supersedes.* A corrected
   curve written under the same =oresmd_uri= would be a new set of observations
   with no pointer from the old version to the new. The candidate is a
   =superseded_by= link or a =valid_to= on the published record. Until then the
   history shows two versions in time order and cannot say which one replaced
   the other.
5. *No notification kind tells consumers a curve changed.* The consumers of a
   published curve are not told it was withdrawn or replaced. The candidate is
   an =ores.inbox.notification_kind= such as =marketdata.curve_withdrawn=,
   seeded beside the kind. Until then the consumers panel states that nobody is
   told, and the change is found only at the next read. The recipients and the
   trigger are stated under *The publish notice* in
   [[id:53F31919-86FA-4E35-8594-9E72197C4A3B][Publish the approved set]].
6. *No read maps a published curve to its consumers.* Nothing returns which
   people or services read a given published curve, so the screen cannot list
   who is affected. The candidate is a read over the party
   subscriptions. Until then the consumers panel names the roles that
   read curves in general, not the ones bound to this curve.
7. *There is no delta against the last published.* A replacement is a change,
   and there is no baseline to show what it changes. The candidate is the delta
   read of the first journey. Until then the change panel is shown empty and
   the trader describes the change in the reason.

* The decision

** Withdrawal and supersession

*** The rule

A withdrawal is an act on the published set, and it is a new version of the
publish record. It is not a deletion and not an edit. The act writes four facts
on the record of the request that published the set: the withdrawing account,
the instant, the reason, and the withdrawal request that authorised the act. The
record keeps its values and its publisher, so the set a desk priced from is
still readable afterwards.

A supersession is the same act with a successor. The corrected set is published
by its own publish request, and the publish record of the set it replaces
carries the link: the successor's request and its published instant. A reader
therefore reads one live set and one superseded set, and never two live sets.

The state is on the publish record, and not on a separate entity:

| State      | What the record holds                                          | What a reader sees                      |
|------------+----------------------------------------------------------------+-----------------------------------------|
| published  | A published instant, a publisher and an authorising request     | The set in use                          |
| superseded | The published facts, and a successor request and instant        | A set that was in use and is not any more |
| withdrawn  | The published facts, a withdrawing account, an instant and a reason | A set taken out of use, and its reason |

The reader reads the state beside the values. A read of a withdrawn curve
returns its last published values and the words that say it is withdrawn, with
the instant and the reason. It does not return an empty series, because an empty
series cannot be told from a curve that was never published.

*** Who may withdraw

The maker is the account that holds the kind's raise permission. The checker is
an account that holds the kind's decide permission and did not raise the
withdrawal. The publisher of the set has no special right to withdraw it: an
account that did not publish the set may take it out of use, because the reason
is the error and not the person. Any account that may raise a publish may raise
a withdrawal.

A tenant sets the kind's approval count. The recommended value is one, so a
second pair of eyes reads the reason before the set leaves use. The alternative
is a count of zero, for a tenant whose operators act alone at night; the price
is that the record then names one person for both the error and the act.

*** How a replacement differs from a withdrawal

A replacement puts a corrected set in place and keeps the curve in use. The
corrected set is a candidate at a new as-of, it is raised as a publish request,
and it is decided as every publish is. The replacement is not a second kind and
not a second operation: it is a publish whose subject is the successor, and the
link from the old record is what makes it a replacement.

A withdrawal takes the curve out of use and leaves it out until somebody
publishes again. Pricing and reporting read a withdrawn curve as last-known,
and they state the withdrawal. A curve that must resume needs a publish, and a
publish needs a request.

The trader chooses between the two acts at step 4 of this journey. They are not
two screens.

*** Why this answer

The publish record already holds the facts of the act: the request, the
publisher and the instant. A withdrawal adds the end of the act to the same row,
so the history of a curve is one stream of versions of one row and not a join of
two tables. It also makes a list of published curves answer one question with
one read: which set is in use for each series, and what ended it.

*** The alternative

A withdrawal is its own entity, =ores.marketdata.curve_withdrawal=, keyed by its
own request and pointing at the publish record it ends. It carries the reason
and the instant, and the publish record keeps the published facts alone. The
screens then join the two reads. This is the answer if a tenant needs many
withdrawals for one publish, which the model does not expect: a set leaves use
once.

*** Open questions

1. Does the withdrawal apply when the last decider approves, or does the trader
   apply it? The recommendation is that it applies on the decision. A publish
   waits for the trader because the trader owns the set that becomes live; a
   withdrawal takes a risk out of the book, and a second press adds a wait and
   no check. The alternative is the publish's path, with an apply call that
   names the approved withdrawal request.
2. Which reasons may a withdrawal name? The recommendation is a lookup of its
   own, seeded with the reasons a curve leaves use: a wrong quote, a stale
   pillar, a recipe changed after the fact, a duplicate publish, and a
   correction. A withdrawal names a code and a comment, as a hold does.
3. May a withdrawn set be published again unchanged? The recommendation is no:
   the route is a new candidate at a new as-of, so the record never reads as if
   the error never happened. A tenant that must restore exactly the same values
   raises a publish of that set, and the reason states the restoration.
4. Does a withdrawal request expire? The recommendation is that it does not: a
   withdrawal that nobody answers leaves a wrong set in use, and the ask must
   stay visible rather than close in silence. The alternative is the two-week
   window the publish kind carries.
