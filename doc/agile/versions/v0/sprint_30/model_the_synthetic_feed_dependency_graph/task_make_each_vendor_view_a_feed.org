* Acceptance

- A view is a feed row of its vendor's collection, in an FX volatility folder, naming its latent surface and its vendor's noise profile; the surface's contributor links are gone.
- The latent dataset publishes one view for each surface and contributor, into that contributor's vendor collection.
- The dashboard and the Provider tab list and count views under their vendor; a view has its own page: Details, Graph, Messages and History, and Stop and Start.
- The surface's Contributors tab becomes a read-only Views list, each linked to its page.
- The service runs views as today: the vendor's source holds the shared surface and publishes the view's points; the graph's view nodes are the view rows.
- A run's start and stop are written to the node's log, with why: demanded, by hand, released.
- Live: Bloomberg lists 14 views; stopping one stops that vendor's quotes of that pair; the view's log shows its run starting and stopping.

* Plan

One PR, in slices that each end in a check. Agreed with the owner on
2026-11-23: a view is a feed of its vendor's collection, and its name and page
say plainly that it is one vendor's noise over a latent surface.

1. *The view entity.* The link =fx_vol_surface_feed_contributor= becomes the
   feed =fx_vol_view=, the way the stopped source became the stopped feed node:
   the model is renamed, so the generated layers, the permissions and the roles
   follow. It keeps its two inputs, =feed_id= (the latent surface) and
   =contributor_id= (the vendor's noise profile, still one per vendor and
   shared by its pairs, referenced and not copied), and gains what a feed of a
   collection has: =config_id= (the vendor's collection), =folder_id=,
   =source_name= (=synthetic.<vendor>.fx.fx_option.<pair>=), =enabled= and
   =auto_start=. Check: codegen clean; SQL and codec tests.
2. *The publish.* The latent dataset stages one view for each surface and
   contributor, and the publish writes each into its contributor's vendor
   collection, under an FX volatility folder there, named by the scheme. The
   clean =demo= contributor becomes a =demo= view with its zero-noise profile.
   Check: the names test covers views; a publish test that Bloomberg's
   collection holds 14 views.
3. *The service.* The registry reads view rows where it read the links; the
   offers, the holds and the graph's view nodes come from them, the view node
   carrying the view's own id. Run time is unchanged. A run's start and stop
   are written to the node's log with why: demanded and run by a source,
   started or stopped by hand, released when the demand drops or the source
   stops. Check: service tests; graph cases.
4. *The screens.* The dashboard and the Provider tab list and count views
   under their vendor. A view page: Details (its surface and its noise profile,
   linked), Graph, Messages, History, and Stop and Start. The surface's
   Contributors tab becomes a read-only Views list linking each view; adding a
   vendor to a surface is adding a view. BFF and =ores.http= serve the view
   entity. Check: web tests.
5. *Proof.* Rebuild, recreate, provision; Bloomberg lists 14 views; stopping
   one stops that vendor's quotes of that pair; the view's log shows its run
   starting and stopping.

* Result

A vendor's view of a latent surface is now a feed of the vendor's collection,
=fx_vol_view=, named =synthetic.<vendor>.fx.fx_option.<pair>=, filed in an FX
volatility folder of that collection. It references the latent surface it reads
and the vendor's shared noise profile. The publish writes one view for each
surface and contributor; the registry, the offers and the graph read view rows,
and the graph's view node carries the view's own id. The Provider tab lists and
counts views under their vendor. A view has its own page whose calibration tab
says plainly that it is the vendor's noise over the surface, linking both
inputs, with Graph, Messages, History, and Stop and Start. The surface's
Contributors tab is now a read-only Views list.

The node messages now have one architecture. The surface's diagnostics
interface became =node_journal= (=say=, =raise=, =clear=), the one place every
part of the service reports a node's events, with telemetry its one
implementation. The runner says why each run starts and stops, and each entry
carries the market's operational time. The design page states the rule that
keeps the journal apart from the scheduler trace: what a person reads goes in
the journal, what a chart plots goes in the trace.

Live, on a recreated database: Bloomberg lists 14 views; a view's log shows its
run started by demand; a stop by hand releases the run and records who stopped
it and why; a start runs it again, and the log shows each step with its market
time.
