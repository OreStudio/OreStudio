This page documents a [[id:F44766FA-1E9C-416F-849A-E131101B2ECA][user journey]]: A trader or quant reads what a published curve is built from and what its mathematics say - the market pillars, the zero rate, the implied forward, the discount factor and the shift at each pillar, with the conventions they rest on.

A user journey describes what one person is trying to do, before anything is
built. It is a design document that outlives any sprint, so it names the
screens, the entity fields each screen shows, and the operations and messages
each step needs.

* Goal

"I want to read this curve properly: the rates the market quoted at each pillar,
the zero rate and the forward the curve implies, the discount factor I would
price with, and the conventions all of it rests on." The journey is done when
the person can quote the curve's zero rate at a pillar and say why it differs
from the market.

* Actor and trigger

The actor is the trader or the quant. The trigger is a trade to price, a curve
that moved, a forward that looks wrong, a curve a colleague published, or a
question about compounding and interpolation. The journey starts at *Market data
/ Curve* — reached from the instrument search — and ends when the person leaves
the page.

The journey reads and changes nothing. It reads a curve that already exists: a
published set, or the curve a blueprint last built. It is not the candidate check
(that is *Check a curve's fit*, where a candidate is judged against the quotes it
came from) and not the publish record (that is *Published curves*).

* Steps

1. *Open the curve.* The person opens *Market data / Curve* on =AUD-AONIA*. The
   system states the party, the kind, the publish the curve came from, the spot
   date and the as-of.
2. *Read the conventions.* The system states the index, the day count, the
   compounding, the interpolation, the calendar, the spot lag and the pillar
   count, because the numbers below mean nothing without them.
3. *Read the market pillars.* One row per pillar: the tenor, the maturity date
   the server computed, the year fraction, the rate the market quoted, the
   provider that sent it and its age.
4. *Read the bootstrapped curve.* One row per pillar: the zero rate, the implied
   forward, the discount factor and the shift. The client draws these; it does
   not compute them, because two clients implementing discount-factor
   interpolation would drift and one would be wrong about compounding.
5. *Read the shift.* The system states the distance between the bootstrapped
   curve and the market at each pillar. The person does not assume it is an
   applied parallel shift: the column says what it is.
6. *Compare with the prior close.* The person turns on the comparison. The
   system states the prior publish's value at each pillar and the change in
   basis points, and draws it over the current curve as a dashed line.
7. *Change the axis.* The person switches the metric (zero rate, forward,
   discount factor) and the axis (tenor, maturity). The numbers do not change;
   only the drawing does.
8. *Meet a stale verdict.* The system marks the whole curve stale when its
   publish is older than the threshold, and says how old it is.
9. *Meet no published curve.* The party has no published set. The system says so
   and draws nothing: it does not show a previous publish as if it were current.
10. *Leave.* The address holds the instrument, the as-of, the metric and the
    comparison.

* Operations and messages

#+ATTR_HTML: :class hug-leading
| Step | Operation | Subject | Status |
|------+-----------+---------+--------|
| Open the curve | the published curve of the party | =marketdata.v1.ops.get_published_curve_sets= | exists |
| Read the conventions | the curve's conventions and pillars | =marketdata.v1.ops.get_curve_analytics= | missing |
| Read the bootstrapped curve | zero rate, forward, discount factor, shift | =marketdata.v1.ops.get_curve_analytics= | missing |
| Compare with the prior close | the prior publish's analytics at the same pillars | =marketdata.v1.ops.get_curve_analytics= | missing |
| Read a discount factor only | the bootstrapped point a curve config produces | =marketdata.v1.ops.compute_curve= | partial |

=marketdata.v1.ops.compute_curve= answers ={point_id, date, discount_factor}= and
nothing else, and only for a bootstrap config. The year fraction, the zero rate,
the implied forward, the shift and the conventions are not stated anywhere, so
each client would derive them and the two would disagree.

* What is missing

- =marketdata.v1.ops.get_curve_analytics= — the conventions, the market pillars
  (tenor, maturity, year fraction, rate, provider, series) and the bootstrapped
  rows (tenor, maturity, year fraction, discount factor, zero rate, implied
  forward, shift), plus the publish it came from. The arithmetic is stated once
  in the protocol so two clients cannot implement it differently.
- The decisions the arithmetic needs: whether =zero_rate= is always continuously
  compounded (the prototype assumes it is) while =compounding= describes the
  curve's own convention; what =implied_forward= means at the last pillar (the
  prototype states the segment ending at the pillar, the first row carrying its
  zero rate); and what =shift= means (the prototype states curve less market, not
  an applied shift).
- The comparison read: the same analytics for the prior publish, so the delta is
  the server's and not a client subtraction.
