* Summary

The quotes view is the market as the vendors send it: one row for each datum a
vendor quotes, updated in place as it ticks, whatever the asset class. FX spot,
IR pillars, FX option vols and swaption vols are rows of one table. The session
watches the feeds it may watch for display; the system tenant watches every feed
of the catalogue, routed or not. A picked row draws its last hour: one history
read, then each live tick.

* Detail

** The question

Is the view one row for each datum, or one row for each series that opens into
its points? A vol surface quotes many points, and a flat table may bury the FX
and the rates around it; a grouped table hides the points until a person opens
them. And how does a person see one series as several vendors quote it?

The product owner's answers (2026-11-19): both layouts, as layouts of one screen;
and the vendors are a selection, one or several, not a layout. With one vendor
picked, a row is its value, change, time and age; with several, a row has one
column for each picked vendor, the spread between them and the age of the newest
quote, so GBP/USD reads as Bloomberg and Tradeweb quote it side by side. A picked
row draws one line for each picked vendor.

** The layouts and the vendors

Switch the layout in the page, or with =?layout=; pick the vendors on their cards,
or with =?vendors=bloomberg,tradeweb=.

| Layout   | Name        | What it shows |
|----------+-------------+---------------|
| =datum=  | Every datum | One row for each datum the picked vendors quote. |
| =series= | By series   | One row for each series, with its point count and, for several vendors, how many points each quotes; a series opens into its points. |

The asset class filter is the asset class codes table, the one market series and
routes name (FX, Interest Rate, Credit, Equity, Commodity, Inflation, Bond,
Scripted, in its display order), and the instrument type narrows it. The asset
class catalogue maps each instrument type's oresmd namespace onto a code: =fx= to
=fx=, =ir= to =interest_rates=.

** The states

- =?state=party= — Acme Corporation Plc, which may watch four feeds for display.
- =?state=system= — the system tenant, which watches all seven feeds, including
  three that no Acme party routes to.
- =?state=none= — a party with no subscription that holds the display right. The
  page says why the list is empty and where a subscription is granted.
- =?state=silent= — the party's view with one vendor that stopped ticking: its
  age grows in the vendor list and its quotes fade.

** What is fake

Every value, time and age is mock. The ticks are a random walk on a timer, and the
hour behind a picked row is made up. The datums each vendor quotes, and which
feeds a party may watch, are fixed in the page. Each
vendor's quote of a datum has its own value and time, as the feed subjects
keep them apart.
