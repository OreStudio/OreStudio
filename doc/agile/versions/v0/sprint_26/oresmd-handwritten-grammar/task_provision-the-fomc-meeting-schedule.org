* Acceptance

- A new tenant's tenor convention resolutions carry their schedule_code and schedule_step_count once its base datasets are published.
- A new tenant has the US.FOMC calendar, the central_bank_meeting diary entry type and the central_bank_meeting calendar events.

* Plan

Decided with the owner on 2026-10-04: the chain reaches a tenant by dataset
publish, as calendars already do. Lookup vocabulary is copied at provisioning;
market reference data is published.

1. The provisioner copies the diary entry types. They are vocabulary with no
   dependencies, as the calendar names and tenor kinds it already copies are.
2. The =refdata.calendars= dataset gains =US.FOMC=, so the calendar publish
   brings it after the countries.
3. A new dataset, =refdata.tenor_schedules=, publishes =ROLL_QUARTER= and
   =FOMC_MEETING= and depends on =refdata.calendars=. Its publish function
   also writes the system tenant's =schedule_code= and =schedule_step_count=
   onto the tenant's resolution rows, because those columns can only be set
   once the schedules exist. The provisioner keeps copying the resolutions
   without them.
4. A new dataset, =refdata.calendar_events=, publishes the meeting dates and
   depends on =refdata.calendars=.
5. Both datasets join the =base= bundle after =refdata.calendars=, with their
   artefact types and publish subjects registered.
6. A database test provisions a tenant, publishes the chain, and resolves =1F=
   to the first meeting on or after spot.
7. A live ACME run shows the =usdsofrfomc= feed publishing.

* Notes

** The cause

The feed =synthetic.2026realistic.usdsofrfomc= has published nothing in a
provisioned tenant since at least 2026-08-12. Its template starts with the
pillar =SPOT= to =1F=, and =1F= to =8F= are SPECIAL tenors of the convention
=RATES_SPOT_FOMC=. A resolution row of that convention names the schedule
=FOMC_MEETING= and a step count, so =1F= is the first meeting on or after spot.

On 2026-10-04, a database query found that the system tenant has these rows,
but the ACME tenant has the rows without =schedule_code= and
=schedule_step_count=. The provisioner's copy in
=projects/ores.sql/create/iam/iam_tenant_provisioner_create.sql= does not list
the two columns. A row with neither column falls back to the calendar axis,
which is spot plus the row's offset. The offset is =DAY 0=, so in the tenant
every FOMC tenor resolves to spot. The first pillar then has zero length.
Before the market tick change, the curve pricer refused it with "year_fraction
must be positive". After that change, =make_pillar_quote_key= refuses it first.
The cause is the same.

The provisioner also copies no calendar events, and row security on
=ores_refdata_calendar_events_tbl= shows a tenant only its own rows. So after
the columns are fixed, the resolver finds no meeting dates and stops with "no
event-lookup dates were supplied". Both gaps must close.

The events cannot simply be copied. The insert trigger of
=ores_refdata_calendar_events_tbl= checks that the tenant has the event's
calendar and its diary entry type. On 2026-10-04 the ACME tenant had 60 of the
system tenant's 61 calendars, all but =US.FOMC=, because its calendars come
from a dataset publish rather than the provisioner. It had no diary entry
types, because nothing copies them. So the tenant also needs =US.FOMC= and
=central_bank_meeting= before its meeting dates.

No pillar check stops a curve from publishing past data. A curve's dates are
calculated from its own as-of date, and only a pillar of zero length from spot
is refused.

** The dependency chain

Each step's insert trigger checks the step before it, in the same tenant:

1. countries, which a tenant gets only from a dataset publish, after
   provisioning;
2. the =US.FOMC= calendar, whose trigger checks its country;
3. the diary entry type =central_bank_meeting=, which nothing copies;
4. the tenor schedule =FOMC_MEETING=, which checks the calendar and the diary
   entry type;
5. the resolution rows' =schedule_code=, which checks the tenor schedule;
6. the meeting dates, which check the calendar and the diary entry type.

The provisioner runs before the publish, so it cannot create step 2. It copies
no tenor schedules either, so =ROLL_QUARTER= is missing too, and the
=CREDIT_CDS_IMM= resolutions lose their schedule in the same way as the FOMC
ones.

** What was built

- The provisioner copies the diary entry types.
- =refdata.calendars= carries =US.FOMC=.
- =refdata.tenor_schedules= and =refdata.calendar_events= are new datasets in
  the =base= bundle. Both depend on =refdata.calendars=. Their artefact tables
  are generated from the =tenor_schedule= and =calendar_event= models, and
  their seeds copy the system tenant's own rows, so the schedules and the
  meeting dates are listed once.
- The calendar event publish matches events on calendar, date and diary entry
  type. A tenant's event keeps its own id.
- The tenor schedule publish sets the schedule columns of the tenant's
  resolutions from the system tenant's rows. A second publish changes none of
  them.
