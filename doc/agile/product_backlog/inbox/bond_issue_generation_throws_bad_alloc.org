#+title: Generating a bond issue fails with std::bad_alloc
#+description: The first bond issue of a generated population fails the composite write with std::bad_alloc, once. It has not been reproduced: the retry of the step drew different terms and completed. Whether the fault is value-dependent or transient under load is open, and the replay of the original config is the experiment that settles it.
#+type: capture
#+level: cross

This page is a [[id:671F18E4-E09C-4B3B-BD24-D33DF8AE38A6][capture]] in the *inbox* bucket of the product backlog — a pre-sprint idea, not yet pulled into a sprint as a story.

* What

The first bond issue of a generated trade population fails its composite write
with =std::bad_alloc=. It has happened once and has not been reproduced. The
request is about a kilobyte, so payload size does not explain it. Two
explanations remain live, and the evidence so far does not choose between them:

- *A size taken from a drawn value.* The retry of the step drew different bond
  terms, so its success says nothing about the failing draw.
- *A transient allocation failure while the host was loaded.* At 14:05 two
  other environments were building on this machine.

* Why

The failure stops a provisioning run, and the message names no cause beyond
=std::bad_alloc=. The step-level retry recovers, so it is not urgent. But if the
cause is an allocation sized from data, the next draw can hit it again, and the
message cannot tell anyone why.

** What is proven

- One failure, at =trading.v1.ops.put_bond_issue_composite=, in the trading
  service's =trade_operations_handler=, at 2026-11-13 14:05:15.767.
- The request is small: the bond issue builder emits 1 leg, 2 amounts, 1 rate
  and 1 schedule row, and the writer sends no schedule dates, amortisations,
  call dates or conversion targets
  (=ores.trading.api/src/builders/bond_builders.cpp=, =bond_issue_builder::build=).
- The retry of the step completed: 17 of 17 steps, 6,541 trades, 37 bond
  issues, 80 surface publishes.

** What is not proven

- That the fault is value-dependent. The retry mints a *new* config, and the
  drawn terms are seeded by the config id, so the retry is not a replay of the
  failing inputs. Its success shows only that a different draw passes.
- That the service was short of memory, or not. The trading cgroup
  (=memory.max= 8 GiB) now shows =memory.peak= 276 MiB, but that peak may cover
  only the process started at the 14:10 restart: the service was restarted, and
  the sandbox could not query systemd to learn whether the cgroup was recreated.
  So the peak is not evidence that the failing process stayed small.
- That the fault is transient. It happened once, under a loaded host, and one
  occurrence cannot separate the two explanations.

** Evidence

- Failing call: =trading.v1.ops.put_bond_issue_composite=, in the trading
  service's =trade_operations_handler=, at 2026-11-13 14:05:15.767:
  =ores.dev.merry_newton.trading.v1.ops.put_bond_issue_composite failed:
  std::bad_alloc=.
- The request is small: the bond issue builder emits 1 leg, 2 amounts, 1 rate
  and 1 schedule row, and the writer sends no schedule dates, amortisations,
  call dates or conversion targets (=ores.trading.api/src/builders/bond_builders.cpp=,
  =bond_issue_builder::build=).
- The trading service's cgroup (=memory.max= 8 GiB) now reports =memory.peak=
  276 MiB
  (=/sys/fs/cgroup/user.slice/user-1000.slice/user@1000.service/app.slice/ores.trading.service-merry_newton.service/memory.peak=).
  That peak is consistent with the service never being near its limit, but it
  may cover only the process restarted at 14:10, so it does not settle whether
  the failing process was short of memory.
- The retry of =generate_trades= on the same run completed: 17 of 17 steps,
  6,541 trades, 37 bond issues, 80 surface publishes
  (=select count(*) from ores_trading_trades_tbl= and its siblings).
- =generate_trades= records a *new* config on every run, and the generated
  terms are seeded by the config id, so the retry drew different bond terms and
  is not a replay of the failing inputs. The failing draw is the one for config
  =b1640880-afea-5340-9c14-3c83dce8334d= (bond, =1Y= to =10Y=,
  =ACME ACMCOR bonds=, book =Group Intercompany Loans=), whose first issue is
  =POP5ZCJP1NEQ1=.
- Led to the retry by the story
  [[id:BA56F4F5-AA1F-468E-AA7B-B322F20B6382][Make a workflow step survive a slow request]]; the helper classifies
  =std::bad_alloc= as permanent, so it is the step-level retry that recovered.

** Where to look

- =trade_operations_service::put_bond_issue_composite= and the generated
  repositories it calls
  (=projects/ores.trading/core/src/service/trade_operations_service.cpp:669=).
- =entity_rows= sizes a vector from the driver's row count,
  =std::vector<Entity> out(static_cast<std::size_t>(r.rows()))=
  (=projects/ores.database/include/ores.database/query_builder/execute.hpp:147=).
  A negative count becomes =SIZE_MAX= and fails as =bad_alloc=. It is reached
  by =answer_if_stored='s =issues.read_latest=.
- The numeric codec, which sizes work from a value's own text
  (=projects/ores.database/src/pq/codec.cpp:172=), and every =reserve= or
  =resize= fed by a drawn term.
- The draws themselves: =security_face_value=, =coupon_rate=, =tenor_years= and
  =security_age_months= in
  =projects/ores.synthetic/core/src/service/trade_population_planner.cpp=.

** How to reproduce

Run the population for config =b1640880-afea-5340-9c14-3c83dce8334d=, whose
draws are seeded by its id, with the trading service under =gdb -ex 'catch
throw'= so the =bad_alloc= yields a stack. Re-running =generate_trades= itself
draws new terms and does not reproduce it.

** What settles it

Replay the failing config. Its draws are seeded by its id, so running the
population for =b1640880-afea-5340-9c14-3c83dce8334d= again either fails the
same way, which proves a value-dependent allocation, or passes, which points at
a transient allocation at 14:05. There is no request subject for a single
population run today, so the replay needs a small harness or a test that plans
from that config and calls =put_bond_issue_composite= with the plan's first
issue rows. Run it under =gdb -ex 'catch throw'= and the =bad_alloc= yields a
stack either way.
