* Plan

The gap is one problem with seven faces. A provision publishes market data for
a subset of the objects the trade catalogue can draw from, so the generator
books trades on instruments that no series answers. Take the classes one at a
time, and for every address choose one of two outcomes: the market answers it,
or the generator stops drawing it and this task states the reason.

1. *Know the gap, per type.* Measured: 124 of 133 addresses, over 100 trade type
   and class rows. Evidence in =evidence/generated_population_gap.txt=.
2. *Separate what the market cannot name from what it does not publish.* The
   tenant publishes two asset classes and no more: FX prices, IR curves, and the
   fixings the system tenant derives from them. The other five classes — equity,
   commodity, credit, inflation and security — have no series of any kind in this
   tenant, so their 84 addresses need a market data *source* before any name can
   match, and no amount of renaming closes them.
3. *FX and IR: the machinery exists, the closure does not.* Read against the
   architecture in =evidence/fx_and_ir_publishing_setup.txt=: the feeds, scopes,
   catalogue and durable fixing store all do their jobs, and the system party
   receives 325,571 fixings. The subscription check is implemented too
   (=demand_calculator.cpp= refuses =not_entitled= and =terms_not_accepted=), and
   the demand carries a hook for the party's own required items which
   =adapter_supervisor.cpp:229= never supplies. What is missing is the closure

       trades -> conventions -> catalogue items -> suppliers -> entitlements.

   The trade names a *fixing convention* — who calculates the rate, at what time
   (the ECB reference rate, the 4pm London fix ORE spells =TR20H=, ICE LIBOR,
   NY Fed SOFR). The *supplier* is who sells us that convention — LSEG, Bloomberg,
   a central bank's portal, the platform's own derived feed — and it is the thing
   a party is entitled to. The model carries both axes already: the convention is
   inside the oresmd URI, the supplier is =market_series.provider_code=; the word
   =source= names the convention there and the supplier everywhere else.

   So, in order: (i) give every convention a supplier in the catalogue — today no
   vendor supplies any FX fixing, so the 4pm London convention the generator names
   has none; (ii) wire the demand's requirements hook to the closure over the
   party's trades, so demand is the party's items and not its provider's whole
   catalogue; (iii) refuse a booking whose conventions no entitled supplier
   covers, naming the supplier that is missing; (iv) read a fixing by convention
   and supplier, not by series, which is what the target in
   [[id:F4BBEF34-D84C-4AE6-A3AA-DC47C3EE06EE][Public sources publish daily fixings through an adapter]] already states.
4. *The other five classes.* Either build a market data source for the class —
   a price series and then a fixing series per object — or take the class's types
   out of the generator, in which case this task states the reason in the
   generator's own terms. Decide per class, not for all five at once.
5. *Prove it.* A provisioned Acme's reports record no market data error for the
   generated trades, or one asserted list names each with its reason.

Steps 3 and 4 are independent of each other, and each ends in a verifiable
state: the class's addresses answer, and no address of another class has stopped
answering.

The architectural gap this task does not close, and cannot without its own story,
is the choice of supplier: a requirement is symbolic, and resolution picks one
supplier for it under a per-tenant market data configuration, by a stated rank,
and records the choice. There is no such configuration in the schema, so a trade
stores a frozen identifier and the difference between what the trades ask for and
what the market holds surfaces at the export instead of at booking.

* Notes

- From the baseline run of 2026-11-17 (=evidence/run_674d2887_errors.txt=):
  70 trades name a LIBOR index. The system generates LIBOR on purpose; the
  trades failed because the configuration named no published curve, and
  because some tenors are not bootstrapped. The tenors are added by
  [[id:2189859D-6B0B-493E-A858-0435091D2365][Bootstrap every IBOR tenor the generated trades book]]. The baseline also lists the smaller causes this
  task owns: basket reference data for the credit index products, the
  script library for =ScriptedTrade=, and a pricing engine for
  =EquityOutperformanceOption=.
- The baseline's error rows and this task's missing addresses are the same
  finding seen from two sides. The run says "equity not in the market" 118
  times; the market says 11 equity addresses have no series. Both are true: one
  index answers many trades, and one trade names many indices.
- The first version of this measurement counted market series over the whole
  table and so reported credit, commodity, equity, security and inflation series
  the tenant does not hold: every one of them belonged to the =marketdata_corpus_*=
  tenants the market data tests leave behind. On this machine the table answers a
  question about the machine. Every count here is scoped to the acme tenant or to
  the system tenant, and the evidence file names which.
- The IR half of the gap is a curve gap, not a fixing gap. Every one of the 15
  failing IBOR addresses names an index and tenor that the 51 fixing series the
  system tenant publishes does not carry, and a fixing is derived from its curve.
  Two of them go further: the =GENERIC= Ibor conventions the catalogue holds for
  92 currencies state no tenor, while a trade's leg states one, so even a
  generated curve for that convention would not produce the address the trade
  names. =COP IBR= and =USD SOFR= are the same shape.
