This page is the deal component of a cut, one of the objects a
[[id:B5214F3A-A72E-421E-82BC-79338B2E95CB][cut]] can fix. The
[[id:B5214F3A-A72E-421E-82BC-79338B2E95CB][Cuts]] structure note states the
general notion, the kinds and the composition; this page states what the deal
half of the object holds.

* Summary

A trade cut is the *deal population* an object fixes at the timepoint: every
live deal as it stands at that instant, with the state it holds, the book it
belongs to, and the corrections applied before the cut was authorised. It is
frozen by its own act, because deals keep arriving and keep being corrected
while the day is still open. A deal links to another deal, and a population that
froze one without the other would leave a valuation reading half a position. It
is not the same thing as a query over the trade store, because a query has no
identity, nobody signed it, and its answer moves as rows are corrected.

* What it fixes

- *The deals*. Every live deal at the instant, by identity and by the version
  that was current.
- *The state each holds*. Live, expired, fixed or settled, as it stood at the
  cut, so a settled deal is not read as a live one.
- *The book and the centre*. Which book owns the deal, and therefore which
  centre's market data it values against.
- *The corrections*. Any manual correction a person applied before
  authorisation, with the reason.
- *The linked deals*. A deal and the deal it links to are captured together, so
  a hedge and its hedgee cannot land in different populations.
- *The identity and the authorisation*. The label, the timepoint, who signed it,
  and the version.

A population that answers only the first of these is a report of the trade
store, and not a cut.

* Why it is its own act

The hub states our working convention that the deals are frozen first and the
market data second. The reason the deal half needs its own act is that its
review is different in kind. A person reviews a population for completeness and
for deals that do not look like what the desk booked. Nobody reviews rates that
way, and nobody reviews a deal by comparing it to a curve.

* Where ORE Studio stands

Deals are stored as versioned rows. Each row carries an identity, a version and
the interval it was current for, in
[[id:4304A441-E532-45FB-837A-378F13693CAE][ores.trading.trade]], so a past state
of the store can be reconstructed by a query.

A query is not a trade cut. It has no identity of its own, nobody signed it, and
two calls at two moments can answer differently as rows are corrected. Nothing
in the repository holds a population that was fixed at an instant and approved.

The nearest object we have is per run, not per day. A report's trade population
is partitioned into trade sets, recorded against the run, and each set's market
data is resolved at its as-of. See
[[id:AC05783F-3917-4401-902B-40C5277300A7][Gather each trade set and its market data]].
That is a package assembled for one run. It is not the day's population signed
once and reused by every report.
