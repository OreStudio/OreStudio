#+title: The lifecycle record of a derived market object
#+description: One publish record for every kind of derived market object: the columns every kind shares, the subject part each kind adds, and what a reader of any kind reads.
#+type: knowledge
#+level: cross
#+filetags: :architecture:marketdata:publish:approval:data:

* Summary

A published derived market object is one record, and the record has one shape
whatever the kind. It names the approval request that authorised it, the party,
the as-of, the candidate version it published and the output series it wrote,
the account and the instant of the publication, the set it superseded and, where
the kind has one, the withdrawal that ended it. The kind adds a subject part and
nothing else: the bootstrap configuration of a curve, the currency pair of a
forward set, the blueprint of a surface. The separation is what makes a single
lifecycle possible, because the parts that the acts read and write are the same
for every kind, and only the words that say what the set is of differ.

The record holds no numbers. The values of a set are the candidate's, and its
checks are the candidate's too. The record holds the authority over those
values: who asked, who decided, who published, when, and what ended it.

* Detail

** The three records of one publish

A publish is an act over a candidate that an approval request authorised, and
each of the three is a record of its own. The separation decides where a fact is
written, and every act of the lifecycle writes to one of them.

| Record | What it holds | Who writes it |
|--------+---------------+---------------|
| The approval request | Who asked, the kind, the reason, the decisions, the rule that closed it | The inbox's library, in the act's transaction |
| The candidate | The points, their lineage, and the result of every check | The kind's compute |
| The publish | The authority: party, as-of, subject, candidate version, output series, publisher, instant, and the end of the set | The apply, and the withdrawal's apply |

** The columns every kind shares**

The publish record is one table of the lifecycle, and the columns fall into five
groups. A reader of any kind reads all five, so a screen that lists sets, or a
report that binds one, does not know which kind it reads.

| Group | Columns | What it states |
|-------+---------+----------------|
| Identity | the publish id, the output series id | Which set this is, and which series it was written to |
| Authority | the approval request, the party, the as-of, the publisher, the published instant | Who put it in use, over which party's data, at which as-of |
| The published version | the candidate id and its version | Which numbers were decided. A request names one version, so a decision never covers numbers computed after it |
| Supersession | the superseded instant, the request that replaced it, the as-of that replaced it | That a newer or equal as-of was applied after it, and by which act |
| Withdrawal | the withdrawing account, the instant, the reason code, the withdrawal request | That the set left use, why, and by which act |

The state of the set is read from those columns and is not a column of its own,
because a state that were stored could disagree with the facts beside it.
[[id:B05A31CC-FA22-4534-848E-601A30822992][Withdraw or replace a published curve]] states the derivation: a set with a
published instant is published; one that also carries a successor is superseded;
one that carries a withdrawing account is withdrawn. The supersession columns
record a fact and do not decide what a read takes, because the read's own rule
decides that ([[id:86158AAD-423A-421A-8BE2-CC5123109297][As-of resolution of published market data sets]]). A superseded
set is taken for an as-of before its successor's, and again after its successor
is withdrawn.

** The subject part**

The subject part is the only table a kind owns in the record, and it is keyed by
the publish. It states what the set is a set of, in the words of the kind's own
domain, and it carries the foreign keys of that domain so the database checks
them as it checks every other row.

| Kind | The subject part names | The columns it adds |
|------+------------------------+---------------------|
| IR curve | A bootstrap configuration of the party | the configuration's id |
| FX forward set | A currency pair of the party | the pair, the spot date and rate, the two curve publishes it was built from, the convention version, the calendars and the method |
| FX volatility surface | A surface blueprint of the party | the blueprint's id |

The subject part holds the inputs a set was computed from where those inputs are
publish records themselves, as an FX forward set holds the two curves it read.
That is what lets the apply refuse a set whose input was withdrawn after it was
computed ([[id:86D8986F-5247-4138-85C7-F8899C029F24][The trader applies an approved publish, which supersedes the live set and tells its readers]]).

** What the record does not hold**

Three kinds of fact are deliberately outside the record, and a reader that wants
them reads the record they belong to.

- *The values of the set.* They are observations of the output series, each with
  its own oresmd identity, written by the compute and never by the apply.
- *The result of the checks.* A blocking check that failed stops the raise, so a
  published set passed every blocking check; an advisory one, such as the
  largest pillar error of a calibration, is a fact the decider weighs and it
  belongs to the candidate version the decision covered.
- *The source series the set was computed from.* Each derived point carries a
  lineage row naming the series it read, their weights and the version of the
  recipe ([[id:573F9165-C20B-463C-B0B3-F324AFE4E81F][Curve point provenance]]). The source is therefore a fact of
  the point and not of the publish, and a set whose blueprint gained a
  contributor after the publish does not thereby change its own record.

** Why one record**

Two consequences follow from the shape, and both are the reason for it.

The history of a set is a stream of versions of one row, and not a join across
tables. A withdrawal adds the end of the act to the row that already holds its
beginning, so the set a desk priced from is still readable after it leaves use,
with the reason beside it. A list of a party's sets answers one question in one
read: which set is in use for each subject, and what ended the one before it.

The lifecycle can then be implemented once. Every act reads and writes the same
columns, and the kind supplies the compute, the checks, the subject part and its
two approval kinds ([[id:186B44DD-51EA-45AA-971E-0C2D4D21A6DD][The publication lifecycle of derived market objects]]).
Nothing in the raise, the apply, the withdrawal or the read branches on which
kind it serves.

** The distance to the code**

The record is one shape in this page and three shapes in the tree. Each kind
carries its own table with the shared columns repeated, and the columns a kind
does not yet need are absent rather than empty.

| Kind | Shared columns | Subject part | Withdrawal columns |
|------+----------------+--------------+--------------------|
| IR curve | all five groups | its own table, carrying the configuration | present |
| FX forward set | identity, authority, the published version | its own table, carrying the pair and the inputs | absent, and no supersession columns either |
| FX volatility surface | identity, authority, the published version, supersession | its own table, carrying the blueprint and the calibration it published | absent |

The surface table also carries the calibration's verdict, which belongs to the
candidate and not to the publish. That is the clearest sign that the three
records are copies rather than one record with three subject parts.
