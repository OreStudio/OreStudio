#+description: The one shape and behaviour of every ores.web screen that authors and runs a synthetic feed, for every asset class: the feed list as a dashboard, the feed page, the process editor and its previews, the engine and its conversion, saving a process, runs, ticks, history and the quant help.

* Summary

A feed is a record that produces market data: it holds a process, and while it
runs, the process makes a tick at a fixed rate. The FX price feed screen is
the first feed screen, and its shape is the standard for every asset class
that follows: IR curves, and any other kind of synthetic feed.

A feed is still a record. Everything this page does not state follows the
[[id:2BF7F03F-6BF4-45FF-8E26-4D3B4E2D943D][Record screen standard]]: the list
and its paging, the header, the edit dialog, change reasons, delete, the
states and the words. This page adds what a feed has and a plain record does
not: a run state, a process edited as one set, an engine that decides what the
numbers mean, previews, live ticks, and the quant help that makes the numbers
readable.

Each rule names the shared part that carries it, so a new feed screen gets the
rule by using the part.

Feeds have two scopes. A /system feed/ simulates a real provider, such as
Bloomberg, Refinitiv or an exchange; it is run once for the installation and a
tenant consumes it through a feed binding. A /test feed/ is one a tenant or a
party creates for itself. Today every feed is a party's test feed, and the FX
price feed screen lists one party's FX feeds. In the organisation that
[[id:0A32284A-5F8C-4A99-9539-34923631DED3][Organise feeds by provider and scope]]
brings, a feed browser organises system feeds by provider and kind and holds
the per-kind screens of this page as its leaves, at a scale of many thousands
of feeds.

* Detail

** Where this standard applies

A /feed screen/ is a screen whose subject is the feeds of one kind: it lists
them, starts and stops them, opens one, and edits its process. The FX price
feed screens under *Market data* are feed screens. The IR curve feed screen
will be one.

These are not feed screens:

- The collection screen. A collection holds feeds and starts and stops them as
  one; it is a record screen with its own task.
- The model catalogue. Process types are reference data.
- The Qt market simulator, which
  [[id:5779B40E-1C82-478D-951E-C81C13F7DFEA][The market simulator screens]]
  describes.

** Words

| Word | Meaning |
|------+---------|
| /Feed/ | The record: what it simulates, its rate, its engine and its run settings |
| /Process/ | The parts the engine draws from, such as an FX feed's Gaussian components |
| /Engine/ | The price process that reads the parts: geometric, arithmetic or mean reverting for FX |
| /Draft/ | The process as it stands on the screen, before Save |
| /Run/ | The feed producing ticks. A feed is /running/ or /stopped/ |
| /Tick/ | One value the running feed produced, with its time |
| /Share/ | A part's weight over the sum of the weights, as the engine normalises it |

The tab that edits the process carries the Qt editor's name for it. For FX it
is /Price behaviour/.

** What a feed kind declares

A kind of feed declares, once:

- its record resource and the resource of its parts, with the field that
  names the parent;
- its engines, and for each engine what each number of a part means;
- the server operations its screens call: the sample paths, the engine
  conversion, and any shape preview;
- the subject its ticks are published on;
- the permission that gates the kind.

** The list is the dashboard

The list follows the record standard, and it is also where feeds are run.

- A *Status* column shows *Running* or *Stopped* as a tag. The screen reads
  the running feeds every five seconds, because a feed can start or stop from
  anywhere.
- A writer gets a choice box on each row, and *Start chosen (n)* and *Stop
  chosen (n)* in the list header.
- Each chosen feed is its own call, so one refusal does not stop the others.
  The answer counts what started or stopped and names each refusal with the
  server's reason. A feed is named by its source name, never by its id.
- Starting a whole collection belongs to the collection screen.

** The feed page

*** Header

- The flag of what the feed simulates, such as the pair, then the feed's name.
- The line under the name: the key, the version and the engine, such as
  "EUR/USD · version 3 · Geometric".
- The status tag, then *Start* or *Stop*, then *Edit* and *Delete*.
- No access mark. The actions offered already say what the person may do.

*** Tabs

| Tab | What it holds |
|-----+---------------|
| /Details/ | The feed's fields, as the record standard draws them |
| /The process tab/ | The process, the engine, and the previews. For FX: /Price behaviour/ |
| /Ticks/ | The last ticks of the feed |
| /History/ | One stream of the feed and its parts |

*** Edit, add and delete

- The edit dialog does not offer the engine. The engine changes on the process
  tab, where the process is converted with it.
- The add dialog offers the engine, so a new feed starts with one. A new feed
  opens on the process tab.
- Each field of the form can carry help, shown under it. A choice is explained
  by its option.
- Delete stops the feed, deletes its parts, then deletes the feed.

** The process tab

*** Layout

#+begin_src text
  [ 2 unsaved changes ]        Engine [Geometric ▾]  [Add] [Discard] [Save]
  ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
  │ # Description            Profile   μ        σ        w      Share   │
  │ 0 Primary · calibrated…  Normal    0        0.001    0.95   95.0%  ↑↓ Delete │
  │ 1 Tail · 4x primary…     Custom    0        0.004    0.05    5.0%  ↑↓ Delete │
  └─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
  ┌ Return distribution ─────────┐ ┌ Sample paths ────────────────┐
  │ chart                        │ │ chart                        │
  │                              │ │ price · ticks · paths · seed │
  └──────────────────────────────┘ └──────────────────────────────┘
  ⓘ The engine's note
#+end_src

- The toolbar holds the unsaved count, or "No unsaved changes.", the engine,
  and *Add*, *Discard* and *Save*. Buttons are one word.
- The list spans the full width. The previews sit side by side below it, and
  stack on a narrow screen. The engine's note sits under the previews.

*** The list

- The list is read only. A row opens the part in a dialog.
- The description shows its name highlighted and its note subdued: the text
  before the first colon is the name. A long description ends in an ellipsis,
  with the whole text in its tooltip.
- Numbers are right aligned in the mono face.
- A computed column states what the engine does with the numbers. For FX, the
  /Share/ column gives each weight as a percentage of the sum, and its header
  tooltip gives the sum. An engine that reads no shares has no such column.
- Every column header has a tooltip in the engine's terms, and every row a
  tooltip that says what one part means to the engine.
- Each row has *↑*, *↓* and *Delete*. The first row cannot move up and the
  last cannot move down.

*** Unsaved changes

Nothing is written until Save, and what is not saved is visible:

| Change | Mark |
|--------+------|
| A new part | The row tinted, and tagged /New/ |
| A changed value | The cell tinted, and the row tagged /Changed/ |
| A moved part | The row tagged /Moved/ |
| A deleted part | The row kept, struck through, tagged /Deleted/, with /Undo/ |
| A changed engine | The engine choice outlined |

*Discard* puts the saved process and engine back.

*** The part dialog

- The description is a text area of four lines, with a hint for the
  "Name: note" form.
- Each number has the engine's help under it, and the dialog refuses a value
  that is not a number or is negative before *Done*.
- *Done* puts the part back into the draft. It writes nothing.

*** Saving

*Save* is enabled while there are changes and no problems. It opens a dialog:

1. One line per kind of change: the engine change, such as "Engine:
   Geometric → Arithmetic.", and the counts added, changed and deleted.
2. The change reason and commentary, or the new record note when every change
   is new.
3. When the feed applies the change. A running feed keeps the process it
   started with, so the dialog says "The feed is running on the old mixture."
   and offers *Restart it to apply*, ticked. A stopped feed is told "It applies
   on start."

The save writes the feed's engine first, then deletes the removed parts, then
writes the changed parts, all under one reason. A restart, when chosen, stops
and starts the feed after the writes.

*** The engine

The engine decides what the numbers of a part mean. Changing it changes the
meaning of the saved process, so the screen converts the draft with it:

- The screen asks the server to convert the draft. The conversion is never
  computed in the browser.
- The converted values show as unsaved changes. A notice says what was
  converted and at which price, and says when a preview price stood in for a
  feed with no fixed price.
- For FX, the server operation is =convert_fx_mixture=: μ and σ scale by the
  initial price between log-returns and price units; into the mean reverting
  engine only the first part stays, with κ from a 15 minute half-life and no
  μ; out of it the one part takes the whole weight.

*** Previews

- The previews read the draft, not the saved process, so a person judges a
  change before saving it.
- The distribution is drawn per part, dashed, and as the whole.
- The sample paths come from the server's simulation, with their settings
  under the chart: the initial price, the ticks, the paths and the seed with
  *Reseed*. The screen waits 300 ms after typing before it asks.
- Neither preview saves anything.

** The Ticks tab

#+begin_src text
  ┌ Ticks shown [200▾]  LAST PRICE  TIME  MOVE (200 TICKS)   ☐ EMA 20  [Export CSV] ┐   ┌ PROCESS  [Geometric] ┐
  ├ EUR/USD synthetic price path              High 1.17652  Low 1.17601 ┤   │ 1800 ticks per hour  │
  │ price chart, axis on the right, grid, fill                          │   │ #0 Primary   w 0.95  │
  │                                                                     │   │ note, subdued        │
  └─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘   └──────────────────────┘
#+end_src

- A strip of labelled figures over the plot: the ticks shown, the last price,
  its time and the move over the ticks shown, green or red.
- The chart card heads with what it shows and the high and the low.
- The price chart has its values on the right, a grid of labelled levels, a
  light fill under the line, and labels in the page's 12 px mono at any width.
- An optional moving average, an EMA over 20 ticks, drawn dashed.
- *Export CSV* hands the ticks shown to the browser.
- A column beside the plot shows the process that made the ticks: the engine,
  the rate, and each part as a card with its name highlighted, its note
  subdued, its weight in the part's colour, and its other numbers.
- A running feed's ticks are read again every two seconds.
- The tab says what it can and cannot show. The web server keeps the last 500
  ticks of each running feed from when it began to listen, and forgets a run
  when the feed stops, so the lead says so.

** History

The History tab is one stream of the feed and every part it holds, drawn as
the timeline of the
[[id:BD492939-118E-4B55-AE5B-300F7A1C3B41][Entity screen specification]]:
the event chart above a rail of one-line entries, each naming its record. Each
version older than its record's newest offers *Revert* to a person who may
write it. A part offers no history of its own in the list.

A part deleted from the process is not in the stream, and the tab says so in
one line under the timeline.

** Quant logic lives on the server

What the numbers mean is the engine's, and the engine is on the server. A
feed screen asks the server for every quant answer that decides a value
written to the feed: the sample paths, the conversion between engines, and any
calibration. The screen draws what the server answers.

** Words on a feed screen

- Buttons are one word: *Add*, *Discard*, *Save*, *Start*, *Stop*, *Undo*,
  *Done*.
- Notes are one short sentence. "The feed is running on the old mixture." says
  what the person needs; a paragraph that explains the engine does not belong
  in a dialog.
- The long explanations live in tooltips and field hints, where a person who
  wants them looks.

** The shared parts

| Part | Where | What it carries |
|------+-------+-----------------|
| The list | =RecordList=, with =syntheticStore= | Paging, search, sort and columns |
| Runs | =marketdata/feedRuns.tsx= | The running read, the status tag, start and stop of many |
| The process tab | =marketdata/MixturePanel.tsx= | The list, the marks, the engine, the previews |
| The part dialog | =marketdata/ComponentDialog.tsx= | One part with its help |
| The save dialog | =marketdata/SaveMixtureDialog.tsx= | The reason, the order of writes, the restart |
| The process rules | =marketdata/mixture.ts=, =marketdata/processHelp.ts= | Draft, save plan, changes, shares, profiles |
| Charts | =marketdata/LineChart.tsx= | Lines, the price chart, measured width |
| Ticks | =marketdata/TicksPanel.tsx=, =marketdata/ticks.ts=, =bff/src/feed-ticks.ts= | The strip, the chart, the EMA, the CSV, the BFF buffer |
| History | =marketdata/FeedHistory.tsx= with =Timeline= | One stream of the feed and its parts |
| Conversion | =ores.synthetic.api/feeds/fx_mixture_conversion.hpp= | The engine change, on the server |

Most of these parts are written for the FX mixture. The second feed kind turns
them into parts that take the kind's declaration, as the record parts take a
store.

** Building a feed screen for a new asset class

1. Declare the kind: its resources, its engines, its server operations, its
   tick subject and its permission.
2. Give the server the conversion between the kind's engines, and its
   previews, as operations. Test them there.
3. Build the list from =RecordList= with the status column and the choice of
   feeds.
4. Build the feed page with the four tabs, taking the parts above.
5. Write the engine's words: the note, the column help, the row help and the
   field hints, in every language.
6. Check the screen against each section of this page, and add its row to the
   conformance table.

** Gaps

- The Ticks tab shows only what the web server heard. The stored observations
  need the feed's series identity, which
  [[id:3CD588CB-1B74-40BA-896F-75CFF8157EAF][Name a feed by its oresmd URI]]
  brings; then the tab reads its history from the store.
- The browser still computes some figures it only displays: the shares, the
  mean reverting half-life and band, the profiles and the distribution curve.
  Each moves to the server when a screen first needs it from there.
- The Qt editor's simple mode, sliders for drift, volatility and jumps, waits
  for [[id:AACBF6DA-53E9-46F4-BBD7-A476625E1732][Give the mixture a simple mode]].
