#+title: A forced password change is advisory
#+description: An account the provisioner marks as owing a password change can still call everything it is authorised for: the flag travels with the login answer and the forced change is enforced by the screen, not by the service that answers a request.
#+type: capture
#+level: cross
#+filetags: :iam:security:login:session:
#+created: 2026-09-29
#+updated: 2026-09-29

This page is a [[id:671F18E4-E09C-4B3B-BD24-D33DF8AE38A6][capture]] in the *inbox* bucket of the product backlog — a pre-sprint idea, not yet pulled into a sprint as a story.

* What

Refuse every request but the password change itself while an account owes one.
The account already carries the fact: the provisioner sets
=password_reset_required= on the login information, the login answer reports it,
and the journey's first sign-in holds the person to it. What is missing is the
service side. Nothing in the request path reads the flag, so the account's
session is a normal one from the moment it is issued.

* Why

A rule that only a screen applies is a rule a caller can skip. The shell, a
script, or any client that ignores the login answer's flag can use an account
that is supposed to be stopped at the door, which is exactly the case the flag
exists for: an administrator hands over a tenant with a password the person
must replace, and until they do, the password is known to somebody else. The
screen is what makes the flow pleasant; the service is what makes it true.

Two shapes are possible and the choice belongs to the story that takes this:

- the session records that the change is outstanding, and the request path
  refuses every subject that is not the change, which needs the flag in the
  session rather than only in the login answer;
- or the account is created without the ordinary grants until the change
  happens, which needs no new check but couples authorization to a first-run
  state.

* References

- The flag: =login_info.password_reset_required=, set by
  =tenant_provisioning_service::provision= and reported by
  =ores.iam.core/messaging/auth_handler.hpp= on both login paths.
- The screen that enforces it today:
  =projects/ores.web/packages/web/src/journeys/FirstSignIn.tsx=.
- The route the change goes through: =/api/account/password= in
  =projects/ores.web/packages/bff/src/server.ts=, which calls
  =change_own_password= on the session's own client.
