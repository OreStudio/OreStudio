* Acceptance

- A second thread owns the tick writer connection and copies a batch while the worker plans and numbers the next, and one connection is still the only one the copy uses.
- A tick is republished after the copy of its batch, in the order of the batches, and a failed copy still republishes without a store, with the same backoff as today.
- The burst tests give a median above 110,000 rows per second through NATS over ten runs, and the raw output is in the evidence folder.
- A test shows that no tick is lost or stored twice when a copy fails while the next batch waits.

* Plan

Step 5 of the parent task showed that the loop prepares a batch and then waits for its =COPY=.
A bare =COPY= takes about 0.26 s of the 0.55 s for 60,000 rows, and the loop takes the rest,
one after the other in one thread. The loop now has three stages that work on three batches at
once, with a place for one waiting batch between two stages.

1. The worker prepares a batch: it plans the ticks, finds the series, numbers the datums and
   builds the rows. It hands the batch to the next stage through a bounded handoff of one batch.
2. A second thread owns the use of the tick writer, copies the batch, and marks each item stored
   or unavailable, with the same backoff as before.
3. A third thread records the activity and republishes. The first version had two stages, and
   the copy stage still held the republish, which took as long as the copy.
4. A flag of the loop runs the stages in the worker, one after the other. The tests use it to
   compare the two modes, and =handle_batch= uses the same stages in the calling thread.
5. The tick writer lets a test double replace its write, so a test can make one copy fail.

* Result

* Notes

- A run with the machine at a load of 28 on 8 CPUs showed no gain, because a second thread has no
  free CPU to use. The comparison runs wait for a load below 6.5 and then alternate the two modes.
- The two-stage loop reached about 119,000 rows per second against about 111,000 for the serial
  loop in the same conditions. The copy stage was the limit, because it also republished.

* Result

The loop copies one batch while the worker prepares the next and a third thread republishes the
one before. Through NATS the drain of a burst of 60,000 ticks has a median of 128,100 rows per
second over ten runs, against about 97,600 before. In pairs of samples at a load of 6 to 7.5, the
three stages gave a median of 135,200 against 95,600 for the serial loop. The criterion of more
than 100,000 rows per second through NATS, with margin, is met.

What a reader needs to know:

- A handoff of one batch lets a slow stage stop the one before it, so the batches do not pile up
  in memory. Each stage holds one batch and one batch waits between two stages, so at most five
  batches are in the loop at a time, and the memory of the batches is bounded by five times the
  largest batch.
- A tick is republished after the copy of its batch, in the order of the batches. A store that
  does not answer leaves a batch unstored and republished, and the backoff is the one of before.
  The test =a_failed_copy_loses_no_other_tick_and_stores_none_twice_while_the_next_batch_waits=
  makes a copy fail while a burst is in the loop. It counts the rows of the table and the
  republished ticks.
- The adaptive size of a batch now follows the time of one turn of the stages, which is as long
  as the slowest stage.
- The loop still uses one connection for the copy. The series, the numbers and the activity use
  the pool of the service, as before.
- The numbers vary with the load of the machine. The runs here had a load of 6 to 12 on 8 CPUs.
  One of ten runs through NATS gave 86,000 rows per second.
- The age of a tick at republish is the time from its observation to its arrival on the republish
  subject. A burst of 60,000 ticks waits less in the pipelined loop, because the drain is shorter.
  A slow stream waits more: the median is 13.9 ms against 6.0 ms, and the p99 is 42.1 ms against
  16.9 ms. The cause is a guess: each of the two handoffs wakes a thread that has to wait for a
  free CPU at a load of 6 on 8. The task =Run a quiet loop in one thread= removes this cost.
