* Goal

ACME's books hold interest rate trades: vanilla and overnight index swaps, swaptions, caps and floors, forward rate agreements, cross-currency swaps and the rest of the ten routed types. The family carries 42% of the population and it is the curve backbone every other class discounts on. The planner draws the trade types from the catalogue and the mix from Trade Population Weights.

* Plan

** What the tree already holds

The work is smaller than the family's size suggests, because three layers are
already in place.

- *The routing.* =trade_type_routing.hpp= sends ten types to
  =rate_instrument=: Swap, CrossCurrencySwap, ForwardRateAgreement, CapFloor,
  Swaption, FlexiSwap, BalanceGuaranteedSwap, CallableSwap, KnockOutSwap and
  InflationSwap. Routing decides which array a trade type may carry, so those
  ten can book a rate instrument today.
- *The writer, service side.* =write_instrument_batch= already writes
  =rate_instruments=, =swap_legs=, =swap_leg_amounts= and =swap_leg_rates=.
  No trading service change is needed to store a swap.
- *The vocabulary.* The legs draw on codes that are seeded: twelve day count
  fractions, seven business day conventions, nine payment frequencies, 33
  benchmark rates and the currency set.

What is missing is the synthetic side: a rate payload, a planner and a writer
arm. It has neither.

** Decisions

- *D1 — the ten.* They are the routed ten, and InflationSwap is among them.
  Booking it here as a rate-shaped swap keeps the count true and stops this
  task and the inflation task from both claiming it; the inflation task adds
  =inflation_swap_instruments= and the inflation market data on top.
- *D2 — the leg shape.* Every routed type books a =rate_instruments= header
  with two =swap_legs=, and each leg one =swap_leg_amount= and one
  =swap_leg_rate=. A vanilla fixed-float swap is the shape all ten reduce to,
  and the specialised arrays (=vanilla_swap_instruments=, =cap_floor_instruments=
  and the rest) stay empty until a pricing need justifies them. The leg type is
  data, so one model carries the variety.
- *D3 — the unrouted types.* RateDigitalOption, SwaptionStraddle and
  ForwardVolatilityAgreement appear in the routing table not at all, so a
  request carrying any instrument array for them is refused as misrouted. Two
  are rate products whose option data the =instrument_option= arrays already
  hold, so routing them to =rate_instrument= is a table change; the third is
  named out of scope here, and the reason is recorded.
- *D4 — CashPosition.* The weights page gives it 1.5% of the class, and the
  catalogue holds the trade type, but no instrument table routes it and it is
  not a rate derivative. It is named out of scope, and the weight it carries
  leaves the class share this task can meet. That has to be stated rather than
  silently absorbed.

** Steps

Each step ends in a state that can be checked on its own.

1. *The payload.* Add =rate_terms= to =trade_population_plan.hpp=: the
   currency, both legs' convention codes and economics, the notional, and the
   start and maturity dates. Add it to the =trade_terms= variant. It carries
   codes and numbers only, so nothing is invented downstream.
2. *The scope.* The plan needs the currencies and the floating indices to draw
   from, resolved from refdata by the caller, as =commodities= already are.
   Add them to =trade_generation_scope=.
3. *The planner.* =rate_trade_planner= in the registry under
   =interest_rates=, cycling the routed types over the scope's currencies and
   indices, and banding notional and tenor from the scope.
4. *The writer.* Fill =rate_instruments=, =swap_legs=, =swap_leg_amounts= and
   =swap_leg_rates= from =rate_terms= in the population writer.
5. *The routing.* Route RateDigitalOption and SwaptionStraddle to
   =rate_instrument=, and record ForwardVolatilityAgreement and CashPosition as
   out of scope with their reasons.
6. *The profile.* Give ACME's seed profile a rate population, so a provisioning
   run produces the family without a hand edit.
7. *The proof.* Run a generation, then measure: every routed type present at
   least once, every trade with two legs and their economics, the class share
   against the weight, and the export and reverse map.
8. *The record.* Measurements, Result, and the gaps this task cannot close.

* Notes

** Two defects the live runs found

Both were found by a provisioning run and neither by a compile.

- *The leg group.* The first run failed on
  =ores_trading_swap_legs_tbl_leg_group_check=: the column accepts null or the
  groups a credit linked swap states, and the writer stated ="main"=. An
  ordinary swap leaves it null.
- *The wrong catalogue.* The second run failed with =Invalid
  floating_index_type: AMERIBOR=. A leg states the FpML name of an index,
  =GBP-SONIA=, and the store holds a closed set of those; the scope was loading
  benchmark *rate* names instead. It now loads the floating index types and the
  planner prefers the indices of the book's own currency.

** A rate trade without a fact row does not export

The plan said the specialised fact arrays could stay empty until a pricing need
justified them. That was wrong, and an exporter test caught it.

The store rebuilds a rates instrument from a header *and* one fact row per
product: =rebuild_instrument= looks for the header in =rate_instruments= and
then for a leaf in =fra_instruments=, =vanilla_swap_instruments=, or one of the
seven others. A trade that states a header and legs and no leaf rebuilds
nothing, and the exporter writes it as its trade type alone — no =SwapData=, no
legs, no index. The trade books, queries and reports, and the engine would have
nothing to price.

Two exporter cases now hold both sides of that. One states a header and legs
and asserts the document carries no instrument; the other adds the fact row and
asserts the legs and the index come out. The writer states the fact row its
trade type needs, and a query over a live population counts twelve fact rows for
twelve rate trades.

** The inflation swap leaves this family

Its fact row states an inflation index code, and this generator has no
vocabulary for one. Booking it here would have meant inventing a code the store
does not hold. The inflation task owns it, and this family books the other nine.

** The class share is not yet the weight

The measured share is 27%, against a weight of 42%. That is expected and not a
defect: the share is a property of the whole population, and only bonds and
rates are populated. Bonds carry 32 trades because four entity configs each
book eight; the two families' ratio is therefore bond-led. The share can only
be measured once the other six families are populated, and rebalanced then.

** The three unrouted types are out of scope, and why

ORE states each of them as its own instrument: =external/ore/xsd/instruments.xsd=
holds =SwaptionStraddleData=, =RateDigitalOptionData= and
=ForwardVolatilityAgreementData=, and the generated portfolio reader maps all
three. This repository holds no correspondings fact row: the instrument batch
has no straddle, rate digital or forward volatility array, and the database has
no table for any of them.

Routing a type to =rate_instrument= is not enough on its own, which is what the
fact row finding already showed: a trade whose product has no fact row cannot be
rebuilt for export, so it would book and then fail to price. Each of the three
therefore needs a table, a batch array, a writer and an export mapping — a
schema feature with its own task, not a line in the routing table.

** What is not proven yet, and what the export did prove

- *The export, partly.* Two exporter cases hold both sides: a rate trade with a
  header and legs and no fact row writes nothing but its trade type, and the
  same trade with its fact row writes =SwapData=, its index and its legs. A live
  population counts twelve fact rows for twelve rate trades. What remains
  unproven is the whole portfolio the ORE service gathers for a run, because no
  shell verb drives that path.
- *The report.* The shell runs no report, so the family has not been priced
  against its discount and forward curves. Nothing here claims it has.
- *The class share.* Measured at 27% against a 42% weight, which is expected
  while only bonds and rates are populated. It is a story-level check.

* Result

The interest rate family books. A provisioning run completes all nine steps and
writes twelve rate trades across nine of the routed types: Swap,
CrossCurrencySwap, ForwardRateAgreement, CapFloor, Swaption, FlexiSwap,
BalanceGuaranteedSwap, CallableSwap and KnockOutSwap. Each states a rate
instrument header, two legs — one fixed against one floating — a notional, and
the rate or spread that leg pays. The floating index is drawn from the tenant's
floating index catalogue, filtered to the book's own currency, so a GBP book
states GBP-SONIA, GBP-LIBOR-3M or GBP-LIBOR-6M.

A rate trade is a header, one product fact row and two legs. The fact row is not
decoration: the store rebuilds an instrument from the header and one fact row,
and the exporter test proves that without it the trade writes no instrument at
all. The writer states the fact row its trade type needs, and a live population
counts twelve fact rows for twelve trades.

Three things are not done, and the record says so rather than implying
otherwise: the inflation swap belongs to the inflation family, which owns the
inflation index vocabulary its fact row needs; RateDigitalOption,
SwaptionStraddle and ForwardVolatilityAgreement are out of scope because ORE
states each as its own instrument and this repository has no fact row for any of
them; and the family has not been priced, because the shell drives no report
run.
