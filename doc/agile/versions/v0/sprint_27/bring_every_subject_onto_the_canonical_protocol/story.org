* The census

The rule is written down and normative:
[[id:C650CBC9-FDED-4C68-981F-7902E296928E][NATS entity protocol specification]].
Its subject grammar is ={component}.v1.{resource}.{verb}=, every segment is
lower snake_case, the resource is a plural noun, all four segments are
mandatory, and the verb is one of a closed set of eight: =get=, =get_many=,
=list=, =list_by_<relation>=, =put=, =put_many=, =delete=, =delete_many=. "A
component's resource may support any subset, but it may not invent a verb
outside this set." Operations that are not entity verbs go to a reserved
namespace, ={component}.v1.ops.{operation}=, whose discriminator is stated:
"an operation that acts on an entity the common way belongs in the common set,
and an operation that merely has an unusual name does not qualify as a domain
operation."

The tree does not match it. Every =nats_subject= literal in the headers, 2455
distinct, against the grammar:

| Finding | Count | Example |
|---------+-------+---------|
| Conforming | 2328 | =refdata.v1.currencies.put= |
| Not conforming | 127 | |
| — an operation that never moved to the =ops= namespace | 73 | =iam.v1.accounts.lock=, =trading.v1.trades.book=, =workflow.v1.instances.steps= |
| — a verb in kebab-case, where every segment must be snake_case | 31 | =iam.v1.accounts.change-password= |
| — a common verb renamed | 10 | =iam.v1.accounts.update=, =workspace.v1.trade-scope.set= |
| — five segments instead of four | 8 | =iam.v1.roles.permissions.put=, =ore.v1.ore.import.execute= |
| — three segments instead of four | 5 | =workflow.v1.start=, =marketdata.v1.tick= |
| A subject-part spelled twice, snake_case and kebab | 3 pairs | =inbox.v1.approval_requests= (8 subjects) / =inbox.v1.approval-requests= (5) |

(=*.v1.ops.*= is the reserved namespace for domain operations and is exempt
from the verb set. Five subjects already live there and conform.)

The raw census and the full classification are in =evidence/subject_census.txt=
beside this story, produced by reading every =nats_subject = "..."= literal in
the tree.

Three of the findings are worth naming separately because they are not the
same defect as the rest:

- *Five segments.* =iam.v1.roles.permissions.put= is a sub-resource spelled
  with a dot. The specification says a collection that belongs to an entity is
  named ={resource}_{collection}=, so that one subject is
  =iam.v1.roles_permissions.put=. The same shape appears at
  =telemetry.v1.nats.server-samples.list= and in the =*.import.execute= pair.
- *Three segments.* =workflow.v1.start=, =marketdata.v1.tick=,
  =marketdata.v1.export=, =marketdata.v1.import= and =scheduler.v1.status=
  have no resource segment at all.
- *Two spellings of one resource.* =dq.v1.lei-entities= and
  =dq.v1.lei_entities= are 1 and 8 subjects; =inbox.v1.approval-requests= and
  =inbox.v1.approval_requests= are 1 and 8; =reporting.v1.report-definitions=
  and =reporting.v1.report_definitions= are 1 and 8. In each pair the kebab
  spelling is the operation-only one, which means the resource was named twice
  rather than the operation being misplaced.

* Why this happened, and what stops it

Nothing checks. The specification's own § Conformance says "Every clause above
that can be checked mechanically is checked mechanically, and a generated
message that violates one fails the check rather than passing review", and then
says the components that have adopted it are named by one list -- and neither
the gate nor the list exists in the tree. The checks that do exist,
=check_protocol_twin_coverage.py= and =check_handler_permissions.py=, ask
whether a twin was generated and whether a code is seeded; neither reads a
subject.

So the gate comes first and the migration follows it. A migration without the
gate is a migration that grows back.

* Acceptance

- Every subject has four segments, is lower snake_case, and its verb is one of get, get_many, list, list_by_<relation>, put, put_many, delete, delete_many.
- An event subject is ={component}.v1.{resource}_events.{action}= with the action =created=, =updated= or =deleted=.
- No resource is spelled twice: no subject-part exists in both snake_case and kebab-case.
- A sub-resource is named ={resource}_{collection}=, never ={resource}.{collection}=.
- An operation that is not an entity verb lives in ={component}.v1.ops.{operation}=, and its model states why it is not one.
- A gate runs the census and fails a non-conforming subject, with the accepted exceptions recorded in one file beside their reason.
- The specification's Conformance section names a list of adopted components; that list exists as an artefact.

* Decisions

- *The gate comes before the migration.* A migration without a check grows
  back, and this one had 129 violations to grow from. The gate reads the
  models, so a defect is named at its source rather than in generated output
  that the next regeneration would rewrite.
- *This is one story and not a per-component sweep.* The defect is the rule
  being unenforced, not any one subject, and the same four classes appear in
  six components. Fixing them component by component would leave each
  component's rule as its own opinion.
- *The existing compliant models show the shape.*
  =ores.variability.operations= and =ores.reporting.operations= already declare
  domain operations in the reserved namespace, and each states in its own prose
  why the operation is not an entity verb. That justification is what the
  specification asks for, so it is copied rather than reinvented.
- *The IAM migration is shared with the credentials story.*
  [[id:117140A2-19CE-41EC-8734-A5648312385A][Separate the account's credentials from its profile]]
  has to move =iam.v1.accounts.{change-password,reset-password}= and friends
  anyway, and will move them onto the grammar while it is there. What it does
  not take is the rest of =iam=: =save=, =update=, the =iam.v1.system= and
  =iam.v1.auth= parts, and the account operations it does not touch.

* Result

No subject in the tree is outside the specification. The gate reads every
=:subject:= a model declares -- 159 of them -- and finds none that violates the
grammar, and the baseline that excused the 133 that did is empty. The census
above is the record of the state before the fix, kept because it is what the
work was scoped from.

The migration rewrote the 133 from a reviewed mapping, regenerated every
affected component, and fixed the fallout across 270 hand-written files: the
SQL populate seeds, the HTTP recipe scripts whose routes moved with their
subjects, the shell scripts, the documentation, the diagrams, and one runtime
request literal in =ores.nats=.

Three defects the work exposed, fixed here:

- =ensure_stream= returned as soon as a stream existed, without checking that
  its subject filter still matched. A renamed subject left a stream that no
  longer covered what its own service published, and every publish failed with
  "no responders". The filter is now reconciled.
- =publish_from_dq_function= parsed a suffix the migration removed, so every
  expansion function came back unnamed.
- The local runner ran =npm ci= and two regenerating checks in parallel with
  the checks that read what they write.

Verified at =2f80858540=: the local gate passes 44 of 44 checks, including the
full build, the database recreation and all 94 ctest suites.

* Out of scope

- The encoding, the transport, and which subjects a component owns. The
  specification puts those outside itself.
- Renaming a subject that already conforms. A subject in the closed set with a
  four-segment snake_case name is left alone even where a better name exists,
  because the point is conformance and not taste.
