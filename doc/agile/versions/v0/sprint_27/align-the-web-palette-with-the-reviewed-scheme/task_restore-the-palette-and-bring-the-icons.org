* Goal

The theme was moved onto the scheme the landing-page review argued, and the scheme was tried and rejected: the blue-black ramp on the slate neutrals read as flatter than the palette it replaced. Put the project site's palette back. Keep what the review contributed that is not a colour, and take the one thing it had and the application does not: a glyph on every card.

* Acceptance

- The theme's colours are the project site's again.
- The review's non-colour contributions survive.
- Every journey card carries an icon.

* Plan

Try the scheme before arguing about it. The theme is one block and every screen
reads it, so the palette moved in one commit and the only way to know whether
it is better is to run it and look at it. It was not better.

The revert is the same block. What the review contributed that is not a colour
stays, because none of it was the objection: the tints, the elevation, the
radius scale and the micro-label type were all things the theme lacked. The
glyphs are the one thing the review had and the application did not, so they
are the part that gets built rather than reverted.

* Result

The palette is the project site's again, and the grid has a face. Every tile at
the three call sites now leads with a glyph in a tinted square, takes the accent
on its border and a tinted elevation on hover, and carries a rule across its
foot with the arrow that goes in.

The scheme was rejected on sight, which is the honest reason and the reason the
commit that introduced it gave for trying it: a palette is not argued about
from contrast arithmetic, it is looked at. The measurements taken before the
move are sound and are why the accent survived at all — but they cannot say
whether a scheme reads well, and this one did not.

The glyphs are lucide, the set the review drew with, so a tile that is not
built yet can wear the future hue and the grid says which tiles can be gone to
before a word of it is read.
