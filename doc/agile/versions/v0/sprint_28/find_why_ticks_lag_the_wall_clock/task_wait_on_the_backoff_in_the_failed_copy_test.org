* Goal

The test of a failed copy does not fail on a loaded machine. It waits for the store to answer again, and it sends a burst that the queue of ticks does not overflow.

* Acceptance

- The test =a_failed_copy_loses_no_other_tick_and_stores_none_twice_while_the_next_batch_waits= has no fixed sleep for the backoff of the store. It waits until the store takes a probe tick, or reads the end of the backoff from the loop.
- The burst of the test is smaller than the queue of ticks, so the count of the republish cannot fall short because the queue dropped a tick.
- The counts of the test do not change with the number of probe ticks.
- The test passes ten times in a row at a load of 20 on 8 CPUs.
- A test that waits for a count of republished ticks does not wait out its deadline when a tick is dropped as stale or by the queue. It reads the drop counters of the loop, or it states the drop in its failure message, so the cause shows.
- The tests =a_failed_copy_loses_no_other_tick_and_stores_none_twice_while_the_next_batch_waits= and =a_small_batch_does_not_pass_the_batches_that_are_in_the_stages= both follow this rule.

* Notes

- Observed while reviewing the quiet loop (PR #3628): at a load of 16 to 43 on 8 CPUs, the failed copy test failed in 3 of 10 runs with the quiet path turned off and in about 3 of 9 runs with it on. The failing check is the wait for =republished == sent= after the burst of 20,000 ticks. The cause is a guess: a tick older than =max_tick_age= (5 s) is dropped when the machine is slow, so the count falls short. No run read the drop counters to confirm it. The failure is therefore not new with the quiet path.
