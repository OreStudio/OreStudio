* Goal

A fixing states who published it, so a simulated fixing must name the administrator whose fixing it stands for, as a public one does. The simulator serves a source of that publisher through the adapter contract, exactly as it serves a vendor's quotes; the catalogue names the publisher, its source, its notation and its vendor ids, and the system party routes each fixing item's publisher to one source.

The architecture is already stated in doc/knowledge/architecture/synthetic_generation_of_fixings.org: for each administrator of a simulated source the bundle installs a publisher of kind reference (wmr for LSEG's WM/R rates, emmi for EURIBOR, iba for the legacy LIBOR settings), a simulated source of that publisher, and a vendor instrument per fixing in the publisher's notation. A fixing item has one producer at a time: an overnight rate with a public adapter is never simulated, and a licensed cut such as WM/R is always simulated.

Today the catalogue has one publisher for all of it, simulated-fixings, with 82 vendor ids whose id_scheme is oresmd and whose external id is the item address, so the publisher has no notation of its own. It states 36 FX items across three of the seven cut codes and 46 IR indices. Thirteen overnight indices (AONIA, CAMARA, CDI, CORRA, CZEONIA, ESTR, FTIIE, NOWA, SOFR, SONIA, SORA, SWESTR, TONA) are also served by their public publisher, which the one-producer rule forbids.

* Acceptance

- The system bundle installs a publisher of kind reference for each administrator of a simulated fixing, each with one simulated source on session synthetic-local, a shadow rate card and vendor instruments in that administrator's notation.
- The WM/R fixing items are mapped by the wmr provider in the wmr notation, whose vendor ids are the cut keys of ores_refdata_cut_codes_tbl, and no fixing item is served by both a public publisher and the simulator.
- Neither KRW GENERIC nor JPY EYTIBOR exists anywhere in the catalogue or the store, and no fixing item is published by a source whose publisher administers nothing.
- LIBOR fixes under iba, and iba states that the settings are retired and kept for the teaching material.
- On a fresh provision the fixings screen shows one row per fixing item per publisher, each naming its administrator, and no simulated series exists for an index whose publisher the system party routes live.

* Decisions

- *No invented indices.* =KRW GENERIC 3M= is ORE's fallback name for a currency
  whose IBOR ORE does not know, and =JPY EYTIBOR 3M= names a benchmark the market
  retired. Neither has an administrator, so neither is a fixing the platform can
  state. Both are deleted with their series, their curve configs and their
  derived instruments. Nothing floats on them: no leg of the portfolio names
  either.
- *LIBOR keeps publishing.* Its administrator retired the settings between 2021
  and 2023, and the teaching material still uses them. The simulator publishes
  LIBOR as if the settings continued, under =iba=, the administrator that
  published them. The exception is deliberate and is recorded here and on the
  page that owns the subject, so a reader does not read it as stale data.
- *The three real term indices stay derived.* =USD SOFR 1M=, =COP IBR 3M= and
  =PLN WIBOR 6M= are computed by us from the run's overnight curves, so
  =derived= publishes them, as it publishes the FX volatilities we infer. Naming
  CME, Banco de la República or GPW Benchmark would attribute our arithmetic to
  them; when a real source of the same term index arrives, the rank rule replaces
  the derived one.
- *=simulated-fixings= goes.* With every index attributed and the invented ones
  removed, no fixing is left without an administrator, so the catch-all publisher
  has nothing to publish.
- *The demo portfolio is not re-pointed.* The 279 legs that float on LIBOR stay
  where they are, for the reason above.

* Plan

The pattern is the one the system already runs for quotes: provider → source →
session → adapter → vendor ids → mapping → item, with one adapter per source
and the simulator chosen by its session code, never by its name. Only the
catalogue rows and the ownership of the generators are wrong. The order below
matters in one place: a publisher has to exist before its notation does.

1. *Publishers per administrator* (system bundle SQL), of kind =reference=, each
   description naming what it administers: =wmr= for LSEG's WM/R rates and its
   seven cut codes; =iba= for the LIBOR settings, stating that the settings are
   retired and stay published for teaching; =emmi= for EURIBOR; the administrator
   of each remaining simulated index — =snb=, =danmarks-nationalbank=, =mnb=,
   =bok=, =rbnz=, =nbp=, =banxico=, =cbc=, =rbi=, =kofia= and the rest, using the
   provider the catalogue already holds where one exists.
2. *One simulated source per publisher.* =wmr-a=, =iba-a=, =emmi-a=, …, on
   session =synthetic-local=, service =end_of_day=, =is_default=, with a shadow
   rate card, built as =bloomberg-a= is built today.
3. *Notations and vendor ids.* A =wmr= notation whose ids are the cut's own
   keys, which =ores_marketdata_cut_codes_tbl.fixing_source_code= already holds
   (=NYC1000= → =WMR1000NYC=, =LON1600= → =WMR1600LON=), registered in the
   vendor notation registry. The rate publishers need no new notation: their
   instrument is the currency, the index name and the tenor, which is how the
   administrator names a setting (=USD LIBOR 3M=). Then the vendor instruments
   and mappings for every item. The degenerate =oresmd= notation goes when
   =simulated-fixings= does.
4. *Generators per source.* Each fixing generator attaches to the source of its
   publisher, as its node name already suggests
   (=synthetic.bloomberg.ir.yield_curve.sofr.usd=). The simulator keeps the
   generators; the catalogue keeps the sources.
5. *One producer per item.* Stop generating the thirteen indices a public
   publisher already serves, and add a check that fails when a fixing item is
   demanded from two providers the system party routes.
6. *Delete the invented indices.* =KRW GENERIC=, =JPY EYTIBOR= with their series,
   their curve configs and their derived instruments, so the catalogue states no
   index that no one administers.
7. *The screen.* Once a fixing names its administrator, the list states it: the
   publisher's name beside the source, and the benchmark the item carries, so a
   row reads =AUD/JPY · WM/R 10:00 New York= under a *Stated by* column.
8. *Proof.* A fresh provision: every fixing row attributable to an administrator,
   no item with two producers, neither invented index anywhere, the seven cut
   keys as vendor ids, LIBOR still fixing, and the screen reading as a production
   one does.
