* Goal

A service's pool has a floor it keeps and a ceiling a burst may reach. When
callers wait and the ceiling is above the floor, the pool opens a connection; when
a connection above the floor has been idle for a while, the pool closes it. A step
that runs two steps and four lanes gets the connections it asks for, and an idle
service holds only its floor.

* Acceptance

- The pool takes two numbers: the connections it keeps and the ceiling it may
  burst to, with the ceiling never below the floor.
- A connection is opened only when a caller is waiting for one and the ceiling
  permits it, so an idle service opens none beyond its floor.
- A connection above the floor is closed after an idle period, which is stated
  and configurable, and the pool never closes a connection it is leasing.
- A pool at its ceiling still fails its caller with the retryable "no connection
  came free" error, and the ceiling is large enough that a service's own work does
  not reach it: step workers times lanes, plus the handler work beside them.
- The number each service asks for is stated in its =.env=, and a service whose
  work grows states a larger ceiling without changing code.
- The unit tests state the growth, the reap and the ceiling, including a burst
  that empties the pool and a floor that is never closed.
- The marketdata service runs a provisioning step with its four lanes and records
  no acquisition failure where it previously recorded one.

* Plan

=pq::pool= is one class with one mutex, one condition variable and a vector of
idle connections; its constructor opens everything and =acquire= only waits. The
change is local: open on demand up to the ceiling, timestamp a connection when it
is given back, and reap above the floor on acquire or on a small timer.

The settings replace =db-pool-size= with a floor, a ceiling and an idle period,
read the way the others are (=ORES_<APP>_DB_POOL_*=).

The plan expected to keep a single-number form so that an environment stating one
number kept its meaning. Measured before the work: no environment stated it, so
the setting is replaced rather than aliased. What survives is the type-level
form - =pq::pool= still takes one number and keeps exactly that many - which is
what the tests and a deliberately fixed pool mean.

Sizing is the other half and belongs with
[[id:06195DFF-0D7C-4298-A0CF-464338CE728E][Size the connection pool and the
step's lanes together]], which owns the numbers this task only carries.

** What the pool became

=pq::pool= now carries =pool_settings= - a floor, a ceiling and an idle period -
and its constructor opens the floor under the open policy, as it always did.
=acquire= takes one of two paths under the lock: it takes an idle connection if
there is one, and otherwise reserves a slot when =open_ < ceiling= and opens a
connection outside the lock, so a slow login does not hold the pool for everyone
else. A reserve that fails gives the slot back and rethrows.

The reap is a thread the pool owns, started only when the ceiling is above the
floor: it waits on its own condition variable until the oldest idle connection
has been idle for the period, moves it out, unlocks, closes it, and takes the
lock again. Closing outside the lock is what keeps a teardown that blocks from
blocking a lease. A connection above the floor is reaped; the floor never is.

=give_back= stamps the connection as idle now and pushes it to the back, so
=acquire= takes the hottest and the reaper closes the coldest from the front.
The wait an acquirer does is on "something is idle, or a slot is free", so a
caller cannot sleep through a reap that made room.

** What the settings became

=db-pool-size= is gone. Three settings replace it, in the model, in =database_options=,
in =context_factory::configuration= and in the CLI:

| Setting | Field | Default | Means |
|---------+-------+---------+-------|
| =--db-pool-min= | =pool_min= | 2 | the connections the pool keeps |
| =--db-pool-max= | =pool_max= | 8 | the ceiling a burst may reach |
| =--db-pool-idle= | =pool_idle= | 30 | seconds an idle connection above the floor lives |

A non-positive floor, a non-positive idle period, and a ceiling below the floor
are each refused in words at the boundary, as =db-pool-size= was. The pool itself
raises a ceiling below the floor, and treats an idle period of nothing as the
shortest wait rather than as no wait, so a pool built directly from settings
cannot hold a ceiling under its floor or a reaper that spins.

The single-number form survives at the type level: =pq::pool= has a constructor
that takes one number and keeps exactly that many, which is what every test that
says =pool(options, 1)= means.

The .env states all three per service, in =env_init.py=: every service keeps two
and bursts to eight, the marketdata service bursts to sixteen because it runs two
provisioning steps with four lanes each, and the gateway bursts to sixteen because
it serves every HTTP request of the environment. The .env format version is 34.

* Result

A service's pool now keeps a stated floor and may burst to a stated ceiling,
closing what the burst leaves idle, so the number a service asks for is what its
work needs rather than what the server can spare. The marketdata service states
sixteen because it runs two provisioning steps with four lanes each.

Evidence, at =177587f9e9=:

- the nine targets the change reaches build, 347 of 347, in 28 minutes, with no
  warnings;
- the seven suites that assert on pool settings pass, 1221 test cases;
- =db-pool-min=, =db-pool-max= and =db-pool-idle= are stated per service in the
  environment, and the .env format is 34.

What is still owed: the acceptance line that the marketdata service runs a
provisioning step with its four lanes and records no acquisition failure. The
pool can now serve that burst, but proving it means running a provisioned party
against the fleet, which is a fleet run and not a unit test. It is carried by
[[id:36FDD33A-6406-4CF6-9687-807BF36A172E][Four lanes of a provisioning step meet
no acquisition failure]] rather than claimed here, and that scenario is PENDING
until somebody runs it.
