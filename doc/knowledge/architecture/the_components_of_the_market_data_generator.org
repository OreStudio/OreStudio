* Summary

This page is the implementation counterpart to the domain pages it cites: where
[[id:BB2B7C4C-FD4E-4DCA-BF1D-00F929622B87][Synthetic market data generation: approach]] decides *what* is generated and by
which method, this page says *which types do it*, how a run is put together, and how
one tick moves every part in step. A run's market is one =run_market= that owns three
collaborators: a loop that decides when, a latent market that decides why prices move
together, and a board that holds the values feeds share. Feeds are built by a factory
from the config plane, tick on the loop, and publish both to NATS and to the board.

Read it before changing a generator, the scheduler or the board, and read
[[id:B7A03C80-6566-4E97-9D45-14A69726C381][Adding an asset class]] when the change is a new asset class rather than a change
of shape.

* Detail

** The pieces

| Piece | Type | Owns |
|-------+------+------|
| The run's market | =run_market= | the loop, the noise and the values, and the block-to-series map between them |
| The loop | =tick_scheduler= | operational time, the series heap, the arrival laws, the pace, the heat, the regime, the history and the trace |
| The noise | =latent_market= | named factors, their keyed paths, per-series loadings, and the driver a series reads |
| The values | =market_board= | a curve per currency and a spot per pair, each with the time it was last published |
| A generator | =i_feed= | one config row's quotes: its process, its tick body and the subjects it publishes on |
| The factory and the registry | =feed_factory=, =feed_kind_registry= | the kind seam: one string selects the build input and the builder |
| The engines | =fx_vol_surface_generator=, =fx_vol_surface_emitter=, the process library | the mathematics a generator holds: SSVI state, mixture and GARCH variance, short rates |

The pieces are the domain pages': the loop and its arrivals are
[[id:F135B9AF-902F-46C5-847C-0820D8C92D28][One scheduler draws every feed's gaps]], the noise is
[[id:25C467BE-07A3-432C-8CB8-6B194E748570][Shared Brownian factors give every synthetic series a signed correlation]], the
processes are [[id:A8C46639-0639-4587-B07D-B8B006C46618][Stochastic Processes]], and the values are
[[id:675E89FC-B5AA-4250-8B86-78062F0F22BC][The forward a synthetic surface is struck against]]. This page does not restate
their reasoning; it states the shape that carries it.

** How they fit

The dependency rule the diagram is drawn to: *the loop never depends on the noise or
on the values*. A generator reaches values through =run_market::values()=, so its use of
another generator's output is visible where the generator is built, and the loop stays
one thing: time.

** The types and their contracts

#+caption: The types of a run's market, the generators that use them, and the interfaces a generator implements.
[[proj:doc/knowledge/architecture/the_components_of_the_market_data_generator_classes.png]]

Source: [[./the_components_of_the_market_data_generator_classes.puml][=the_components_of_the_market_data_generator_classes.puml=]]. Regenerate with
=plantuml -tpng doc/knowledge/architecture/the_components_of_the_market_data_generator_classes.puml=.

- *=run_market=* is composition and nothing else: it builds its own loop, installs the
  latent market's driver hook on it, and hands out the three collaborators. It carries
  no price, no timing policy and no state of its own beyond the map from a feed's series
  to the factors that drive it, which is noise wiring.
- *=tick_scheduler=* fires a series' body when the series' mark is reached and gives it
  a =tick_context=. It knows nothing of prices.
- *=latent_market=* answers one question: which standard normals does this series read
  for the interval it is stepping? It writes them into a buffer the caller owns.
- *=market_board=* is a table. Publishing replaces a member's values and its timestamp;
  reading takes a view of that storage; a member is created once, by the feed that owns
  it, and referred to afterwards by a small integer.
- *=i_feed=* is the generator seam: =start=, =tick=, =stop=, plus the identity a
  control plane needs. Everything an asset class adds is behind it.

** Starting a feed

#+caption: Starting one feed: the config plane resolves the row, the factory builds it, and the loop takes its body.
[[proj:doc/knowledge/architecture/the_components_of_the_market_data_generator_start.png]]

Two things in that sequence are load-bearing. The board member is resolved in the
feed's own constructor, off the loop, once; nothing on the tick path looks a member up
by name. And the body the loop takes captures the feed, so the feed outlives the
scheduling of it -- =remove= waits until the loop is no longer in the body, which is what
lets a feed be destroyed after its =stop=.

** The dynamics

*** Time

One operational time, $\tau$, belongs to the run. Every series is scaled by one
bracket: a series with sensitivity $s$ whose law draws a gap $g$ ticks when $\tau$ has
moved by $g/s$. The mark is fixed when the gap is drawn, so a change in the market --
a pin, a heat jump, a speed change -- moves $\tau$ faster and moves no mark
([[id:9D778F72-8FF6-4F01-AF4B-696DC7BA855A][The market's volatility channel]] states what that coupling means for the
prices).

A body reads the clock it is given rather than assuming a tick is a fixed step:
=context.elapsed_seconds=, =context.simulated_seconds= (the market's speed times the
market time it covered), and =context.tail_tilt=. A spot feed multiplies the simulated
seconds by its own ticks a second to get the step it must take; a curve feed steps one
fixed simulated step, because a calendar day per tick is its unit; a surface feed steps
one nominal tick. That is why a run with a thousand feeds and mixed arrival laws still
has one coherent time: the clock is shared, the steps are each feed's own.

*** Arrivals, the heat and Hawkes

#+caption: The regime chain, the shared heat and a series' own self-excitation.
[[proj:doc/knowledge/architecture/the_components_of_the_market_data_generator_regime.png]]

Source: [[./the_components_of_the_market_data_generator_regime.puml][=the_components_of_the_market_data_generator_regime.puml=]]. Regenerate with
=plantuml -tpng doc/knowledge/architecture/the_components_of_the_market_data_generator_regime.puml=.

Each series carries one arrival law, and the laws are the four in
[[id:F135B9AF-902F-46C5-847C-0820D8C92D28][One scheduler draws every feed's gaps]]:

- *Fixed*, an interval, for a curve that should tick on a cadence.
- *Poisson*, memoryless, whose rate is the configured one.
- *Regime-switching*, two states whose intensities differ by the regime's multiplier --
  the market is calm or active, and every series of that kind moves with it.
- *Hawkes*, self-exciting: a tick raises the series' own intensity, which decays. The
  branching ratio says how many ticks one tick begets on average and the decay says how
  fast that fades, both in the series' own operational time.

Above them sits the *heat*: every event of every series adds to a shared intensity that
fades, so a burst anywhere lifts the low end of the market. Its long-run mean is the
event rate times jump over decay, its loop gain is jump over decay, and a series takes
the share of it its own rate is of the total. It is a known property of this channel
that with the heat on, the average tick rate sits above the configured one; that is
stated by the arrival page and is not a defect of the implementation.

The three are deliberately layered, and the layering is what keeps them composable: the
*regime* is exogenous and seeded, so it is reproducible from the run's seed; the *heat*
is endogenous, so it is reproducible only up to the events that fed it, which is what
contagion means; and a *Hawkes* series adds its own excitation on top in its own time.
A person pins the regime from the Market tab when a demonstration must be steady, and a
pin is a state rather than a rate, so it reaches every series at once.

*** Noise, and where each random number comes from

=ores.analytics.quant.math::rng_stream= is counter-based (Philox4x32-10), so a draw is
addressed by a key and an index rather than read in order, and a stream is named by
={run seed, series, purpose}=. The purposes are what keep the run reproducible while
still letting a burst move one feed and not another:

| Purpose | Stream | Draws |
|---------+--------+-------|
| =market= | one for the run, and one per latent factor | the regime chain's switches, and each factor's keyed path |
| =arrival= | one per series | that series' gaps, whatever its law |
| =price= | one per series | that series' own noise, and the draws its process makes |

The heat is not drawn: it is computed from the events that fed it, which is why a run
replays only up to the events it saw. The latent market turns a series' *price*
randomness into a shared quantity: each factor
is a Brownian path keyed on a fixed grid of cells in simulated time with a dyadic
bridge inside a cell, so a factor's value at any instant is a function of the seed and
the time and of who asked nobody. A series' driver is
$W_i(t) = \sum_k b_{ik} F_k(t) + d_i E_i(t)$, so two series correlate by
$\mathbf{b}_i \cdot \mathbf{b}_j$, exactly and whatever their tick times. A block of
series that must correlate beyond the common factors -- a pair's spot and its surface --
shares a *local* factor, and that is what makes a surface lean with its own spot.

The driven step is the seam that makes this cost nothing when it is unused: a process
whose =next_with_normal(z)= is called still runs its own law -- the mixture still picks
its component, GARCH still sets the day's variance, the tail tilt still shapes it -- and
only the Gaussian it scales is shared. A series the market does not drive reads an empty
driver span, never a zero, so a feed cannot mistake "not driven" for "drawn zero".

*** Prices, per asset class

- *FX spot.* A Gaussian mixture over the day's components, whose draw is standardised
  and scaled by a GJR-GARCH variance that persists between ticks
  ([[id:46FF8EF4-3874-4C9A-9222-69DA691E793F][The FX spot producer]]).
- *IR curves.* One short-rate model per currency, chosen from the five in
  [[id:D6FDEFC6-ECF9-4C9A-A566-9BB61FCDBAC1][the yield curve process type catalogue]], whose =discount_factor(ticks)= prices
  every pillar it publishes and is the curve it puts on the board
  ([[id:FB57F761-0FC3-4283-AD32-CCF2FE68AD2E][The IR curve producer]]).
- *Volatility surfaces.* An SSVI surface whose state is a Cox-Ingersoll-Ross forward
  variance per expiry segment, a hyperbolic tangent of an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck skew and a
  logistic of a second OU for curvature, every process reverting to where it started, so
  each emitted surface is inside the bounds that keep it free of static arbitrage
  ([[id:D4C7C4A7-CD2D-4307-AB06-DA9BE3C3A334][A synthetic contributor publishes noisy observations of a latent surface]]).

The measures are deliberately split, as the approach note sets out: the states are
real-world, the surfaces are a risk-neutral parameterisation, and the guarantee is
static arbitrage per emitted surface, not dynamic arbitrage between one day's surface and
the next.

*** Values: the board

#+caption: A run's board after a few ticks, and the three members one surface reads.
[[proj:doc/knowledge/architecture/the_components_of_the_market_data_generator_board.png]]

Source: [[./the_components_of_the_market_data_generator_board.puml][=the_components_of_the_market_data_generator_board.puml=]]. Regenerate with
=plantuml -tpng doc/knowledge/architecture/the_components_of_the_market_data_generator_board.puml=.

The board holds what a vendor would not publish: its own curves and spots, which its own
feeds must agree on. A curve member is a spot date at one and a discount factor per
pillar date, so a reader interpolates from a spot date outwards; a spot member is one
number. Each member carries the time it was published, which is what tells a quiet market
from a stopped feed.

*** One tick

#+caption: One firing of a series: the loop asks for its drivers, hands them to the body, and the body publishes.
[[proj:doc/knowledge/architecture/the_components_of_the_market_data_generator_tick.png]]

Source: [[./the_components_of_the_market_data_generator_tick.puml][=the_components_of_the_market_data_generator_tick.puml=]]. Regenerate with
=plantuml -tpng doc/knowledge/architecture/the_components_of_the_market_data_generator_tick.puml=.

The loop releases its lock around the body, so a body never takes a lock the loop holds;
that is what allows a feed to publish and to ask the board in the middle of a tick. A
burst of ticks costs one driver call each, and a body that steps a longer span keeps the
process's own draw rather than pretending the span was one tick.

*** The surface's tick, which is the one that reads the board

#+caption: A surface's tick: it reads three board members, resolves one market per expiry, and emits.
[[proj:doc/knowledge/architecture/the_components_of_the_market_data_generator_surface.png]]

Source: [[./the_components_of_the_market_data_generator_surface.puml][=the_components_of_the_market_data_generator_surface.puml=]]. Regenerate with
=plantuml -tpng doc/knowledge/architecture/the_components_of_the_market_data_generator_surface.puml=.

This is the whole reason the board exists. The surface is a pure generator of noise -- its
own SSVI state -- and it needs the *forward* to turn a delta into a strike, and the
forward is the ratio of two curves and a spot that other feeds own. It resolves the
market every tick, so a curve's move reaches the surface's quotes without a restart, and
the quant block =math::pair_market_at= is the one rule both it and the party-side
calibration use ([[id:675E89FC-B5AA-4250-8B86-78062F0F22BC][the design page]] states why
that matters).

The decision is two free functions rather than logic buried in the body:
=fx_vol_board_inputs= answers the three values or names the member it could not read, and
=fx_vol_expiry_markets= answers one market per expiry. What is left in the body is the
walk over the points and the publish. So a test publishes to a board, reads it back
through the surface's own read, and asserts both the sentence and the markets without a
socket -- =feeds_fx_vol_surface_tick_tests.cpp=.

*** What a person can change, and what it moves

| Control | Reaches | Effect |
|---------+---------+--------|
| The regime pin | every series | a forced calm or active state, applied at once and in the trace |
| The market's pace | every series | a speed change is applied from the loop's now and reaches every simulated second from then |
| A feed's sensitivity | that series | scales its intensity and its step, without moving any other series' marks |
| A feed's arrival law | that series | its gap distribution, which the loop builds when the feed is added |
| The loadings | the driven series | the correlation between two series, set from the shell, read by the latent market |

** Where a new asset class lands

A generator is code plus config, and the shape is designed so that a new asset class adds
a kind rather than a case. Write the five edits in
[[id:B7A03C80-6566-4E97-9D45-14A69726C381][Adding an asset class]], and then:

- a generator that others read from -- a spot, a curve, a strip -- publishes a member to
  =values()= under a kind and a code, and reads nothing new;
- a generator that reads others -- a surface, an option, a cross-currency instrument --
  holds =run_market= and reads =values()= in its constructor, so its dependency is a line
  where it is built;
- a volatility surface calls =add_block(anchor, name, ...)=, which is already generic and
  only named for FX where it is called;
- nothing is added to =run_market= and nothing is added to the loop.

** The rules the shape holds

1. *The loop owns no values.* It calls one driver hook and nothing else; a scheduler that
   can hand out market data is a scheduler that will.
2. *Noise owns no prices, and values own neither.* Three collaborators that do not know
   each other is what keeps each of them testable alone.
3. *A feed writes only its own member*, and its member id is resolved once, off the loop.
4. *Nothing on the tick path allocates per lookup.* The board deals in ids, and a
   member's values live in a buffer the publisher reuses.
5. *No generator writes a market data table.* Generators publish; the ingest stores, and
   nothing downstream can tell a simulated source from a real one
   ([[id:69287DA4-3558-4EAE-972B-82BC7F9296E9][The simulator stops at the adapter seam]]).
6. *One normal is one nominal tick.* A step over a longer span keeps the process's own
   draw, which is what keeps a burst from inventing extra randomness.

** Performance and memory

- The board is sized when the run is built, so a member never moves and an id is stable
  for the run's life. Publishing assigns into the member's own buffer; a tick allocates
  nothing once a curve's size holds, which the tests pin by checking the buffer's address
  across a hundred republishes.
- Creating a member is the only locked operation, and it happens off the loop. Publishing
  and reading take no lock, because a member's values belong to the thread that ticks
  them.
- A curve of thirty years is about 30 points, so a currency's member is a few hundred
  bytes of points and every calendar the platform holds would fit in under a megabyte.
  The board is not a cache and needs no eviction policy.
- Per tick the loop pays one =std::function= call for the drivers and one =std::string=
  from the body, against a wire encode and a publish that are orders of magnitude larger.
  At ten thousand ticks a second the dispatch is well under a millisecond a second, so
  the rule worth enforcing is the fourth above rather than micro-optimising the loop.

** Two-way prices and depth, when they come

Both are out of scope today -- every quote is a mid and valuation runs on mids
([[id:95541791-E25C-44A5-9625-C8A1E44976D7][the story]] defers bid and ask to a later level of work) -- and they land
very differently on this shape.

*Fits as it stands.*

- *A feed's body.* A book feed is a new =i_feed= kind: the loop, the arrival laws,
  the adapter seam and the runner need no change. Hawkes is already the right law
  for order flow, which is the one thing this architecture buys for free.
- *The heat.* A burst of orders exciting more orders is the channel's own design,
  per series and market-wide.
- *A stochastic spread as state.* The spread is one more *block member*: the latent
  market already hands a series K normals from its loadings, and a block sizes K from
  the members it declares, so a log-Ornstein-Uhlenbeck or CIR spread process -- mean
  reverting, positive, stepped through =next_with_normal= -- is a member and a loading
  row, not a mechanism. Its correlation with the mid, with volatility and with the
  regime is then the loading it is given, and a spread that widens in the active
  regime is a regime loading.
- *The contributor layer.* A contributor already has its own stream and noise profile,
  and the latent-surface note states the rule: bid and ask are an extension of the
  contributor and leave the mid path unchanged. A width per contributor per point is a
  second field in the profile, and the clean mid stays what tests compare against.

*Additive, but a real decision each.*

- *The published point.* =market_tick= and =fx_vol_point_tick= carry one value. A
  two-way quote needs a pair, so either the scalar message is widened or a second
  message carries the pair. The rule to keep is that a mid consumer never changes:
  widen the *store* (a side, or a spread) and add a message, rather than bending the
  one every consumer reads.
- *The board.* A two-sided curve is a new member *kind* -- two dated discount series,
  bid and ask -- kept flat as two spans rather than one nested value. It is additive
  because a member is a kind and a code, and it is the kind of thing that would be
  expensive only if the board had been built as a curve cache with curve-shaped
  accessors.
- *The calibration.* A tighter quote is more informative than a wide one, so contributors
  gain weights by width, and a two-sided calibration fits the mid with a spread model or
  fits both sides. That is a change of weights, not of shape.

*Needs design, not extension.*

- *Depth is not a scalar observation.* The market data path is a series of
  (instant, value) observations; a book is state with structural updates -- levels,
  sizes, cancels -- so it needs its own datum and its own store, snapshots plus deltas,
  and an identity per instrument rather than per point. A book must *not* become a board
  member: the board's values are dated scalars in reused buffers, and a book would
  break both of those properties.
- *Two sources of truth for the spread.* If a book exists, the top of the book is the
  spread; a stochastic spread model beside it would give two answers. The split worth
  choosing is the one the current vendor model already implies: where there is depth the
  book is primary and the spread is derived; where a contributor quotes without depth --
  volatility surfaces, curves -- the state process is the only source there is.
- *Matching.* A market data vendor publishes book updates, which is not the same as
  publishing trades. Trades need either a matching step inside the run, which would be
  the loop's first deterministic per-event computation, or the venue role left out.

*What to keep true now so that stays cheap.*

1. =market_tick= stays a scalar; two-way arrives as its own message.
2. A board member stays a dated scalar, and a book is never one.
3. A block sizes its loadings from the members it declares, so a spread is a member.
4. =i_feed= stays the only generator seam: a book feed is a kind, not a runner change.
5. A contributor's width is the contributor's, not the instrument's.

** Where the code is today

The shape above is the code: =run_market= lives in =ores.analytics.quant=, owns its
loop, its noise and its values, and installs the driver hook on the loop it owns. The
board is not the loop's, and no feed reaches values through it.

What remains of the board task is the half the shape was built for: a surface still
takes its markets when it is built rather than building them from =values()= on every
tick, so a curve's move does not yet reach the surface's quotes
([[id:DDF8585F-75EE-4190-B20B-8A913DC1F883][Build the run's market board and strike every surface against it]], stage 4).
