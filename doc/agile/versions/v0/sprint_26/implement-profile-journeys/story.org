* Goal

The three Profile journeys run in =ores.web= against the real server. A member sets their own photo, name, job title and contact details, and a tenant administrator corrects a colleague's. The screen is one screen with panels, and each panel says who may write it. A reporting-line change is a proposal that a hierarchical superior or an administrator approves, not a plain edit.

* Acceptance

- The three Profile journeys run in =ores.web= against the real server: a
  member sets their own photo, name, job title and contact details, and a
  tenant administrator corrects a colleague's.
- The self-service subjects exist, and each is restricted to the caller's own
  account and to the fields a member owns. Every other field is refused with a
  code the screen branches on.
- The photo can be replaced: an upload path into =ores.assets= returns an image
  id, and the accepted formats and the maximum size are stated once and
  enforced by the picker and by the server.
- One Save writes both records and names the record that failed. Nothing on the
  screen claims the pair is atomic.
- A reporting-line change is a proposal. The screen states who must approve it,
  the value does not change while the proposal waits, and no approval is
  claimed that nothing performs.
- The screen says which panel the caller may write, and every panel the caller
  may not write says why.
- The two email fields are labelled so that neither reads as the other.
- A signed-in person reaches the journeys from the shell's navigation, and the
  trigger that opens it shows the person's own name and photo once the account
  has been read.
- Every gap the three journey documents record is closed or stated on the
  screen, and the statement is recorded.

* Decisions

- *The shell is a menu, the cards, and the avatar.* The header carries a menu of
  areas, an area opens its journeys as cards, and the person's own journeys are
  under their name and photo. Nothing else: there is no sidebar and no second
  list of what the cards already say. (Prototype verdict, 2026-10-01.)
- *The mode is read from the sign-in, never chosen.* A super administrator works
  in the system tenant's context, a tenant administrator in a tenant's, and a
  party user in a party's — the contexts the server already scopes everything
  by, so the mode cannot disagree with the data and there is no switch to get
  wrong.
- *People is the privileged party user's area.* Managing the people of a party
  is a party job done by somebody with authority in it, not a tenant
  administrator's job, and a regular party user is not shown the door to it at
  all.
- *The bootstrap journey is a door, not an area.* First run belongs to a plain
  installation, where there is no account to sign in with and therefore no shell
  to draw a menu in. It is the way out of the state the shell cannot be entered
  from, so no menu offers it. The system administration menu is the *Tenants*
  area, which holds the tenant management journeys: creating a tenant, and
  retiring or resetting one. *Tune the tenant* is the tenant administrator's and
  stays in their mode. (Product owner, 2026-10-01.)
- *The mode is stated by the server and read by the browser.* The session view
  and the login answer carry it, and the shell draws its menu from the value it
  is given. The BFF states it today, from the tenant the login answered with,
  because that is the fact the session already holds and the rule stays out of
  the browser; when =ores.iam= states it on the login answer the rule goes and
  nothing on the client moves. (Implementation, 2026-10-01.)
- *A mode with no implemented journeys shows the session, not an empty menu.*
  The application mode declares no areas because a tenant-scoped session and the
  permission codes it needs do not exist yet. It shows the tenant and the party
  the person works in, which are facts, rather than drawing areas it cannot
  fill. (Implementation, 2026-10-01.)

- *The screen is one page, and each panel saves itself.* Variant *B* of the
  prototype is accepted: the profile and the contact record carry their own
  versions and their own change reasons, so each panel writes its own record
  with its own reason and a half-finished edit never rides along with the one
  that is ready. The rail variant is rejected because this screen is a page a
  person returns to, not a run with a beginning and an end, which is what makes
  a rail right for the registration door. (Prototype verdict, 2026-09-30.)
- *The member writes their own record.* =iam.v1.accounts.update-self= and
  =iam.v1.account_contact_informations.update-self= are added, restricted to
  the caller's own account and to the fields a member owns: =full_name=,
  =job_title=, =image_id= and the contact record. Without them two of the three
  journeys are read-only, which is the largest gap the group document records.
  (Product owner, 2026-09-30.)
- *The photo is replaced, not only chosen.* An upload path into =ores.assets=
  returns an image id, and the accepted formats and the maximum size are
  stated. Step 2 of the flagship journey cannot happen at all otherwise.
  (Product owner, 2026-09-30.)
- *A reporting-line change is a proposal.* The person whose line it is proposes
  the change; their hierarchical superior or a tenant administrator approves it,
  and the line changes only then. An administrator acting on a colleague's
  record is the approver, so their own change takes effect. (Product owner,
  2026-09-30.) It is not a plain edit on the profile screen, and it is not a
  plain edit on Membership's tree either: both documents change to record the
  proposal.
- *One Save, two writes, and the failure is named.* The profile and the contact
  record are separate records and no composite operation is added now, so the
  screen sends both and names the record that failed. (Product owner,
  2026-09-30.)
- *Two emails stay two, and both are labelled.* =account.email= is the address a
  person signs in with; the contact record's =email= is the address colleagues
  use. The screen shows both, labelled apart, and neither reads as the other.
- *The sign-in and access panel is read-only here.* It belongs to
  [[id:5FB73D18-4734-485B-B414-9EBD1B76A239][Protect my account]] and
  [[id:3CA48F40-B601-497B-BA78-9822DBEBA8E5][Know what I may do]], so the
  profile screen carries it as a summary with the reason stated on it.

* Out of scope

- The approval itself. Nothing in the platform holds one today: the workflow
  engine can hold the instance, but the queue, the notification and the
  decision belong to the [[id:36FC0D92-F532-4B6C-99FE-B669DB87C0FE][Draw the reporting line]] journey and to the Membership
  group. The profile screen draws the proposal and states who decides; it does
  not build the decision.
- The roles a person holds, which is the Access group's work, and the password,
  the sign-in state and the sign-in audit, which are the Credentials group's.
- Which of the two email fields other screens treat as authoritative. The
  screen labels them apart; the modelling decision is its own work.
