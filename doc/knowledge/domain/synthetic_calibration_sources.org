#+title: Synthetic prices are calibrated from free public sources
#+description: Which free public sources set the initial values and the volatility of the synthetic ticks, what each source's terms say, and what is still unchecked.
#+type: knowledge
#+level: cross
#+filetags: :marketdata:synthetic:calibration:
#+created: 2026-10-23
#+updated: 2026-10-23

* Summary

A synthetic tick needs an initial value and a volatility. The values come from free public sources, never from a vendor, and each generator config names its source. The sources are the ECB euro reference rates and the Federal Reserve H.10 release for FX, read through the Frankfurter API, and the US Treasury par yield curve with the central banks' overnight rates for curves. A retrieval step reads the value that is as close to today as the source allows, stores it with the time of retrieval, and the screen shows that time. The table in "What was read, and when" says which terms were read from an official page and which were not.

* Detail

** Why a calibration source and not a vendor

The synthetic ticks are native to the vendor: a Bloomberg generator sends Bloomberg ids. The level of the price has no link to the vendor. The level comes from the world, so it comes from a public source that anyone can check. The calibration source is a property of the generator config, and it does not depend on the provider.

** Frankfurter as the access route for FX

[[https://github.com/lineofflight/frankfurter][Frankfurter]] is an open source (MIT) exchange rate API. It reads the daily rates of about 105 central banks and official sources, among them the ECB, the Federal Reserve through FRED, the BIS and the central banks of Taiwan, Chile and Brazil. Its [[https://frankfurter.dev][documentation]] says the rates go back to 1948, that there are no quotas but requests are rate-limited, and that storing daily data locally is encouraged. A call to =/v2/rates= returns the latest, a past date or a time series, as JSON, CSV or NDJSON. The hosted API is =api.frankfurter.dev=, and a Docker image runs it on our own host with a SQLite file. Some providers need a free key of their own, such as FRED.

It answers the coverage gap, because one client reads the pairs that neither the ECB nor the H.10 carries. A call to =/v2/providers= lists a terms page for most providers, and some providers have none.

Three rules keep the provenance honest:
- The retrieval step asks for one provider at a time and never takes the blended rate. The blended rate is Frankfurter's own calculation, and it hides which bank the number comes from. Each stored value keeps the provider key, so the credit and the terms of that provider apply.
- The hosted service is a convenience. The step reads the address from a setting, so a team can point it at its own Frankfurter in Docker without a change of code.
- Frankfurter is the route for FX only. The Treasury curve and the overnight rates are read from their own sources.

Two things are not settled. The licence page of Frankfurter was not read, because its address redirects. The terms of each provider are the ones its own page states.

** What this page does not settle

- Whether the Treasury feed carries a current licence. The next step is to read the current site policy of the Treasury.
- Which series of the Bank of England database other than SONIA fall under the Open Government Licence.
- Whether the FRED terms allow a copy of a series in a stored snapshot. The H.10 notes of each series say whether the word "Copyright" appears. The other route is the Board's own H.10 download, which the Board's public domain notice covers.
- Which of the 26 FX pairs of [[id:3D0E46BD-02A4-4A2F-863A-B948B7E44FCD][the mapping inventory]] the ECB and the H.10 do not cover. The pairs that no source covers keep a fixed level, and the config says so.

** How a generator uses a source

Each generator config points at a published quote: an item, a policy and a window for the volatility. The initial value is the latest value on or before the date of the market clock, and the volatility is the spread of the log returns over the window. The config does not load anything. A separate loader reads the public sources into the table of published quotes, and a reader returns the value with its provenance. See [[id:F4BBEF34-D84C-4AE6-A3AA-DC47C3EE06EE][Published quotes are loaded once from public sources and read by the calibration]].

A copy of a source's statistics keeps the credit and the notice that its terms ask for. The ECB rule is the strictest, because it allows reuse when the statistics are not modified. So each ECB value is stored exactly as the ECB published it, with the credit "Source: ECB statistics". A cross rate, such as USD/JPY from EUR/JPY and EUR/USD, is computed when the generator starts, from the two stored values. It is an input to a synthetic price and is not published as an ECB statistic. That is our reading of the policy, and the owner of the ECB policy has not confirmed it. If it proves too strict, the pairs that need a cross take their level from a source that quotes the pair directly.

** What this replaces

The 2016 ORE sample vintage no longer calibrates any generator. The datasets that depend on it are replaced when the sources are in place. The uniform demo is not calibrated from any source: its exaggerated volatility is the point, and it keeps fixed levels.
