* Summary

This document describes the target state of volatility surface construction in
ORE Studio. A surface is not observed in the market; contributors quote a sparse
ladder of points, and a calibration fits a smile model to those quotes so that a
valuation can read a volatility at any expiry and strike. The document follows
that construction from the blueprint that states the recipe, to the pillars that
name the market series they read, to the calibration that produces the surface,
to the candidate it writes, and to the publication that makes the set
authoritative. It states what the identification system and the lineage records
have to hold at each step, and it names the changes that reach the target. It is
the counterpart, step for step, of
[[id:446F28DF-155A-47EC-8D66-FDB90A8FBFA7][Bootstrapping a curve: from quotes to a published set]],
and it differs from it only where a surface differs from a curve.

* The problem: a surface is not observed

A valuation needs the volatility of each option it prices, at that option's
expiry and strike. The market does not quote that volatility. It quotes, for a
pair, an ATM volatility, risk reversals and butterflies at a few deltas and a few
expiries, under conventions of the pair, and different contributors quote
different numbers for the same point. A surface is the function of expiry and
strike implied by those quotes under a smile model and a set of conventions. The
construction is therefore an inference, and it is a decision: which
contributors, which expiries and deltas, which smile model, which interpolation
across expiries.

Two consequences follow, as they do for a curve. The surface is derived, so its
identity must say so: a calibrated point is a datum of a calibrated surface
series, not an observation of a contributor's quote. And the inference must be
recorded, so a point can be traced to the quotes it was calibrated to and to the
version of the recipe that calibrated it.

* The recipe: the blueprint and its pillars

A blueprint is a named recipe. It states the party, the pair, the smile model,
the interpolation across expiries, the output grid of expiries and strikes, and
the output series the surface will be written to. It refers to the pair's
quoting convention in =fx_option_convention= (its ATM type, its delta type, its
butterfly style and the expiry at which the long-term conventions take over),
and to the =fx_volatility_config= and =fx_volatility_smile= that describe the
surface to ORE. The concepts are in
[[id:5B513D53-5DF7-4540-99C0-60D909678873][FX Volatility Surface]] and
[[id:5910304A-FA45-461E-B2A2-8F24FA798769][SVI Volatility Parameterisation]].

Its pillars are the quoted points the inference uses. A pillar states an expiry
and a strike label: =ATM=, a risk reversal or a butterfly at a delta, or a call or
a put at a delta. Unlike a curve's pillars, a surface's pillars have no order the
construction depends on; each pillar names one series per contributor it reads,
with the weight that contributor carries in the calibration.

Two rules belong to the recipe rather than to the engine. The smile model sets
how many pillars each expiry needs: SSVI and SABR need an ATM, a risk reversal and
a butterfly at one delta at least, and a five-point spline needs two deltas. And
the conventions decide what a quote means: a butterfly read in the smile
convention and the same number read as a broker strangle are different surfaces.
Both are part of what the blueprint decides, and both are checked before any
quote is read.

* The inputs: named series, the as-of and the dates

In the target state a pillar names the market series its quote comes from. The
series is an observed quote series, an oresmd URI such as
=oresmd://fx/EUR?type=quote&instrument=fx_option&quote=rate_lnvol&ccy=USD&expiry=1Y&strike_label=25RR=,
and the pillar holds a soft reference to one such series per contributor, as
[[id:7AE57098-08D4-4D4A-83D6-918FE520B68E][A pillar names the market series it reads]]
requires. The identity of the input is deliberately not the identity of the
output: the quote is observed and the surface point is inferred, and the two
must remain distinguishable.

At an as-of, the compute reads the observation of each named series at that
instant. The pillar's expiry and strike label are the datum's coordinates. The
expiry is resolved to a date through the reference data, as a curve's tenor is
([[id:0220F3F7-C674-47EE-91CE-1C04804B4558][Tenor and Curve Bootstrapping Architecture]]),
and each strike label is resolved to a strike through the pair's delta and ATM
conventions and the forward to the expiry, which the compute reads from the
published curves of the two currencies.

A missing observation is not a refusal by itself, because contributors are
expected to leave points out. A pillar with no observation from any of its
contributors is reported, and an expiry left without the pillars its smile model
needs is refused before the calibration begins, with the pillars and series it
could not read named.

* The calibration: from quotes to a surface

The engine calibrates the smile model to all the quotes at once. A curve's solve
has an order, because one pillar's factor feeds the next, and that order is what
makes its method a bootstrap; a surface's quotes have no such order, so its method
is a calibration: the model's parameters minimise the weighted squared difference
between the quotes and the volatilities the model gives at the pillars' strikes.
For SSVI the parameters are the ATM total variance of each expiry, the skew and
the curvature; for SABR they are fitted expiry by expiry. A butterfly quoted as a
broker strangle enters the calibration as the price of the strangle at its own
strikes rather than as a smile volatility, as
[[id:5B513D53-5DF7-4540-99C0-60D909678873][FX Volatility Surface]] explains.

Between the expiries the blueprint's interpolation applies, in total variance so
that the interpolated surface stays free of calendar arbitrage where the
calibrated expiries are. The calibrated surface is then sampled on the
blueprint's output grid, which is what the candidate holds. The engine sits
beside the curve engine in =projects/ores.analytics/quant=.

* The checks

Two kinds of check run, where a curve has one.

The first kind runs before the quotes are read, because each is a property of
the recipe and none needs the market.

1. Every expiry has the pillars its smile model needs.
2. The expiries are in strictly increasing order and the output grid lies
   within them, or the blueprint states its extrapolation.
3. Every strike label is one the pair's convention can resolve.
4. The blueprint names a quoting convention and the convention names a butterfly
   style.

The second kind runs on the calibrated surface, because a calibration, unlike a
bootstrap, can reproduce its quotes and still admit arbitrage.

5. Total variance does not fall with expiry at any strike of the grid, so the
   surface has no calendar spread arbitrage.
6. The density implied at each expiry is non-negative, so the surface has no
   butterfly arbitrage. For SSVI the four conditions of
   [[id:5910304A-FA45-461E-B2A2-8F24FA798769][SVI Volatility Parameterisation]]
   are checked on the parameters; for other models the density is checked on
   the grid
   ([[id:3F7A2C91-84BE-4E12-B5D0-1D6E5F08A432][Vol Surface No-Arbitrage Conditions]]).
7. The calibration error of each pillar is recorded, and a pillar beyond the
   blueprint's tolerance is reported.

The first kind is a property of the blueprint and is held against its version,
as the curve's checks are in the target state. The second kind is a property of
one calibration and is held against the candidate.

* The candidate

The calibration produces a surface. The compute writes it as observations of the
blueprint's output series, dated at the as-of, with each grid point's expiry and
strike in the datum identity. The output series is a calibrated surface series,
so the identity says the points are inferred rather than observed; the blueprint
is the only writer of that series. The series is described in
[[id:C3E053CA-0D4B-480B-9119-E11530160EC1][oresmd: ORE Studio Market Data URI]].

Every point carries a lineage record naming the source series the pillars read,
the contributors' weights, and the version of the blueprint that produced it, so
the inference is recorded, not merely performed. The result is the candidate: it
exists, it is traceable, and it is not authoritative. A candidate is not
consumable by a report; the published state is what makes a set authoritative,
and the next section is that act.

* The hand-off to publication

The candidate becomes authoritative in the three acts that make a curve
authoritative: the maker raises a publish naming the blueprint and the as-of, a
second person decides under the four-eyes rule, and the publish applies the
approved request once, stamps the publisher and the instant, ends the set it
replaces and sends the notice.

The published set is what the rest of the system consumes. The report references
it through its market binding, as
[[id:FB8F701C-E56A-49A9-AA90-1B6BD0B02092][A report binds to a published curve]]
describes for a curve; the screens read it; a candidate never reaches either. The
ORE export writes the published grid as ORE volatility quotes at the boundary,
with the =fx_volatility_config= that describes the grid, and ORE interpolates
between the grid points by its own rules. The grid therefore has to be fine
enough that ORE's surface reproduces the calibrated one at the strikes a trade
needs. The trader's side is
[[id:89537A61-5C48-4A8D-AE3A-8DD4069433DF][Calibrate or refresh a surface]],
[[id:F2E3F72C-60B9-4D23-A51F-B426B481470D][Inspect a surface's slices]],
[[id:478EFA03-AB47-4286-A3CE-583C7438632D][Adjust a curve or surface by hand]] and
[[id:28D1545F-1BE8-4C14-8B69-292DC12BC45F][Watch a curve or surface evolve]],
with the publish journeys the curve shares.

* The changes that reach the target

Six changes remain, and each is a change to a named part of the system.

The first is the surface blueprint. No record states a surface's recipe today;
the refdata entities =fx_volatility_config= and =fx_volatility_smile= describe a
surface to ORE, and the blueprint is the record that adds the pillars, their
series, the contributors' weights, the smile model and the output series.

The second is the calibration operation. A request that names the blueprint and
the as-of and a response that carries the calibrated points and the error per
pillar, the =marketdata.v1.surface.calibrate= subject the calibrate journey
names, with the engine behind it.

The third is the validation operation. Both kinds of check run in the service,
the first held against the blueprint's version and the second against the
candidate, so a caller that is not a screen is held to the same rule.

The fourth is the calibrated surface series. oresmd needs the instrument type
whose identity names a calibrated FX volatility surface, created as part of the
blueprint's lifecycle as a curve's output series is.

The fifth is the surface and slice reads, which the slices journey needs and
which read the published set rather than the quotes.

The sixth is the synthetic surface feed, which publishes into the series the
pillars name, through the contributors of
[[id:D4C7C4A7-CD2D-4307-AB06-DA9BE3C3A334][A synthetic contributor publishes noisy observations of a latent surface]].

Surfaces of other asset classes are not changes to this construction: an equity
or commodity surface quotes by strike or moneyness and an IR cube adds the swap
tenor, and each is a blueprint and a template of its own over the same steps.

* Conclusion

In the target state a surface is constructed, not observed, and the construction
is recorded rather than merely performed. The blueprint states the recipe, the
pillars name the series they read, the calibration infers the surface under the
conventions, the checks hold the recipe to its own rules and the surface to the
absence of arbitrage, the candidate names the source series and the version that
produced it, and the publication makes the set authoritative for the report. The
identity system distinguishes the quote from the point; the lineage records
connect them; and the published set is the single object the rest of the system
consumes. Every step is the curve's, and the two differences, calibration in the
place of a bootstrap and a check of the result as well as of the recipe, are the
two ways a surface differs from a curve.
