* Goal

The system's feeds play the vendor, as Bloomberg would. A party receives raw quotes through its subscriptions, into its own series, and each delivery is metered against it. Everything a party derives from them is its own. Before this task two Acme parties could not build funding curves: New York received no IR quotes and no USD spots, and London lacked four of the 20 funding currencies.

* Acceptance

- Every operational Acme party receives live IR quotes in all 20 funding currencies, and the USD spot of each of the other 19, into its own series.
- A party keeps its own vendor. A party whose vendor does not quote the funding set also takes Bloomberg.
- A party that the subscribe step names twice gets one subscription and one route for each provider. A second run writes nothing new.
- Every tick carries the party that receives it, and deliveries are metered per party.

* Plan

The delivery design already exists, from sprint 29. A route ties a party and a provider to one source, and the demand for that route is every item that the provider's catalogue maps. Ticks land in =ores_marketdata_ticks_tbl= under the party. The system's curves read the system party's Bloomberg series in the same way.

The gap is coverage. Each synthetic collection quotes only its own markets. Only Bloomberg quotes the IR ladders and USD spots of the whole funding set. The demo collection quotes a short ladder in 20 currencies, and four of them are outside the funding set. LSEG quotes 14 crosses and no IR.

So London and New York take Bloomberg as a second provider. The subscribe step already writes one subscription and one route per party and provider. It now lets a profile name a party more than once. The other way, widening the demo and LSEG collections, needs new generators and vendor ids, for no gain to the surfaces.

Dropped from the first draft: one shared copy that parties read directly. The owner chose delivery into each party's own series. The lineage of a party's curve belongs to the funding curves task.
