#+title: State each family part's cardinality and refuse a family write that breaks it
#+description: A family model's Parts table names its parts but not how many rows each takes, so the generated family put accepts a commodity future with no contract terms or a cross currency swap with no leg, which the ORE export then refuses.

This page is a [[id:671F18E4-E09C-4B3B-BD24-D33DF8AE38A6][capture]] in the *inbox* bucket of the product backlog — a pre-sprint idea, not yet pulled into a sprint as a story.

* What

A family model's Parts table gains a cardinality column: =one= for a part
every convention fills, =optional= for a part it fills at most once, and a
group for parts that are alternatives, as the tenor basis swap's are. The
generated family put refuses a write whose parts break it, with a validation
verdict that names the part.

* Why

The unique index on =(tenant_id, convention_id)= stops two rows of one part,
but nothing stops a missing required part. The family put accepts a commodity
future with no contract terms, or a cross currency swap with no leg. The ORE
export then refuses that convention, and the failure surfaces far from the
write that caused it. Each composite kind states its rule only in its ORE
mapper today: =reverse_commodity_future=, =reverse_cross_currency_basis= and
=reverse_cross_currency_fix_float= throw when a part is missing.
