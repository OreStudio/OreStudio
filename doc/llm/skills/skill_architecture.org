* Summary

This document is the hub of the /compass skill framework/. The objective of the
framework is to define an ontology of core domain concepts and associated
operational processes. These aim to establish the computational primitives
required for both human and [[id:0D919421-2B73-4322-9F79-9FDA38A67EA1][LLMs]] to operate agent harnesses in an autonomous or
semi-autonomous manner. The framework is build on top of academic foundations
such as [[id:74C5D8A2-F781-4E23-A6FC-2921AD5879AD][Cybernetics]] and [[id:BA0E3273-DC4B-7CD4-447B-06227B7D09A8][MASD]], but also rests on empirical foundations such as
[[id:4ACD003D-5CEB-4D2E-BE10-BA77D1043FB8][skills]] used by other developers. We have tried to attribute provenance
adequately, but if any citations are missing please report it.

The objective of the hub page is to provide a summary introduction to the key
actors, and link to the spoke pages where these are treated in more detail. The
document starts by defining the key concepts of the framework, moves on to it's
entry points and concludes with an index of all the pages relevant to the
framework.

* Detail

** Key Concepts

"A *skill* in agent tooling is a document that an assistant loads to perform a
/kind/ of work" (/c.f./ [[id:4ACD003D-5CEB-4D2E-BE10-BA77D1043FB8][Agent skills]]). A /skill/ as understood by the compass
[[id:87D41A08-0B37-41CE-8483-B59B36BA87FA][skill]] framework is a refinement of this general concept: in simple terms, it is
one /action/ applied to one /typed target/. A skill is defined in compass via a
specific document type, one of many of the [[id:8B29A8D8-E004-4E87-B81D-ABEAF68F2B98][documentation ontology]]. As we shall
see, this very same ontology then facilitates the definition of the type
targets. The compass skill framework builds on top ideas from [[https://flaviocopes.com/pstack/][pstack]] and other
open source tooling such as [[https://karthinks.com/software/fifteen-ways-to-use-embark/][Emacs' embark]], which were analysed as prior art and
in some cases copied /verbatim/.

This analysis led us to try to create a small but consistent action model,
composed of three core concepts:

- *[[id:F5CB5A45-6557-410B-9E4F-542957561A16][Planes]]*. Artefacts are classified into planes by the relation an agent bears
  to them: the agent /loads/ grounding, /cites/ a stance, /sequences/ a method,
  /executes/ an action, /declares/ a specification, and /convenes/ a
  deliberation. The [[id:F5CB5A45-6557-410B-9E4F-542957561A16][planes]] document explains in detail its taxonomy and
  rationale.
- *[[id:3018B7FE-2DF7-49E5-A57F-A75C02C4D498][Dispatching]]*. Operations are registered against a target's /type/ rather than
  against the command that produced the target. This allows applicable verbs to
  be computed rather than remembered, and removes the combinatorial growth a
  hand-written catalogue tends to suffer.
- *[[id:5365DE9F-E3F9-4B5F-ACC1-B347B5FCE2A9][Functions]]*. Actors declare a function. The function's [[id:926B916E-A57E-498E-99AB-720B583C0362][Cybernetic Level]]
  determines the relevant subset of the skills catalogue. FIXME: what does this
  mean: "binding at the audit level and advisory elsewhere."

To attain its automation goal, the framework uses these core concepts to convert
a "unit of work" --- often a [[id:31C868B9-306E-4282-BA8C-07E647021B34][Task]], but not always. Units of work start from an
unverified beginning and are lead through well-defined flows, themselves
determined dynamically, to a verified end state via a [[id:E8311826-9900-4932-AFFB-A8AF663414DF][method]]. The method's
phases cite the principles that govern their judgement and call the actions that
act on relevant artefacts. In this process, [[id:D8CB6EED-1F6A-4EE5-840B-2FE28DE17911][code generation]] is preferred where
possible. How far that work must climb the [[id:DEE49C55-1626-470D-BB0F-D58A11D35769][verification]] ladder depends on what
it changed. An agent can be configured and [[id:54AD5986-34A0-4B19-9F20-D9A024225036][left running]] against a checkable
finish condition.

** Entry Points

The composition model was designed to be independent of specific use cases.
There are three use cases we have in mind, of equal importance. The most direct
is to *invoke an action*, when the operation and its target are both already
known. The second is to *dispatch on a target* with =compass-act=, when the
target is known and the operation is not. The third is to *enter the mode*,
=compass-helm=, which declares a function, matches the work to a method, and
routes to the other two as the method's phases fire.

The =compass-helm= mode is a convenience over methods rather than a precondition
for them. A method is invocable on its own; so is an action. This layered
approach allows us to test each part individually.
