This page is a [[id:671F18E4-E09C-4B3B-BD24-D33DF8AE38A6][capture]] in the *inbox* bucket of the product backlog — a pre-sprint idea, not yet pulled into a sprint as a story.

* What

ORE Studio has no concept of a product as a trading desk uses the word. It models
instruments at the granularity of ORE's =<TradeType>=, which is a pricing-level
abstraction, and that granularity does not match the desk's.

A desk treats a Bermudan swaption, a cross-currency swap, a tenor basis swap and
a vanilla fixed-float swap as four distinct products. It quotes them separately,
books them to different desks, and reports them on separate lines. ORE represents
all four as =Swap= or =Swaption= carrying different legs and exercise data,
because from a pricing perspective they are the same builder with different
inputs.

Both views are correct for their purpose. ORE Studio currently holds only the
pricing view, so the desk view cannot be expressed at all.

The capture is for a *product type*: a desk-facing classification that maps
many-to-one onto ORE trade types, and that carries the name the desk actually
uses.

* Why

The immediate need is trade population generation. The distribution in
[[id:A1310399-0ACD-4FA1-9645-AB24B3C8BEB8][Trade Population Weights]] assigns 19% of a population to =Swap=, which is not a
usable instruction. A generator given that number cannot know what proportion
should be OIS, cross-currency or tenor basis, and those choices decide which
curves, discounting paths and leg structures the population exercises. The
weights need desk-facing granularity to be actionable.

The need is not confined to generation. Any surface where a person chooses,
filters or reports on what was traded wants the desk's vocabulary rather than
ORE's. A blotter listing nineteen thousand rows all labelled "Swap" is not
useful, and neither is a risk report aggregated at that level.

There is also a modelling argument. The product a desk trades and the builder
that prices it are different concepts that happen to coincide for simple
instruments. Holding one and calling it the other is why the distinction is not
expressible today.

* Shape of the work

Not designed here. The pieces that appear necessary:

- A reference table of product types, desk-facing, with the names desks use.
- A mapping from product type to ORE trade type, many-to-one, plus whatever
  discriminates one product type from another within a trade type. Exercise
  style separates a Bermudan swaption from a European one; leg currencies
  separate a cross-currency swap from a single-currency one; leg index tenors
  separate a basis swap.
- A rule for deriving the product type of an existing trade, so the
  classification applies to trades already booked rather than only to new ones.
- The trade population weights restated at product-type granularity.

The discriminators are the hard part. Some are a single field and some are a
predicate over the legs, so the mapping is not a lookup table alone.

* What done looks like

The test is the distribution. Every weight must name a product a desk would
recognise, and no weight may sit on a trade type that covers several of them.

Concretely, the 19% currently held against =Swap= becomes a set of weights that
sum to 19%, one per desk-facing product. Illustrative only, since arriving at the
split is the work rather than its premise:

| Desk-facing product     | Weight | ORE trade type | Discriminator                                           |
|-------------------------+--------+----------------+---------------------------------------------------------|
| Vanilla fixed-float IRS |  7.00% | =Swap=           | One currency, fixed against Ibor                        |
| OIS                     |  5.00% | =Swap=           | Floating index is an overnight rate                     |
| Cross-currency swap     |  3.50% | =Swap=           | Legs in two currencies                                  |
| Tenor basis swap        |  2.50% | =Swap=           | Two floating legs, one currency, different index tenors |
| Cross-currency basis    |  1.00% | =Swap=           | Two floating legs, two currencies                       |

The same treatment applies to =Swaption=, where European and Bermudan exercise
are separate products selecting different pricing models, and to =CapFloor= and
=Bond=.

Two properties the result must hold. The weights are stated per desk-facing
product, so a generator never has to invent a split. And they roll up to the
trade type totals, so the population's shape at ORE's granularity is unchanged by
the finer statement.

* Status

Deferred. It was found while writing the trade population weights, and it is not
that story's subject. Recorded so the distribution's coarseness at =Swap= and
=Swaption= is a known limitation rather than an oversight.
