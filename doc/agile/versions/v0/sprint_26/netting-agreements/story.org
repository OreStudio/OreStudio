* Acceptance

- netting_agreement, netting_set and csa exist in ores.refdata, generated.
- A netting set's counterparty and legal entity are those of its agreement, and a CSA references exactly one set with at most one active at a time.
- A netting set carries the ORE details (call type, initial margin type) and round-trips through nettingsetdefinitions.xml.

* Decisions

- A netting set's counterparty and legal entity are optional, because ORE
  names a set by id alone; a check makes both required once the set has an
  agreement, and a pinned key holds them to the agreement's.
- The agreement's parties are fixed columns, so a set's copy of them stays
  true when the agreement is amended.
- The active flag is the CSA's, not the set's: ORE's examples keep the
  details of CSAs that are switched off.

* Result

Netting agreements, netting sets and credit support annexes are generated
entities in =ores.refdata=, and ORE's netting set definitions read and
write through them.

- =netting_agreement= and =netting_set=, with the set's parties pinned to
  its agreement and the agreement's parties fixed (PR 2537). Codegen gained
  fixed columns for this.
- =csa= and =csa_eligible_currency=, with at most one active CSA per set
  and each currency position used once (PR 2538).
- =netting_set_mapper=, round-tripping all 99 netting set documents in
  ORE's examples.
- The credit limit the netting sets analysis names is filed as a capture,
  because no credit limit entity exists yet.
