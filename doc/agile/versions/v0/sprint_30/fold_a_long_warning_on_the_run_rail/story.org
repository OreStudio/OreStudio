* Goal

A step that finishes with a warning shows the reason under its name on the
run rail, and a warning that names hundreds of items keeps that sentence short
enough to read: the first sentence stands, and the rest waits behind a
disclosure beside it.

* Acceptance

- A warning of one short sentence is read where it stands, as it is now.
- A warning long enough to bury the rail keeps its first sentence and puts the
  whole text behind a disclosure labelled with that sentence.
- The folded text is scrollable and wraps, so a list of hundreds of items
  neither widens the page nor runs past its end.
- The step's own log still holds every message, unchanged, for the person who
  opens the details.

* Decisions

- *The rail folds, the setup report keeps its list.* The setup report already
  shows one warning per entry under a counted disclosure, and a person reading
  it there wants every line. The rail is a summary of the run, so it is the one
  that folds.
- *The first sentence, not a character count.* A step that warns of one thing
  says it in its first sentence; a step that warns of many leads with the count
  and then names them.

* Out of scope

- Rewriting the step's message on the server so it says less.

* Result

A setup warning that names hundreds of items no longer buries the run rail: its first sentence
stands in the summary and the whole text waits behind a disclosure that scrolls, while a warning of
one short sentence is read where it stands.
