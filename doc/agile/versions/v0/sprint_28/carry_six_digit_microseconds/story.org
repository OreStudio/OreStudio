* Goal

Every timestamp the platform writes carries six digits of microseconds, and the parser reads them back, so a time_point survives a round trip through the database or the wire to the microsecond.

* Decisions

- *The writer and the reader change together.* Reason: a writer that states a
  fraction the reader ignores looks fixed until the value is read back.
- *Six digits, always.* Reason: PostgreSQL states none at zero microseconds,
  so the reader accepts an absent fraction, but a writer that varies its width
  makes the wire form unstable.
- *The market ingest assertion is tightened back to the millisecond.* Reason:
  main had answered the same defect by weakening it to a whole second and
  recording the truncation as intended; the fix makes the millisecond true.
- *The platform and wire layers change; the entity layer does not.* Reason:
  =db_timestamp= is =rfl::Timestamp=, which wraps a =std::tm= with no
  sub-second field, so no format string can give it a fraction.

* Out of scope

- Replacing =db_timestamp= so the entity layer's audit columns and its mappers
  also carry microseconds. That is a change to every entity and mapper, not a
  format string, and it needs its own story.
