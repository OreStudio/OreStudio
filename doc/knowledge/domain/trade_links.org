* Summary

Trades rarely stand alone. A close-out answers a live trade, a
back-to-back routes a client trade's risk, a fixing belongs to the
forward it fixes. A *trade link* is the typed, directed relation that
records this. The type names the reason, and each end plays a role:
original and closing trade, parent and generated trade, hedged and
hedging trade.

The link type decides behaviour through one property: its economic
effect. A link with an economic effect is a trade event; it changes the
trade and may need a confirmation. A link with no economic effect is an
annotation; the system records and reports it, and it changes no term.

This page fixes the vocabulary, describes how links come into
being, and states the invariants they must satisfy. The catalogue
of types is on [[id:04F5EFF1-D4B5-4BD0-8CBC-4EF5DDF97921][Trade link types]];
the prior art behind the term is at the end of this page.

* Detail

** What a link is

A link joins two trades and says why. It is directed: the close-out
link runs from the original trade to the closing trade, and the
misbooking link runs from the correction trade back to the erroneous
original. Each end plays a role, and the role is part of the record,
not something a reader infers.

A link is typed. The type is the reason: close-out, novation, roll,
fixing, allocation. The reason cannot be recovered from the two trades
alone — a cancelled trade and a live one are the ends of a close-out, a
misbooking and a compression alike — so the system stores the link as
data.

Most links join two trades. Not all do: a trade's cash flows
(see [[id:AAF5F9CB-D854-40A8-AEB5-50D998A9F0C0][Linked cash]]), and the
trades a single process run generates, which the run's own page owns.
Pre-trade objects — orders, quotes, RFQ records — sit outside the trade
model, and their links are out of scope here.

** The property that decides behaviour

A link with an economic effect is a *trade event*. The two linked
trades are the before and after of an economic change: the trade
changes, a version is cut, and the customer may have to agree again.
Close-out, novation, roll, merge and compression are trade events.

A link with no economic effect is an *annotation*. The trades stand on
their own; the link records how they came about, why they sit together,
or who asked for them to be joined. Fixings, funding rolls, sweeps,
allocations, hedge marks and user links are annotations.

Annotations are not second-class. They carry the audit trail. A
risk-routing chain can be traced back to the client trade, and a fixing
back to its forward, only because the process records the link even
where it could price the new trade without it.

[[id:FC31FC9C-7A6E-4EFD-8A62-6DE4137F7160][Trade Structures and Deal
Composition]] states the rule as one of its four ground rules for
development on structures. It governs every decision about link
behaviour: whether to version, whether to confirm, whether to notify
downstream.

** How links come into being

Several routes carry every link.

- /At booking./ Assembling a structure links the legs to it. A
  back-to-back pair is generated with the client-facing trade, so the
  two sides of the risk route exist together (see
  [[id:9E364BEF-12EE-42F8-902C-B1E68A3095B8][Wash books and risk
  routing]]). A book move pairs a back-out trade with the trade in the
  destination book in a single transaction; where ownership differs,
  the move goes through a novation-like close-out and rebook, linked
  both times (see [[id:07CEDE6A-21B7-4F87-9745-708FDCA8C362][Book
  lifecycle]]). A correction trade references the erroneous original
  through =successor_trade_id= (see
  [[id:8BC3A226-6DD8-49C8-959A-F1AAC97A3F97][Trade Versioning]]).

- /From a process./ Funding rolls, sweeps, fixings, expiry runs and
  cleardown generate trades, and each generated trade is linked to its
  origin as the run proceeds. The run then reads as a unit, and it
  audits as one: the requirement is to trace a trade back through the
  split, route or move to the trade that caused it (the audit section
  of [[id:9E364BEF-12EE-42F8-902C-B1E68A3095B8][Wash books and risk
  routing]] states the principle for the risk-routing case).

- /By hand./ The blotter's Link / Unlink actions join trades a user
  chooses: an ECN block to its allocations, a hedge forward to the
  option it hedges, legs of a structure booked separately. The linked
  forms carry the work — Link Amend carries a change from the primary
  trade to its counterpart, Link Net nets eligible trades, and Link
  Split breaks a link. See
  [[id:3976695F-F526-4C2A-80ED-89B54854CF6D][Trade Blotter]].

** Invariants

1. /Traceability over convenience./ The system records a link even
   where a process could produce the correct numbers without it. A
   back-to-back could be valued knowing only its own terms; the link
   exists because audit must trace it back to the client trade.

2. /Back to origin./ Every generated trade keeps a path to the trade
   that caused it. The path survives the operation that created it — a
   split, a route, a move, a correction.

3. /Created together./ Trades a single act generates — a back-to-back
   pair, a book move's two sides — are written in one transaction, or
   not at all. No reader sees half of the act.

4. /Propagation./ Where two sides of a link must stay aligned, a
   change to the primary trade carries to the counterpart. Link Amend
   is the blotter's form of this rule.

5. /Explicit roles./ A link is directed, and its ends are named. The
   record says which trade is the original and which is the outcome,
   as =successor_trade_id= does for a correction trade.

** Naming and prior art: why "trade links"

The concept is industry-wide and the names differ. The table records
what each source we follow calls it.

| Concept | Name in the source we follow |
|---------+------------------------------|
| Generic typed link between trades | FpML =linkedTrade= — a =linkId= and a =linkType=, where the type carries the reason |
| Lifecycle traceability | ISDA CDM /lineage/ — each business event ties an =after= trade state to the =before= state it replaces |
| Identifier continuity across a lifecycle event | =prior UTI= — the report of a replacement trade names the identifier it supersedes |
| Allocation between a block and its children | FpML =blockTradeId= and =allocationTradeId=, now folded into =linkedTrade= |
| A group of trades under one agreement | the netting set of the ISDA documentation |
| Clearing relationships | /give-up/ and /take-up/; the beta and gamma trades of a clearing chain |
| Order relationships | FIX /parent/ and /child/ orders |
| The user's action in a blotter | /link/ and /unlink/ |

We chose *link* over *linkage* for several reasons. FpML standardises
=linkedTrade=, so the word has a standards precedent. The blotter verbs
are already Link and Unlink, so the noun matches our own vocabulary.
And in market usage "linkage" more often names settlement netting than
the relation between two trades, so reusing it would blur both senses.
"Deal" is rejected for the same reason "trade" is preferred throughout:
one word per concept.

The material analysed, in full:

- the FpML 5.12 schema — the =LinkedTrade= complex type, and
  =PartyTradeIdentifier= with =linkId=, =linkType=, =blockTradeId=,
  =allocationTradeId=, =originatingTradeId= and =resultingTradeId=;
- the FpML 5.10 messaging framework, which carries =tradePackage= as a
  payload type;
- the ISDA Common Domain Model event model, where lineage ties each
  trade state to the one before it;
- the CPMI-IOSCO UTI guidance, which fixes when an identifier continues
  and when a replacement trade needs a new one;
- published vendor material on the same subject. Vendor portals are not
  public, so the vendor picture rests on published course catalogues
  and product pages — Calypso's training catalogue names the operations
  (allocation, rollover, termination, netting) and Murex markets
  life-cycle management at both trade and bulk level — not on internal
  documentation. The analogous vocabulary for composition concepts is
  on [[id:FC31FC9C-7A6E-4EFD-8A62-6DE4137F7160][Trade Structures and
  Deal Composition]].
