* Goal

Carry reference data from a blank database to a usable one, deliberately. Today
a recreate seeds DQ artefacts, nothing publishes them, some tables are seeded
directly instead, and there is no screen anywhere that shows what is published.
This story settles the model, implements it, and gives it a place to be seen and
driven.

* Acceptance

- The publishing model is documented: what a blank database holds, what a
  recreate publishes, what a tenant is provisioned with, and in what order.
- Every dataset is classified as base-system or ACME, with the reason stated.
- No dataset is seeded and left unpublished, and no refdata table is populated
  by a second, silent path without that being deliberate.
- A recreated database can value every trade type the system books.
- ores.web shows each dataset, its artefacts, whether it is published, and
  offers the publish action.


* Approach

The investigation found one question behind most of the defects: *what turns a
blank database into a usable one?* Four documents answered it four ways. The
mop-up therefore starts by settling the levels, because every other task depends
on being able to say which bucket a dataset lives in.

| Level | Holds | Published | By |
|-------+-------+-----------+----|
| Installation | schema, system vocabulary, the system party, WRLD | at migration | populate |
| World | calendars, currencies, commodity and equity underlyings, conventions | once, into the system tenant | one world bundle |
| Tenant | the tenant's own rows, and its copies of what a party may override | per tenant, declared | the tenant bootstrap |

The sequence is deliberate: 1 settles the vocabulary, 2 makes bring-up complete,
3 removes the silent copy that made the gap invisible, 4 collapses the doubling,
5 makes the remaining promises true, 6 documents what settled. Each closes at
least one defect in the investigation, and none depends on the screen work.

The earlier task *Publish every seeded dataset at the level it belongs* is
superseded by these six: its scope is now split across settling the levels,
publishing at bring-up and collapsing the double writers. It stays in the
backlog as history until it is closed as abandoned.

The screens task stays last. "Is my reference data complete?" is a question about
levels, so it can only be designed once 1 and 2 have an answer.

* Decisions

- The analysis precedes the implementation. The two are separate tasks because
  the shape of the fix depends on which datasets are base-system and which are
  per-tenant, and that is not yet settled.
- The DQ UI belongs in this story rather than beside it. A publishing model with
  no way to see publish state is the reason the gap went unnoticed: a recreate
  looked successful while the catalogues were empty.

* Out of scope

- Re-seeding the corpus. This story moves what exists to where it belongs; it
  does not add new market data.
