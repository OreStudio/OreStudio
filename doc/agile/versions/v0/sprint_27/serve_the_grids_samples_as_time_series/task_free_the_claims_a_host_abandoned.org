* Acceptance

- After a soak no result stays in progress and the ledger holds no real entry.
- The compute service log shows no database pool warning during the soak.

* Plan

Two soaks of the Acme tenant left about a fifth of the jobs, 64 and then 59 results, in progress for good, held by hosts that were alive.

- *Cause one.* The service handlers share one database pool, and its default size is 2. During a burst the claims waited for a connection past the 30 second timeout of the node. The log showed =No available database connections in the pool= 13 times in 12 minutes. A claim that timed out for the node had often been served: the service had taken the entry and marked the result in progress, and the reply came too late.
- *Cause two.* A heartbeat extended the lease of every entry of its host. The node never reported the lost claim, so the entry stayed leased for as long as the host lived, and the reaper never saw it.

The fix has three parts.
- The heartbeat names the job the node runs (=result_id=). The service extends the lease of that job only.
- A host that asks for work is free, because it runs one job at a time. The claim first returns to the queue every entry the host still holds, with the attempts kept, so a lost reply costs the time until the host asks again. An entry with no attempts left stays for the reaper.
- The handlers' pool is 8. The loops of the service keep their own pools.

* Result

A lost claim reply no longer strands a job. The 59 results that the last soak left in progress finished as soon as their hosts asked for work again, and a 10 minute soak of 21 nodes finished every one of its 741 results. The service log shows no pool warning. The soak showed the party split that was asked for.
