* Plan

1. *Read the binding first.* The agreement's =ProtectionFee= is a list of legs,
   and its =Underlying= holds legs, an optional option block, a naked-option
   flag and an optional treasury lock whose data is a bond. The design had a
   money column for the fee and no underlying, so the plan follows the binding.
2. *Facts.* The agreement's table gains the participation rate (a continuous
   fraction, so a binary float), the settles-accrual flag and the naked-option
   flag.
3. *Legs by group.* The fee legs are the =protection_fee= group of =swap_legs=
   and the underlying's legs are the =underlying= group. The shared leg mapper
   learns cashflow legs: a dated payment is a dated amount row, and the leg type
   tells it from a notional.
4. *Schedules.* An underlying leg keeps its own schedule as shared schedule rows
   under the swap owner, because the header's dates are the protection period
   and not the leg's.
5. *The option.* The underlying's option block goes to the shared option rows.
   The agreement has no option of its own, so the question of telling an
   underlying's option from a trade's own does not arise: a trade holds one
   option block, and for an agreement it is the underlying's.
6. *Known limit.* The treasury lock holds a bond, which the bond family stores.
   It is not stored, the import logs a warning, and the corpus has none.
7. *Prove it.* Tests read the fields, the digits of the fee, the leg schedules
   and the option block back, and the directory round trip shows both agreement
   documents re-read.

naked-option flag. Its protection fee is the =protection_fee= legs and its
underlying's legs are the =underlying= legs, each with its own schedule. The
underlying's option block is the shared option rows, which the agreement owns
because it has no option of its own. Both corpus documents export as valid ORE
and re-read.
