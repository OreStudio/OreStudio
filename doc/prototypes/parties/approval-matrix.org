* Summary

The journey
[[id:AEA0BD34-1114-41C2-874D-84501B2C40CE][Approve what a party and its books may trade]] draws two sets on two
record pages that already exist. The judgement of the journey found four
placements the [[id:2BF7F03F-6BF4-45FF-8E26-4D3B4E2D943D][Record screen standard]] does not settle, and this
prototype draws every one of them so the owner can accept or refuse it before
any code is written.

* Detail

** What it argues

The approval belongs on the record it constrains. A person reads and changes the
party's set on the party, and the book's set on the book, and never leaves the
record to do either. The alternative the prototype also names is a screen of its
own, reachable from both: it is the placement the first question asks about.

The two sets are drawn as links and not parts: a product type exists on its own,
the party carries a set of them, and a book carries a subset. A link's panel
carries no history of its own (RS-RELATED-03), which is why the tab and the panel
have no /History/ row of their own and the record's own history is the history of
the change.

** The screens the prototype has to sit on

The two records already have screens, and they are not alike.

- The party is a full record page: =PartyPage= in
  =projects/ores.web/packages/web/src/refdata/parties.tsx=, with a record
  header, tabs held in the address, and a dialog for each card. A
  /Market approvals/ tab joins =details=, =identifiers=, =contacts=,
  =memberships=, =centres=, =counterparties= and =history=, and it is drawn only
  for a reader who may read the sets, as the other tabs are.
- The book is a frame and a grid of facts: =BookPage.tsx= draws its name, its
  portfolio, its status, its purpose, its currency and its codes, and nothing
  else. It has no tab bar and no write path, because a book is published from
  the data quality dataset rather than edited in the web.

Both sets are edited, so the two placements differ. The party's set is a tab of
a page that already has tabs. The book's set is a panel under its facts, because
the page has no tab bar to put a tab in and one panel does not justify one: the
set is the first part of the book the web lets a person change.

The book's set is a subset of the party's, and the database holds that rule in
three triggers. The panel therefore lists only what the party permits, so a type
outside the approval cannot be picked, and a stale page that names one is
refused under the panel in the database's words.

** The placements the standard does not settle

| Placement | What the prototype draws | What is asked |
|-----------+--------------------------+---------------|
| P1 Where the set lives | The party's tab, and a panel under the book's facts, with a link between them | Accepted: the two records keep the set. A screen of its own was the alternative and is refused |
| P2 How the two sets read together | The book's table carries a /Party permits/ column and a /This book carries/ column, so a permitted type the book does not carry is a dash and never a mark | Whether this is the shape, or the party's set is the panel's background |
| P3 What a party that states nothing looks like | /Permits every product type the catalogue states/, in words, above an empty table | The words the standard takes, if the owner accepts them |
| P4 Where a refusal stands | Under the panel, in the operation's words, naming the type and the party's set | Whether the refusal is inline or a dialog |

** The states

Each state is an address, and the rail at the top of the page opens it:

| State | What it shows |
|-------+---------------|
| =?state=party-unrestricted= | A party that states no set: the words, and nothing listed |
| =?state=party-empty= | A party that permits a list and has none yet: an empty set that permits nothing |
| =?state=party-list= | A party that permits three types, with /Change/ |
| =?state=party-refused= | The save refused because a book carries a type the save would remove |
| =?state=book-inherits= | A book that follows its party: the party's set, whole |
| =?state=book-narrows= | A book that carries one of the party's three |
| =?state=book-refused-stale= | A save refused from a stale page, naming the party's set |

** The rules each part follows

| Part | Rule |
|------+------|
| The tab, held in the address | RS-PAGE-09 |
| The state line and the empty set | RS-PAGE-07 |
| The two-label select that states whether a set exists | RS-WRITE-01 |
| The set as a links panel, with /Add/ and /Remove/ | RS-RELATED-01 to RS-RELATED-03 |
| /Save/ at the panel's head, the refusal under the panel | RS-PAGE-03, RS-WRITE-05 |

The book's two-column comparison and the words for a party that states nothing
are not in the standard. They are this prototype's proposal, and the owner's
verdict decides whether they join it, as the calendars topic added RS-HIST-01.

** What is fake

- The product types are eight mock rows, not the trading catalogue, and the
  search the picker would carry is not drawn.
- The party and the books are mock records; the versions, the tabs and the
  breadcrumb stand in for the real page.
- No control writes. /Change/ moves between the states, and /Save/ adds a line
  that says so, because the prototype answers what the screen looks like and not
  what the server does.
- The refusal is written by the page. The server's own words are a gap the
  journey filed as [[id:DEC66AE2-4EB0-46B5-BA47-5D332B1D68B2][a capture]].
- The comparison of the two sets reads the party's set from the page's own mock
  data, because no operation answers the set a book may carry yet:
  [[id:8B1C5367-304E-4723-89B9-4FA6C16AAFDF][the other capture]].

** Where the code lives

[[file:approval-matrix/index.html][=index.html=]], [[file:approval-matrix/prototype.css][=prototype.css=]] and
[[file:approval-matrix/prototype.js][=prototype.js=]], with no build step and no session. The tokens are the
application's own, copied from =projects/ores.web/packages/web/src/styles.css=,
so a reviewer sees the screens in the colours the real ones use.

The seven states were rendered with Playwright and read back before the owner
saw them. The design note and the prototype live on the branch that proposes
them; an accepted prototype merges into =main= and stays as the design record.

** Verdict

Signed off by the owner on 2026-11-25, with the desk added: the chain is the
party, the desk and the book, each equal to or narrower than the one above, and
the three screens draw it in tandem. The two set comparison, the words for a
level that states nothing and the refusal under the panel are accepted, and the
screen pipeline's build step may start from this prototype.
