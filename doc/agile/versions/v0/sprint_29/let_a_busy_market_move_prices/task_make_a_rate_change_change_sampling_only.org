* Goal

A rate change changes how often a series is observed and not how much time its
process covers, for every process the feeds use. An operator who doubles a rate
gets twice the samples of the same path, not twice the realized volatility.

* Acceptance

- Variance per wall-clock second is invariant under a rate multiplier of two,
  measured on a long run, for every process a feed can build.
- The three processes that answer =supports_variable_step() == true= keep their
  behaviour: the Gaussian mixture, the arithmetic mixture and the
  Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process.
- The processes that answer false gain a time-scaled law, so a feed can hand them
  the gap. They are converted, not refused: refusing would break every feed that
  runs today.
- A non-periodic arrival mode is still refused for any process that cannot take a
  gap, as it is today.
- The existing time-rescaling test stays green, and the new test names the process
  it measures.

* Plan

1. Enumerate the processes and their =supports_variable_step()= answer; the list
   is the test's parameter.
2. Give each process that answers false a law in elapsed time, keeping its
   existing parameters and its one-tick behaviour exact.
3. Add the invariance test: the same seed, the same window, one run at the
   configured rate and one at twice it, and the two realize the same variance per
   second within sampling error.

* Result

A run now has one simulated clock, and it runs on the market's tau. A tick
steps its price by the simulated time since its feed last ticked, the market
speed times the market time that passed, so a feed's rate and its sensitivity
change how often it is sampled and not how far its world moves, and a spot feed
and a curve beside it share one calendar. A curve's step is the speed over its
rate, which at the default of 1440 and one tick a minute is a calendar day, as
before, and a curve whose step an Euler or tree scheme cannot carry is refused
by name. The speed is set for the run, logged at start, and shown in the
shell and on the Market regime tab. On Acme every feed priced at the default,
with no failed tick and no refused curve. The branch also mends three breaks
main carried: a stale use of the retired acting party filter, a test writer
missing three methods, and a test target missing the iam and inbox libraries.

* Finding, before any code

The task was raised against the claim that a rate change moves time. Measuring the
class first shows the claim is half wrong, and the half that is wrong is the half
this task would have spent effort on.

- *Spot feeds are already time-scaled.* The three processes that take a gap are
  the Gaussian mixture, the arithmetic mixture and the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process,
  and those are exactly what the three FX engines build: "geometric" is the
  mixture, "arithmetic" the variant, "ornstein_uhlenbeck" the mean-reverting one.
  A rate change on a spot feed already changes sampling only.
- *Every other price process is a curve engine*, and all of them answer
  =supports_variable_step() == false=. A curve feed steps one **simulated calendar
  day per tick** by design, so its rate is a calendar speed — one a minute means
  one simulated day a minute — and not a sampling rate at all.

So the two feed classes use one word for two quantities. That is a real problem,
but it is a naming and a decision problem, not a bug in the spot path, and a
volatility loading — which belongs to the price law — is unaffected by it.

Measured at =4b49b4ef2e=: 13 price processes, 3 time-scaled (the FX engines), 10
one-tick (the curve engines).

* Proposed re-scope

Replace this task with one that decides and states the curve convention, in the
model, the config and the screen: either a curve keeps a calendar-speed rate and
both surfaces say "one simulated day per tick", or curves move to sampling like
spot feeds, which changes what a curve's configuration means and needs its own
migration story for existing rows. Until that is decided, no code changes here,
and the story's second task (the volatility loading) proceeds on its own because
it applies to spot feeds.

* Decision

=v=, the simulated seconds per wall second, belongs to the **market factor**: one
number for the run, beside the state and the heat, visible on the dashboard and on
the Market regime tab. A feed's rate is pure sampling, and a feed derives
=Delta = v / r= for its process.

The default =v= is the one that keeps a curve at one tick a minute producing what
it produces today (=v = 1440=). Spot feeds deliberately move to the run's =v=,
which is the inconsistency the task exists to remove: today they run at real
speed while the curves beside them run fourteen hundred times faster.

* Acceptance, as decided

Rewritten on 2026-10-27 for the operational clock: simulated time runs on the
market's tau, which every feed shares, and not on the wall clock.

- The run carries =v= (=--market-speed=, logged at start), publishes it with the
  regime, and shows it on the Market regime tab.
- Every tick steps its price by the simulated time since the feed last ticked,
  =v * Delta tau=; a curve's step is =v / r=, so the =1 / 365= constant is gone
  from the feed.
- The default =v= reproduces today's curve step at one tick a minute, with a
  test that pins it.
- Each process states the largest step its scheme can take, and a feed refuses a
  rate whose step would exceed it, naming the bound.
- The same seed at two rates gives the same path statistics per unit of
  simulated time.
- The note states =r=, =Delta= and =v=, and records that a step is calendar time.

* Plan, as decided

Rebuilt on 2026-10-27 on the operational clock. The first pass put =v= on the
old market_regime, which the scheduler work deleted; its code was reset and its
design carried onto tau, with the step bounds kept as they were.

1. =market_config= carries =v= (=speed=), fixed for the run by the service's
   =--market-speed= and logged at start. The scheduler tells each tick the
   simulated time it covers, =v * Delta tau= since its series last ticked.
2. The FX feed steps by that simulated time. A curve's step is =v / r= in
   years (=curve_step_years=), so the =1 / 365= constant is gone from the feed,
   and a variable-step curve counts its nominal ticks from the simulated time.
3. Each approximate scheme states its step bound; a curve refuses a rate whose
   step would exceed it, naming the bound.
4. The regime read, the pin reply, the shell and the Market regime tab show =v=.
5. The tests: the curve step pinned at the default, a tick's simulated time at
   =v * Delta tau= whatever the pace, and the step bounds.
