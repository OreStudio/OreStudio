#+title: The conventions pick-lists route cannot reach the bus
#+description: /api/conventions/pick-lists answers 503 with a transport error in forty-five milliseconds, while the six lists it reads answer from the shell and /api/books/pick-lists answers from the same route shape.
#+type: capture
#+level: cross
#+filetags: :web:bff:refdata:conventions:bug:
#+created: 2026-10-25
#+updated: 2026-10-25

Found by sweeping every GET route the BFF declares, after the list requests were completed from the protocol. Every route answered or asked for missing parameters, except this one.

* What is seen

#+begin_example
$ curl -b cookies http://127.0.0.1:20802/api/conventions/pick-lists
{"code":"upstream-unavailable","message":"Cannot reach the message bus."}   [503]
real    0m0.045s
#+end_example

The answer is immediate, so this is not a timeout. =TransportError= is raised by the wire client rather than by a service refusal, and a refusal would have been a =502= with the code, as the missing-member refusals were.

* What is ruled out

- The lists answer: =calendar_names= returns 471 rows, and =business_day_convention_types=, =floating_index_types= and =payment_frequencies= each return rows, all through =compass shell=.
- The route shape works: =/api/books/pick-lists= reads its lists through the same =readAll= and answers 200 from the same session.
- A large reply is not the cause: =calendar_names list --limit 500= answers 471 rows from the shell.
- The wire shape is not the cause: the requests now come from the generated table, and none of these six subjects is missing from it.

* What is left

Either the web service's own NATS credentials may not publish one of the six subjects, or its connection rejects the publish for another reason; the client reports both as a transport error. The next step is the web service's journal at debug level for the failing subject, then a comparison of its publish permissions with the shell's.

It is not the sweep's class, which is why it is captured apart from it.
