#+title: Synthetic feed naming scheme
#+description: How a synthetic feed's source name is built from who produces it and the oresmd identity of what it observes.
#+type: knowledge
#+level: cross
#+filetags: :synthetic:naming:oresmd:
#+created: 2026-11-22
#+updated: 2026-11-22

* Summary

A synthetic feed's source name is its key, part of the subject its ticks
travel on, and the seed of its random streams, so it is chosen once and by a
rule. This page proposes that rule: a name states who produces the feed and,
in the vocabulary of [[id:C3E053CA-0D4B-480B-9119-E11530160EC1][oresmd]], what it
observes, as =synthetic.<source>.<asset_class>.<instrument>.<subject>=. The
scheme replaces names built ad hoc in the publish step, which say nothing of
a feed's kind and file a vendor-neutral vol surface under one vendor. It is a
proposal: no feed is renamed until the owner signs it off, and the sign-off is
recorded on [[id:642ED805-A23C-404F-B494-8A74BB8AEB12][the task]] that designs it.

* Detail

** What a source name does

- *The feed's key.* A feed config row is unique by its source name in its
  tenant, and the screens show it as the feed's title.
- *Its tick subject.* A feed publishes on =synthetic.v1.ops.tick.<source name>=.
- *A board key.* A spot feed publishes its level to the run's market board
  under its source name, which a vol surface reads.
- *A seed.* A feed's random streams are seeded from the run seed and its source
  name (=price_stream_for=), so a renamed feed draws different paths.

Because of the last point a rename is a change to the generated market, and
it should happen once, for every feed, by one rule.

** The scheme

: synthetic.<source>.<asset_class>.<instrument>.<subject>

| Part | Meaning | Spelled as | Examples |
|------+---------+------------+----------|
| =synthetic= | the simulator's namespace | fixed | =synthetic= |
| source | who produces the feed | the provider code of the catalogue, or =latent= for a vendor-neutral market no vendor publishes | =bloomberg=, =lseg=, =latent= |
| asset_class | the asset class observed | the =oresmd= asset class, the URI's authority | =fx=, =ir= |
| instrument | what is observed | the =oresmd= instrument spelling | =fx_spot=, =fx_option=, =fx_vol_surface=, =yield_curve= |
| subject | which one | the identifying fields, ordered so names sort usefully | =audusd=, =sofr.usd=, =libor.gbp.3m= |

The rules:

- Every part is lower case, from =[a-z0-9_]=, and the parts are separated by
  dots, so a name is a valid sequence of NATS subject tokens.
- A name is derived from the feed's config by one function and never typed.
  Two feeds that would derive one name are refused.
- A name is not parsed back into its parts. The series a feed publishes are
  the feed's own list (=i_feed::series_uris=), not a reading of its name.
- An FX pair is written as the market writes it, base then quote, joined:
  =audusd=. A curve's subject is its index family, then its currency, then its
  index tenor when it has one: =sofr.usd=, =libor.gbp.3m=, =sofr.usd.fomc=. The
  index comes first so the curves of one index sort together.

** One name for each kind of feed

| Node | What it publishes | Name |
|------+-------------------+------|
| A vendor's FX spot | =oresmd://fx/AUD?type=quote&instrument=fx_spot&quote=rate&ccy=USD= | =synthetic.bloomberg.fx.fx_spot.audusd= |
| A vendor's yield curve | its pillar quotes: =mm=, =fra=, =ir_swap= under =oresmd://ir/USD= | =synthetic.bloomberg.ir.yield_curve.sofr.usd= |
| A vendor's index curve with a tenor | its pillar quotes | =synthetic.ice.ir.yield_curve.libor.gbp.3m= |
| A latent vol surface | nothing; the vendors quote it | =synthetic.latent.fx.fx_vol_surface.audusd= |
| A vendor's quotes of that surface | =fx_option= quotes of the pair | =synthetic.bloomberg.fx.fx_option.audusd= |

The two vol nodes follow a precedent =oresmd= already sets: it spells a whole
calibrated surface =fx_vol_surface= and a quote of it =fx_option=. The latent
surface is the former and each vendor's view the latter, so the latent market
sits under the =latent= source and each vendor's quotes sit in that vendor's
namespace, beside its spots and curves.

** Open points, each with a recommendation

1. *The instrument of a whole curve.* A curve feed publishes quotes of several
   =oresmd= instruments (=mm=, =fra=, =ir_swap=), and =oresmd= has no instrument
   for a curve.
   - /Recommended:/ =yield_curve=, as the name of the whole, the way
     =fx_vol_surface= names a whole surface. It is used in names only; it joins
     the =oresmd= instruments if a whole curve is ever published as a series.
   - /Rejected:/ =discount=, ORE's instrument for a discount factor. The latent
     curve is a set of discount factors, but the feed publishes rates, so the
     name would mislead.
2. *The place of an index tenor.* The tenor goes last, after the currency, in
   ORE's period spelling (=1w=, =2w=, =1m=, =3m=, =6m=, =12m=), and =fomc= takes
   that place for the FOMC curve. A plain text sort puts =12m= before =1m=.
   - /Recommended:/ keep the market's spelling, and let a screen sort by the
     tenor field, which it reads from the config, not by the name.
   - /Rejected:/ zero-padded or day-counted tenors (=012m=, =365d=), which sort
     but read badly and differ from every other place a tenor is written.
3. *How a name maps back to what a feed publishes.* The map runs one way: the
   config derives the name, and the feed lists the series it publishes. A
   spot's name corresponds to one series; a curve's and a vendor view's names
   correspond to many.
   - /Recommended:/ one derivation function per kind, tested so that no two
     feeds of the seeded datasets derive one name, and no parser.
