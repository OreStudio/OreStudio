* Goal

Every currency the synthetic IR feeds support has a published overnight-index curve in the system tenant, bootstrapped from that currency's quotes, and those quotes start from the currency's public overnight rate. The FX volatility surface reads the forward to each expiry from the published curves of both currencies of a pair, so the eight currencies of the derived FX pairs (AUD, CAD, CHF, EUR, GBP, JPY, NZD and USD) come first; the remaining twenty of the twenty-eight follow.

* Acceptance

- A table names, for each of the 28 currencies, its overnight index, its public source and the state of its terms, its calibration policy, its bootstrap config and its latest published curve.
- A freshly provisioned environment holds a published curve for each of the eight currencies of the derived FX pairs, with no person acting. A currency whose feed has no usable public source, such as CHF without a SIX licence, gets its curve from its synthetic quotes at their fixed level.
- Each synthetic overnight-index feed with a usable public source starts from the latest public rate, and each without one keeps a stated fixed level with the reason recorded.
- The development database holds no curve publish that a test wrote.

* Decisions


- Split from [[id:9AE251D9-F9B9-4690-98C6-0D71F81FB3E1][Build the IR curves of every supported currency from public data]] on 2026-11-08 at sprint 29 close. The acceptance above is copied from that story; its done tasks are recorded there.
- Reviewed on 2026-11-08 at sprint 30 start. Read the public overnight rates through an adapter overlaps Read the public interest rate sources. Do that story first, and drop this task if it covers the same sources.
- Restructured on 2026-11-09. The adapter task was abandoned, and [[id:6E93FD89-3443-4BED-8E2F-9295E773BFB7][Read the public interest rate sources]] now reads the public overnight rates, with one specialised adapter for each publisher. This story keeps the use of those rates: the calibration, the bootstrap cadence and the coverage screen.
- The order of the work: the public rates are stored first, then the calibration reads them, then the coverage screen shows the result. The bootstrap cadence, the replacement of ceased or misnamed feeds and the test cleanup do not depend on the public rates.
- A currency with no usable public source keeps its synthetic feed at a stated fixed level, with the reason recorded. On 2026-11-09 that is AUD and NZD (no route that a script can read), JPY (the Bank of Japan's permission) and CHF (a SIX Data License) among the eight currencies of the derived FX pairs.
- Calibration, on 2026-11-12. The IR class has one policy, seeded with the thirteen publishers and a 90-day window. A feed of price source =fixing= starts from the newest overnight fixing of its currency in that order. A fixing older than the window is skipped, with its age in the record. A policy of =keep_fixed= keeps the stated level with the reason; a policy of =refuse= stops the curve by name.
- The start record covers a fixed curve too. A curve that took no fixing records the level it kept, so a later edit of the config does not rewrite where an earlier run began.
- A forward set reads its pair's calendars to lay out the ladder. A fresh provision therefore builds the calendars before the forwards; the two ran at once, and a pair whose calendar was still being materialised was refused.
- The thirteen rates are already stored, so AUD, NZD and JPY now start from a public rate. CHF keeps a fixed level until the SIX Data License question is settled.

- Corrected the ceased and misnamed feeds on 2026-11-13, and kept the IBOR feeds. ILS now
  names SHIR, which replaced TELBOR on 2025-06-30, and HUF names HUFONIA, the overnight
  average, where it named BUBOR's overnight quote. CNH keeps HIBOR and TWD keeps TAIBOR: the
  O/N fixing of each family is the currency's overnight rate, and no other family exists for
  either. PLN keeps POLONIA, the central bank's overnight rate; what is unsettled is the route
  that would read it, so the feed keeps its fixed level with the reason.
- The LIBOR feeds stay, as learning material: LIBOR is retired, and a simulated IBOR curve is
  a case a person should be able to build and compare against the overnight curve. The ICE
  dataset and the Tradeweb LIBOR tenors are seeded again, described as retired benchmarks. They
  are term curves: the publisher gives every =libor= or =euribor= feed the role =projection=,
  so no consumer takes an IBOR curve for a discount curve, and the automatic calibration never
  starts one from a public overnight fixing because their price source is =fixed=.
- No public rate is stored for SHIR or HUFONIA, so the two feeds keep their synthetic level,
  as the calibration already does for a currency with no source. The conventions carry the
  two indices; the ACME discount curves name them. The ORE sample artefacts (northwind,
  acme_uk) keep their own index names, because they copy external ORE documents.
- The next step for IBOR is the visual comparison: a bootstrap config for the IBOR curve of a
  currency beside its overnight curve, so a person can open both, and the coverage screen
  groups by currency and family rather than taking one row per currency.
