* Goal

Every IBOR feed has a bootstrapped curve beside its overnight curve, marked as a projection curve, kept out of discounting and surface calibration, and readable in the three views the inspector has (zero, forward, discount factor), so a person can compare any currency's two curves and price from either.

* Acceptance

- Every IBOR feed of every currency publishes a bootstrapped curve beside its overnight curve, with the currency's overnight curve as its discount curve.
- The IBOR curve publishes with role projection, and no consumer takes it for a discount curve or a surface input; a test states the rule.
- The curves screen and the coverage screen show every curve of a currency together, with the index family, the role and the three views.
- One page states the difference the comparison teaches: an overnight average is compounded and discounts, a term IBOR is quoted and projects.
- The data supports pricing from an IBOR curve: a projection curve is linked to its discount curve, so a pricing screen can read both when one exists.

* Plan

Earlier work on this task, merged before 2026-11-19, bootstrapped every IBOR
feed, published it with role projection beside its funding curve, and taught
the curve analytics to draw a projection beside the curve it discounts off.
What remained was the coverage screen and the page that states the difference.

The coverage took one curve per currency: the newest published of its configs.
Each row now carries every curve its bootstrap configs build, the funding curve
first, with its role, a projection's index tenor and the curve it discounts
off, its latest publish, and a link to the curve analytics, which holds the
three views. The currency is covered by its funding curve. Before, a projection
that published later than a stale funding curve made the currency look
covered.

The page that states the difference is the curve analytics itself. When the pair
on screen is a funding curve and a projection, a panel states the four points
the prototype set out: the underlying, how each fixes, what each is for, and why
the shape differs. The curve names and the index tenor come from the pair on
screen, so the text is about the two curves the person is reading.

* Notes

- The live run found a defect already on main: the web client required a
  =price_source= on each IR coverage row, which the BFF never sends and nothing
  reads, so the interest rates calibration failed to parse for every reader. The
  field is gone from the web schema, in its own commit on this branch.
- Calibration is a system control, so the harness reads it as the system
  administrator.

- The IBOR feeds are already seeded and publish as projection curves: ICE's five LIBOR
  curves (USD 1M/6M, GBP 3M, CHF 3M, JPY 3M) and Tradeweb's three (GBP 1M, CHF 6M,
  JPY 1M), plus EURIBOR. Evidence: =evidence/ibor_feeds_2026-11-13.log= in
  [[id:4B5701D6-16C6-445B-BDD4-C60050FED95C][Replace the synthetic feeds of ceased or misnamed indices]].
- The curve inspector the comparison should reuse is captured in
  [[id:FB59FE7A-B0D1-4DF7-AFB1-07F426D0C9D9][Inspect a curve's pillars and conventions]].
- The coverage screen takes one feed config per currency today, so it shows AONIA for AUD
  and never the IBOR curve; the grouping is part of this task.
- The bootstrap config seeds live in =refdata_ir_curve_bootstrap_configs_populate.sql= and
  name a discount curve, so AUD-LIBOR-3M points at AUD-AONIA rather than at itself.
- The comparison prototype is =doc/prototypes/market_data/ibor-beside-overnight/index.html=: the
  inspector drawn for two curves at once, with the conventions strip for each and a panel
  that states the four differences (underlying, fixing, use, why the shape differs). It is
  static data for the design; the task builds the live version against it.
- Signed off on 2026-11-13. The inspector keeps all three read-outs of a curve — zero rates,
  forward rates and discount factors — because each carries part of the comparison: zero for
  the level, forward for where the term premium sits, and the discount factor for what a
  curve that discounts actually does. Forward is the view to open first when a term curve is
  present. The build must carry the switch; it is not a prototype-only convenience.

* Result

** The pair view, on the curve analytics screen

Commit =37891753d7=. The screen is
=projects/ores.web/packages/web/src/marketdata/CurveAnalyticsPage.tsx=.

The picker names each curve's role and index tenor, so a projection is a
projection before it is opened. Opening one reads its =discount_curve_config_id=
without being asked and draws that curve beside it, and the pair reads pillar
by pillar in whichever of the three metrics is showing. The funding curve leads
that table, so the gap is the term premium whichever of the two was opened
first: =USD-LIBOR-1M= over =USD-SOFR-FOMC= was +28.0 and +26.0 bp at 1M and 3M
on the seeded fixture. Either curve can be drawn alone, as the prototype offers,
and a projection opens on its forward rates rather than its zero rates.

The pair is the read the screen already makes, asked a second time against the
BFF's =/api/market/curve-analytics=, so no protocol, BFF or server change was
needed. A deferred second curve states the reason the server gave, because the
read answers a deferred curve with its words rather than an empty page.

** What the environment holds

Measured on 2026-11-14 against the running database. Every projection config
names the currency's overnight curve: =USD-LIBOR-1M= points at
=USD-SOFR-FOMC=, =EUR-EURIBOR-1M= at =EUR-ESTR=, =GBP-LIBOR-3M= at
=GBP-SONIA=. =USD-LIBOR-1M= and =USD-SOFR-FOMC= each hold 462 published points,
so the pair has both sides to draw.

** What is still open

- The curves screen and the coverage screen do not group a currency's curves
  yet: the coverage screen still takes one feed config per currency, so it
  shows =AUD-AONIA= and never =AUD-LIBOR-3M=.
- No page states the four differences the comparison teaches, though the
  prototype carries the text.
- Nothing consumes a published projection curve for pricing yet, so "price from
  either" is unbuilt rather than untested.
- The acceptance item that a test states no consumer takes a projection for a
  discount curve is still unmet.

** The coverage, the teaching panel, and the close

A currency's IBOR curves now stand beside its overnight curve on the two
screens a person reads them from. The coverage screen lists every curve of a
currency, the funding curve first, each with its role, a projection's index
tenor and the curve it discounts off, its latest publish, and a link to the
curve analytics. The curve analytics draws a projection beside its funding
curve in the three views, and it now states what the pair teaches.

All five acceptance points are met. The first, second and fifth were met by the
earlier work on this task: every IBOR feed bootstraps and publishes with role
projection, and a projection names its discount curve. This branch meets the
third and the fourth, and the harness proves both against the running services.
