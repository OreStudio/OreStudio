* Plan

** What the journey asks for

Journey 5 draws a second business centre and admits it draws a gap rather than
a field. The counterparty carries one =business_center_code=, so the screen can
offer the one column and nothing more. The journey also needs the parties that
may trade with a counterparty, and the only read on
=ores_refdata_party_counterparties_tbl= is by party.

** Three changes

| change | shape |
|--------+-------|
| a counterparty-to-centre junction | a new =ores.codegen.junction= model, in the shape of =party_country_junction= |
| the single column goes | =business_center_code= leaves the counterparty, with its validation row and its four presentation rows |
| the counterparty-side read | =list_by: true= on the right of =party_counterparty_junction= |

The junction takes =counterparty_id= on the left and =business_centre_code= on
the right, so the generated protocol carries =list_by_counterparty_id= and the
screen reads a counterparty's centres in one call. The right column takes the
spelling the rest of the schema uses, =business_centre_code=, which also
retires the counterparty's lone American spelling.

The read on the party-counterparty junction needs no new index: the junction
create template already emits an index per side, and the right side's comment
already says it serves a counterparty-side lookup.

** What the removal touches

Only the counterparty model. A census of =business_center_code= over the
component finds it in the model and in the generated surfaces that derive from
it, and nowhere hand-written: the hits in =refdata_publish_from_dq_create.sql=
are the business unit's column of a similar name, not the counterparty's.

** What the next unit still owns

The screen. The flags reach the wire; the counterparty screen is its own task.

* Result

Every acceptance criterion the plan set holds.

=ores_refdata_counterparty_business_centres_tbl= holds the centres a
counterparty deals through, one row per centre, with the counterparty on the
left and =business_centre_code= on the right. The generated protocol carries
=refdata.v1.counterparty_business_centres.list_by_counterparty_id=, so a screen
reads a counterparty's set in one call.

The counterparty no longer carries =business_center_code=. A grep over every
=C++=, =SQL=, =TypeScript=, =org= and =mustache= source finds no reader or
writer of it left outside the pgTAP assertion that pins its absence.

=refdata.v1.party_counterparties.list_by_counterparty_id= now exists, so the
parties that may trade with a counterparty are read from that side.

The GLEIF counterparty publisher wrote the column, and a census missed it the
first time. It now records the centre in the junction, from the country the
registered address sits in, and the country-to-centre list lives in a temp
table so one copy serves both statements.

A pgTAP suite makes five assertions: a counterparty takes one centre, then a
second, holds both, upserts the first when it is written again, and no longer
has the single column.

**What this task does not land.** The screen. The junction and the two reads
reach the wire; the counterparty screen is its own task, and it is the one that
decides how a tenant states several centres rather than one.
