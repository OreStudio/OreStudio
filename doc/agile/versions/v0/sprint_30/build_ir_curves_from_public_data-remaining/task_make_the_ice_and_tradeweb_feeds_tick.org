* Acceptance

- The ICE and Tradeweb notations write the external id, field and oresmd item for every instrument those providers quote, and a test covers the spellings.
- The catalogue seeds a feed, a route and a system-party subscription for both providers.
- A feed run stores its LIBOR and EURIBOR quotes into the series their identities name; a check states it.
- The vendor instrument seed carries the ids for both providers, so no quote is left without one.

* Plan

(Implementation strategy. Written when work starts; key decisions
are distilled into the parent story's =* Decisions= at close, but the
plan itself stays — it is the historical record of what we did.)

The chain that fails: a feed's quote gets an adapter target only if its provider has a
notation; without one the feed emits an internal market_tick and the ingest drops it as
unbound. ICE and Tradeweb have neither a notation nor a feed, a route or a subscription, so
their quotes reach nothing.

1. Write =ice_notation= and =tradeweb_notation= against the =provider_notation= contract,
   modelled on the LSEG pillar spelling: currency, index family, instrument and the two
   tenors, with the provider's own scheme. These are simulated providers, so the header
   states the spelling is ours and must read back what it writes.
2. Register both in the one place that lists notations.
3. Test the contract: a round trip for every instrument each provider quotes, plus the
   refusals (wrong scheme, malformed, not representable).
4. Seed the catalogue so each provider has a feed, a route and the system party's
   subscription, as Bloomberg does; without the feed the tick is still unbound.
5. Regenerate the vendor instrument seed with =tools/vendor_catalogue= now that the ids can
   be written, so no ICE or Tradeweb quote is left without an external id.
6. Prove it at the seam that failed: a quote resolves to an adapter target, the feed binds,
   and its tick lands in the series the identity names.

* Notes

- The live attempt: both adapters announce and the ICE and Tradeweb generators start (the log
  shows 35 ids demanded, 35 accepted, 5 generators running per provider), and the marketdata
  service opens ingest loops for =ice-a= and =tradeweb-a=. The 30 IBOR quote series now exist
  with the identities the design names, for example
  =oresmd://ir/CHF?type=series&instrument=ir_swap&quote=rate&fwd_start=0D&tenor=3M=; before this
  task there were none.
- The tick landing was the open item, and the merged provisioning fixes closed it. After
  rebasing onto main and rerunning the provisioning, the tick store holds =ice-a= 259,
  =tradeweb-a= 245, =bloomberg-a= 8184, =demo-a= 3746, =lseg-a= 2190 and =derived-fx-vol= 350;
  the =ice-a= ingest reports 210 ticks with the last 0 s ago.
- The provisioning run still fails later, at =publish_party_surfaces=, because the surface
  quotes it carries are dated a day before the spot date. That is a separate fault and does not
  touch these feeds.
- That is environment provisioning, not this change: the same timeout affects Bloomberg's feeds.
  With a provisioned database the live check is one observation query.

- The notation is registered in =projects/ores.marketdata/core/src/vendor/provider_notation.cpp=
  (only =bloomberg=, =lseg= and =demo= today). A provider with no notation gets no adapter
  target (feed_adapter_targets.hpp), so its feed publishes an internal market_tick only.
- The catalogue to extend: =projects/ores.sql/populate/marketdata/marketdata_catalogue_populate.sql=
  seeds the providers, feeds, routes and subscriptions; =ice-a= and =tradeweb-a= do not exist.
- The instrument spellings to write: ICE's LIBOR tickers (USD 1M/6M, GBP 3M, CHF 3M, JPY 3M) and
  Tradeweb's composites (EURIBOR 1M/6M, GBP 1M, CHF 6M, JPY 1M). The simulated seed rows are the
  source of the (currency, index family, tenor) matrix.
- The vendor instrument seed already carries an unmapped row for each of those instruments
  (marketdata_vendor_instruments_populate.sql: the "no notation" block). Once a notation exists,
  regenerate that block with =tools/vendor_catalogue= and the ids appear.
- Proof: a feed run must store a quote into the series its identity names, and the oresmd item
  must resolve. The identity of an IR quote is (currency, instrument, fwd_start, tenor, term);
  a LIBOR 3M pillar is =fwd_start=3M&tenor=3M=, not the overnight =tenor=1D=.

* Result

Shipped and verified live. ICE and Tradeweb now have a notation, a feed, a route and a
subscription, and their quotes reach the store.

- =ice_notation= and =tradeweb_notation= write an interest rate pillar under the schemes
  =ice_ticker= and =tradeweb_ticker=, with the field =BID=. The id carries the curve's index
  tenor, because one currency may quote two curves of a family that differ only in it.
- The contract gained =ir_curve_pillar::index_tenor= and
  =provider_notation::names_index_tenor()=; only a notation that names the tenor is given one,
  and the others refuse a non-empty one. Without it, ICE USD 1M and 6M collapsed onto the same
  ids and one mapping pointed at two items.
- The catalogue seeds =ice-a= and =tradeweb-a= with rate cards, the system party's
  subscriptions and =ir= routes. The vendor instrument seed is regenerated: 35 ids for each
  provider, none without.
- The provider pages now state what the simulator writes and keep it apart from the live
  providers' own schemes.

Measurements: 30 IBOR quote series where there were none; ticks stored for both feeds; the
marketdata core suite passes. The live path was proven by the provisioning run after the
synthetic start fix landed.
