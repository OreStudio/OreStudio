#+title: A stalled service listener blocks every DROP DATABASE on the shared server
#+description: prime_origin's marketdata service stopped reading its LISTEN connection at 02:53 and held the backend in ClientWrite for about 16 hours, which blocked every other environment's database recreate until another session killed it.

This page is a [[id:671F18E4-E09C-4B3B-BD24-D33DF8AE38A6][capture]] in the *inbox* bucket of the product backlog — a pre-sprint idea, not yet pulled into a sprint as a story.

* What

A service's =postgres_listener_service= holds one dedicated connection that
LISTENs on its notify channels. In prime_origin, the marketdata service's
listener stopped reading that socket at about 02:53 on 2026-10-02 while the
service kept running and reporting healthy. PostgreSQL kept the backend (pid
2299136, user =ores_prime_origin_marketdata_service=, LISTEN on
=ores_refdata_crm_...=) in =ClientWrite=, waiting to deliver notifications
nobody read. Because the server is shared by every worktree, that one backend
blocked every =DROP DATABASE=, so another environment's =compass db recreate=
could not finish until a session terminated the backend by hand. A restart of
the service restored the listener.

* Why

Two defects, both worth finding: the listener thread stalls without the
service noticing (the heartbeat and readiness kept reporting fine for about 16
hours), and one environment's stalled connection can block every other
environment on the shared server. Neither the cause of the stall nor whether
the backlog came from the large corpus imports run that day is known yet.

* Resolution

Fixed by [[id:D745C35C-B206-4585-A112-01B16B10063D][Hotfix: a service listener falls behind a burst of
notifications]]. The listener did not stall: it paused 100 ms after every poll
and so drained at about 460 notifications a second, while this environment's
corpus imports sent one per inserted row. It recurred after a restart because
the cause was throughput, not a stuck thread.
