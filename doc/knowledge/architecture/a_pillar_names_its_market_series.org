#+title: A pillar names the market series it reads
#+description: A curve pillar names its input series, and no runtime identity is an ORE key: an oresmd URI is the identity and an ORE key lives only at the ORE XML boundary.
#+type: knowledge
#+level: cross
#+filetags: :marketdata:curves:bootstrap:architecture:decision:
#+created: 2026-10-28
#+updated: 2026-10-29

* Summary

A curve blueprint is a recipe. Its pillars are its inputs, and today a pillar
does not name the market series it reads. It carries a start tenor, an end tenor
and a curve role, and the bootstrap composes the series identity from the
blueprint's currency, its index tenor and those tenors, in the ORE corpus's
vocabulary.

The decision has two parts. A pillar names the market series it reads, as a soft
reference to the party's series. And no runtime identity is an ORE key: an
oresmd URI is the one internal identity, and an ORE key exists only at the ORE
XML boundary, where market data is read from and written to ORE's own files.

The second part matters because the first would otherwise only move the
composition. Today four runtime paths speak the ORE vocabulary as if it were the
identity. Naming the pillar's series settles one of them. The rule settles all
four.

* The question

Should a pillar state its input series, or keep deriving it? And, underneath
that: which spelling is a market datum's identity inside the system?

The blueprint already names its output series, as =output_series_id=. A reader
can say which series the curve is written to. The reader cannot say which series
the curve is read from. A pillar is the one place the input belongs, because a
pillar is one quote, and one quote lives in one series.

* Today

The blueprint carries =currency_code=, =index_tenor= and =output_series_id=. A
pillar carries =start_tenor_code=, =end_tenor_code= and =curve_role_code=, and
no series.

=curve_pillar_reader.cpp= reads a pillar by composing a key from the blueprint's
currency, its index tenor and the pillar's tenors:

#+begin_example
make_pillar_quote_key(ccy, index_tenor, start_tenor_code, start_date, end_date, point_tenor_code)
#+end_example

The key's vocabulary is ORE's: =IR_SWAP/RATE/<CCY>/<start>/<index tenor>/<end>=.
=pillar_series_uri= and =pillar_datum_uri= project that key onto the oresmd
identity, and the observations table stores the datum URI. So nothing is stored
under the ORE key. The key is a spelling the reader uses to construct the
identity it then reads.

The same spelling is used in four places in the runtime.

1. *The curve read.* =curve_pillar_reader.cpp= composes the pillar's series and
   datum through =make_pillar_quote_key=, and =curve_republish_service.cpp=
   validates the output datum by asking =ore_key_codec::write= for its ORE
   spelling.
2. *The feed.* =ir_curve_feed.cpp= derives the series it publishes the same way,
   one key per entry. That shared function is what makes a synthetic quote and a
   bootstrap read meet.
3. *The classifier.* =series_classifier.cpp= keys a series by an ORE series key,
   as =(series type token, metric name, qualifier)=, and the rules table it reads
   is keyed the same way. The runtime ingest paths call it, in
   =feed_ingest_loop.cpp= and =fixing_ingest.cpp=, so every series a feed creates
   is classified through the ORE vocabulary.
4. *The codec.* =oresmd_uri_codec.cpp= refuses a datum that has no ORE key
   spelling, by calling =ore_key_codec::write= as a validation. That makes oresmd
   subordinate to ORE: a datum ORE cannot name is not a datum.

Three costs follow from the pillar's derivation, and each is a cost a person can
meet on a screen.

- A blueprint reader cannot tell which series a pillar reads without running the
  composition in their head, with the blueprint's currency and index tenor to
  hand.
- A pillar can read only a series that some feed named the same way. A series a
  person seeded, imported, or took from a vendor under another spelling cannot
  feed the curve, even when it holds exactly the quote the pillar needs.
- A change to the feed's spelling, or to the conventions behind the dates, moves
  the read to another series or to none, and no record says the input moved.

The other three costs are structural. The ORE vocabulary reaches the ingest path
and the identity codec, so ORE's spelling decides which series exist and which
data are valid. ORE is one consumer of this platform and one boundary format, and
it should not name the things the platform stores.

* The options

** Keep deriving

The pillar keeps its three fields and the bootstrap keeps composing the key.

The good part is that a pillar stays small and a desk never types a URI. The bad
part is the three costs above. It also leaves the identity rule unsaid, so the
classifier and the codec keep treating an ORE key as the truth.

** Name the series on the pillar, and keep ORE at the boundary (decided)

The pillar gains a =market_series_id=, a soft reference to =market_series= in
=ores.marketdata=. A person chooses it in the Add and Edit pillar dialogs, using
the standard record picker, and the row is drawn by its oresmd URI. The bootstrap
reads the observations of that series at the as-of.

The datum coordinate is still derived. A pillar's term comes from its end tenor
through the reference conventions, because the tenor and the conventions are
what say where the point falls. Only the series identity stops being derived.

Alongside it, the rule: an oresmd URI is the runtime identity, and an ORE key is
a boundary format used only by the ORE XML import and export.

** Name a catalogue instrument

The pillar names a vendor instrument, and the party's provider and route resolve
the series.

The good part is that a desk picks the instrument it trades, not a storage
identity. The bad part is that resolution depends on the party's subscription and
route, so one blueprint reads differently at two parties, and a demonstration
cannot point a curve at a hand-seeded series. This stays open; see /What this
does not settle/.

* The decision and why

A pillar names the market series it reads. And no runtime identity is an ORE
key: an internal identity is an oresmd URI and nothing else, and a boundary
format is an ORE key and lives only in the ORE XML import and export.

The derivation hid a dependency. A curve's input is a fact a person must be able
to read, and it was a computation. Naming the series makes the
provenance explicit: the blueprint states its input, the lineage continues to
record the series each point was built from, and the two can be compared.

Naming the series also lets a pillar read any series the party holds: a synthetic
feed's, a vendor's, an official fixing series, a hand-seeded series for a
scenario, or an imported one.

The identity rule is the general form of the same argument. Two spellings of one
datum means two places to keep in step, and the failure is silent: a datum ORE
does not name becomes invalid, and a series ORE names differently becomes another
series. One identity removes the second spelling from every path that decides
what is stored. The ORE key keeps its job at the edge, where a file in ORE's
format must be read or written.

* Consequences

The runtime loses the ORE vocabulary in five places, and each is a unit of work.

- =ir_curve_bootstrap_pillar= gains =market_series_id=, a soft reference to
  =ores.marketdata.market_series=, required on a new row.
- =curve_pillar_reader.cpp= reads the observations of the named series at the
  as-of, and derives the datum's term from the pillar's end tenor as it does
  today. =curve_republish_service.cpp= stops asking the ORE codec to validate the
  output datum.
- =ir_curve_feed.cpp= builds the series it publishes as oresmd datums natively.
  The ORE key text is not the identity, so the feed and the bootstrap no longer
  meet through a shared spelling; they meet because the pillar names the series.
- =series_classifier.cpp= and its rules table move to the oresmd identity, so a
  series is classified by the datum it is, not by the ORE key it would have had.
  The ingest paths in =feed_ingest_loop.cpp= and =fixing_ingest.cpp= change with
  it. This is the largest unit, because the rules are seeded data and every
  series a feed creates passes through them.
- =oresmd_uri_codec.cpp= stops validating a datum by its ORE spelling.
  =ore_key_codec= itself stays, used only by =import_service.cpp= and
  =ore_export_service.cpp= and their tests, which are the ORE XML boundary.
- The pillar model, the C++ domain type, the protocol, the SQL table, the seed,
  the tests and the screens are regenerated or updated together.
- No backwards compatibility is kept. The demonstration tenant is recreated and
  reseeded.

* The migration

The follow-up task [[id:EAD9F02F-3B4B-495E-B37D-71BF766F7970][Name a curve
pillar's market series]] implements this decision. It carries the pillar's named
series first, then the feed building native datums, then the classifier and its
rules moving to the oresmd identity, then the codec's validation removed, then
the seeds and tests, and finally the tenant recreation.

The order matters. The pillar's named series can land first and the system works
while the classifier still speaks ORE. The classifier is last because it is the
unit that changes stored classification rules, and a mistake there misclassifies
every series a feed creates.

The task has landed. The pillar names its series, the read takes the term it
derives from the pillar's end tenor, the feed builds its datums from the market
data model, the classifier is keyed by the oresmd identity, and the codec accepts
every datum the model admits. The four places /Today/ lists above are the state
this page replaced.

* What this does not settle

1. *Whether the datum term should be named too.* The decision leaves the term
   derived from the end tenor. Whether a person may point a pillar at a datum
   whose coordinate differs from its end tenor stays open.
2. *Whether a person picks an instrument instead of a series.* The catalogue
   option above remains available, and it would make the pillar read through the
   party's provider and route. It is a different question, and it would change
   what a blueprint means at two parties.
3. *What a missing series does to a build.* The check tab already reports a
   pillar no series could answer. Whether a missing or removed series fails the
   save, or fails the check, is not decided here.
4. *How a named series is kept in step with the pillar's tenors.* A person may
   choose a series whose span does not cover the pillar's term. Whether the save
   refuses it, or the build reports it, is not decided here.
5. *What the ORE XML boundary guarantees.* The import and export keep the codec.
   Whether an ORE file that names a datum the platform cannot express is refused
   at the boundary, or converted with a stated loss, is not decided here.
