* Goal

Every value we compute names the series it read. A reader walks from a published
surface back through its calibration to the quotes, and from those to the fixings
the derived source inferred them from, without reading a configuration.

* Acceptance

- The derived FX volatility quotes write one lineage row per point, naming the
  fixing series and the as-of they read.
- A fixing the simulator derives from a spot series writes a lineage row naming
  that series.
- A read walks from a published surface to the fixings behind its quotes through
  the lineage alone.
- A test asserts a lineage row for every derived point, and asserts that a quoted
  tick gets none.
- The composites tables are either read by the resolution or deleted. They hold
  the definition-level edge and currently hold no rows.

* Plan

1. *Inventory the writers.* =curve_republish_service.cpp= and
   =fx_vol_surface_calibration_service.cpp= write one lineage row per point today,
   with the config, its version, the source series ids and the as-of.
   =ores.synthetic= writes none, so the derived FX volatility quotes and the
   simulator's fixings record nothing.
2. *The derived source first.* It reads fixings through the market data reader and
   publishes quotes; its lineage names the fixing series and the as-of of the day
   it read. It writes through the market data service rather than the ingest, so
   the writer goes with that path.
3. *The fixing derivation.* A fixing inferred from a spot series names the spot
   series it cancelled and the as-of.
4. *The composites.* Decide: read them in resolution, so a composite's inputs are
   a stored edge, or delete them because the lineage already carries the fact at
   the point.
5. *Prove.* A provisioned tenant publishes a surface, and a read of one of its
   points returns the fixings behind it.

* Notes

- The lineage table already carries the fact: =point_source_kind=, the derivation
  config and version, =source_as_of=, =source_series_ids= and =source_weights=,
  with a check that the weights match the ids. The gap is writers, not schema.
- A quoted tick gets no row by design: quoted is the absence of a row. Every
  writer added here must keep that, or the hypertable's volume argument breaks.
