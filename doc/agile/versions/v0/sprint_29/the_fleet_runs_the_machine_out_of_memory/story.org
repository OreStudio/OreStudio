* Goal

The machine runs the fleet again without running out of memory. On 2026-10-26
it went down overnight: =ores.marketdata.service= grew by about 195 MiB a minute
until RAM and swap were gone. Two independent defects caused it. The NATS client
kept every core message any service received, and the synthetic service's shared
Hawkes heat ran away once more than forty feeds shared it, so ticks arrived at
about 300 times their configured rate. Pinning the market regime to =active=,
which multiplies arrivals by four, made it faster still.

The story also restores the usage records marketdata never wrote, and keeps
terminated tenants out of demand, routing and usage.

* Acceptance

- Every core NATS message is freed once its handler returns, on every path.
- The shared heat stays bounded for any number of feeds, and the stored tick rate
  follows the configured rate and the regime multiplier.
- marketdata memory stays flat under the synthetic feed, in the calm regime and
  pinned to =active=.
- Delivery counts and usage intervals are stored, and a terminated tenant's rows
  neither feed demand nor block another tenant's usage.

* Decisions

- *Own cnats objects through handles.* =nats_handle= is a =unique_ptr= over a cnats
  object and its destroy function, and =ensure_result= replaces hand-written status
  checks. A raw pointer with destroy calls beside it leaks on every path that
  misses one.
- *Share the heat out instead of capping it.* Each feed enrols its mean rate and
  takes the share of the heat its rate is of the total, so the loop gain is jump
  over decay for any feed count. A cap would have hidden the instability.
- *Leave ingest batching as it is.* At the fixed rate the worker writes about seven
  small batches a second and marketdata uses 0.9 percent of a core. Waiting a full
  =target_batch_ms= per batch would delay every live tick by up to a second.
- *Keep memory and CPU measurement outside the services*, and measure the tick
  flow inside them: see the captures this story filed.
