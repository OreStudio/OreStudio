#+title: How do I provision the Smokehouse tenant?
#+description: Provision Smokehouse, the smallest tenant that publishes every kind of derived market object, from the shell.
#+type: recipe
#+level: cross
#+filetags: :recipe:shell:provisioning:smokehouse:
#+created: 2026-11-26
#+updated: 2026-11-26

Smokehouse is the smallest tenant that exercises the publishing lifecycle. It is
one legal entity with one desk, one portfolio and three books, one feed, one
curve per funding currency the feed quotes, the forward sets those curves build,
one surface per pair with volatility quotes, and one trade per book. A check can
watch every kind of derived market object publish, be superseded and announce,
and watch the three trades price against them, without paying for a
demonstration tenant.

It is not a cut-down ACME. ACME pays for four legal entities, forty-five staff,
seventy-two subscriptions, eleven hundred trades and a photograph per person.
Smokehouse orders none of that.

The =ores-shell= block below is tangled into the runnable script library as
=smokehouse_provision.ores=.

* Question

How do I make a tenant that publishes curves, forward sets and surfaces in
minutes, so a check can exercise the lifecycle on every run?

* Answer

Log in as =super_admin= and provision =smokehouse= from the =smokehouse=
starting point. Then log in as its administrator and set the party the account
works in.

The starting point runs fifteen steps on the server:

1. =system_provision= publishes the installation's own data.
2. =publish_system_curves= publishes the system's curve blueprints.
3. =start_auto_approval= lets the run publish without a person approving each
   object, because the run has no person watching it.
4. =publish_bundle= publishes the base reference data and the GLEIF
   counterparties.
5. =build_calendars= builds the calendars the curves and the trades date with.
6. =import_lei_hierarchy= creates the tenant's party from its synthetic LEI.
7. =provision_party= publishes the party's conventions and CRM topology.
8. =load_staff= publishes the party's own datasets: its desk, portfolio, books,
   their approval matrix and its counterparties. No staff accounts are involved.
9. =subscribe_market_data= gives the party the vendors whose quotes its curves
   and its surface read.
10. =give_party_surface_blueprints= copies the surface blueprint of the one pair
    the tenant trades.
11. =publish_party_curves= bootstraps the three curves the profile names, in
    dependency order: the funding curves first, then the projection curve that
    discounts off one of them.
12. =publish_party_forwards= builds the forward sets the party's spots and
    funding curves support.
13. =publish_party_surfaces= calibrates the surface from the forward set and the
    volatility quotes.
14. =generate_trades= books one trade per book: a forward, an option and a swap.
15. =end_auto_approval= turns automatic approval off, so the tenant is left in
    the state a person works in.

Expectations:

- The services are up (=compass services start=).
- The database is fresh. Run =compass services stop=, then =compass db recreate
  -y -k=, then start the services again.

** What the profile states, and what the feed decides

The profile states the curves and the pair, so the tenant pays for the market it
trades and not for every curve the installation holds. Copying the whole
catalogue costs every curve of every currency, and a surface per pair, each one
waiting out its patience for quotes that never arrive.

The feed decides whether the quotes for those curves arrive. A curve whose
quotes the vendor has not published is reported by name and left out, and so is a
pair whose spots or volatility quotes are missing.

The trade population then follows from the curves: a desk books only a pair whose
two currencies the party can price, so two funding curves and one projection
curve in two currencies leave one pair and no choice about it.

** The company

Smokehouse Trading Ltd is fictional. Its LEI is synthetic: =9695SMOKEHOUSE000081=,
on the =9695= test prefix ACME uses, with ISO 17442 mod-97 check digits. Its
business unit, portfolio and books are stated by the smokehouse dataset, in
=projects/ores.seeder/datasets/smokehouse/=, not by this recipe.

Each book permits one product type: the FX book a forward, the FX options book an
option, the rates book a swap. The approval is the book's, so the three trades
need three books.

** Running it again

The tenant stage refuses a tenant code it already holds. Reset the database with
the stop, recreate and start sequence above, then run this script again.

#+name: smokehouse-provision
#+begin_src ores-shell :exports none
connect $ORES_NATS_URL

login super_admin Secure-Password-123
provision tenant --tenant-code smokehouse --tenant-name "Smokehouse" --tenant-hostname smokehouse --tenant-admin-email admin@smokehouse --tenant-admin-password Secure-Password-123 --profile smokehouse
logout

login tenant_admin@smokehouse Secure-Password-123
accounts set-default-party "Smokehouse Trading Ltd"
logout

exit
#+end_src

Run it from a connected shell, or load the generated script:

#+begin_src sh :noweb yes :results output :wrap example
./ores.shell ${log_args} ${connect_args} << 'EOF'
<<smokehouse-provision>>
EOF
#+end_src

* Tested by

Manual: =compass shell -f smokehouse_provision.ores= against a freshly recreated
database.

Proven on 2026-11-26 against =bright_faraday=, through =bootstrap setup= rather
than the tenant stage, because the database was fresh. All fifteen steps
completed and the run ended with no warnings. The tenant carried:

- 1 party, 1 business unit, 1 portfolio and 3 books, each book approved for the
  one product its trade carries.
- 3 curves: =EUR-ESTR=, =GBP-SONIA= and =GBP-LIBOR-3M=, the last discounted off
  the second.
- 1 forward set, for =EUR/GBP=, the only pair whose two currencies the party can
  price.
- 1 volatility surface, for =EURGBP=.
- 3 trades: a forward, an option and a swap, one per book.

Provisioning took 326 seconds on a quiet host, of which the waits for quotes were
the largest part. The floor the same profile cost before it published any market
data was 122 seconds.

** The valuation report

The profile publishes the six documents a valuation reads, and the run reaches
the engine. Triggering the tenant's =NPV= definition with
=report_operations trigger-report-instance <definition_id> <tenant_id>= walks the
run's nine steps: gather the trades, gather the market data, assemble the
bundle, prepare the ORE package, submit it to the grid, collect the results and
finalise. The grid needs the engine package in storage first:

#+begin_src sh
compute publish-package ORE 1.8.17.0 x64-linux --wrapper-version 1.0.0 \
    --file external/ore/packages/ore-1.8.17.0-x64-linux.tar.gz \
    --http-base-url http://localhost:$ORES_HTTP_PORT
#+end_src

The first run assembled everything and refused the market: the export carried no
FX spot at all, because an FX instrument holds currencies and no market address,
so no trade names the spot it converts at. The export now carries a party's FX
spots and volatility surfaces with every run, as it already carried the quotes a
curve is built from, and the market holds =FX/RATE/EUR/GBP= and the pair's
volatility quotes. The forward then prices — the run completes and records an NPV
of 80,815.55 GBP against it — and the other two trades record none:

1. =fx vol curve building failed: Convention 'EUR-GBP-FX' not found= — the
   conventions file carries the entry, but its =AdvanceCalendar= is
   =TARGET,UnitedKingdom.Settlement=, and ORE cannot convert the country suffix
   to a calendar, so it drops the convention.
2. =did not find object 'GBP-LIBOR-12M' of type ibor index= — the curve
   configuration models overnight curves and volatility surfaces and names no
   IBOR curve, so no LIBOR-based swap can price.

The capture =the-report-market-names-what-ore-cannot-resolve= holds the evidence
for both.
Until they are closed the option and
the swap price nothing, and a check that watches all three trades has nothing to
read for them.
