* Acceptance

- The dispatcher publishes no assignment and mints no token.
- The assignments stream and its durable consumer are not created.
- The JetStream and lifecycle pages describe the claim as the way work moves.

* Plan

- The dispatcher and the wrapper stopped using the stream in the task that made the wrapper claim work. This task removes what was left behind.
- Remove the =pull_work= and =result_started= operations: the model entries, the handlers and the registrations. A claim does what both did.
- Trim =work_handler= to the heartbeat and the old reaper, since it no longer needs the NATS client or the verifier.
- Turn =work_assignment_event= into the plain payload the wrapper builds from a claim. It has no subject and is not published.
- Rewrite the JetStream page, the job lifecycle page and its sequence diagram, the interface table and the wire format note so that the claim is the way work moves.
- Leave the =reap_work= handler and its scheduled job. A scheduled job publishes it, and removing the job needs a change to the populate script and to the live job table. The inbox holds a capture for it.
- A deployment that ran the earlier wrapper may hold a stream =compute_assignments= and a durable consumer. Nothing uses them. The JetStream page says they may be deleted.

* Notes

- Deploy order. Upgrade the service and every wrapper together. An old wrapper calls =pull_work= and =result_started=, which the service no longer answers, so Newton and the laptop must get the new wrapper image before they return to the grid.
- After the new service and wrappers run, delete the leftover stream with =nats stream rm <prefix>_compute_assignments=. Nothing reads it.
