* Summary

The equity family is 32 trade type codes of product type =equity=. The
catalogue routes 19 of them, of which 17 route to nine =equity_*=
instrument tables and two route to =composite_instrument=. The other 13
codes have an empty =instrument=, so no table holds them.

Nine routed tables and one child table hold 222 columns together, 17 to
26 per table. Each is the union of a shared option shape and one product's
own fields. 32 of those columns are written by no ORE import mapper (the
generated synthetic-data generators set 20 of them). Four more are written
and never read. The mapper keeps every product's money value in a
bare float, and one money column, =variance_strike=, is stored =double
precision=. The family has no header, so it has no common-date mapping and
the blotter cannot read its dates.

The proposal follows the bond pilot and the credit rework:

- one *family header*, =equity_instruments=, holding what the 17 routed
  codes share: the trade, the party, the trade type, the two common dates
  and the description;
- *product fact tables*, each keyed by the trade, one per ORE product or
  per group of codes that share one ORE binding struct;
- *shared children* that already exist, reused: =swap_legs= with its
  amounts and rates for the swap's legs, =instrument_options=,
  =instrument_strikes= and =instrument_schedules= for the option block;
- one new child, =equity_basket_constituents=, for the names and weights
  of a basket, which today live in a JSON string column;
- exact decimals for every money column, and the digits kept at the ORE
  boundary, as the rates and credit reworks did.

The rework is staged into ten units; the last proves the corpus over the
round trip.

* Detail

** Context: the bond pilot, the credit rework and the instrument table rule

Three bodies of prior work bound this one, and their conclusions are
assumed.

- Section 14 of
  [[id:FDC35F65-CBF0-40A0-96D0-EA180F2237EE][Investigation: the trade shape]]
  states the instrument table rule: a family header names the rows it owns,
  every instrument is keyed by its trade, and the family's trade types
  route through the catalogue.
- The bond pilot, then the rates rework and the credit rework, settled the
  shape: a header, product facts keyed by the trade, trade-owned legs, and
  the shared =instrument_*= children for the list shapes.
- The credit analysis is the proven template for this page and for the
  staging below.

The question this page asks is the one the credit page asked: *what does a
family header hold?* For equity the answer is short. The products share
the two common dates and nothing else: the underlying is singular for some
codes and a basket for others, and the currency is present on most but not
on the positions.

*** The shared children

The tree already has the shapes this family needs, and every one is keyed
by the trade:

- =swap_legs=, =swap_leg_amounts=, =swap_leg_rates=: the equity swap's
  legs, its notional amounts and its fixed or floating rates.
- =instrument_options=, =instrument_option_premiums=,
  =instrument_option_exercise_fees=, =instrument_option_payment_dates=,
  =instrument_option_exercise_prices=: the option block, the premium and
  the exercise rules.
- =instrument_strikes=: the strike, including the second strike of a
  cliquet or a worst-of.
- =instrument_schedules=, =instrument_schedule_dates=: the observation,
  averaging and settlement schedules the accumulator, the Asian option and
  the worst-of state.

The equity mapper writes none of them today. It keeps the strike on the
instrument row and drops the schedules.

*** The shared children

Reused as they are, because every one is already keyed by the trade:

- =swap_legs=, =swap_leg_amounts=, =swap_leg_rates=: the equity swap's
  legs. The leg's currency, day counter and payment frequency are columns
  of the leg, its notional an amount row and its fixed or floating rate a
  rate row.
- =instrument_options=, =instrument_option_premiums=,
  =instrument_option_exercise_fees=, =instrument_option_payment_dates=,
  =instrument_option_exercise_prices=: the option block of the six option
  products.
- =instrument_strikes= and =instrument_schedules= with
  =instrument_schedule_dates=: the second strike and every observation,
  averaging and settlement schedule.

One child table is new. =equity_basket_constituents= is keyed by the trade
and a sequence number and holds the name of a basket member and its
weight. It serves the worst-of basket swap and the two variance-swap codes
the corpus does not yet write. The weight is a continuous quantity, so it
is =double precision=.

*** Rejected alternatives

| Alternative | Why it is rejected |
|-------------+--------------------|
| Keep one wide =equity_instruments= table with a =trade_type= check and nullable product columns | The union is 99 business columns and 32 of them have no writer. A nullable column that only one product fills is cold data on the hot row, and the check cannot keep a column and a code in step. This is the shape E1 measures. |
| Route every equity code to one header and put all facts on it | The facts differ per product, so the header would be the wide table again with extra joins. The credit rework rejected this for the same reason. |
| A second leg table for the equity swap | The swap legs have the same shape as the rates and credit legs, and =swap_legs= is already keyed by the trade. A second table repeats it. The credit rework added =leg_group= instead. |
| A JSON column for the basket | =basket_json= is the current state and E11 measures its cost: no key, no weight, no query. A basket is a list, so it is rows in a child table. |
| A nested =std::variant= carrier for the wire | This is the current shape and E5 measures it. The data-oriented rule carries one typed array per concrete type, joined by the trade id, and a list read carries the central row alone. |
| A header column for the underlying | Not every code states one, and two state a basket. A header column must hold for every code. |
| A =leg_group= column now | The equity family has one leg group (the swap's). It is not needed until a second product reuses =swap_legs= with groups of its own. |
| Withdraw the 13 unrouted codes from the catalogue | The catalogue's =product_type= is the refdata taxonomy and the code is a valid ORE trade type. Only the routing stays empty, which is the catalogue rule. |

** The blotter's common-date view

The header maps =start_date= and =maturity_date= to the common names with
=:common_date:=. No equity product states both, so each code maps the one
it has and the view reads the other as null. The option's =expiry_date= is
a third date and moves to =instrument_options=, which the view does not
read yet, so the option expiry stays out of the view until that child maps
its date. This matches the credit decision.

** What each surface must change

- *Models.* One header model and the fact models are new; nine routed
  instrument models and the underlying child model are removed or rewritten
  as facts; =equity_basket_constituents= is new; the shared children are
  reused unchanged.
- *Catalogue.* The 17 routed equity codes keep their routing to
  =equity_instrument= (the header); the two composite codes keep
  =composite_instrument=; the position sub-trade routing is repaired
  (E6); the 13 unrouted codes stay unrouted.
- *Mapper.* The 17 forward mappings write the header, the fact row and the
  children. The reverse mappings write every required element. The
  =if constexpr= chains give way to the generated dispatch.
- *Importer.* =projects/ores.ore/service/src/messaging/ore_import_execute_handler.cpp=
  is the hand-written dispatcher that copies each family's fields into the
  NATS write requests, and
  =projects/ores.trading/api/src/domain/instrument_batch_mapper.cpp= builds
  the carrier. Both change with every field added, renamed or dropped.
- *ORE binding.* Every bare-float money member becomes a lexical leaf
  through =scripts/reapply_ore_lexical_form.py=, and the mapper writes them
  back with =set_exact=.
- *Generated surface.* The repository, service, messaging, eventing, shell

  generated, and the retired ones are deleted. The synthetic-data generators

- *Tests.* The mapper suite, the goldens and the corpus proof hold the

** Data-oriented-design score

The family is *Regime 2 — persisted domain data*: the database owns the
storage layout and the C++ types are the in-memory shape. The decision
surface is the one that fails. The invariant surface (value semantics, no
shared-ownership webs, codec drift) is a codegen guarantee and holds; the
hand-written =equity_instrument_variant.hpp= is the one exception and it
is reported under Regime 3.

| Check | Verdict |
|-------+---------|
| Identity by key | passes: every table is keyed by =(tenant_id, trade_id)=, but E6 shows the key is written with two different trade types for one trade |
| Table-shaped children | fails: the swap's legs (E4) and the basket (E11) are not rows |
| Core/cold split | fails: no header, and 32 columns with no writer (E1, E7) |
| Batched collection access | passes: the nine instrument models declare =has_batch_read= |
| Explicit variation | fails: nine per-type tables plus a =std::variant= carrier, and two codes routed to the composite table (E1, E5, E13) |
| Exact money at rest | fails: =variance_strike= (E3), and the binding loses the digits (E2) |
| One-way numeric conversion | fails: the ORE money leaves are read through a =double= (E2) |

*Regime 2 score: 1 of 5. Debt band: High.* The worst evidence is systemic,
not local: the family has no core/cold split at all, and the money-leaves
defect touches every product. The anchored table's row 1 names exactly
this shape. Regime 3 also applies and is separately defective.

** Open questions

- *The barrier fact table's shape.* The three barrier codes share one ORE
  binding struct but state one or two levels. The proposal keeps one table
  with an optional second barrier and moves the levels to
  =instrument_strikes=. If unit 5 finds the strike child cannot carry a
  barrier type, the table takes its own two level columns. The check is the
  round trip of the double barrier, which is the only corpus document with
  two levels.
- *Whether the accumulator and the TaRF share a table or split.* They share
  a table today. The proposal splits them because their binding structs
  differ (=accumulatorData= against =tarfData2=) and the common columns are
  four. Unit 7 decides on the round trip.
- *Whether the two variance-swap codes the corpus does not write
  (=EquityPairwiseVarianceSwap=, =EquityBasketVarianceSwap=) get a fact
  table now.* They are unrouted, so the proposal leaves them out until a
  mapper and a corpus document exist.

* Decisions

- *The family header holds the two common dates and the description.* No
  economic field holds for every code. Reason: a header column must hold
  for every code, and only the dates do.
- *One fact table per ORE binding struct, not per routed code.* Three
  barrier codes share =eqBarrierOptionData=, so they share a table.
  Reason: one binding struct means one shape and one writer.
- *Equity reuses the swap leg, option, strike and schedule tables.*
  Reason: they are already keyed by the trade, and a second table would
  repeat them.
- *The basket is a child table, not a JSON column.* Reason: a list is rows,
  and the JSON column loses the weight and the query (E11).
- *Every money column is an exact decimal and a continuous quantity stays a
  binary float.* Reason: the rates and credit reworks showed a float column
  stores an artifact of a money value; a unit quantity and a basket weight
  are continuous.
- *The variance strike is money.* =equity_variance_swap.variance_strike= is
  a level the two parties agree, and it settles a cash amount. Reason: the
  operand of a settlement is a money value, so it is =decimal= in the domain
  and =numeric(38,12)= in the column, exactly as a strike price is. The
  quantity and weight columns stay continuous.
- *The 13 unrouted codes stay unrouted.* Reason: the catalogue rule says a
  type with no importer has no table.
- *The two composite codes stay on =composite_instrument=.* Reason: their
  bodies are a container over sub-trades, not one equity product. Only the
  position sub-trade routing is repaired.

* Out of scope

- The composite family's own rework: =TotalReturnSwap= and
  =ContractForDifference= as containers. This page changes only the
  position sub-trade routing.
- An importer for the 13 unrouted codes. They need a mapper and a corpus
  document first.
- The equity refdata surfaces: equity curves and equity volatility
  (=equity_curve_config.ts=, =equity_volatility_config.ts=), which are
  reference data and not instruments.
- The equity simulation suites, which concern the simulation part of ORE.
- =CashPosition=, which the catalogue classifies as equity but which is not
  an equity product.
