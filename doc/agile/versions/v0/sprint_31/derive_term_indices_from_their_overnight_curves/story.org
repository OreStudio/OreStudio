* Goal

A currency whose market serves only an overnight index can still price a
trade that floats on the term index of that currency: the term index's fixings
are derived from its overnight curve, so the trade has a curve to discount and
project with. A person sees both series, the overnight one and the derived
term one, on the fixings screen and on the curve screens, and each says which
it is.

* Acceptance

- A term index of a currency with an overnight curve holds fixings derived
  from that curve, at each tenor the trades book.
- The derived series is named by the term index's own oresmd identity, and its
  fixings carry the source that states where they came from.
- The fixings screen lists the derived term series beside the overnight series
  it comes from.
- A term index whose currency has no overnight curve stays unserved, and a
  check says so.

* Decisions

* Out of scope

- The FX volatility surfaces the same derivation machinery serves. They are
  bright_faraday's story, and the series they derive stay under their own
  derivation kind until that story files them under an owner.

* Result

The goal is met. A currency that serves only an overnight index now prices a
trade that floats on its term index: the term index's fixings are compounded
from the overnight curve, stated at each tenor the trades book, and named by the
index's own oresmd address.

Five tasks, in the order they had to happen. The term indices were derived from
their overnight curves (=#5143=). The fixings screen then showed every FX and IR
observation across every source, read only (=#5196=, =#5202=). The simulated
fixings were split so that each administrator states its own, in its own
notation (=#5196=). The derived provider was retired: who owns an item and how
its value was made had been the same word, and the series already carried the
derivation (=#5214=). And each series now says which kind of derivation made it,
one kind per table, with the curve record stating the blueprint that built it
(=#5225=).

What a person sees. The system-level fixings screen lists the derived term
series beside the overnight series it comes from, and says which of the two each
is. Three computed term indices — CME's, Banco de la República's and GPW's —
carry =IR_TERM_FIXING= and 520 fixings each under their administrators' own
publishers. The curve screen states how the curve was made, by the blueprint's
name, instead of showing a bare id. Of the 21 fixing addresses an acme
provisioning asks for, none is unanswered.

What it leaves for later, captured rather than lost: a blueprint created in the
application states no derivation until its first republish; the FX volatility
quotes have no owner to file them under; and no test proves a live source
replaces a computed fixing. Each is an inbox capture, and the analytics and
evolution screens still say nothing about how the points they draw were made,
because =observation_lineage= already carries that and the record is where a
person meets the curve.
