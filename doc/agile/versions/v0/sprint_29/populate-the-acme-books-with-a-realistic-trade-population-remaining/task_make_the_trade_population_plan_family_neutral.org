* Goal

Synthetic has one planner interface, one shared plan envelope and one shared writer. The plan carries a payload that belongs to its family, so a bond slot is a bond and a commodity slot is a commodity trade. The asset class a config names selects its planner, and a class with no planner is refused by name. The population size is a config field and not a constant.

The bond planner is the first implementation. The commodity planner is the second, and it is delivered whole rather than as a stub: every commodity trade type books through it, its share matches its weight, and it exports and reverse-maps. Commodity is the proof because it is the one class whose every declared type routes, so the seam is tested on the plan alone and not on routing work.

* Acceptance

- A planner is a pure function of the generation scope and returns a plan.
- The plan carries a family-specific payload, and no slot states a bond
  fact outside the bond family.
- One writer executes any plan through the shared booking client.
- The config's asset class selects the planner, and a class with no
  planner is refused by name.
- The bond planner implements the interface, and the old bond-only free
  function is deleted.
- A commodity planner is added with no change to the writer, the plan
  envelope or booking, and it books all twenty-five routed commodity
  trade types.
- The commodity share of the population matches its weight in Trade
  Population Weights within tolerance.
- The bond population, and the eight commodity shapes the importer maps,
  export as the ORE portfolio the engine reads, and reverse-map without
  loss. The other seventeen commodity codes book but have no round trip,
  and the task names them as an importer gap.
- The population size is a config field, and the =trades_per_book=
  constant is gone.
- A second run of any planner books nothing.

* Plan

1. The envelope stays. =trade_population_plan= keeps the config id, the
   party and the as-of. =trade_population_slot= keeps the keys every
   family shares: the trade id, the slot number, the book, the
   counterparty, the netting set, the trader, the trade type, the trade
   date, the notional and the currency.

2. The payload becomes a variant. A slot carries
   =std::variant<bond_terms, commodity_terms>=. =bond_terms= holds the
   security id, the issue id, the issuer, the issue date, the maturity,
   the tenor in years, the calendar and the reference curve.
   =commodity_terms= holds the commodity, the delivery and maturity
   dates and the price, strike, barrier or averaging fields its shape
   needs. A family is a type, so a reader of a slot sees which facts it
   carries and no others.

3. The payload fills the batch, and the writer does not branch. The
   trading protocol carries one typed array per entity, so a family
   states how its own terms become the batch members it owns. Each
   payload offers its own =apply_to(instrument_batch&)= and the writer
   calls it through a visitor. That is what keeps the acceptance honest:
   adding a family adds a payload type and a planner, and changes
   nothing in the writer, the envelope or booking.

4. The planner becomes an interface. =trade_planner= answers
   =plan(scope)=. A registry maps =asset_class_code= to a planner, and a
   class with no entry is refused by name. The free =plan_population= is
   deleted.

5. The shared validation moves up. Book, counterparty, trader, notional
   band and tenor band are checks every family makes, so they run once
   before the planner is called and not inside each planner.

6. The bond planner becomes the first implementation. It states bond
   terms for each slot and produces what =plan_population= produced, so
   the existing tests keep their meaning.

7. The size moves to the config. =trades_per_book= leaves the planner
   header for a column on =ores.synthetic.trade_generation_config.org=,
   regenerated through codegen.

8. The commodity planner is written against the eight shapes the ORE
   importer already declares, at
   =projects/ores.ore/core/src/domain/commodity_instrument_projection.cpp=
   lines 250-370: forward, option, swap, swaption, variance swap,
   average price option, option strip and basket option. The catalogue's
   twenty-five commodity codes map onto those shapes, so the planner
   states a shape and not twenty-five special cases.

9. Delivery order, one verifiable commit each: the seam with bond; the
   size in the config; then the commodity shapes one at a time, each
   with the test that proves the rows it writes.

* Notes

The catalogue routes all twenty-five commodity codes to
=commodity_instrument=, but the ORE importer maps only eight shapes, at
=projects/ores.ore/core/src/domain/commodity_instrument_projection.cpp=
lines 50-53: =CommodityForward=, =CommodityOption=, =CommoditySwap=,
=CommoditySwaption=, =CommodityVarianceSwap=,
=CommodityAveragePriceOption=, =CommodityOptionStrip= and
=CommodityBasketOption=.

The other seventeen codes route to the table and no projection reads or
writes them. They are =CommodityForwardVolatilityAgreement=,
=IntradayPowerForward=, =CommodityDigitalOption=,
=CommodityDigitalAveragePriceOption=, =CommodityAsianOption=,
=CommoditySpreadOption=, =CommodityPairwiseVarianceSwap=,
=CommodityBasketVarianceSwap=, =CommodityAccumulator=, =CommodityTaRF=,
=CommodityWorstOfBasketSwap=, =CommodityBestEntryOption=,
=CommodityWindowBarrierOption=, =CommodityGenericBarrierOption=,
=CommodityRainbowOption=, =CommodityStrikeResettableOption= and
=CommodityPosition=.

That decides what this task can claim. The planner books all twenty-five,
because the table takes them, but the export-and-reverse-map proof covers
the eight shapes the importer maps. The seventeen are a gap in the
importer and not in the planner, and this task names them rather than
asserting a round trip it cannot show.
