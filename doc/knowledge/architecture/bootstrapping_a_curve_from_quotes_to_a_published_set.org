* Summary

This document describes the target state of curve construction in ORE Studio. A
curve is not observed in the market; the market quotes instruments, and a
bootstrap inverts those quotes into the discount factors a valuation needs. The
document follows that construction from the blueprint that states the recipe, to
the pillars that name the market series they read, to the solve that produces the
discount factors, to the candidate it writes, and to the publication that makes
the set authoritative. It states what the identification system and the lineage
records have to hold at each step, and it names the changes that reach the
target. The companion document, [[id:FDD02CA9-1DAD-4162-95AC-C7C5C611DDDC][Tracking market data identity from fixing to report]],
follows the data before and after this construction.

* The problem: a curve is not observed

A valuation needs a discount factor for each future date it discounts. The market
does not quote those factors. It quotes instruments, each with a rate and a
maturity, and a curve is the set of factors implied by those quotes under a
method and a set of conventions. The construction is therefore an inference, and
it is a decision: which instruments, which day count, which interpolation, which
split between the short end and the long end.

Two consequences follow, and the rest of the document depends on them. The curve
is derived, so its identity must say so: a bootstrap point is a datum of a
discount series with a curve identity, not an observation of the quote it came
from. And the inference must be recorded, so a point can be traced to the quotes
it inverted and to the version of the recipe that inverted them.

* The recipe: the blueprint and its pillars

A blueprint is a named recipe. It states the party, the currency, the index tenor
of the index the curve stands for, the family role (FUNDING or PROJECTION), the
interpolation method, the day-count convention, the tenor convention, the split
tenor, and the output series the curve will be written to. The record is
[[id:73A5FAA1-377C-45A6-999E-4AD4DE406F78][IR Curve Bootstrap Config]].

Its pillars are the instruments the inference uses. A pillar states a start
tenor, an end tenor and an instrument role: DEPOSIT, FRA or SWAP. The order of
the pillars is the fixed-leg schedule of the curve, so a swap pillar's fixed-leg
dates are the end dates of the pillars before it together with its own. The
record is [[id:5DC8A225-A2FD-4ECC-8284-C1D333F0EB3A][IR Curve Bootstrap Pillar]]; the
concepts are in [[id:E808F432-2FDA-47E8-B003-1E8B908870FE][Interest Rate Curves]],
[[id:6EA42D11-94F4-4D3A-9A51-024EC56EFC44][Funding and Projection Curves]] and
[[id:29F0F3B1-91A6-42BD-A53F-97F1E72A4FB9][Interest Rate Curve Families]].

Two rules belong to the recipe rather than to the engine. A funding curve
discounts itself; a projection curve discounts off a funding curve, and the
blueprint names that curve. The split tenor states where the interpolation
method changes between the short end and the long end. Both are part of what the
blueprint decides, and both are checked before any quote is read.

* The inputs: named series, the as-of and the dates

In the target state a pillar names the market series its quote comes from. The
series is an observed quote series, an oresmd URI such as
=oresmd://ir/USD?...instrument=ir_swap&quote=rate...=, and the pillar holds a soft
reference to it. The decision is
[[id:7AE57098-08D4-4D4A-83D6-918FE520B68E][A pillar names the market series it reads]]; the migration is
[[id:EAD9F02F-3B4B-495E-B37D-71BF766F7970][Name a curve pillar's market series]]. The identity of the
input is deliberately not the identity of the output: the quote is observed and
the curve point is inferred, and the two must remain distinguishable.

At an as-of, the compute reads the observation of each named series at that
instant. The pillar's end tenor is the datum's coordinate: the term of the point
in the series' identity. The end tenor is resolved to a date through the
reference data: a tenor is an anchor, a calendar offset and a number of steps
along a named schedule axis, which is why a meeting-dated pillar and a period
pillar resolve through the same machinery. The model is stated in
[[id:0220F3F7-C674-47EE-91CE-1C04804B4558][Tenor and Curve Bootstrapping Architecture]]; the
series identity in [[id:CE99CD2A-B31F-4306-865F-CBC519FBC1B8][The oresmd datum identity]].

A missing observation is a refusal, not a zero. The compute names the pillar and
the series it could not read, so the person sees what the recipe expected and the
market did not supply. The same rigidity applies to the dates: a pillar whose end
tenor cannot be resolved is refused before the solve begins.

* The solve: from quotes to discount factors

The engine solves the factors from the short end outwards, one pillar at a time,
so that each quote is reproduced exactly by the factors already solved and the
factor at the pillar's end. The three instrument roles give three relationships.

A DEPOSIT fixes its factor directly: the factor at its end is one divided by one
plus the rate times the year fraction from spot to the end. A FRA fixes the factor
at its end from the factor at its start, by the same relationship over the FRA's
own period. A SWAP fixes the factor at its end from the par condition: the fixed
leg's annuity, valued with the factors already solved, equals the floating leg's
value, and the only unknown is the factor at the swap's end. This is the textbook
bootstrap, and it is what makes the sequence of pillars a ladder rather than a
set of independent quotes.

Between the pillars the method interpolates. Under LOG_LINEAR_DISCOUNT the factors
are log-linear between adjacent knots; under FLAT_FORWARD_THEN_LOG_LINEAR the short
end holds the forward flat and the long end interpolates log-linearly, with the
split tenor naming the boundary. The engine's own documentation notes that the two
methods are mathematically identical for pillar-to-pillar interpolation; the
difference the person sees is the name of the method and the split-tenor rule the
checks enforce. CUBIC_SPLINE is recognised and is not implemented; the target
state implements it or removes it from the vocabulary, and that change is named
below.

The day count is applied before the solve: every year fraction is computed under
the blueprint's convention, so the factor a pillar implies depends on the
convention as much as on the rate. The engine is at
=projects/ores.analytics/quant=.

* The checks

Six checks run before the quotes are read, because each is a property of the
recipe and none needs the market. They are implemented once, in
=projects/ores.web/packages/web/src/marketdata/curveBlueprintCheck.ts=, and the Check tab draws them.

1. At least one pillar exists, since a recipe with no instrument infers nothing.
2. The pillars are in strictly increasing maturity order, so the ladder has no
   crossing or repeated knot.
3. A DEPOSIT starts at spot, because a deposit is a spot-starting instrument and a
   later start makes it a different one.
4. The split tenor closes the ladder as the method requires: for a single-segment
   method it is the last pillar's end, and for the two-segment method it sits at
   or after the first pillar's end and at or before the last.
5. A PROJECTION names a FUNDING curve, not itself, in the same currency, because a
   projection discounts off a funding curve and the two-tier rule is strict.
6. The curve a projection names is not itself a projection, for the same rule.

The checks are a refusal by the screen, not by the service: the compute will read
the quotes and solve whatever the recipe says. The target state moves the checks to
a validation operation the service owns, so a caller that is not the screen is
held to the same rule. Until then the Check tab is the authority for a person, and
the target says so.

* The candidate

The solve produces one discount factor per pillar. The compute writes them as
observations of the blueprint's output series, dated at the as-of, with the term
of each point in the datum identity. The output series is a discount series, so
the identity says the points are inferred rather than observed; the blueprint is
the only writer of that series.

Every point carries a lineage record naming the source series the pillar read and
the version of the blueprint that produced it, so the inference is recorded, not
merely performed. The result is the candidate: it exists, it is traceable, and it
is not authoritative. A candidate is not consumable by a report; the published
state is what makes a set authoritative, and the next section is that act.

* The hand-off to publication

The candidate becomes authoritative in three acts. The maker raises a publish
naming the blueprint and the as-of; a second person decides under the kind's maker
and checker permissions and the record's four-eyes rule; the publish applies the
approved request once, stamps the publisher and the instant, ends the set it
replaces and sends the notice. The record is the publish record, and the set's
state is published, superseded or withdrawn.

The published set is what the rest of the system consumes. The report references
it through its market binding, as
[[id:FDD02CA9-1DAD-4162-95AC-C7C5C611DDDC][Tracking market data identity from fixing to report]]
describes; the screens read it; a candidate never reaches either. The journeys are
[[id:239E4C3C-12E7-4A87-9DE3-16B9F093BC14][Ask for a publish to be approved]],
[[id:A85B6C4E-0F93-4410-BF2E-7167722849ED][Decide a publish]],
[[id:53F31919-86FA-4E35-8594-9E72197C4A3B][Publish the approved set]] and
[[id:B05A31CC-FA22-4534-848E-601A30822992][Withdraw or replace a published curve]].

* The changes that reach the target

Four changes remain, and each is a change to a named part of the system.

The first is the named series. The pillar names the market series it reads, the
reader reads that series rather than composing its identity, and the synthetic
feed publishes into series it names the same way. This is
[[id:EAD9F02F-3B4B-495E-B37D-71BF766F7970][Name a curve pillar's market series]] and the decision in
[[id:7AE57098-08D4-4D4A-83D6-918FE520B68E][A pillar names the market series it reads]].

The second is the validation operation. The six checks move from the screen to a
service operation, and the result is held against the blueprint's version, so a
candidate states the version it was checked under.

The third is the cubic spline. CUBIC_SPLINE is recognised and not implemented; the
target either implements it as a third interpolation method or removes it from the
vocabulary, so no recipe names a method the engine refuses.

The fourth is the creation of the output series. A blueprint's output series is
created as part of the blueprint's lifecycle, so a person does not have to find
one that already exists. This change is shared with the identity document.

The schedule-resolved pillar is not a change to this construction: the engine
accepts the dates it is given, and the FOMC-dated short end is a configuration of
the tenor machinery rather than new engine code. The open item there is the
build's refusal of a schedule-resolved pillar, recorded in the check journey,
which is a defect of the reader rather than of the bootstrap.

* Conclusion

In the target state a curve is constructed, not observed, and the construction is
recorded rather than merely performed. The blueprint states the recipe, the
pillars name the series they read, the solve infers the factors under the
conventions, the checks hold the recipe to its own rules, the candidate names the
source series and the version that produced it, and the publication makes the set
authoritative for the report. The identity system distinguishes the quote from
the point; the lineage records connect them; and the published set is the single
object the rest of the system consumes.
