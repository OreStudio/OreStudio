* Goal

Replace the current CRUD-style trade entry with a screen built around
*structures*, and settle the decisions that screen forces before anyone
writes production code.

Today a trade is booked on its own through
[[id:5C7A961E-D834-4B2F-AE15-F049B27361D8][ores.trading]]'s CRUD dialogs and
a per-instrument Qt form. That shape is wrong for the way deals actually
arrive: a risk reversal is three or four components, a dual currency deposit
is two, and even a vanilla drags a premium and a brokerage deal behind it.
The screen must let a booker *manage a structure and its components*, choose
a component of any ORE product type, and see a widget built for that product
family — barrier management for a barrier option, schedule management for a
swap.

The destination is a *throwaway clickable prototype* of that screen plus its
companion pricing screen, and the decisions the prototype settles. The
prototype is not production code and is not shipped to users; it exists to
make the layout and the behaviour arguable with something concrete. A later
effort turns the settled decisions into a spec and then into Qt.

Scope of the two screens:

- The *booking screen* captures reference data and validates the booking. It
  is deliberately not interactive: no live market data, no model inputs.
- The *pricing screen* is the dynamic one — ticking market data, tweakable
  vols and rates, a NPV readout — and carries the *Book* button that hands
  the booked economics to the booking screen.

* Notes

The source document is outside this repo, at
=~/Development/Notebooks/summaries/structures-and-deal-composition.org=
(2026-09-09). It defines the composition taxonomy the prototype follows, and
it is the authority for the strategy/package split, the deal topology and the
versioning rule. Structure support is not implemented anywhere in ORE Studio
yet; this prototype runs ahead of it.

That source now has a page in the repo:
[[id:FC31FC9C-7A6E-4EFD-8A62-6DE4137F7160][Trade Structures and Deal Composition]]
(PR #2063, merged 2026-09-11), with
[[id:74721BF2-2290-4AA7-8871-E215DA04351F][Confirmations and Deal Versioning]]
beside it. Tickets cite the page, not the source file.

Decisions taken while charting, which fix the frame for every task below:

- *Composition rungs.* The prototype covers rung 1 (*strategy*) and rung 4
  (*untyped structure / package*) of the taxonomy. Strategy mode fixes the leg
  set and enforces the strategy's invariants. Package mode offers every
  product type and enforces nothing structural. Typed structures between them
  are template mode with a larger leg set, so they need no third mode.
- *Deal topology.* Two levels, with named groups allowed. A component is
  either a leaf deal or a named group of deals. This matches the source
  document's worked example, which nests a =Vanilla Group= inside the
  structure. The document states three or more levels of containment are not
  needed, so the prototype does not offer them.
- *Widget coverage.* Four archetypes carry a real specialised widget, chosen
  to differ from each other: FX vanilla option (schedule and expiry), FX
  barrier option (barrier management), a swap or IRS (two-leg schedule and
  cashflows), and composite (leg list). Every other product type stays
  pickable and falls through to a generic form, which is what proves the
  extensibility story.
- *Version and authorisation.* The booking screen shows both, read-only. The
  structure header carries a version and an authorisation status. Editing a
  component visibly bumps the version; authorising does not.
- *Structure operations.* The screen composes a new structure, and it can also
  form a structure out of existing component trades. Unstructure, split,
  merge and lock/unlock are deferred.
- *Data.* Mock fixtures, built from the ORE example trades under
  =external/ore/examples/Products/Example_Trades=. The prototype does not
  read live services.
- *Delivery.* The prototype lives under =doc/prototypes/=, described by an org
  page. The site build copies and deploys it, so it is viewable from the
  published site.

Existing machinery the prototype must reuse rather than reinvent:

- *Audit display.* [[id:9562F9CA-DCEB-44C6-8DB1-F3FC56D7703A][ores.history]] dispatches
  by =entity_type= to one non-templated history dialog, and
  [[id:85892C17-BB81-4724-9B84-20E21D41C499][History Diff Architecture]] carries the
  design. Audit is a wiring job, not a new widget.
- *Versioning.*
  [[id:BA159E88-74EB-4401-91CC-1DE7DF5AE50A][Temporal Composite Entity Versioning: Target State]]
  already specifies that a child write bumps the parent's version in the same
  transaction, and that a parent at version $N$ composes its children by a
  temporal window join. That is precisely the structures rule "any change to a
  component increments the structure's version", so the prototype must render
  version state in that shape.
- *Valuation runs.* The [[id:98B9FE2A-57FB-464F-A4E5-198461FE6987][Compute Engine]]
  separates run configurations from execution and resolves input tokens into
  versioned snapshots. The initial NPV is the output of such a run.

The related [[id:189E3D42-2702-40B4-A08D-47E37B920ED6][Trade Blotter]] story,
in the product backlog, carries the wider target-state screen design in
[[id:3976695F-F526-4C2A-80ED-89B54854CF6D][Trade Blotter (knowledge doc)]].
This prototype feeds that story's design task; it does not replace it.

* Acceptance

- The prototype covers both screens: the booking screen and the pricing
  screen with its Book handoff.
- The booking screen manages a structure and its components, in both strategy
  mode and package mode.
- The structure panel shows two levels of deal topology, with named groups.
- A component can be any ORE product type, and the four archetype types open
  a specialised widget.
- The booking screen shows the structure version and authorisation status, and
  shows that a component edit bumps the version while authorisation does not.
- The booking screen can form a structure out of existing component trades.
- The booking screen displays the initial NPV with its as-of time and market
  data snapshot reference, read-only.
- An org page under =doc/prototypes/= describes the prototype and links to it,
  and the site build copies and deploys the prototype.
- Every decision ticket under this story is closed, or explicitly ruled out
  of scope.

* Not yet specified

These questions are in scope but not yet sharp enough to write as tasks.
Each one graduates into a task, or is dropped, as the tickets ahead of it
resolve.

- What "correct" means for the booking screen: which validation the screen
  performs, and how it presents a failure. Reference-data completeness,
  template invariants and date or schedule sanity are the candidates.
- Whether the initial NPV belongs to the structure, to each component, or to
  both, and how components aggregate into a structure value.
- Whether the prototype needs an authorisation queue view, or only the status
  field on the structure header.
- Which existing trades are eligible to be formed into a structure, and
  whether the screen enforces eligibility or only reports it.
- Whether structure stripping — a master deal plus a calendar generating $n$
  instances — belongs on the booking screen or on a separate sales wizard.

* Out of scope

- The production Qt implementation. This story produces a prototype and the
  decisions behind it, not shipped code.
- The pricing layer itself: two-way support, live market data, and the real
  valuation. The prototype fakes all three.
- The structures data model in [[id:5C7A961E-D834-4B2F-AE15-F049B27361D8][ores.trading]].
  Persistence is a separate story; this one records what the model must
  support.
- Containment deeper than two levels. The source document does not ask for it.
- The Structured Transaction Approval Request workflow.
- Confirmation and settlement at structure level.
