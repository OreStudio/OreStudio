* Goal

The CRM's drivers are hand-listed in refdata, and the identity the store holds is not consulted. Read the fixing series a party holds, group them by publisher into fixing sources, and hand the chosen source to the policy and then to topology_builder, so a party's topology follows the series it actually holds and the rank the providers carry. A series without ccy or unit_ccy is refused by name rather than skipped.

* Result

The CRM's driver pairs are no longer only a list somebody typed.
=read_fixing_sources= reads the store once per refresh: it takes every series
whose identity is an index of the FX class, groups it by publisher and by the
base that publisher quotes against, and attaches the rank the publisher
carries. =crm_ingest_bridge::refresh()= then asks the topology policy to choose,
and where a party holds fixings they decide both its topology and its pivot.
Where a party holds none, its configured pairs stand.

A series that states no =ccy= or no =unit_ccy= refuses that party by name,
quoting the series URI and both fields, rather than being skipped; and a
publisher whose series quote against two bases at once refuses the party too,
since no base would cancel. A failure to read the store at all is logged and
leaves every party on its configured pairs, so a database fault cannot stop the
fleet serving rates.

Measured: =ores.marketdata.core.tests= passes in 749 seconds and
=ores.marketdata.service.tests= in 106, which includes the montage and curve
analytics cases that build engines through =refresh()=. Both changed files were
compiled with the flags the build itself uses before the build ran, and
clang-format is clean.

What this does not yet prove, and why: no party in this environment holds both
an enabled CRM config and fixing series. The System Party holds 34 fixing
series and no config; every other party holds at most one fixing series and no
config. So the demo exercises the configured-pairs path, and the identity path
is armed but idle until configuration and data meet. That is the per-party
derivation question, and it is where this becomes visible.
