#+title: Investigation: What each asset class needs from a derivation
#+description: The series, transform, coordinates and smile each class needs before a volatility.
#+type: investigation

A point-in-time record, written on 2026-11-28 at =819a1245ee=, of what each asset class needs
before a volatility can be derived for it. It is a snapshot, not a description of the system. The
durable half -- what a class supplies to the engine -- is on the page that owns the derivation.

* Context

The platform derives a volatility from a history, because no feed offers a volatility quote. The
engine is one measurement with three per-class parameters: the transform, the coordinates a quote is
named by, and the smile model.

That leaves a question the engine cannot answer: for which asset classes does the platform hold a
history at all? A derivation with no series is not a small feature with a fake input. It is a
fabrication, and the platform must refuse it by naming the series it lacks. This survey answers the
question class by class.

Each class is judged by one of three verdicts, and the verdict is the finding: *servable now*, when
the series is held and the quote is nameable; *waits on a history*, when the quote is nameable and no
source fixes the series; and *waits on a datum*, when the key grammar cannot name the quote at all.

* Methodology

Three questions were asked of each class, in this order, because each is cheaper than the next and a
failure at any of them ends the enquiry:

1. *Is a series held?* The adapter catalogue says which public sources the release reads, the
   simulator's feed kinds say which series it generates, and the ACME dataset says which curves the
   groups are configured from.
2. *Can a quote be named?* The datum schema says whether the key grammar has an instrument type for
   the class's volatility, with the coordinates the class quotes.
3. *Is a set configured?* The refdata curve configurations say whether a surface of the class has
   anywhere to land.

* Paths taken

Each class is carried as far as the first question it fails. The table states what the engine would
need from the class, and whether it can be answered today.

| Class | Series held | Transform | Coordinates | Smile | Published set | Verdict |
|-------+-------------+-----------+-------------+-------+---------------+---------|
| FX | ECB reference spots through =frankfurter= (real, daily), the simulator's spot paths, and the forwards the curves imply | log return of the spot | pair, expiry, delta label | in delta: ATM, risk reversal, butterfly | a surface over expiry by delta, written as =fx_option= quotes | servable now, and served |
| Interest rates | 13 official rate sources (real, daily, one per official source) and the 35 yield curves' quote series (generated and stored per observation date) | difference of the rate, in basis points | currency, expiry, term, strike, payer or receiver; a cap/floor adds index and index tenor | in strike | a swaption or cap/floor set over expiry by term by strike, in normal volatility with a shift | servable now |
| Inflation | 10 inflation curves configured, and no source that fixes an index | difference of the index rate | index, term, cap or floor, strike | in strike | a zero-coupon or year-on-year cap/floor set over term by strike | waits on a history: an index-fixing adapter |
| Credit | nothing configured | difference of the spread, in basis points | entity or index, expiry, strike | in strike | a CDS volatility set | waits on a history, and on a single-name datum |
| Equity | nothing | log return of the spot | name, currency, expiry, strike, option type | in strike | a surface over expiry by strike | waits on a history |
| Commodity | nothing | log return of the forward | commodity, expiry, strike, option type | in strike | a set per delivery point, over expiry by strike | waits on a history |
| Bond | nothing | log return of the future's price | security or contract, expiry, strike | in strike | a bond future volatility set | waits on a history |

Two classes the taxonomy holds are absent from the table on purpose. =scripted= states no market
data of its own: a scripted trade observes its underlyings, so its volatility is theirs. =bond= is in
the table because the schema names a bond option and a bond future option, and ORE names
=BondFutureVolatilities= as a component of today's market, so the quote is nameable even though no
series exists.

Three of the rows need to be read carefully.

*Interest rates has many series where FX has one.* An FX pair's ladder hangs off a single series:
every expiry is a window over the same spot. A swaption's underlying is the rate at its term, so the
derivation reads the series of that term and a term the curves do not carry has no history at all.
The refusal a person reads is therefore per pillar and names the term, not the currency.

*A generated series is a history, if it is stored.* The yield curves' quotes are written per
observation date by the simulator, so after a run the store holds the term structure's own history
and the derivation can measure it. A fresh database holds none of it, and then the derivation has
nothing to read until the generator has run. The engine's refusal must say which of the two it is
looking at.

*Credit is two cases, not one.* An index CDS option is nameable today: =schema.hpp= carries
=index_cds_option= under the credit asset class. A single-name CDS option is not: the schema names the
CDS spread, the hazard rate and the recovery rate, and no option on a single name. No series exists
for either case, so credit fails on the history in both, and on the datum only for the single name.

* Conclusions

1. *Two classes can be served now.* FX is served today; interest rates can be served as soon as the
   derivation has its three parameters. Between them they cover the volatility of every trade in the
   seed population: the FX options, the swaptions and the caps.
2. *Five classes fail, and the history is the common cause.* Inflation, equity, commodity and bond
   fail on a series alone, and would pass with an adapter each. Credit fails on a series in both its
   cases, and on a single-name datum as well.
3. *Inflation is the nearest third class.* Its quotes are already nameable, ten curves are already
   configured, and the missing piece is an index-fixing adapter -- a market data task, not a
   derivation task.
4. *The transform is a log return or a difference, and the class decides which.* A price or a rate
   that stays positive takes a log return: FX, equity, commodity, bond. A rate or a spread that can be
   negative, or that is quoted in basis points, takes a difference: interest rates, inflation, credit.
   The engine takes the transform as an argument, so the class states it where it builds its returns.
5. *The coordinates differ more than the measurement does.* FX names a delta label, every other class
   names a strike. The ladder a class quotes is the ladder its surface is published on, so the
   coordinates belong to the class and the window rule does not.
6. *The derived provider is FX-named too.* The catalogue row for the =derived= provider describes
   "the FX volatility quotes our own computation states", so a second class renames that row as well
   as the code.

* Recommendations

- Serve FX and interest rates in this story. Refuse the other five by name, with the reason each one
  fails, so an operator reads "no fixing history for the index" and not "unsupported asset class".
- Make the refusal per missing input: the series, the coordinate or the datum. The distinction is
  what tells the next planner whether the work is an adapter, a table or a key grammar change.
- Take inflation as the next class after this story, as a market data task: add the index-fixing
  adapter, then the derivation's three parameters follow with nothing new. The story itself builds
  none of the other classes, by its own decision.
- Treat credit's single-name case as a datum story, because it starts with an instrument the key
  grammar cannot name. An engine that grew a credit branch before its datum would be the fault this
  story avoids.
- Do not serve equity, commodity or bond until an adapter exists. A derived quote with no history
  behind it is indistinguishable from an invented one, and the platform already states it does not
  invent quotes for a party.
- The durable half -- what a class supplies to the engine, and what the engine owns -- is not stated
  here. It is on [[id:099697A6-8526-48E0-A63D-DB3932C06468][Deriving FX volatility quotes from
  historical fixings]], which is the page that owns the derivation.
