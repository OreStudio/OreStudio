* Goal


The system builds and serves volatility surfaces as market data. A surface is calibrated from its quotes, read by pricing, and fed by a synthetic generator that starts from calibrated levels.

* Acceptance

- A surface is calibrated from its quotes, checked for arbitrage and written as a candidate with lineage.
- A second person publishes a candidate, and the published surface and its slices can be read and seen on a screen.
- A report binds to a published surface, and ORE prices an FX option from the exported grid.
- A synthetic generator publishes contributor quotes that a blueprint can calibrate from.
- The synthetic feeds start once from their calibrated levels, take a new calibration at the market day roll, and recalibrate on a schedule.

* Decisions


- *The FX forward ladder journey and prototype are signed off together* (2026-11-09). The owner chose one sign off for both. The screen starts from USD pairs, its ladder start moves near dates only, and its chart spaces tenors evenly.
- Split from [[id:3D2A2BD4-4126-4E54-9046-A9E00966D898][Support volatility surfaces]] on 2026-11-08 at sprint 29 close. The acceptance above is copied from that story; its done tasks are recorded there.
- Reviewed on 2026-11-08 at sprint 30 start. Goal rewritten, because the architecture it named is done. The tasks are two arcs: the surface and the calibration of the synthetic feeds.
- *The tasks run in dependency order, and the surface path reaches pricing* (2026-11-09). Context: three surface tasks had no goal or acceptance, and the construction note's path from a calibrated surface to a report had no tasks for the publish, the ORE export or the screens. No blueprint was seeded. Decision: the task table is in working order, the surface arc first. The arc adds the engine, split out of the calibrate task so unit tests can prove it alone, a seeded blueprint for EUR/USD, the publish, the ORE export and the screens. Consequences: the story closes only when pricing reads a surface; the first surface calibrates from the derived quotes, so the generator can follow it.
- *The calibration fits gamma, as QuantExt does* (2026-11-09). Context: the decision of 2026-11-03 set SSVI with gamma = 1/2 for the synthetic surface. QuantExt calibrates a power-law SSVI with gamma in (0, 1), and its bounds on real-shaped smiles are the ground truth this engine ports (=QuantExt/test/sviparametricvolatility.cpp= and =QuantExt/test/bfrrvolsurface.cpp= in ORE 1.8.17.0). [[https://riskhub.org/blogs/ssvi-and-surface-wide-parameterization][SSVI and Surface-Wide Parameterization]] (riskhub.org) found a power-law gamma of 0.28 on a Heston surface. Decision: the calibration engine fits rho, eta and gamma, with phi(theta) = eta * theta^(-gamma). Consequences: the no-arbitrage conditions are checked expiry by expiry, because the curvature condition depends on theta once gamma differs from 1/2. The generator's own choice of gamma is taken up in its task.

* Out of scope

- The fixings history. Years of daily fixings in every environment are
  [[id:1226D830-9285-4C45-9F5C-72F72AFC7352][Seed the public fixings history from a DQ snapshot]], a story of
  its own: this story works from the adapter's shorter window without it, with
  the priors of the derived quotes dominating and weak butterflies.
- Two-way pricing. Every quote, synthetic or real, is a mid, and valuation runs
  on mids. Bid and ask, for every input and not only vol surfaces, are a later
  level of work, taken up once mid valuation is reliable for every product.
- The rest of the scope is set by the architecture task.
