* Plan

** The question

AUD/USD stopped advancing. Three answers are possible and each leads to a
different fix: the feed does not restart with the service, the ingest loop lost
its subscription, or a third thing. The work is to tell them apart, not to guess
between them.

** What settles it

Three readings of one window. The observations table says when the last tick
landed. The ingest loop's own status says whether ticks reach the consumer. The
synthetic service's log says whether a producer publishes. If two of the three
are healthy, the third names the layer that is not.

The experiment is a restart of the marketdata service, the way the report did
it, with the newest tick read before and after. A restart that moves the tick
clears the ingest loop. A restart that leaves the tick where it was puts the
cause downstream of the bus or upstream of it, and the two service logs then say
which.

** The method

Read the newest observation of every synthetic source. Restart the marketdata
service. Read again two minutes later. Read the ingest status lines and the
producer status lines for the same minutes. Then read the code those lines point
at: the ingest loop, the boot walk of the synthetic service, the read that feeds
the walk, and the deployment of the service.

The answer goes three places: the result of this task, a note in the journey,
and the capture that owns the fix, because the fix is bigger than the time box.
The capture is found by searching the backlog before it is written, so that one
defect does not collect two of them.

** What is not in scope

The fix. It changes which configs a service reads at boot, and that choice is a
design decision with its own trade-offs, so it needs its own task. This task
records it and names it.

* Notes

** 2026-10-18 — the two questions the report separates, answered

The report offers two causes. The evidence rejects the first and confirms the
second, with a correction: the service that matters is the synthetic service,
not the marketdata service.

The ingest loop did not lose its subscription. It subscribes at start, it holds
67 bound sources, and it reports =last_tick=never= for every one of them, which
is what a consumer that is listening and hearing nothing looks like. Restarting
it changes nothing: the tick is at the same second before and after.

The feed does not restart with the service. That is the cause, and it is one
step worse than the report says: the feed does not restart with the synthetic
service either.

** 2026-10-18 — the timeline the logs give

The last AUD/USD tick is at 18:49:06 on 2026-10-17. The synthetic service
instance that was running until 11:52 today had been up for 17h 2min 1.706s, so
it started at about 18:50:01. The tick stops 55 seconds before that start, and
the shutdown of the instance it replaced is not in the journal, which keeps only
from 05:41 today. So what stopped the ticks is not established, and this record
does not claim it: the restart may have stopped them, and it may have found them
already stopped.

What the logs do establish is the part the answer needs. The instance that took
over at 18:50:01 logged =Auto-started 24 enabled feed(s)= and none of them was
AUD/USD's, so the restart did not bring the feed back. Neither did any of the
later restarts of marketdata, and the tick has not moved since.

The binding behind that source records its own creation at
=2026-10-17 17:44:03Z=, which is 18:44:03 local, and its commentary says what
made it: =Created by ACME provisioning: consumes the system-party simulated
market stream=. So the sequence is: provisioning started the Bloomberg feeds by
hand at 18:44, they ticked for five minutes, the service restarted at 18:50, and
nothing started them again.

** 2026-10-18 — why the boot walk cannot start them

The synthetic service starts feeds at boot from one walk over the config rows it
can read (=auto_start_feeds= in =ores.synthetic.service/src/app/application.cpp=).
The walk reads them through =feed_kind_registry::targets=, which reads each kind's
repository, and the repository selects
=tenant_id = ctx.tenant_id() OR tenant_id = system_tenant_id=
(=fx_spot_generation_config_repository::read_latest=). The context is built by
=context_factory=, which defaults to the system tenant when the configuration
names none, and the deployed unit passes no =--tenant=. So the walk sees the
installation's own configs, which are the system-scope sandboxed feeds, and it
cannot see a config that belongs to a tenant.

The 26 Bloomberg FX configs belong to tenant =64927db6= (Acme) and to its System
Party. The walk never reads them, so it never starts them, and the log says so by
silence: it reports =Auto-started 24= and no skip line, because a row that is not
read is not a row that is skipped.

A feed started on demand through the control plane is held only in memory, in
=feed_controller='s map of running feeds. Nothing stores that it was running.
That is why the restart is fatal and why nothing restarts it afterwards.

** 2026-10-18 — what a person sees, and what they cannot do

The binding survives, so the person sees a row that is enabled, quiet, and last
ticked seventeen hours ago. The bindings screen already says =quiet= for it,
because =statusOf= calls a binding quiet when its last tick is older than
=QUIET_AFTER_SECONDS= (600); the screen was read from its code and from the two
reads it joins, not from a browser session. The running
set does not hold the source, so the row reads quiet and not stopped. The series
page shows the newest observation and does not compare it with the clock.

What the person cannot do is start the feed behind the row. The config belongs
to the tenant's System Party, and a session acting as Acme Corporation Plc does
not see it: =synthetic list feeds= lists the 24 sandboxed feeds and
=synthetic start feed synthetic.bloomberg.audusd= answers
=No feed matching 'synthetic.bloomberg.audusd'=. Only the boot walk or a session
acting as the System Party could start it, and the boot walk cannot read it.

** 2026-10-18 — the third cause, ruled out

Ticks published while the marketdata service is down are lost, because the
ingest loop holds a plain subscription and no durable consumer. That is a real
gap and it is not this one: the tick did not fall behind, it never arrived, and
the producer published nothing at all. The synthetic log shows every running
feed publishing on =synthetic.v1.ops.sandbox_tick.>=, a subject the ingest loop
does not subscribe to by design, because a sandboxed feed is a dry run and never
writes observations.

** 2026-10-18 — the fix, and why it is not here

The walk in =auto_start_feeds= says it starts every auto-startable feed of every
kind. It cannot. It starts every auto-startable feed of the installation's own
tenant. The candidate fix is a walk that reaches every tenant's auto-start
configs, which needs a read that crosses tenants and a decision about what a
conflict between two tenants' feeds on one series means. The other candidate is
a stored running set, so a restart restores what was running.

Both are bigger than this time box and neither is proved better than the other
by this evidence, so the fix is not written here. The capture that owns it was
already in the backlog, filed on 2026-10-11, and this task added the measurement
that confirms it. See =Captures=.

* Result

The feed does not restart with the service. That is the cause, and the service
is the synthetic one, not the marketdata one.

The synthetic service starts feeds at boot from one walk over the config rows
its own database context can read, and that context is the installation's system
tenant. The 26 Bloomberg FX configs behind AUD/USD belong to the Acme tenant's
System Party, so the walk never reads them and never starts them. A feed started
on demand lives only in the producer's memory, so the restart of the synthetic
service did not restore it, and nothing has started it since. What stopped the
ticks 55 seconds before that restart is not established, because the shutdown of
the instance that published them is not in the journal. The cause below does not
depend on it.

The ingest loop is not the cause. It subscribes at start, holds its 67 bound
sources, and reports =last_tick=never= for all 287 bindings because no tick
crosses the bus. Restarting the marketdata service moves nothing: the newest
AUD/USD tick is =2026-10-17 18:49:06.55001+01= before the restart at 11:50:11
and the same value after it at 11:51:47.

What a person sees is an enabled binding that is quiet, whose last tick is
seventeen hours old. The bindings screen says quiet already. What a person can
do about it is nothing from their own session: the feed config belongs to the
tenant's System Party, and a session acting as Acme Corporation Plc is told
=No feed matching 'synthetic.bloomberg.audusd'=. The boot walk is the only thing
that starts a feed without a person, and it cannot read the row.

Acceptance is met. The cause is written above and traced to the line of code
that holds it. The journey now says what a person sees when a feed stops and
what the installation does and does not do about it. The investigation ran
inside one working session, so no open question is carried.

The fix is not in this task, and the record says why. Two candidates exist, a
boot walk that crosses tenants and a stored running set, and this evidence does
not choose between them. The capture that owns the defect was already open in
the backlog, and this task added the evidence that proves it rather than filing
a second copy.
