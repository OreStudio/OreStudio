* What it argues

Connectivity is the one place where the three things that make a feed are
joined:

1. The *providers* we configured: who owns the ids, the terms and the licence.
2. The *adapters* the code provides: the modules, their versions, their
   descriptions and the configuration each one needs.
3. The *sources* we configure from them: one adapter instance of one provider,
   with its transport, its scope of underlyings, its schedule and its route.

The words are the code's words, with one example each.

#+ATTR_HTML: :class hug-leading
| Word | Meaning | Example |
|------+---------+---------|
| /Provider/ | Whoever owns the ids, the terms and the licence. | =ecb=, =ust=, =bloomberg= |
| /Adapter/ | A code module the release installs. It has a version, a description and a configuration schema. | =frankfurter= 1.4.0; =ecb-sdw= 1.0.0 |
| /Source/ | One adapter instance of one provider, with its own transport, scope and schedule. | =ecb-frankfurter=, =ecb-ecb-data=, =bloomberg-b= |
| /Kind/ | How a source gets its values. | =live=, =test=, =simulated= |
| /Route/ | The party's one source, for one provider and one asset class. | System Party routes =ecb-frankfurter= for =ecb= and FX |
| /Scope/ | The underlyings a source fetches. No item belongs to two sources of one provider. | =ecb-frankfurter= fetches the ECB's FX items |
| /Schedule/ | The scheduler job that asks the adapter to fetch a source, and its rhythm. | =marketdata.refresh.ecb-frankfurter=, daily 16:00 CET |

The screen has three lists and two records.

* The lists

| Tab | What it draws |
|-----+---------------|
| Providers | The configured providers: kind, classes, terms state, number of sources, credential reference |
| Adapters | Every adapter the release installs: version, description, classes, the fields a source must set, and the sources and providers that use it |
| Sources | Every configured source: provider, adapter, kind, classes, scope, schedule, runtime state |

The Adapters tab is read-only. An adapter is code; the release installs the row.
A person adds a source that uses one.

* The records

A provider record has Overview, Sources and History. It shows the terms and the
credential reference every source inherits, its sources, and the adapters that
fit its classes but have no source yet. From here a person adds a source.

A source record has seven tabs. Each tab answers one question.

#+ATTR_HTML: :class hug-leading
| Tab | The question |
|-----+--------------|
| Overview | What is this source: provider, adapter, kind, default, classes? |
| Transport | What does the adapter need: endpoint, path, settings, credential reference? The fields come from the adapter's schema. |
| Underlyings | What does it fetch? All items of a class, or a chosen set. No item belongs to two sources of one provider. |
| Schedule | Which scheduler job fetches it, at what rhythm, and when did it last run? |
| Runtime | What does the service see: state, heartbeat, ids served, refused and unknown, last tick? |
| Routes | Which party reads it, for which class? One route per party, provider and class. |
| History | Who changed it, and when? |

* Adding a source

=??add=1&provider=ecb= draws the four steps of the initial setup: pick the
provider and the adapter, fill the transport from the adapter's schema, set the
scope of underlyings, then set the schedule and the route. The adapter list is
filtered to the provider's classes.

* What is fake

Every endpoint, credential, version, count and date is invented. Secrets are
masks only; nothing is configured or written. No request leaves the page.

* Where the border is

Connectivity owns the editable facts: who the provider is, what the adapter can
do, and what the source fetches, over which transport, on which schedule and for
which party. Public rates owns the observed facts: the values, their coverage,
their provenance and whether they arrive. A person who reads a value follows its
source here to see where it came from; a person who wants to change the feed does
it here.
