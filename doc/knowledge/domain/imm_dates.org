* Summary

Two different sets of quarterly dates trade under the name "IMM date", and
they are not the same dates. This page states both, because the difference
matters to the resolver, and records which one ORE Studio models.

| Convention | Dates | Used by |
|------------+-------+---------|
| True IMM date | The *third Wednesday* of March, June, September, December — between the 15th and the 21st | Exchange-traded money-market and FX futures and options (Eurodollar, Short Sterling) |
| CDS roll date | *20 March, 20 June, 20 September, 20 December*, exactly, weekend or not | Credit default swap termination dates and quarterly premium dates |

The second set is the one usually meant by "IMM date" in a credit context. It
is not the IMM date at all: it falls close to it, so the market calls it that
by abuse of language. The difference is visible in almost any quarter — in
March 2027 the third Wednesday is the 17th, while the CDS roll date is the
20th, a Saturday.

* Which one this project models, and why

The resolver's =ROLL_QUARTER= schedule models the *CDS roll date*: the 20th of
the quarter month, exactly, with no business-day adjustment. Its only consumer
is =CREDIT_CDS_IMM=, a credit convention, and the CDS rule is the one that
underpins it. The absence of a business-day adjustment is the point of the
rule rather than an omission: CDS standardisation fixes one termination date
per series so that every contract in the series matures on the same day, and
the date is allowed to fall on a weekend. Advancing it to the next business
day would resolve two contracts in one series to different dates.

The true IMM date — the third Wednesday — is *not* modelled. Exchange-traded
STIR futures, which use it, have no resolver schedule today. Adding one means
a second schedule alongside =ROLL_QUARTER=, not a change to it, because the
two rules disagree in most quarters. See the gap noted under "Uses" below.

* Detail

** Definition and notation

The true IMM date is the third Wednesday of March, June, September, or
December. The CDS roll date is the 20th of those months, without adjustment.
Notation =nIMM= refers to rolling forward by n quarters (e.g. =IMM3= is three
roll dates forward), and it inherits the same ambiguity: in a credit
document it counts 20ths, and in a futures document it counts third
Wednesdays. Unlike a standard
[[id:0AC88EB3-DB7F-4135-9DA6-0ED4583FEC29][tenor]], neither date is
computed relative to a [[id:6857871D-CFDE-4D9F-A83A-4A84CD3F4448][horizon
date]] by calendar arithmetic — each is one of a fixed set of four dates per
year, and "the next roll date" or "IMM3 from here" is a lookup into that
fixed set, not an arithmetic offset.

** The roll schedule in the resolution model

In the unified tenor model (decided 2026-08-09 — see
[[id:0AC88EB3-DB7F-4135-9DA6-0ED4583FEC29][Tenor]]'s "How a tenor
resolves" section), the CDS roll dates are the =ROLL_QUARTER= schedule:
*closed-form*, derivable by rule (the 20th of the quarter month, exactly),
so the dates stay code-side in the resolver rather than being seeded as
data. A CDS tenor resolves under =SCHEDULE_STEP= as "anchor + calendar
offset, then n steps along ROLL_QUARTER": =1Y 1RQ= is one year offset
plus one roll step. The old =IMM_ROLL= algorithm row is superseded —
=CREDIT_CDS_IMM= migrates to =SCHEDULE_STEP=; only the =IMM_ROLL=
*anchor* name survives, as a =measured_from= value. The code model is
documented in
[[id:0220F3F7-C674-47EE-91CE-1C04804B4558][Tenor and Curve
Bootstrapping Architecture]].

** Uses

- As an alternative bucketing scheme for position reporting, alongside
  standard monthly [[id:0AC88EB3-DB7F-4135-9DA6-0ED4583FEC29][tenor]]
  buckets. This use wants the true IMM dates, so it is not served by
  =ROLL_QUARTER= today.
- In CDS curves, where all standard tenors resolve relative to the nearest
  following CDS roll date rather than to the horizon date directly — see
  [[id:0AC88EB3-DB7F-4135-9DA6-0ED4583FEC29][Tenor]]'s Credit/CDS Curves
  convention, which this fixed roll-date set underpins. This is the use
  =ROLL_QUARTER= serves.

** Gap: the true IMM date has no schedule

Exchange-traded STIR futures settle on the true IMM date — the third
Wednesday — and
[[id:E808F432-2FDA-47E8-B003-1E8B908870FE][Interest Rate Curves]]'s
Short-Term Interest Rate (STIR) Futures section has the nearest IMM quarterly
contract discounted back to fill the =6M=--=2Y= gap. Nothing in the resolver
computes that date today, and =ROLL_QUARTER= cannot stand in for it: the two
rules disagree in most quarters. A futures IMM schedule is a second schedule
alongside =ROLL_QUARTER=, and it is not built.
