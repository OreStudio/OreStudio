* Goal

The journeys say how a risk or reference data administrator reads and changes
the product approval matrix, at the party and at the book, and every step of
them is judged against the record screen standard: what the standard already
draws, what a kind's own action draws, what no rule draws yet, and which server
operation is missing. The judgement is what the prototype may build on, so it
comes first and it stops for the owner's sign off.

* Acceptance

- The journeys exist under the parties and books groups, or under a group of their own, and state the goal of each before the steps.
- Every step carries a judgement: standard record screen, a kind's own action, a view no rule draws yet, a missing server operation, or a workflow.
- Each gap in the journey links to its capture, and the =* Prototype= section names the placements the prototype must decide.
- The owner signs the journeys off, and their decisions are recorded on the journeys, this task and the story.

* Plan

The journey starts from the person's goal and not from the entity, so it is one
journey over two record pages rather than a screen per table: the party's set
is the maximum, the book's narrows it, and a person approves both in one
sitting. The steps were walked against the record screen standard, and each one
is either a rule the standard already carries or a placement the prototype must
settle. Two of the placements turned out to be server gaps rather than design
ones, and each is a capture.

The group is =parties= and not a new one, because the party owns the approval and
the person reaches the book from it (J06). The book's side links back through
the books group's journey.
