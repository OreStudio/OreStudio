* Goal

A workflow step calls a service over the bus, and every one of those requests
takes the same 30-second deadline, whatever work it asks for. A provisioning run
failed at step 13 of 15 because one calibration took longer than that on a
loaded host. The step had already published 9 of its 10 surfaces. The run failed
on the first timeout, with no retry, and the only thing it reported was
=NATS request timed out=.

After this story a step's requests are bounded to the work they ask for. A
transient failure is retried with backoff and jitter inside the step's own
budget, and only when repeating the request changes nothing. A refusal fails at
once. The service bounds the work one request may do, so a calibration costs
what its blueprint costs and not what the whole catalogue costs. A step reports
its progress while it runs, and both ores.shell and ores.web show that progress
and name the work that failed, with its reason.

The focus is the requests a workflow step makes. This is not a general retry
framework for every call site, and not the whole audit of the NATS patterns.

* Acceptance

- Every request a provisioning step makes either takes the shared default
  deliberately or states a deadline sized to its work, and the reason is in the
  code beside it.
- A transient failure is retried with backoff and jitter inside the step's
  remaining budget, and every attempt is logged with its number and its reason.
- A refusal is never retried, and no request is retried unless repeating it
  leaves the store unchanged.
- A step that retries and then succeeds completes. A step that still cannot
  finish fails with a message that names the work, not just the transport.
- The service bounds the work one request may do, and a request that cannot
  finish inside its deadline answers with a reason.
- A running step reports its progress, and the progress read answers the latest
  line while the step is still in progress.
- ores.shell and ores.web both show that progress, and name the failing work and
  its reason when the run stops.
- A test drives a step against a responder that answers late and one that
  refuses, and proves the retry, the refusal, the progress and the failure
  message.

* Decisions

- Retry only a transient transport failure, and only where repeating the
  request changes nothing. A refusal is never retried, and neither is a request
  whose second attempt would write a second row.
- A deadline is stated against the work, in one place per class of request, so
  no call site reinvents the number. The shared 30-second default stays the
  deadline for a read or a handshake.
- The step's remaining budget is the outer bound of its retries, so a retry
  cannot outlive the step the engine judges.
- Progress travels through the progress read both clients already poll, rather
  than through a new channel. A client that polls late still sees the lines.
- The responder bounds its own work by what the request is about, not by a cap
  the caller imposes.
- A failure names the work: the party, the surface or pair, the attempt and the
  reason. The transport string alone is not a report.

* Out of scope

- An asynchronous calibrate that accepts the work and answers with an id to
  poll. It is the fallback if the bound on the responder's work cannot meet a
  sane deadline, and it would be its own story.
- Retrying every request at the NATS client for every caller. Only the helper
  the workflow steps use changes.
- The engine's dispatch, delivery and claim retries, which are already built.
- Rewriting the provisioning steps that are not requests: the trade generator,
  the calendars and the bundles keep their current shape.
