#+title: Binding mode
#+description: The bound and sandboxed modes, the subjects each one publishes on, and why a sandboxed feed is unreachable by ingest.
#+type: knowledge
#+level: cross
#+filetags: :marketdata:feeds:binding:synthetic:
#+created: 2026-10-06
#+updated: 2026-10-06

* Summary

A =binding_mode= says whether generated market data is authoritative or
exploratory. It is declared in =ores.synthetic.api/domain/binding_mode.hpp=
with two values, =bound= and =sandboxed=, and it is carried on
=market_data_generation_config.binding_mode=.

* Detail

** What each mode does

- A =bound= feed publishes on ="synthetic.v1.tick.<source>"=, the subjects
  the marketdata ingest loop subscribes to. Its ticks are stored and
  republished to the parties whose bindings name the source.
- A =sandboxed= feed publishes under ="synthetic.v1.sandbox.tick."=, a
  prefix the ingest loop never subscribes to. Its ticks cannot be stored
  whatever bindings exist.

The string form of the enum is lower case, and the two spellings match the
SQL check constraint on the config column and the JSON wire format.

** Why a sandboxed feed is unreachable

Reachability is a property of the subject and not of a flag that ingest
checks. The ingest loop's subscription covers the bound prefix only, so a
sandboxed tick is never delivered to a handler. This is also why the feed
controller never creates a binding for a sandboxed feed. A binding naming a
sandboxed source would claim that ingestion happens on the bound subject
when it does not.

** The fact the code shape does not show

Binding mode is orthogonal to scope. Binding mode decides whether the
generated data is authoritative, and scope decides who consumes it. A
party scoped sandboxed feed and a party scoped bound feed differ in
whether their data is ever stored, and not in who the data is meant for.
