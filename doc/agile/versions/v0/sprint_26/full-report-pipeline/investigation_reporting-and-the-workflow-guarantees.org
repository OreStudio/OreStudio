A point-in-time record, written while
[[id:AB57C960-7E15-4D02-88AE-CEA931B6F9A3][Run a full report: scheduling, reporting and compute]]
was open, of an
[[id:A7F8D9E2-3C4B-4A5D-B67C-8E9F0A1B2C3D][investigation]] into whether the
reporting component uses the guarantees the workflow engine now offers it, and
into whether the three documentation clusters that describe the subject agree
with the code.

It is a snapshot, not a description of the system. Whatever it concludes that
the code must keep doing belongs on the page that owns the subject; the pages
below were corrected during this work, and they, not this page, are what later
readers should cite.

* Context

The reporting component was recently brought to the standard of its sibling
components, and the workflow engine recently gained error handling, compensation,
restart recovery and name-addressed step results. Two questions followed.

1. Does reporting use what the engine now offers, or does it work around it?
2. Do the reporting, scheduling and workflow pages still describe the code?

The second question matters more than it looks. Three clusters describe the same
run from three angles, and a contradiction between them is invisible to a reader
who reads only one. A page that states a rule the code no longer follows is worse
than a missing page, because it is the page a reader finds first.

* Methodology

Four read-only surveys ran in parallel against the tree at =d3325300d5=, each
with a different remit: the reporting and report-processing documentation, the
scheduling documentation, the workflow documentation together with the engine's
error handling, and every enumeration of report types in the repository. Each
survey cited the code it read. Every claim that this page acts on was then
verified directly in the tree, by reading the file and line named.

The claims verified in the code, rather than taken from a survey, were: the two
chain shapes and the step names in =report_execution_workflow.hpp=; the
configuration columns on =report_definition.hpp=; the reporting to compute
handoff in =report_submit_handler.cpp=; the dispatcher in
=workunit_dispatcher.cpp=; the batch close and bridge in
=result_submit_handler.hpp= and =batch_workflow_bridge.hpp=; the absent
idempotency check, warning answer and deadline in =ores.reporting=; and the
scheduler trigger path in =nats_publish_action_handler.cpp=.

* Paths taken

** The reporting documentation is missing, which is false

The expectation was that the reporting pages would need writing. They exist: a
structure note and nine pages under
[[proj:doc/knowledge/architecture][=doc/knowledge/architecture/=]], plus three
PlantUML diagrams. The cluster is more complete than the scheduling and workflow
ones. The work was therefore correction, not authoring.

** The report execution workflow has a page, which is also false

The [[proj:doc/knowledge/workflows][=doc/knowledge/workflows/=]] folder holds
pages for the identity and tenant-provisioning workflows only. The reporting
workflow, which is the largest registered definition, had no page and no row in
the workflow inventory. That page was written during this work.

** The reports have no catalogue, which is false

[[id:04C68B55-E0B5-41CD-B90E-D670C17530B6][Risk reporting]] carries
22 market risk reports, each with five classification axes, a purpose and its
measures, plus a ten-report end-of-day batch. What it does not carry is anything
per report that a reader can act on: no audience in a role's terms, no ORE XML,
no parameters. That is the gap the report document type fills.

** One list of reports, which becomes three

The search for the report list found three enumerations that do not agree: 22
business reports on the risk reporting page, 31 operational definitions in
=reporting_report_definitions_populate.sql=, and 35 ORE analytic types in the
schema-derived taxonomy under =.audit/ore-taxonomy/=. The repository has been
treating "the reports" as one list when it is three, at three levels of
abstraction. The report inventory now states which is which.

** A phase list that is not a step list

The pipeline page decomposes a run into six phases. The workflow implements seven
steps, or four when pre-processing is substituted. The two decompositions do not
map one to one in either direction: pre-processing is four steps rather than one,
and retrieval and post-processing are one step rather than two. A reader who
learns the six phases cannot predict the chain, which is why the pipeline page now
carries the mapping.

* Conclusions

** Reporting does not use three of the engine's guarantees

The engine offers a consumer more than reporting takes. Three gaps are material.

1. *Idempotency.* No handler in =ores.reporting= calls the engine's idempotency
   check, and =assemble_bundle= generates a fresh bundle identifier on every
   invocation
   (=projects/ores.reporting/core/src/messaging/report_execution_handler.cpp:295=).
   A step re-dispatched after a restart therefore does its work a second time: a
   second bundle row, a second export, a second upload. The engine's own record
   keeps the run from moving twice, which is why a green run never shows it. This
   is the most serious of the three, because it is a correctness defect that
   recovery creates, and the reporting pages already state the convergence
   obligation the code does not meet.
2. *Warnings.* No handler answers =completed_with_warnings=; =warn()= is never
   called. A phase that partly succeeded must today answer either completed or
   failed, and answer failed when it means "completed, with a caveat". The engine
   does not compensate a warned step, so adopting warnings is a behaviour change
   and not only a cosmetic one.
3. *Deadlines.* The engine has no step timeout, and no reporting phase arms one.
   A phase whose service never answers leaves the run in progress indefinitely,
   re-dispatched on every restart. The engine's guarantee here is an absence, and
   reporting inherits it.

** Compensation is used, but it is a terminal transition rather than an undo

Every one of the eight steps declares the same compensation subject, the message
that marks the report instance failed. Compensation therefore collapses to a
single first-writer-wins transition, and what a completed phase produced stays
where it is. That is a defensible design for a report run, where the phases
produce shared objects rather than private state. It was not stated anywhere, and
two pages implied the opposite by describing compensation as an undo. Both now
say what it does.

** The documentation contradicted the code in four places

| Page | Claim | Reality |
|------+-------+---------|
| =report_execution_pipeline.org= | a run is six phases, with no chain selection | the run configuration selects two chains, of seven steps and four |
| =report_execution_orchestration.org= | the phase builder ignores its three inputs | it reads the run configuration and throws on one it cannot build |
| =scheduling_and_workflows.org= | the scheduler publishes =workflow.v1.start= itself | the scheduler calls the reporting trigger, and reporting is the only publisher of the start |
| =scheduling_and_workflows.org= | the run's identity comes from the firing | the trigger carries the firing as the instance's trigger run; the run's identity is generated when the instance is created |

All four are corrected. The first two were stale text from before the run
configuration landed. The last two described a design that the reporting route
does not follow, and the page now states both routes rather than one.

** The engine has no failure policy, so no consumer can declare one

=workflow_definition= carries a type name, a description and a step builder.
There is no policy field, and the compensate-or-stop choice the workflow
knowledge pages describe is documentation rather than a mechanism. Every
registered workflow compensates, because that is the only behaviour the engine
has. A page that asks a workflow to declare its policy asks for a field that does
not exist.

* Recommendations

1. *Use the engine's idempotency check in the reporting handlers.* It is the
   highest-value fix in this cluster, because it turns a class of silent
   double-work into a checked one. It belongs to
   [[id:CA025E1C-CA41-42C1-92BB-F8E723E744E2][Define and enforce the orchestration contract]],
   which owns the phase contract, and is tasked as
   [[id:4171EE8C-47A0-4641-A26A-510B29689BAE][Use the engine's idempotency check in the reporting handlers]].
2. *Decide the deadline question rather than leaving it implicit.* Either give a
   step a deadline, or state that a silent phase requires a person. Today the
   behaviour is the second, and no page said so.
3. *Decide whether any phase answers with warnings.* Until one does, the engine's
   warning path is dead code for reporting, and the pages should not imply it is
   available.
4. *Keep the three report lists distinct.* Do not merge the business catalogue,
   the seeded definitions and the ORE analytic types. State which is authoritative
   for what, and let a report page be where the three meet for one report.
5. *Give a workflow a failure policy only when the engine has one.* Until then,
   the workflow pages should state the engine's single behaviour instead of asking
   each workflow to declare a choice it cannot make.
