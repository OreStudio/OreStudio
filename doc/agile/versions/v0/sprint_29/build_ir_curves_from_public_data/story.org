* Goal

Every currency the synthetic IR feeds support has a published overnight-index curve in the system tenant, bootstrapped from that currency's quotes, and those quotes start from the currency's public overnight rate. The FX volatility surface reads the forward to each expiry from the published curves of both currencies of a pair, so the eight currencies of the derived FX pairs (AUD, CAD, CHF, EUR, GBP, JPY, NZD and USD) come first; the remaining twenty of the twenty-eight follow.

* Decisions

** A provisioning run publishes curves, and a person does not approve them

A curve this story publishes is the system's baseline, bootstrapped from the
quotes its own feeds publish, and a fresh environment must hold it before any
person arrives. So the provisioning step calls the approval-gated
=publish_curve= and the request closes at once, with the system as its decider,
because the curve publish kind states through =auto_approval_code= that a raise
in the system tenant -- the installation acting on its own behalf -- needs
nobody. The maker and checker pair stays where it pays: a raise in a tenant is
somebody's book, a desk's candidate and the tenant's own party's curve no less,
and it still waits for Market Risk. The policy is data of the inbox's, with a
deployment switch beside it, so whoever reads the kinds or the requests sees
what is enforced and what is not. See
[[id:619AF477-DA8F-424F-8722-E1058F60FD68][Model auto-approval as a stated policy]]
and [[id:317DE09B-BE7B-4D47-B210-9D64B4558A97][Bootstrap and publish the curves in the provisioning system run]].

The synthetic IR feeds cover 28 currencies: AUD BRL CAD CHF CLP CNH CNY COP CZK DKK EUR GBP HKD HUF ILS INR JPY KRW MXN NOK NZD PLN SEK SGD TRY TWD USD ZAR. Every one starts from a fixed level: the calibration policies cover FX alone.

Acme held 82 bootstrap configs, per-party copies of one USD SOFR curve across 79 parties, and 75 curve publishes. Every publish was written by the integration tests, not by the system: random user names and an as-of of 2026-03-02.

** Why this comes before the FX volatility surface

A surface's strike labels resolve to strikes through the pair's delta and ATM conventions and the forward to each expiry, which the compute reads from the published curves of the two currencies. The derived FX pairs need the curves of AUD, CAD, CHF, EUR, GBP, JPY, NZD and USD, and none is published.

* Out of scope

- The FX volatility surface itself, which reads these curves: [[id:3D2A2BD4-4126-4E54-9046-A9E00966D898][Support volatility surfaces]].
- Projection curves of term rates such as EURIBOR, beyond what the LIBOR decision needs.
- Per-party curves in a tenant: this story publishes the system tenant's curves.
