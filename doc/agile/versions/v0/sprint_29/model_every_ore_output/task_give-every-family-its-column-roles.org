* Acceptance

- Each family states =key_columns=, =qualifier_columns=, =measure_columns= and whether its measures are open.
- A column every variant of a family states is required; a column only some variants state is a qualifier another analytic added or an open measure, and the family says which.
- A configuration-driven family states its measures as open, so a curve, factor or quantile the samples do not show is still a measure.
- The catalogue's test fails when a family's required columns disagree with a sample it covers.

* Plan

The corpus states what a column holds and the writers state what it is for.
Every column gets exactly one role: a key, a qualifier or a measure.

The derivation is the corpus's own evidence. A column that every header variant
of a family states is required, and a key is required by definition, so a name
only some variants state is never a key. A text, date or time column that is not
a key states context, so it is a qualifier; so does a number that names a
position rather than a figure, such as =ShiftSize_1=, =MaturityTime= or
=DateIndex=. Every other numeric column is a measure.

A family's measures are open when its variants disagree on which measures they
state, because that is what a column filled from the configuration looks like:
=curves.csv= states one column for each curve the run built, =var.csv= one for
each quantile requested, =dim_regression= one for each regressor. A reader can
then accept a measure it has not seen in that family and refuse one in a family
whose measures are closed.

The roles are part of the generated data and the API exposes them through
=role_of=. A test proves they partition the columns: no column without a role,
no column with two, and the three lists together are the family's columns.

* Notes

The three causes of a variant, from the corpus: configuration columns (curves, dim regression, var, scenario), analytic augmentation (exposure under stress or sensitivity) and a stale expected file (the 25-column =flows.csv= in MinimalSetup is a subset of the writer's 27).

* Notes

- The three roles come from the corpus, not from a hand-written table, so a
  family added tomorrow states its own roles. The one judgement that is not the
  corpus's is which numbers name a position rather than a figure: =ShiftSize_1=,
  =ShiftSize_2=, =MaturityTime=, =DateIndex=, =Depth= and =TimeStep= are
  qualifiers by name, and the list is one constant in the generator.
- =Currency= is a qualifier, not a measure, because it states what a figure is
  in rather than being one. The reader is expected to promote a column named
  exactly =Currency= to the metric's currency.
- Openness is derived: a family is open when its variants disagree on their
  measures. That finds the five families a writer fills from the configuration
  where the samples show more than one shape. It cannot find a configuration
  column in a family the samples only ever show one shape of, so a reader that
  refuses an unknown measure must say so rather than guess.

* Result

Every family states the job of each of its columns. The catalogue reports 112
keys, 240 qualifiers and 1 232 measures over the same 1 584 columns, so the
roles partition what the catalogue already named, and five families say their
measures are open: curves, dim_evolution, dim_regression, stress_scenarios and
var.

The suite proves the partition rather than trusting it: no column without a
role, no column with two, and the three lists together equal the family's
columns. It also proves a key is always a required column, and that npv states
NPV and Notional as measures while Maturity and NpvCurrency are qualifiers.

Deriving the roles turned up two facts worth keeping. A column that only some
variants state cannot be a key, which is why the keys come from the required
set and not from the union of the samples. And openness can only be seen where
the samples show more than one shape, so a family that gains a configuration
column in a shape the corpus never shows reads as closed; the reader that
refuses an unknown measure must say what it refused rather than guess.
