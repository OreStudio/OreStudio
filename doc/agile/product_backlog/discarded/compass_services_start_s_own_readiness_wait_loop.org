#+title: compass services start's own readiness poll is far slower than systemd's own Type=notify tracking
#+description: compass services start's own readiness-wait loop (the '22 unit(s) (Service ready)...' progress bar) is the actual bottleneck, taking well over a minute even though systemd itself already reports every unit active/running within 1-5s (units are Type=notify with NotifyAccess=all, so systemd already tracks real readiness via sd_notify). compass appears to run its own separate readiness check (NATS ping/heartbeat polling?) redundant with what systemd already knows, rather than just trusting 'systemctl is-active'/'systemctl --user show -p ActiveState,SubState' or waiting on the units' own Type=notify readiness signal. Should rely on systemd's own readiness tracking instead of a slow independent poll. This page is a [[id:671F18E4-E09C-4B3B-BD24-D33DF8AE38A6][capture]] in the *discarded* bucket of the product backlog — a pre-sprint idea that was triaged and not pursued.

* What

=compass services start='s readiness-wait loop (the '22 unit(s) (Service
ready)...' progress bar) was observed taking well over a minute even
though systemd itself already reported every unit active/running
within 1-5s. The suspicion was that compass ran its own separate
readiness poll redundant with systemd's own =Type=notify= tracking,
and should instead trust =systemctl is-active=/=ActiveState= or the
unit's own readiness signal.

* Why

Discarded on review: the fleet has since fully migrated onto systemd
units driven via =systemctl --user= (=compass_services.py=), and
readiness there is already log-content based (=_wait_for_logs=
watching for "Service ready", falling back to =systemctl is-active=
for units with no readiness log) -- not a separate heartbeat/NATS
poll racing systemd's own tracking as this capture suspected. The
premise no longer matches the current implementation; if a real
slowness re-surfaces post-migration it should be re-captured fresh
against the current code, not resurrected from here.
