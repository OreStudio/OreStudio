* Goal

Today every feed belongs to a party, under that party's one Synthetic folder, and the FX feed screen lists a party's FX feeds flat. That fits a test feed a quant builds, and it does not fit the market a desk reads: Bloomberg, Refinitiv, ICE, CME, LSE and brokers publish what every tenant sees. Make a provider a system concept, let the installation run provider feeds once and let tenants and parties consume them through feed bindings, keep test feeds in a tenant or party, and give feeds one place to browse: a tree by scope, provider and feed kind, with run status rolled up at every node, that stays fast with many thousands of feeds, order books among them. The FX price feed screen becomes the leaf editor for one kind inside that tree.

* Acceptance

- A provider is a system-level record: a code, a name, its kind (data vendor, exchange, broker), its logo, and the asset classes it covers; the installation is seeded with the main vendors and exchanges.
- A feed has a scope: a system feed is owned by the installation under one provider and read by every tenant without being writable by it; a test feed is owned by a tenant or a party, as feeds are today.
- System feeds are organised provider, then feed kind (FX spot, IR curves, and later surfaces and order books), then folders the provider's data needs; test feeds keep their collections.
- A tenant or party subscribes to a provider's feeds through feed bindings, from a screen that shows what is available, what it consumes, and since when.
- Market data has one feed browser: a tree of System feeds by provider and My test feeds, run status rolled up at every node (running, partial, stopped), start and stop at any node by the folder cascade, and the kind's list and feed page for the node chosen.
- Every read scales: the tree loads one level at a time with counts from the server, every list is paged, searched and sorted on the server, and no screen reads all feeds; the design is checked against tens of thousands of feeds.
- The feed screen standard states the browser, the two scopes and the subscription, and the FX price feed screen conforms.
- Every path a feed is known by marks it as synthetic: its source name, its tick subject, its folder path and the provider name a screen shows.

* Decisions

- *Two scopes, one feed shape.* A system feed and a test feed are the same
  record with the same process and the same screens; the scope decides who
  owns it, who may write it, and who may read it. The FX price feed screen
  needs no second editor.
- *Providers are system data.* A provider (Bloomberg, Refinitiv, ICE, CME,
  LSE, a broker) is reference data of the installation, in the system tenant,
  as the other shared catalogues are. Its feeds run in the system tenant,
  once, and publish to the bus once.
- *Consumption is a binding, not a copy.* A tenant or party that wants a
  provider's FX spot does not get a copy of the feed; it gets a feed binding
  onto the provider's source, which the market data ingest already honours:
  the tick is stored once per enabled binding, under the binding's tenant and
  party. This is the consistent-world model the bindings were built for, and
  it is why
  [[id:78F38558-7F15-47BF-BF57-F2C04B41C4F5][A feed binding's existence claim is keyed on the ORE key alone]]
  and [[id:3CD588CB-1B74-40BA-896F-75CFF8157EAF][Name a feed by its oresmd URI]]
  come first: many parties will bind one source.
- *Organised by provider, then kind, then the provider's own folders.* The
  folder hierarchy stays the mechanism, and the system tenant's root is
  /System feeds/ with one folder per provider. Under a provider, a folder per
  feed kind (FX spot, IR curves, later surfaces and order books), then what
  the kind needs, such as a pair group or an exchange segment. A tenant's
  test feeds keep the party root and its collections.
- *One browser, kind screens as leaves.* Market data gets a feed browser: a
  tree on the left (System feeds by provider, My test feeds), the chosen
  node's feeds on the right through the kind's list, and the kind's feed page
  when one is opened. The FX price feed list and page become that leaf, and
  the per-kind cards under Market data become entry points into the tree.
- *One way to set up feeds.* Feeds come from a synthetic feeds dataset (DQ
  artefact type =synthetic_feeds=), published by one function into the party
  provisioning names; the "theme" was that dataset and is renamed to it. A feed
  row names its provider, and its source name follows the provider. Until the
  services read system collections, a provider's feeds are published as a
  party collection like any other.
- *Built for scale from the first screen.* Order books and wide provider
  catalogues mean tens of thousands of feeds. The tree loads one level at a
  time and asks the server for each node's counts and run roll-up; every list
  is paged, searched and sorted on the server; start and stop act on a node
  through the folder cascade the service already has; the running read and
  the tick buffers are checked at that size. No screen reads all feeds.
- *Every path says the feed is synthetic.* A simulated Bloomberg feed must
  never be read as Bloomberg. Every name a feed is known by starts with the
  synthetic mark: the source name (=synthetic.bloomberg.fx.eurusd=), the tick
  subject (=synthetic.v1.ops.tick.synthetic.bloomberg.fx.eurusd=), the folder
  path (/Synthetic feeds › Bloomberg › FX spot/), and the provider as a
  screen names it (/Bloomberg (synthetic)/). A real provider connection added
  later uses its own namespace and cannot collide.
- *Source names carry the provider.* After the synthetic mark, a system feed's
  source is qualified by its provider and a test feed's by its party, so two
  scopes never claim one source.

- *A system collection is the system tenant's.* It belongs to the system
  tenant and its system party, the party the super administrator works in,
  as every other shared catalogue does. The earlier null-tenant reading of
  system scope could hold no feeds, because a feed must share its
  collection's tenant.
- *One bound provider per pair, for now.* The collection's conflict guard
  stays: one enabled, bound system collection at a time. So the first step
  creates the providers and binds one of them, chosen arbitrarily, as the
  authoritative source of a pair such as EUR/USD; the other providers'
  collections run sandboxed. The second step is a rate blending service that
  reads the providers' spots and owns the blended EUR/USD itself.

* Open questions

- Does a provider feed's process belong to the installation administrator
  only, or may a tenant tune a private copy of a provider feed as a test feed?
- Is a tenant's subscription one binding per feed, or one per provider and
  kind with the feeds under it implied?
- Do order books share the feed record and the process editor, or are they a
  kind with its own model (levels, depth, events)?

* Out of scope

- Real connections to Bloomberg, Refinitiv or an exchange. These are
  simulated providers.
- Order book feeds themselves; this story makes the organisation and the
  browser ready for them.
- Entitlements and billing of provider data per tenant.
