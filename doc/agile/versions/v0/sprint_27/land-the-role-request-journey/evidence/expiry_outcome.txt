What the sweep left behind, read from the store after the 01:15 firing.

The walk is read by the request id the ask printed, not by "the newest role
request": the lifecycle suite raises requests of its own and sweeps them, and
the sweep reaches every tenant, so after a suite run the newest role request in
the table is the suite's and this walk's may already be closed by it. Run as:
compass sql -- -f expiry_walk_check.sql

Two things the walk establishes.

The request closed on the scheduled path. Nobody decided it and nobody
withdrew it: the scheduler fired the job at 01:15:00, the job published the
inbox operation, and the row now states what the decide path states --
system.update, with the commentary that gives the same reason. The version went
from 2 to 3, so the backdate and the close are both in its history.

The person who asked was told. The notice was raised at 01:15:00.164, about a
sixth of a second after the fire, so the firing is what raised it and nothing
else did; it names the kind as a person reads it ("Role request") and not the
code the row holds, and it is addressed to the asker.

The 01:10 firing ran before the request was backdated, so it closed nothing.
That is why the check reads two firings and one outcome.

The walk does not establish that a decider arriving after the sweep is
refused, because no decider arrived. That rule is the decide path's, and the
lifecycle suite covers it.
