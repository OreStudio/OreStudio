* Goal

The ACME curve configurations name their quotes with ORE keys that are based on tenors. The synthetic feeds name their series by date based pillar keys, and another environment is refactoring the feed names to a URI form. A curve whose quotes do not match a series cannot be built. When the feed refactor lands, compare the quote keys of every ACME curve configuration with the feed series, list the mismatches, and fix the side that is wrong.

* Plan

1. Read the naming rule of the refactored feeds.
2. List the quote keys of every ACME curve configuration.
3. Match each key to a feed series and list the keys that have none.
4. Fix the side that is wrong, and record the rule in a knowledge page.

What was done, in that order:

1. The rule the refactor left is the one
   [[id:7AE57098-08D4-4D4A-83D6-918FE520B68E][A pillar names the market series it reads]] states: an oresmd URI is the
   identity and an ORE key is a boundary spelling. The pair that turns one into
   the other was never written down for a curve configuration's quotes, so it is
   now a section of that page.
2. The keys are the 420 rows of =ores_refdata_curve_quotes_tbl= a provision
   publishes, and they are all of one form: =IR_SWAP/RATE/<ccy>/<start>/<index
   tenor>/<term>=.
3. The match is a function, not a query run once:
   =ores_marketdata_curve_pillar_series_uri_fn(quote_text)= states the series
   address a quote names, and =ores_marketdata_curve_quote_coverage_fn(tenant,
   party)= lists the quotes no series answers. The SQL pair is asserted against
   the codec's own answer by a pgTAP test and a unit test, so neither spelling
   can drift.
4. No side was wrong. Every quote a provisioned ACME holds is answered by a
   series the synthetic IR curves publish. The term the key ends with is the
   point within the series, which is why reading the keys and the series as
   text made them look unmatched.

* Notes

The inbox capture "Feeds required to run the ACME reports" lists the series each report needs.

A quote key and a series address look unmatched when read as text, and that is
how this task was written: the key ends with a term and the series does not. The
term is the datum's coordinate, so a series never carries it, and the two are
one datum. What settles the question is the schema's statement of which fields
are identity, which is where
[[id:7AE57098-08D4-4D4A-83D6-918FE520B68E][A pillar names the market series it reads]] already points.

The check reads the interest rate swap rate form only, because it is the only
form a provision stores: all 420 quotes are one. A quote of another form is
reported as one the check cannot answer rather than passed over silently.

The review of PR #4959 found three faults in the first cut, each now closed
with a case:

- A session that could not see the party got no rows, which reads as "no gaps".
  The call is refused with 42501 instead.
- A quote was answered by a series of the party or of the system tenant. The
  export writes a run's market data from the party's own quote series and takes
  from the system tenant only fixing series, which answer an index and not a
  quote, so the rule is the party's own series.
- The reader was laxer than the codec: it upper-cased the type and quote
  tokens, accepted an empty token, and passed a key whose start was a date and
  whose end was a period. It now refuses what the codec refuses, and the second
  round found it still looser about dates and periods: a dashed date, a day
  that does not exist, a date in the index tenor slot and any word as a period
  all passed. Each is checked now, and a null party is refused rather than
  answered with no rows.

* Result

A curve configuration's quote and the market series that answers it are one
datum in two spellings, and the difference between them is the term: a key ends
with one and a series never carries one, because the term is the point within
the series. A provisioned ACME holds 420 quotes across four configurations and
the market answers every one, so no side was wrong and nothing needed fixing.

What the branch adds is the statement of the pair and a way to read the gap:

- =ores_marketdata_curve_pillar_series_uri_fn(quote_text)=, the series address a
  quote names.
- =ores_marketdata_curve_quote_coverage_fn(tenant, party)=, the quotes of a
  party's curve configurations that no series answers, one row per quote with
  its reason.
- A pgTAP test over a staged and published configuration, and a unit test that
  asserts the codec's own answer against the same address, so the SQL spelling
  and the codec cannot drift apart unseen.
- The pair written into
  [[id:7AE57098-08D4-4D4A-83D6-918FE520B68E][A pillar names the market series it reads]], which is where the naming rule
  already lived.

The review of PR #4959 changed the shape of the answer without changing the
reading: a row now says whether the quote is unanswered or unreadable, the rule
is the party's own series rather than the party's or the system tenant's, and a
session that cannot see the party is refused instead of being told there are no
gaps. Re-measured after the change: still 0 gaps of 420.

Gates: site-page, no-committed-credentials, boost-deps, format, compass-ng (0
new diagnostics), build, db, ctest (106/106), ticks-smoke, pgtap (every file)
and step-consumers all pass on the first cut; site-page, db and pgtap were run
again over the review fixes.

The check reads the interest rate swap rate form only. That is every quote a
provision stores today, and a quote of another form is reported as one the check
cannot answer rather than passed over. When a configuration holds a quote of
another family, this check must learn that family's spelling before it can be
trusted about it.
