* Acceptance

- The dropped elements of the credit default swap, the index swap, the index option, the synthetic CDO and the credit linked swap are stored in the product's fact table and exported.
- The index option stores its own elements in its fact row, the wrapped index swap's elements in the index swap's table, and its option block in the shared option tables.
- A mapper test per product holds its elements through the round trip.
- The ORE suite passes.
- The risk participation agreement is out of scope: unit 7 takes it, because its protection fee is a list of legs and its underlying can hold a treasury lock on a bond.

* Plan

1. *Widen the fact tables.* Add a column only with its writer: the accrual and
   payment flags, the payment time, the protection start, the upfront date and
   fee, the trade date and the cash settlement days of the swap-like products,
   the option's knock-out flag, index term, strike type and front-end start, and
   the credit linked swap's default payment time. A flag is a boolean and the
   upfront fee is an exact decimal.
2. *Two rows for the index option.* The option wraps an index swap, so the trade
   has its option row and a row of the index swap's table. The carrier holds the
   option row as its fact and the wrapped swap's row beside it. This undoes the
   one-row shortcut of unit 5, which could not hold the wrapped swap's columns
   without copying them.
3. *The shared option block.* The swaption's option mapping becomes public as
   =map_option_block= and =rebuild_option_block=, and the credit carrier holds
   the option rows. The header's delete cascade, the export service and the
   import handler carry them.
4. *Prove it.* A test per product reads its elements back, and the suites run
   against a recreated database.

* Notes

- After the review the trading API, trading service, shell application and ORE
  core suites passed through =compass test run=, the codegen suite passed with
  1,397 cases, and a misspell scan of every file the branch changes found
  nothing.
- The ORE binding showed what the analysis missed. The agreement's
  =ProtectionFee= is a list of legs, not an amount, and its =Underlying= holds
  legs, an optional option block, a naked-option flag and an optional treasury
  lock whose data is a whole bond. The design had a money column for the fee.
  The agreement became unit 7, and the design now says so.
- The synthetic CDO states its upfront fee as text that is empty or a number.
  The mapper stores a number as an exact decimal and an empty text as null.
- The ORE flag type has thirteen spellings (Y, YES, true, 1 and so on). The
  tables hold a boolean, so the export writes Y or N. A document that wrote
  =false= comes back as =N=. Both are valid ORE.
- The index option's exercise rules beyond a list of dates (an exercise
  schedule) go to the shared schedule tables through the swaption's mapping.

* Result

Each swap-like credit product stores the elements the mapper used to drop. The
credit default swap, the index swap and the synthetic CDO hold their accrual and
payment flags, payment time, upfront date and fee, and the swaps their protection
start, trade date and cash settlement days. The credit linked swap holds its
settles-accrual flag and default payment time. The index option holds its
knock-out flag, index term, strike type, trade date and front-end start in its
own row, the wrapped swap's elements in the index swap's table, and its option
block in the shared option tables. All of them come back through the export.

Acceptance met for the five swap-like products. The risk participation
agreement is unit 7.
