This page documents a [[id:F44766FA-1E9C-416F-849A-E131101B2ECA][user journey]]: A trader or quant checks a candidate curve against the quotes it came from: the quotes overlaid on the candidate, the pillar residuals, the forward-rate smoothness, and the calendar and roll logic.

A user journey describes what one person is trying to do, before anything is
built. It is a design document that outlives any sprint, so it names the
screens, the entity fields each screen shows, and the operations and messages
each step needs.

* Goal

"I want to know whether this candidate curve still stands on the quotes it was
built from, and whether its forwards and its dates make sense." The journey is
done when the person has seen the raw quotes against the candidate, the pillar
residuals, the forward-rate smoothness, and the calendar and roll logic, and
can say whether the candidate is sound. The person does not publish here.

* Actor and trigger

The actor is the trader or the quant. The trader owns the candidate; the quant
owns the method and reads the same screen. Neither decides to publish here: an
approver does that in a different journey. The trigger is a bootstrap or
refresh just run, a residual that looks too large, a forward curve that steps,
or a pillar that settles on an unexpected date. The journey starts at a
candidate curve and ends when the person leaves it, still a candidate.

This journey reads and diagnoses; it changes nothing. It is the check the maker
runs before the candidate goes to a second pair of eyes.

* Steps

1. *Open the candidate.* The person opens *Curves and surfaces* and selects
   the candidate curve. The system reads the =market_series= row and its
   =derivation_kind=/config/version, which says the curve is derived and by
   which recipe.
2. *Read the candidate points.* The person chooses *Read candidate*. The
   system reconstructs the latest snapshot of the curve with
   =marketdata.v1.curve-snapshot.get=, one point per =point_id=, at now. The
   read has no as-of parameter, so the person cannot look at the candidate as
   of an earlier instant.
3. *Read the raw quotes.* The system lists the quotes the curve was built
   from with =marketdata.v1.market_observations.list_by_series_id=, one row
   per =oresmd_uri= with its value, source and time. The quotes and the
   candidate are two separate reads.
4. *Overlay the quotes on the candidate.* The person reads each raw quote
   against its candidate point. No operation joins the two lists: they come
   from different subjects and are keyed differently (the quote by an
   =oresmd_uri=, the candidate by a =point_id=), so the screen places them
   side by side by reading both and matching on the coordinate. See /What is
   missing/.
5. *Read the pillar residuals.* The person reads the residual at each pillar,
   meaning the distance between the quote the curve was built from and the
   rate the curve returns at that pillar. No operation serves a residual; the
   compute reply carries bootstrapped points and no per-pillar error. See
   /What is missing/.
6. *Read the forward-rate smoothness.* The person reads the forward curve
   between pillars and looks for a step. A server-side health checker exists
   that flags a negative forward rate as =INFO= and a leave-one-out outlier as
   =WARNING=, but no operation calls it, so nothing reaches the screen. See
   /What is missing/.
7. *Read the calendar and roll logic.* The person checks how each pillar
   tenor resolves to a date and which calendar and roll convention applied.
   The system reads the recipe with
   =refdata.v1.ir_curve_bootstrap_configs.get= and its pillars with
   =refdata.v1.ir_curve_bootstrap_pillars.list=, and resolves a tenor through
   =refdata.v1.tenor_convention_resolutions.list_by_convention_code=. The
   resolved dates are not stored on a point, so the screen derives them.
8. *Recompute if needed.* If the person suspects a stale candidate, they choose
   *Recompute candidate*, which calls =marketdata.v1.curve_bootstrap.compute=
   with the config and an as-of and returns the bootstrapped points. The
   operation exists but no caller sends it today.
9. *Leave.* The person returns to the list. The candidate is unchanged; the
   person did not write or publish anything.

The compute and the snapshot reads are server-side and synchronous, so the
journey carries no state of its own. Recomputing a candidate returns points; it
does not publish them.

* Screens and wireframes

One screen. The candidate and its as-of sit at the top; the quotes overlaid on
the candidate, the residuals and the forwards sit in the middle; the calendar
and roll logic sits at the foot, so the person reads the market, then the fit,
then the dates it settles on.

=marketdata.v1.curve_bootstrap.compute= and =.republish= both exist and no
caller sends them today; the preview half is the one this journey would use.
The snapshot read is =partial= because it always reads now and has no as-of
parameter. The generic history read is =marketdata.v1.history.get= with =entity_type=
=ores.marketdata.market_series=; =market_series= and =observation_lineage=
register history providers.

* What is missing

1. *There is no residuals or diagnostics operation.* The subject
   =marketdata.v1.curve_bootstrap.diagnostics= would have to be added to
   return, per pillar, the quote the curve was built from, the rate the curve
   returns at that pillar, and the residual. Until then the screen reads the
   candidate and the quotes separately and matches them by coordinate, and it
   cannot say what the bootstrap engine itself measured.
2. *The health checker has no caller.* =curve_health_checker= exists in
   =ores.analytics.quant= and flags a non-positive discount factor, a negative
   forward rate as =INFO= and a leave-one-out forward-rate outlier as
   =WARNING=. Nothing calls it outside its tests, and the compute reply carries
   points only, so no finding reaches the wire. The candidate is a subject
   such as =marketdata.v1.curve_bootstrap.health=, or a findings field on the
   compute response. Until then the screen plots the forwards and marks that no
   check ran.
3. *A read cannot be taken as of a time in the past.* The snapshot read always
   reads now, so the person cannot check the candidate as of the instant it
   was built. The candidate is an =as_of= parameter on the snapshot request.
   Until then the screen reads the current snapshot only.
4. *The quotes and the candidate are not joined.* They are keyed differently,
   the quote by an =oresmd_uri= and the candidate by a =point_id=, and no
   operation returns both. The candidate is an overlay read that returns a
   quote and its candidate rate together. Until then the screen joins the two
   lists itself and shows an unmatched quote as a gap.
5. *A pillar's resolved date is not stored.* The recipe names a tenor
   convention, and a resolution read returns the scheme's rows, but the date a
   given pillar resolved to is not on the point, so the screen must resolve it
   again and the two can disagree. The candidate is the resolved date on the
   lineage of a derived point. Until then the screen derives the dates and
   labels them as derived.
6. *The curve-fit screen exists for curves only.* There is no surface or
   slice read and no surface residual, so a surface fit cannot be checked on
   this screen. The candidate is a surface read and a surface residual
   subject. Until then a surface fit is checked on the concurrent journey
   [[id:89537A61-5C48-4A8D-AE3A-8DD4069433DF][Calibrate or refresh a surface]],
   where the same gap is recorded.
