This page documents a [[id:F44766FA-1E9C-416F-849A-E131101B2ECA][user journey]]: A finance officer chooses the kind and the timepoint, assembles the body of the day's cut, reviews what it holds, takes it and has it authorised, or reads the gaps that stop it.

A user journey describes what one person is trying to do, before anything is
built. It is a design document that outlives any sprint, so it names the
screens, the entity fields each screen shows, and the operations and messages
each step needs.

The decision behind it is
[[id:F6362451-D569-4609-97A3-F1C785AAAF02][The cut is the body a run prices
from]]: a cut records the body it fixed and the two acts that made it, its state
is derived from those facts, and the person who takes one cannot authorise it.
The [[id:B5214F3A-A72E-421E-82BC-79338B2E95CB][Cuts]] structure note fixes
what the body must hold, and
[[id:33E879B7-496A-406D-B3F2-72637379AD02][Kinds of cut]] fixes what each
occasion contributes.

* Goal

"The centre has closed and the day's reports are waiting on a body of data nobody
has fixed. I want to say which cut this is, see exactly what it holds before I
commit it, be told plainly and early when a part cannot be assembled, and have
somebody else sign it off so the numbers the reports show can be reproduced next
year. When it is authorised I want the cut to be an object I can point at, and not
a date that means something different tomorrow."

The journey is done when the cut holds a body, a second person has authorised it,
and the cut's page says so — or when the body cannot be assembled and the page
says why, in items a person can chase.

* Actor and trigger

A finance officer runs this journey for the end of day kind, and a market-risk
owner for the independent price verification. Both act inside their own party and
inside their own tenant, on the books whose centre they own. A desk runs the same
journey for a flash cut at an instant it states.

Three permissions decide what the person sees. Assembling and taking a cut needs
=reporting::cuts:take=, which is the raise permission of the authorisation
request. Reading the cut and its gaps needs =reporting::cuts:read=. Authorising
one needs =inbox::approvals:decide_finance=, which is the part
[[id:E21DFC83-9F3F-45BE-AD3F-48262126DFAC][Four-eyes framework]] seeds, and the
person who took the cut does not hold it for that cut. A person who may not take
a cut is drawn no button, as the
[[id:2BF7F03F-6BF4-45FF-8E26-4D3B4E2D943D][Record screen standard]] requires.
The Reporting area is drawn for a person who holds =reporting::report_definitions:read=, which is the read the area already asks for; the Cuts card inside it is drawn for =reporting::cuts:read=.

The trigger is the close of a business day, or a correction that moves the day's
population after its cut was taken. The person opens the cuts list because the
reports of the day cannot run, or because the deal cut must be retaken until the
population is clean, as the [[id:B5214F3A-A72E-421E-82BC-79338B2E95CB][Cuts]]
note records. The journey ends on the cut's page, in a state the page draws.

This journey takes a cut and has it authorised. It does not approve or withdraw a
published curve — those are
[[id:239E4C3C-12E7-4A87-9DE3-16B9F093BC14][Ask for a publish to be approved]]
and [[id:B05A31CC-FA22-4534-848E-601A30822992][Withdraw or replace a published
curve]] — and it does not run a report:
[[id:9123193E-352D-4F46-8174-7763FA0A44CD][Price a report from a cut]] is the
journey that does. It does not author a convention, a calendar or a curve
configuration: it reads the version in force and records it.

* Steps

** Step 1: reach the cuts, and read where the day stands

1. *Open Cuts.* The area is Reporting, and the trail is Home, Reporting, Cuts. The
   list is the [[id:2BF7F03F-6BF4-45FF-8E26-4D3B4E2D943D][Record screen standard's]]
   list: a search over the label and the scope, a filter on the kind and a filter
   on the state, the shared pager, and *Take a cut* as the primary button, drawn
   only for a person who may take one.

   | Column | What it holds |
   |--------+---------------|
   | Cut | The derived label, monospace, and the scope under it |
   | Kind | The occasion: End of day, Independent verification, Flash, Start of day, or the process that named the instant |
   | Timepoint | The instant, exact, with its timezone, because it is what the body is fixed at |
   | State | The derived state: Draft, Incomplete, Taken, Authorised, Refused or Superseded |
   | Taker | The account that took the cut |
   | Authoriser | The account that decided it, and the decision beside it |
   | Runs | How many runs priced from it, right-aligned |

2. *Read the day.* The default order is the timepoint, newest first, so the day's
   cut is the first row. A superseded cut sits under the one that replaced it and
   says so, because the person reading the list is asking which body the next run
   will price from.
3. *Read what is missing.* A day with no cut for the centre yet says *There are no
   cuts for this scope yet.* and offers *Take a cut*. The sentence is the list's
   empty state, and it is the one state that must not read as a fault.

** Step 2: choose the kind and the timepoint

4. *Press Take a cut.* The take is a guided sequence of four steps, because it
   fixes a body across four parts and the review is the point of it. Its rail is
   the [[id:6A63AAC7-4869-493A-97E7-55B919874784][journey runtime's]], and its
   address is =/reporting/cuts/take=.
5. *Choose the kind.* The first step offers the kinds the party may take. Each row
   is a kind, the instant it fixes and what it is for, in the words of
   [[id:33E879B7-496A-406D-B3F2-72637379AD02][Kinds of cut]]. The end of day kind
   offers the centre's close, the start of day kind the centre's open, and the
   flash and process kinds an instant the person states. A kind the party has no
   centre for is not offered, and the step says which centre each kind would use.
6. *Fix the timepoint.* For the end of day kind the timepoint is the close of the
   centre the scope revalues against, read from the cut times reference data and
   drawn so the person reads it rather than types it. For a flash cut or a cut a
   process names, the instant is stated with its timezone, and the step refuses a
   time in the future, because a body cannot be fixed at an instant that has not
   happened.
   The label is derived beside the fields and never typed: the kind, the scope and
   the date, exactly as
   [[id:F6362451-D569-4609-97A3-F1C785AAAF02][the decision note]] derives it.
7. *Read the scope.* The step states the books the cut will value and the rates
   centre whose market data it will read. The scope is a fact and not a choice at
   the take: it is the set of books that revalue together, and a scope spanning two
   centres is refused here rather than discovered at the assembly.

** Step 3: assemble the body, and read the gaps

8. *Assemble.* The second step runs the assembly and draws one row per part of the
   body, and one for the taking identity. Each row is a part, what the assembly
   resolved, and its state.

   | Part | What the row holds |
   |------+-------------------|
   | The deals | The population at the timepoint: the deal count, how many are live, expired, fixed or settled, the books they belong to, and the corrections inside the body |
   | The market data | The fixed snapshot: the published sets each market role resolves to, their publish records and as-of, the fixings in force, and the value count |
   | The reference data | The versions in force of the conventions, the calendars, the curve configurations and the engine run settings the run reads |
   | The engine and the code | The engine version and the wrapper version the work will run, and the code revision of the services that assembled the body |
   | The taking identity | The account, the party, and the instant the body was fixed |

9. *Read a gap, where a part cannot be assembled.* The step does not fail as a
   whole. Each part that cannot be closed is drawn with the item, the reason and
   the remedy, and the take offers *Save as a draft* in place of *Take the cut*.

   | Gap | The reason | The remedy the row names |
   |-----+------------+--------------------------|
   | A series with no value at or before the instant | The store holds no observation for the series, so the snapshot would carry a hole | Publish a set for the series, or state an instant at which the store holds one |
   | A deal at no version | The population read returned a row with no version current at the instant | Correct the deal, or state why it is absent |
   | A curve in the candidate state | Nobody approved the set, so a valuation cannot price from it | [[id:239E4C3C-12E7-4A87-9DE3-16B9F093BC14][Ask for a publish to be approved]] |
   | No engine version recorded | Nothing in the store names the version that would compute the result | Register the app version, or name the version the run will use |
   | A scope spanning two centres | The books read two centres' market data, so one market data half cannot value them | Take a cut per centre, or narrow the scope |

10. *Save the draft, and leave.* A body that cannot be assembled is kept as a
    draft in the *Incomplete* state, with every gap beside the part it belongs to.
    The step states what happens next: no run may price from the cut, and the take
    is resumed from the cut's page once the gaps are closed. The person is not
    asked to remember the list, because the list is the record.

** Step 4: review the body against the previous cut

11. *Review what the cut holds.* The third step is the review, in the shape the
    [[id:E69C008D-7092-4855-98EC-47A75F422F61][Configuration screen standard]]
    uses for a configuration's review: the counts, the differences from the
    previous cut of the same kind and scope, and the sentence that states what the
    cut decides and what it does not.
12. *Read the differences.* A cut is read against its predecessor, because the
    movement between two cuts is what the day's reports state. The step lists the
    deals that entered the population, the deals that left it, the deals whose
    state moved, the market data items whose value moved, and the reference data
    versions that changed. A cut with no predecessor says so and draws no
    difference.
13. *Read what it decides.* One sentence, in the standard's words: it fixes the
    body one valuation reads and it records the engine and the code, so a later run
    of the same cut with the same engine reproduces its result. It does not decide
    the day's prices, the conventions or how a product is priced: it records what
    was in force.

** Step 5: take the cut, which raises the authorisation

14. *Press Take the cut.* The press writes the body and raises the authorisation
    request in one act, as
    [[id:F6362451-D569-4609-97A3-F1C785AAAF02][the decision note]] requires. The
    cut moves to *Taken* and the request moves into the inbox of the part the kind
    names. The dialog states what the press did: the body is frozen, no field
    changes from here, and the request is with the deciders.
15. *Read the state before authorisation.* The cut's page states it in one
    sentence: the body is fixed and nobody has signed it, so no run may price from
    it. *Authorise* is not drawn for the taker, and the reason is stated rather
    than left to a refusal: a person cannot authorise the cut they took.

** Step 6: authorise, or read the refusal

16. *Decide, as the decider.* The decider opens the request from Requests, and the
    request's page draws the body's four counts and the gap list, which is empty
    for a taken cut. *Approve* and *Refuse* are drawn for the part's holder who is
    not the taker. *Refuse* needs a reason.
17. *Read the authorisation on the cut.* When the part approves, the cut moves to
    *Authorised*, the instant and the decider are recorded, and the previous cut of
    the same kind and scope moves to *Superseded*. The page states all three facts
    and links the authorisation request, so the signature is readable from the cut
    rather than inferred from it.
18. *Read the refusal.* A refused authorisation moves the cut to *Refused*, keeps
    the body readable, and states that no second decision can be taken on it. The
    remedy is a new take, which the page offers as *Take it again*, and the new cut
    carries the next ordinal in its label.

** The audit of the take**

19. *Read the cut's history.* The record's *History* tab is the shared timeline:
    the two acts, each with its account and instant, and the body's own facts. The
    body is immutable once taken, so the history of a taken cut has two entries and
    no more.
20. *Read who is waiting.* The cut's page names the request, the part that must
    decide it and the account it waits on, so a person chasing an authorisation
    knows whom to ask.

* Screens and wireframes

Three surfaces: the cuts list, the take flow with its four steps, and the cut's
record page.

The list is the standard's. The take flow is the one place where a rail of steps
earns its place, because the work is four acts over one body rather than one edit:
the kind and the instant, the assembly with its gaps, the review against the
previous cut, and the press that takes and raises. The record page is the
standard's record page with four tabs: *The body*, *Authorisation*, *Runs* and
*History*.

* Prototype

The [[file:../../prototypes/cuts/the-cut/][the cut prototype]] draws this
journey, and [[file:../../prototypes/cuts/the-cut.org][its design note]] says
what it argues, which states it draws and what is fake. It belongs at
=doc/prototypes/cuts/the-cut/=.

It is one plain page with six surfaces behind the query string: the cuts list,
the cut's record, the take flow, the picker that consumes a cut, the run that
names one, and the named cut times reference data. For this journey it draws a
day with no cut, a draft, an incomplete body with its gaps, a taken cut with no
authorisation, an authorised cut, a refused cut and a superseded cut. Every value
on it is a fixture, and the page says so.

The take is one call and not three, because the body, the request and the cut's
state land together or not at all. A take that wrote the body and then raised the
request would leave a cut nobody is asked to sign.

* What is missing

1. *No cut entity and no component.* No cut component exists, no table holds a
   cut, and no service answers a read of one. The decision note settles what the
   record must hold; the model, the store and the operations are the work that
   follows.
2. *No read answers a population at an as-of.* The trade store is versioned, so a
   query over its intervals reconstructs a past state, and no operation exposes
   it. The [[id:19630B5D-A163-42B5-B9C4-61A87BAE8F5F][Trade cut]] page states the
   second half of the gap: the interval a row states is rewritten when a
   correction arrives, so a re-run of the same query returns a different body.
   The cut therefore copies the population it fixed, and the copy has no home
   yet.
3. *No fixed market data snapshot.* The
   [[id:DD59B04C-DAD2-458C-B35E-3F6B0B3D6DC6][Market data cut]] page records that
   =marketdata.v1.curve_snapshot.get= re-resolves on every call and carries no
   as-of, and that the resolution-side snapshot exists as a definition and no
   entity. A cut records the identity of a snapshot that nothing materialises.
4. *Nothing records the service build.* The engine version survives because a
   workunit names an app version. The revision of the services that gathered the
   body does not survive anywhere, and a result reproduced from a different build
   of the gathering code is not reproduced from the same software, which is the
   condition the [[id:B5214F3A-A72E-421E-82BC-79338B2E95CB][Cuts]] note quotes.
5. *No gap list exists anywhere.* A configuration's import lists what it could not
   read, and nothing lists what an assembly could not fill. The screen needs one,
   and the prototype defines the shape the standard would take.
6. *The kind is not a closed vocabulary in the store.* The four occasions are
   stated in a knowledge page and in no table. A kind held as a code in a
   populated list is the smaller change, and the decision note names it as
   following work rather than settling it.
7. *No route and no screen.* No BFF route serves reporting, and no reporting screen
   exists in the web application, so the cuts list, the take flow and the record
   are all new.
8. *The cut times reference data does not exist.* The close of a centre's business
   day is a fact a person needs and no table holds it for our centres. The
   prototype draws the reference data the take reads, and the
   [[id:3B39B2E2-6E05-4DF8-B68B-6ACA12F2FCE9][option-expiry cut code]] page
   states the row the expiry sense needs from the same list.
