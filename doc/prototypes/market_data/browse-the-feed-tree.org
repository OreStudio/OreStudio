* Summary

The feed tree prototype argues what the web's *Feeds* screen under *Market
data* should show before it is built. It is the successor of the =ores.qt=
Market Simulator window and of the
[[id:768BEEF8-EAF1-46DF-99A4-17FE68D0ACCC][Market simulator prototype]], which
argued the same window on the desktop, for three collections and sixteen feeds.

It argues one thing the earlier prototype did not have to: the tree is the screen
that manages runs, not the one that edits feeds. A person finds a node, reads
what runs under it, and starts or stops it with one request. A leaf opens the
feed's existing page, =/market-data/fx-feeds/:id= or =/market-data/ir-feeds/:id=,
where its process, mixture, curve template and history already are.

It is throwaway, with no service and no build step. Open the
[[file:browse-the-feed-tree/][feed tree]].

* Detail

** The question

The feeds are now organised by provider and scope (see
[[id:0A32284A-5F8C-4A99-9539-34923631DED3][Organise feeds by provider and scope]]):
/System feeds/ by provider, then feed kind, and /Test feeds/ by party, then
collection. Bloomberg alone holds 55 feeds, and the story expects many thousands
once more providers and order books arrive. The open question is which layout
keeps a node's running state and its start and stop in reach at that size.

The tree carries the Qt window's ideas: a node is running, stopped or partial
with its count; start and stop act on everything under a node through one
folder-scoped request; and a pair reads as its two flags. It drops what belongs
on the feed's own page: editing, the mixture and the live chart.

** The variants

Switch with the bottom bar, or =?variant=. The default is =a=.

| Variant | Name | The structural argument |
|---------+------+-------------------------|
| =A= | tree and node | The Qt layout. The tree on the left with a roll-up meter on every node; the selected node on the right with its counts, Start and Stop, and a table of its children. A leaf shows its source, series, process and binding, and links to its page. |
| =B= | tree table | One expandable table. Every node is a row with what it holds, its count, its state, its ticks and its actions, so a whole subtree is read and acted on without a second pane. |
| =C= | columns | Drill-down columns, one per level, each loading a page of children. The layout for thousands of feeds: no level is ever rendered whole, and the path stays visible across the columns. |

*A* is the closest to the window the desk knows. *B* reads best when a person
wants to compare siblings. *C* is the only one that does not degrade when a
folder holds hundreds of feeds.

** The states

Switch with the bottom bar, or =?state=. The default is =mixed=.

| State | What it shows |
|-------+---------------|
| =mixed= | The Bloomberg FX spots running and its IR curves stopped, so Bloomberg reads 26 of 55; one test feed running. |
| =stopped= | Nothing running. |
| =scale= | Six providers and about 2,850 feeds, with an /Order books/ kind marked planned under each new provider. |
| =search= | The search holds "usd sofr": the matching curves with their paths, and the match count. |

** Driving it

Nodes select, folders expand, and the search and the /Running only/ filter
narrow the tree. Start and Stop ask first, saying how many feeds the request
touches, and that stopping a bound feed stops every party bound to it; the
report then says what started, what was already running and what was skipped,
and every roll-up recomputes. /Open/ names the feed page it would open. Every
control is prototype chrome or mock behaviour.

** What the prototype assumes

- Bloomberg sits under /System feeds/, the story's target. Today it is a party
  collection published with the tenant, because the services do not yet read
  system collections.
- The 2016 ORE Samples collection sits under the /Generic/ provider, since it
  stands for no provider.
- The counts and ticks are mock values; a built screen reads them from the
  feed status the synthetic service already reports.
