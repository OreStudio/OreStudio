#+title: Generators belong to the system tenant and the system party
#+description: Why every collection, folder and generator is system-owned, who may author them, and how a party can still look at one.
#+type: knowledge
#+level: cross
#+filetags: :marketdata:synthetic:generators:ownership:
#+created: 2026-10-06
#+updated: 2026-10-22

* Summary

A simulated feed stands in for one real-world observation. There is one value for
each vendor feed, so every party and every tenant must see the same value. All
collections, folders and generators therefore belong to the system tenant and the
system party. No generator is party-owned or tenant-owned.

* Detail

** Ownership

- The system tenant and the system party own all generator rows.
- Row-level security lets every tenant read them and only the system tenant write
  them.
- A party reads a generator to find out how a simulated value is made. The
  simulator does not hide itself.

** Who authors

A person with the generator permission in the system tenant authors collections,
folders and generators. A tenant administrator has no write path. A tenant that
wants different data takes a different provider or a different source.

** Why not party scope

A party-scoped generator would give two parties two values for one vendor
instrument. A vendor does not do that. It would also need a binding step between
a generator and a party, and the system would then know that the source is
simulated.

** How a generator reaches a party

It does not. A generator serves the simulator's universe. The simulator publishes
ticks. The service delivers them according to subscriptions and routes. See
[[id:37B27CA8-9F44-4A61-9B02-3EAABD8C3818][A party routes to exactly one source for each provider]].
