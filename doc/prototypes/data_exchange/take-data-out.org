* Summary

The support export and the archival export are one screen. They differ in
scope, in what completeness means and in where the file goes — not in the act,
which is "choose a scope, read the parts, check the manifest, take the files
away". A promotion package is the same act with a target named. A sandbox is a
fourth scope: exporting one is how a defect is reproduced elsewhere. The
receiving side is the import prototype; this is the writing side.

It is throwaway. Open [[file:take-data-out/][the export]].

* Detail

** What it argues

- The purpose changes the defaults and the completeness list, not the layout.
  "Smallest readable set", "dated copy" and "package for a target" are three
  answers to the same questions.
- The manifest is the artefact. Party, source installation, build, instant,
  each part, its checksum and what was left out. No export writes one today;
  the person types it.
- The read is scoped by rights. Three books are excluded and named. The export
  refuses rather than returning an empty file, which is the support journey's
  rule.
- One instant or none. The toggle shows today's parts read seven minutes apart,
  and the archive checklist fails; a set that never existed together cannot be
  signed off as complete.
- An archive is not a backup. The screen says so in the scope card and repeats
  it on the checklist: no audit columns, no row versions, no history, no book
  rights.
- A promotion package carries the order. Currencies, portfolio, conventions,
  curves, market, run documents — written on the manifest, not kept in a
  person's head.
- A sandbox is exportable, and that is how a defect travels. The scope selector
  offers the official tree and every sandbox the session may see. A sandbox
  export names the source sandbox and its anchor in the manifest, and reads the
  sandbox's portfolios and virtual books as the sandbox part. The other parts
  stay party-wide, because only books and portfolios have a =sandbox_id=. The
  receiving import creates a new sandbox; a sandbox id is fixed for life and is
  not reused.

** Variants and states

=?purpose=share|archive|promote=,
=?state=ready|written|refused|partial=,
=?instant=one|apart=, =?actor=reader|ops=,
=?sandbox=official|sb-import-repro|sb-import-onboard|sb-sample-seed|sb-import-feed=.
The bar switches the purpose, the actor, the instant and the state; the scope
card switches between the official tree and a sandbox.

** What is real and what is not

The three export verbs, the parts each writes, the rights filter, the
no-as_of gap, the missing manifest, the missing ticket integration and the
PostgreSQL-backup caveat are the journeys'. The sandbox rules are the model's: a
sandbox is anchored at an official portfolio, a book and its portfolio share one
sandbox, a sandbox root portfolio has no parent, and a virtual book has no
ledger reference and is not sweepable. The counts, sizes, checksums, manifest
id, sandbox names and snapshot id are invented. No export screen or route
exists.

** The verdict

Not signed off. The screen asks the owner whether an archive should be an
ORE-shaped set at all, or a database-level copy with the audit columns the ORE
form loses — and therefore whether the archive and the support export really
share a screen. The sandbox scope adds a narrower question: when a sandbox is
exported to reproduce a defect, may the receiving import write into a new
sandbox only, or must the receiver choose between a sandbox and the party?
