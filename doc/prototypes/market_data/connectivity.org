* What it argues

Connectivity is where the delivery of market data is configured. It is a tree,
not a list: a supplier has sessions, a session is one configured adapter, and a
feed binds one publisher to one session and one service.

#+ATTR_HTML: :class hug-leading
| Word | Meaning | Example |
|------+---------+---------|
| /Publisher/ | Owns the ids, the credit and the terms. | =ecb=, =imf=, =ust=, =bloomberg= for its own data |
| /Supplier/ | Who we contract with and connect to. | =frankfurter=, =bloomberg=, =lseg=, =ecb-data-portal=, =fred= |
| /Adapter/ | Code module that speaks one supplier protocol. | =frankfurter= 1.4.0 |
| /Session/ | One configured adapter: supplier, environment, endpoint, credential, settings. | =frankfurter-eu= (production) |
| /Environment/ | Where a session runs. | =production=, =test=, =dr=, =simulated= |
| /Service/ | What a session offers; it decides the feed's shape. | =real_time=, =delayed=, =end_of_day=, =reference=, =historical= |
| /Feed/ | One publisher on one service through one session, with key, scope and schedule. | =ecb-frankfurter= |
| /Route/ | A party's feed for one publisher and one asset class. | System Party, =ecb=, FX → =ecb-frankfurter= |

Frankfurter is a supplier that delivers the ECB's data; it is not the publisher.
Bloomberg is both: the supplier of a session and the publisher of its own values.
That distinction is the point of the screen.

* The records

- A *publisher* record has Overview, Feeds and History.
- A *supplier* record has Overview (contract, support) and Sessions.
- A *session* record has Overview (endpoint, environment, credential, settings,
  services), Feeds, Runtime and History.
- A *feed* record has Overview (publisher, session, service, provider key,
  default), Underlyings, Schedule, Routes and History.

A session owns the endpoint, the credential and the settings; a feed owns the
publisher, the service, the provider key, the scope and the schedule. The
endpoint is entered once per session.

* The two setup flows

=??add-session=1&supplier=frankfurter= configures a session: adapter,
environment, endpoint, credential and schema settings, with a test connection.
=??add-feed=1&publisher=ecb= adds a feed to an enabled session: service,
provider key, scope, schedule and route.

* What is fake

Every endpoint, credential, version, count and date is invented. Secrets are
masks only. No request leaves the page.

* Where the border is

Connectivity owns the editable facts: publishers, suppliers, adapters, sessions,
feeds, scopes, schedules and routes. Public rates owns the observed facts:
values, coverage, provenance and freshness. A value names its feed; the feed
names its session and supplier; the supplier is who we call.
