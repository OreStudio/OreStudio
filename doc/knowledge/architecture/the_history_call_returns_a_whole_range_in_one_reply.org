#+title: The history call returns every tick of a range in one reply, or refuses the range
#+description: The contract of get_series_ticks: a request for a span of time, one reply with every tick of the span, and a refusal that names the ceiling when the span holds too many.
#+type: knowledge
#+level: cross
#+filetags: :marketdata:ticks:reads:architecture:
#+created: 2026-10-23
#+updated: 2026-10-23

* Summary

=marketdata.v1.ops.get_series_ticks= takes a series and a span of time. It answers with
every tick of the span in one reply, with no pages and no cursor. A span that holds more
ticks than the ceiling is refused, and the refusal names the ceiling. The service never
returns part of a span as if it were the whole. The client that calls it is described in
[[id:10E479D2-990E-4627-95A2-1DA0FA5D68D5][A chart reads a series with one history call and one live subscription]].

The shell command =marketdata ticks <series-id>= calls it. The command takes =--from=,
=--to=, =--coordinate= and =--csv=. A span with no =--from= starts 10 minutes ago.

* Detail

** The request

- *The series,* named by its series id, the id that the series catalogue returns.
- *From,* the first instant of the span, inclusive.
- *To,* the end of the span, exclusive. A request without it means now.
- *The coordinates,* optional. A client that plots one pillar of a curve names it, and the
  reply holds that coordinate only. A request names at most 1,000 coordinates, and a longer
  list is refused with =filter_too_large=, as the filters of the list reads are.

The request has no count. A caller asks for a span of time, never for "the newest N". A
span has the same meaning on both tiers, and it is what a chart shows.

** The reply

- The coordinate URIs of the series come first, once each.
- The first instant of the reply follows, in the wire shape that every instant takes. See
  [[id:4AAD3581-CFA7-4B44-A2DD-0236784E939F][Time and Timestamps: Architecture and Conventions]].
- The ticks follow as three arrays of equal length: the gap in microseconds from the
  previous tick, the index of the coordinate, and the value. The first gap runs from the
  first instant. The order is by time, then by coordinate.

A gap is a duration and not an instant, so the rule that every instant has one wire shape
holds. An instant in that shape takes 27 characters, and a gap of seconds takes 5 bytes.
The client adds the gaps up to get each time.

The reply holds every tick of the span that the window holds or that the store has
committed. It makes no stronger promise. See the two tiers below for the one case that
falls between them.

A tick has no id and no source in the reply. A chart needs neither, and each field adds to
the size of a reply that the client reads whole. The tick table keeps the =source_id= for
audit.

A series that does not exist yet, or a span with no tick, gives an empty reply and
success. An empty series is a normal state, as it is for the snapshot.

** Why one reply and not pages

A client that opens a chart wants the whole span at once. Pages add a round trip for each
page, a cursor that the server must keep correct while ticks arrive, and a partial chart
that the user watches fill. One reply costs one round trip and one probe of the index on
series and time.

** The ceiling

The reply is one message, and the broker caps a message at 1 MB. The service declares a
ceiling of 25,000 ticks for each reply. A tick costs about 15 bytes in three msgpack
arrays, because each element carries a type byte: 5 for a gap of seconds, 1 to 3 for the
coordinate index and 9 for the value. That is about 375 KB, little more than a third of
the cap, which leaves room for the coordinates and the envelope. This is an estimate, and the measurement of the first
build confirms or changes it. Packing each array as one binary block would cost 8 bytes a
value and would raise the ceiling.

A span over the ceiling is refused with the same rule as the instant ceiling of the slice
and the evolution: the answer names the ceiling and the service never truncates. See
[[id:4EE26B23-003B-4DF1-AA83-1196CC6810C3][Snapshots and cuts]].

The ceiling is also a limit on the cost of one read. The readers of the store share one
pool, and a chart that asks for a month of a busy series would hold a connection that other
sessions need. [[id:3A3752D7-9322-45F0-9932-762B727642E1][Throttling]] limits how much one
caller may ask for in a period, and the ceiling limits how much one caller may ask for in a
call. Both protect the callers that ask for little.

A client that needs more than the ceiling narrows the span. A body larger than the message
cap would travel as a [[id:1B641A2B-7E8B-4BDF-8C1C-6401FE1DF637][Claim Check]]: the reply
holds a key and the bytes sit in storage. This page does not define that path, because a
narrower span serves every chart.

** How the service answers a decision

The service decides four things before it reads: the context has a party, the request names
no more than 1,000 coordinates, the span does not end before it starts, and the span holds
no more than the ceiling. It reports each one as an outcome in the body, and not as a refusal
off the body, because each is a decision about the data. The codes are =party_required=,
=filter_too_large=, =range_inverted= and =range_too_large=, in the outcome catalogue. A span that starts and ends at one instant is empty and is not
refused. A span with no end runs to now, so a start ahead of the clock of the service gives
an empty reply, because nothing has ticked after now. See [[id:7C23D4E5-F607-4B82-9DA4-B5C6D7E8F901][Report a Failure in the Body]].

A context with no party gets =party_required= and not an empty reply. The party policy
of the tick table shows such a context no tick, and an empty reply would look complete.
The as-of reads face the same case. See [[id:4EE26B23-003B-4DF1-AA83-1196CC6810C3][Snapshots and cuts]].

The read uses the tenant-aware pool. The policies of the tick table decide what the
session sees, and no role skips them.

** Two tiers behind one reply

The lane keeps a window of recent ticks, and the store holds the rest. See
[[id:6F3F1A80-5B4C-4F33-ADF0-F42CDE8FF8C2][The tick ingest pipeline: freshness first]] for
the window. The service answers a span from both and sends one reply:

- The span from the start of the window to now comes from the window.
- The span before the window comes from the tick table.

The client cannot know where the window starts, so the join happens inside the reply. It is
not a merge of two sources of the same span. The window and the store cover different
spans, and the service prefers the window for the span that it covers. A span that the
window does not reach comes from the store alone. A restart empties the window, and the
store then answers the whole span.

A datum that no consumer watches keeps only its last tick in the window. A span for it
comes from the store alone, and the store trails the lane by one batch. A tick that the
lane took before the client subscribed, and that the store has not yet committed, is in
neither the reply nor the buffer. How the lane learns that a datum is watched is open on
the pipeline page. If the subscription tells it, the window fills from the subscription
on, and the case shrinks to the ticks of one batch.

** Open questions

- Does the first build keep the ceiling of 25,000 ticks? The measurement of reply size and
  of read time decides.
- Can a tick fall between the store and the window, as the two tiers section describes?
  The measurement of the batch delay against the first reply decides whether the call
  must wait for the commit of the batch in flight.
- The live subject names a series by the percent-encoded series URI, and this call names
  it by id. The client reads both from the series catalogue. Should the live subject carry
  the id instead, so that one name serves both calls?
- Should the reply carry the =source_id=? This page says no. A client that audits a tick
  reads the store.
