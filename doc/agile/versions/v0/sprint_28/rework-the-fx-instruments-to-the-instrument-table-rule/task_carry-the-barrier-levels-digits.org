* Goal

Carry the barrier levels' digits. A barrier's levels are the one money page the
digits unit left as bulk floats: =barrierData_Levels_t.Level= is an
=xsd::vector<float>=, so every barrier level, in every family, crosses the ORE
boundary as the nearest float rather than the number the document wrote.

* Acceptance

- The level list's element is a lexical leaf, patched by =scripts/reapply_ore_lexical_form.py=, never by hand.
- Every writer appends the decimal, not a =float=, so the digits reach the list.
- Every reader that took the level through a =double= cast reads the leaf's own digits instead.
- The goldens that move carry numeric text only.
- The ORE core suite passes.

* Plan

1. Teach =scripts/reapply_ore_lexical_form.py= the vector shape: the member, its
   element info and its per-element serializer.
2. Share one =push_money= helper from the boundary header, so a writer appends a
   decimal rather than formatting a float by hand.
3. Convert the three mappers that write a level: FX, equity and the rates
   knock-out swap. =make_barrier= takes a decimal rather than a =double=.
4. Read the level with the lexical overload of =exact_decimal= where a reader
   took it through a cast.
5. Move the drifted goldens aside, bootstrap them, and read every changed line.

* Result

A barrier's levels now carry the digits their documents state, in every family
that states a barrier. The list element is a lexical leaf, eleven writers
append a decimal through one shared helper, and the two readers that took a
level through a cast read the leaf instead. The ORE core suite passes: 669
cases, 483,180 assertions.

Twelve goldens change, twenty lines in total, and every one of them is a
=Level= line. The new values are the documents' own spellings, which is the
proof the digits survive: =3500.00=, =1.0=, =0.00800=, =1.21000= and =0.009=,
where the serializer had written =3500=, =1=, =0.00800000038=, =1.21000004= and
=0.00899999961=.

With this unit every money page the FX mappers read is a lexical leaf. The
remaining half of finding 1 is the accumulator's range bounds, which its own
unit removes when it gives them a child table.
