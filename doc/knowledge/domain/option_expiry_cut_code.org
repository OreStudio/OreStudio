#+description: A cut code names the time of day at which an option expires: why an expiry needs an instant, what the label is, why the instant enters the price, how the label relates to the cut time and timezone, and where ORE Studio stands.

This page states the expiry sense of a cut. The
[[id:B5214F3A-A72E-421E-82BC-79338B2E95CB][Cuts]] structure note gives the
definition and the body, and the
[[id:33E879B7-496A-406D-B3F2-72637379AD02][Kinds of cut]] page classifies the
purposes a cut serves. This page takes the same shape of label and timepoint
into the instant at which an option expires.

* Summary

A cut code is a *label* for the time of day at which an FX option expires on its
expiry date. The definition of a cut names a body of data and an instant, and the
expiry supplies an instant for one contract. A date contains many instants, and
an option's payout is conditioned on an observation at one of them, so the date
alone leaves the contract's end underdetermined. The market settles the
indeterminacy with a name, chosen per currency pair, and the timezone says which
clock reads the time. A reference row holds the name beside the time it names.
The repository holds the design of that row and not the row.

* Why an expiry needs an instant

An FX option is a contract whose payout depends on a rate observed at its expiry.
An expiry date identifies a day, and a day contains a range of instants. The
observation therefore needs one instant within the day, and the contract's value
depends on which instant the parties meant.

The dependence has three sources, and each states a mechanism rather than a
symptom.

- *Remaining life.* The time value of an option is a function of the time left to
  expiry. Two instants on the same date leave different amounts of life, so they
  produce different values, and the difference grows as expiry approaches.
- *Observation of the barrier.* A barrier is monitored to an instant while the
  option expires at an instant. Where the two instants differ, a rate observed
  between them changes whether the barrier was touched and whether the option
  ends in the money.
- *The surface's tenor.* Volatility is quoted at tenors measured to the expiry
  instant. A surface quoted to one instant prices a contract expiring at another
  with an error that is small, systematic and attributable to the mismatch.

* What the label is

The market names the expiry instant once per currency pair rather than once per
trade. A cut code is that name, and the naming convention explains the rest of
its behaviour. Because the name attaches to the pair, every option on the pair
inherits it, and a contract whose terms specify a different instant carries an
override.

The override is a fact worth recording. It changes the instant at which the
contract is observed, and therefore changes the set of market data the contract
reads. A trade carrying an override is priced on a different clock from its
pair's other trades, and a reader who compares the trade with its neighbours
needs to know that.

* The label and the cut time

The label and the time are two facts, and a system that stores one without the
other can neither apply the convention nor explain it. The *cut code* is the
name. The *cut time* is the time of day the name resolves to, with its timezone.

A reference row holds the name, the time and the timezone together. The row has
a function: it translates a name that market participants use into an instant a
valuation can compute with, and it does so in one place rather than in each
consumer.

* What the label fixes, and what it leaves open

The label fixes an instant for one contract. Measured against the definition of a
cut, it supplies the timepoint and leaves the body of data unstated. A cut code
therefore belongs in this cluster as the third sense of the word: the same shape
of label and instant, applied to the expiry of an instrument rather than to the
data a valuation reads. A cut code on a trade and a market data cut on a book
answer two independent questions, since neither constrains the other.

* Where ORE Studio stands

The repository holds the cut code as designed reference data. The design is a
lookup entity carrying code, name, description, cut time and timezone, referenced
by =currency_pair.default_cut_code= and by any other field that needs a cut time,
in
[[id:4B4ED152-1670-4E81-895A-24D747382D4C][Model market data cuts as proper ORE Studio reference data]].
The same story records that no entity, table or foreign key target exists, and
that the field was removed from the currency pair rather than kept as free text
until a target could be referenced.

The model confirms the position.
[[id:E1EE950D-FC22-4DAB-A93F-8B2A15196031][ores.refdata.currency_pair]]
carries the pair's identity, its classification and its conventions, and carries
no cut field.

The field list that states what the pair should carry, and why an override of the
default deserves a flag, is on
[[id:CC6D86D8-33FC-41D6-AC27-F35FA942F436][Currency pair and currency entity fields]].

* What the sources establish

No claim on this page rests on an external source. The times themselves are
market convention, the page records no table of them, and the definitions are
ours. The statements about the repository link the model and the story that show
them.
