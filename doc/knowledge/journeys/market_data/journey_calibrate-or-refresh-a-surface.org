This page documents a [[id:F44766FA-1E9C-416F-849A-E131101B2ECA][user journey]]: A trader builds or refreshes a candidate volatility surface from the day's market, reads its calibration error and its slices, and leaves it unpublished.

A user journey describes what one person is trying to do, before anything is
built. It is a design document that outlives any sprint, so it names the
screens, the entity fields each screen shows, and the operations and messages
each step needs.

* Goal

"I want today's quotes turned into a candidate surface I can price from, and I
want to see how well that surface fits before anyone signs it off." The journey
is done when a candidate surface exists for the chosen as-of, the person has
read its calibration error and its slices, and the surface is still a
candidate: nothing has been published, so pricing still reads the surface it
read before.

* Actor and trigger

The actor is the trader, acting as maker: the person who holds the market-data
management role for this party. The trader owns the surface and is the only
person who builds or refreshes it; an approver owns the decision to publish,
in a different journey. The trigger is the start of a trading day, a market
move, a fresh quote set, or a request from pricing. The journey starts at the
list of curves and surfaces and ends when the trader leaves the surface
unpublished.

A surface is a *derived* object. It is a candidate until it is approved and
published, and only the published object is visible to pricing and reporting;
see /What is missing/ for the model and the engine that do not exist yet.

* Steps

1. *Find the surface.* The person opens *Curves and surfaces* and filters to
   surfaces. The system lists the market series whose =series_subclass= is
   volatility, one row per =oresmd_uri=, with its asset class and its
   =derivation_kind=. There is no surface table to list, so the catalogue row
   is the only row the screen can show.
2. *Choose the surface and an as-of.* The person selects a series and an as-of
   timestamp. The system reads the =market_series= row and the observations
   at or before that instant. The chosen as-of is the snapshot the calibration
   would read; the point-in-time read is a repository method
   (=read_as_of=), not an operation on the wire.
3. *Read the market it is built from.* The system lists the quotes the surface
   is built from, one row per =oresmd_uri=: the value, the =source= tag that
   published it, and its =observation_datetime=. An observation is evidence the
   market sent; nobody signs it off.
4. *Confirm the parameterisation.* The person checks the surface's axes, its
   strike or moneyness convention, and the model the fit would use. The system
   carries none of these: it shows the series' =derivation_kind= and its
   =derivation_config_id=/=derivation_config_version=, and it marks the fields
   the model does not hold as absent rather than showing them empty. See
   /What is missing/.
5. *Fit the quotes to the parameterisation.* The person chooses *Calibrate
   candidate*. The system has no calibration engine and no operation to call,
   so the step names the subject it needs and changes nothing. See /What is
   missing/.
6. *Read the calibration error.* The person reads the fit error for the
   candidate, by slice and overall. Nothing serves it: the only curve compute
   the server offers returns bootstrapped discount points, not a surface fit
   error.
7. *Read the slices.* The person inspects the smiles across tenors and the
   surface mesh. The snapshot read is keyed by an =oresmd_uri= and reconstructs
   curve or grid points; it has no surface or slice shape, so the screen shows
   the quotes alone.
8. *Refresh or leave.* The person either excludes a quote, changes the as-of
   and refits, or accepts the candidate. Refitting repeats step 5.
9. *Leave unpublished.* The person chooses *Leave unpublished* and returns to
   the list. The system records no publication: the surface stays a candidate,
   and the published object pricing reads is unchanged.

Calibration, if it existed, would be a server-side computation that writes a
derived series, so the journey would not carry state between steps: the reply
would carry the candidate. Nothing in this journey publishes.

* Screens and wireframes

One screen. The surface row and its as-of sit at the top, the quotes and the
parameterisation sit in the middle, and the calibration error and the slices
they produce sit at the bottom, so the person reads cause above effect. The
state line at the foot says the surface is a candidate only.

The generic history read is the one with =entity_type=
=ores.marketdata.market_series=; =market_series= registers a history provider.
The snapshot and buckets reads are keyed by the =oresmd_uri=, not by a typed
surface identity, and both read now: =.get= has no as-of parameter and
=.buckets= serves five half-hour buckets ending now. The approval subject is
the generic one; the approval kind that would carry how many approvals a
surface publish needs and which group decides it does not exist yet.

* What is missing

1. *There is no surface model.* No table carries a surface's axes, its
   quoting convention or its parameterisation, so the screen cannot show what
   the surface is. The candidate is a surface entity, and its axe and
   parameter entities, in =ores.marketdata= or =ores.refdata=. Until then the
   screen lists =market_series= rows whose =series_subclass= is volatility and
   marks the missing fields as absent.
2. *There is no calibration engine and no operation to call it.* The subject
   =marketdata.v1.surface.calibrate= would have to be added, with a request
   that names the surface and the as-of and a response that carries the
   fitted points and the fit error. Until then the *Calibrate candidate*
   button names the step and stays inert.
3. *There is no quoting convention and no strike-label vocabulary.* Nothing
   says whether a strike is an absolute level, a delta, or a moneyness, so a
   strike axis cannot be labelled and the quotes cannot be mapped to the
   surface's parameterisation. The candidate is a vol quoting convention in
   =ores.refdata=. Until then the screen shows the raw =oresmd_uri=
   coordinates and no axis labels.
4. *There is no calibration-error read.* The subject
   =marketdata.v1.surface.calibration.get= would have to be added to return
   the residual per quote and the error per slice. Until then the calibration
   error panel is empty and labelled.
5. *There is no surface or slice read.* =marketdata.v1.curve_snapshot.get=
   reconstructs curve or grid points at one instant and has no surface shape,
   and there is no slice subject. The candidate is a surface read, with a
   slice subject such as =marketdata.v1.surface-slice.get=, on top of the
   modelled surface. Until then the screen shows the quotes alone; the
   concurrent slices journey
   [[id:F2E3F72C-60B9-4D23-A51F-B426B481470D][Inspect a surface's slices]]
   records the same gap from the reading side.
6. *A read cannot be taken as of a time in the past.* Both snapshot reads
   always read now, so the as-of the person chooses cannot be served over the
   wire; the point-in-time read exists only as the repository method
   =read_as_of=. The candidate is an =as_of= parameter on the snapshot
   request. Until then the screen offers the current snapshot and cannot
   reproduce yesterday's calibration.
7. *The surface has no typed identity at the wire.* Every read is keyed by
   the =oresmd_uri= string, so a caller cannot ask for "the USD swaption
   surface" without knowing the URI spelling. The candidate is a typed
   surface read, =marketdata.v1.surfaces.*=. Until then the screen shows the
   URI as the identity.
