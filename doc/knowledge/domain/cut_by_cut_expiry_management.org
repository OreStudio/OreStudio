#+title: Cut-by-cut expiry management
#+description: Options are expired per cut and pair against spots that do not move under the user, with a preview and undo.

* Summary

Expiry management is the operational process that decides every option at a
cut. The cut is the unit of work: an operator selects a date, a cut and a set of
pairs, and the system lists the options to decide against spots loaded once and
held still, so that options do not change state while the operator works. A
tolerance highlights options near the money, actions are applied in bulk, a
timeline shows which of the day's cuts are done, and a preview shows the trades
and the P&L impact before anything is booked. Every action can be undone.

* Detail

** The working set

The operator chooses the expiry date, the cut (or all cuts), the pairs (one, a
set, or all), and optionally books, portfolios and counterparties. Several pairs
can be expired in one go, or books separately, but the system must say when
options in the cut were left out. The cut and its cut code are defined in
[[id:3B39B2E2-6E05-4DF8-B68B-6ACA12F2FCE9][The option-expiry cut code]]; the cut as a frozen body of data is
a different sense of the word, in [[id:B5214F3A-A72E-421E-82BC-79338B2E95CB][Cuts]].

** Spots held still

Spots are loaded at the start or on demand and are not refreshed on their own,
because a moving spot would move options between actions under the operator.
Stale spots and stale trades are marked: a trade that was booked, amended or
cancelled since the last load is flagged, and running on stale data raises a
warning and a final check. The operator can override a spot, or give a range.
A tolerance, in pips or in percent, highlights the options whose decision a
small move would change; see
[[id:970D334D-2576-41BF-8664-1AA4CB76D31B][Pin risk in the expiry of near-the-money options]].

** Actions and views

The actions are exercise, expire, fix and leave, applied to one option or many.
/Fix/ shows the non-deliverable fixings due; /leave/ sets an option aside for a
later decision, and must be signed off. Two views serve two needs: a grouped
view, with a pane per action, shows options moving between actions and suits a
cut with many options near the money; a flat view, with pair, book, party,
product, amounts, strike, rebate, status and action, shows many options at
once. Column profiles suit vanilla and exotic cuts. Trades with other banks are
marked, because they are confirmed with the counterparty, particularly where
the bank is long. The trades the process generated, and any write-offs, have
views of their own.

** The cut timeline

A timeline shows the cuts of the day that are done and those still open.

** Non-deliverable options

A non-deliverable option takes two steps: it is exercised into a
non-deliverable forward, and that forward is then fixed. The forward's fixing
date is shown beside the option; it is normally the same day, but holidays can
move it. See [[id:53DB3AF1-6FA4-4B53-9D8E-A65F739F6F4D][Fixing dates and holiday calendars]].

** Netting, undo and export

Cash settlements of a structure are netted by default; see
[[id:04278F00-8B96-4987-9AF1-61E971B9087C][Default expiry actions and the expiry of structures]].
The operator can list the actions the process took and undo some or all of
them, unexpiries and unfixes included; see
[[id:0E638BE0-E3DA-4D05-A2FC-0F52FC1D24FC][Reversal of expiry events and post-expiry option states]]. The result exports as the
expiry report, by portfolio.

** Preview before commit

A preview creates every trade the actions would book without booking them, and
shows the risk and P&L impact. Committing a preview fails, with an explanation,
if trades or rates have changed since it was made.

** Queues and automation

Expiries can also be worked as queues beside the authorisation queue: a /flow/
queue for vanillas, barriers, digitals and resettables, and a /non-flow/ queue
for more complex products, configured by operations. Expiry can be automatic
under defined conditions, such as a spot that matches the expiry rate from an
automatic source, but is manual by default. This sits beside the documentation's
own presumption: the 1998 FX and Currency Option Definitions presume automatic
exercise unless the parties state otherwise, which the 2005 Barrier Option
Supplement asks them to do expressly. A system should therefore record, per
trade, whether automatic exercise applies. An /expiry report/ lists the options
expiring over a short horizon, highlights those in the money beyond a threshold,
and can be scheduled.
