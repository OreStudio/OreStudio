The tenant administrator withdraws a role through the browser's own route and
puts it back. This is what "the tenant can say which roles a member may ask
for" means as behaviour rather than as a column, and it is the half a seed
cannot show.

Commands, in one session against the running BFF on port 21402:

    POST /api/session           as tenant_admin@acme_corporation
    POST /api/session/party     the tenant administrator holds several parties
    GET  /api/roles
    PUT  /api/roles/<Operations id>   {"...","requestable": false}
    GET  /api/roles
    ... then the member asks for it from the shell ...

Output:

    before:   cb4fad75-3d63-4dd6-a24d-d976c5556840 version 1 requestable True
    PUT status:204
    after:    version 2 requestable False

The member, asking for the same role that was accepted in
=evidence/requestable_ask.txt=, now reads:

    > role_request_operations ask-for-roles cb4fad75-3d63-4dd6-a24d-d976c5556840 "I cover the trading desk next week"
    {"result":{"outcome":"denied","code":"not_requestable","message":"This role is not one a member may ask for.","fields":[]},"request_id":""}

The role was taken back up afterwards, so the environment is as it was:

    now:      version 2 requestable False
    PUT back status:204
    restored: version 3 requestable True
    offered count: 8

Two things follow. The answer is the tenant's and not the platform's, because
the same role and the same member give opposite outcomes either side of one
write. And the write goes through the ordinary role path, so the row carries
the version and the audit tail every role change carries rather than a special
one for this flag.
