* Goal

Pricing reads the surface. A report binds to a published surface as it binds to a published curve, and the ORE export writes the published grid as ORE volatility quotes with the =fx_volatility_config= that describes the grid. See [[id:FB8F701C-E56A-49A9-AA90-1B6BD0B02092][A report binds to a published curve]] and [[id:53D14F31-EE32-493E-BBEB-140AA4906BE4][Constructing a volatility surface: from quotes to a published set]].

* Acceptance

- A report's market binding can name a published surface, and cannot name a candidate.
- The export writes each grid point as an ORE volatility quote and writes a matching =fx_volatility_config=.
- ORE loads the exported surface and prices an FX option from it.
- At the strikes of a test trade, ORE's interpolated volatility matches the calibrated surface within a tolerance the test states.
- A report binds only to a surface of its own party.

* Plan

Two parts.

*Part 1, merged in #4577.* The market data export writes a surface only as published: the newest published set at or before the close, each grid point an FX option volatility quote whose strike is a delta label (=DEL/Spot/Put/25= is =25P=, =ATM= stays =ATM=). A candidate is left out. A pair with a published surface takes its option quotes from the surface alone, because the pair's raw quotes share its keys. This also fixed a break: the calibration's points have no ORE key, so before it the export failed for any party with a calibration.

*Part 2.* The matching configuration and the ORE check, for every pair the system quotes. All initial data goes through DQ. A generation script writes it, a methodology says where each value comes from, and a dataset carries the provenance. Three units, one PR each:

- *2a. Curate the surface blueprints through DQ.* The derived source quotes ten pairs (AUD/USD, EUR/CHF, EUR/GBP, EUR/JPY, EUR/USD, GBP/USD, NZD/USD, USD/CAD, USD/CHF, USD/JPY), each on the same 35 points: 1W to 1Y by ATM, 25RR, 25BF, 10RR and 10BF. Only EUR/USD has a blueprint, from a hand-written seed. A generator in =projects/ores.seeder/datasets/fx_vol_surfaces/= writes one SSVI blueprint for each pair, with its methodology, into a =refdata.fx_vol_surface_blueprints= dataset of the =system_core= bundle. A new publish, =refdata.v1.ops.publish_fx_vol_surface_blueprints_from_dq=, writes each blueprint with its pillars and sources, and the pair's derived and output series. The hand-written seeds go. Provisioning already copies every system blueprint to every party.
- *2b. Curate each Acme party's FX volatility configuration through DQ.* =generate_configuration_data.py= gains an FX volatility curve of dimension Smile and smile type Delta for each quoted pair whose two currencies the party builds curves for: the blueprint's expiries, deltas =10P,25P,ATM,25C,10C=, the pair's FX option convention, FX spot and funding curves. The party's today's market names it under the pair. The smile needs a DQ artefact (=fx_volatility_smiles= has none) and a place in the curve configuration publish. A methodology file states the choices and their sources. Coverage: Group 3 pairs, UK 10, US 6, HK 3.
- *2c. The ORE check.* A scripted check runs the ORE binary of the ORE build (=App/ore=) on the exported quotes and that configuration with an FX option at a test strike, inverts the NPV to a volatility, and compares it with the SSVI surface at that strike within a stated tolerance. It sits beside =projects/ores.ore/scripts/quantlib_fx_dates=.

Realism is outside this task. The derived quotes have plausible ATM levels (3% to 10%), but their smiles are near flat: a 25-delta risk reversal of 0.01 to 0.17 vol points against a market 0.3 to 1.5, a 25-delta butterfly under 0.02 against 0.15 to 0.3. The derived source infers skew from daily fixings, which carry little of it. Realistic initial surfaces need curated smile levels in the synthetic generator task.
