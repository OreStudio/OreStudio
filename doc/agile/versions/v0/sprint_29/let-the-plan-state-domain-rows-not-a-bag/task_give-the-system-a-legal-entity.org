* The measurement, first

- =ores_dq_lei_entities_artefact_tbl= holds 38,213 rows, all under the system
  tenant =ffffffff-ffff-ffff-ffff-ffffffffffff=.
- The artefact type is registered, with two target subjects:
  =refdata.v1.ops.publish_lei_parties_from_dq= and
  =refdata.v1.ops.publish_lei_counterparties_from_dq=.
- Neither carries the LEI. =ores_refdata_counterparties_tbl= has a short code, a
  full name and a party type, and no identifier column at all, so the LEI is
  lost on the way to anything a party can read.

So the publication machinery exists and publishes the wrong thing for this
purpose: a party gets names, and a credit trade needs an identifier.

* The fork

Two shapes, and the choice decides where a legal entity lives.

*A: a legal entity in refdata, published from DQ, as world data.* A new domain
table for the legal entity with the LEI as its key, a publish operation from the
LEI artefact, and a read operation the synthetic service and any screen use.
Names, jurisdiction, status and classifications come with it, so a counterparty
can later reference it instead of carrying a name. This is the larger change and
the one that makes the reference real.

*B: publish the LEI artefact into each tenant inside DQ.* The rows become the
party's own copy and =list_lei_entities= answers for a party session, with no new
entity. Cheaper by far, and it leaves legal entities living in a data-quality
artefact rather than in the system's model: a screen cannot show them, and a
counterparty still cannot reference one.

Recommendation: *A*, because the question that started this was what a reference
means. B makes the credit population book without making the reference true.

* Result

Legal entities are a refdata entity of their own, keyed by LEI, and the LEI
reaches a party through the DQ publish. Measured on a recreated database: the
tenant holds 42,388 legal entities with their LEIs, published from the GLEIF
LEI artefact every tenant already receives.

Credit books on the back of it: five routed products, one trade each, every one
stating =LEI= and a real identifier rather than the curve name the ORE import
keeps.

* What the path is

=ores_dq_lei_entities_artefact_tbl= -> =ores_refdata_publish_legal_entities_from_dq_fn=
-> =ores_refdata_legal_entities_tbl= (tenant's own copy) -> =list_legal_entities=
-> the synthetic scope -> a credit trade's typed reference.

The publish is registered as a third target of the existing LEI artefact, so it
runs wherever the LEI dataset already publishes its parties and counterparties,
and the LEI stops being lost on the way.

* Two defects found by doing it

- The schema manifests are hand-maintained: =refdata_create.sql= and
  =dq_create.sql= list every file with =\ir=. A generated table that is not
  added to them is never created, and the failure appears as a missing relation
  at publish time rather than as a missing file.
- =legal_name= cannot be a natural key. The dataset holds thousands of entities
  that share a name, so the uniqueness the natural key implied made the publish
  fail on the first duplicate.

The legal entity landed with #4074 and the LEI publish reads the DQ artefact the
tenant already holds: a provisioned tenant carries 42,388 entities, and a credit
trade references one by its twenty-character LEI.
