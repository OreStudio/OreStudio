* Plan

Two documents and no code.

1. Rewrite =doc/knowledge/architecture/polymorphic_types_over_nats.org= as a
   deprecated page: what the pattern was, why it is wrong, the RFL constraint
   that produced it, and the rule that replaces it.
2. Add the rule to the Regime 3 section of
   =doc/knowledge/architecture/data_oriented_design.org=, since that page
   pointed at the deprecated one as the authority for polymorphism.
3. Record the replacement design on the story that does the work.

The rule: no =std::variant= field in a message; no =std::string= field holding
an encoded value; a heterogeneous result set travels as one typed array per
concrete type, joined by key, and the arrays are generated where the types
they hold are generated.

* Result

* Result

The polymorphic-payload pattern is deprecated and the replacement is recorded.

- =polymorphic_types_over_nats.org= now opens with a deprecation notice, states
  the rule, keeps the RFL constraint that produced the old designs, and records
  =instrument_payload= as the violation it was.
- The Regime 3 section of the data-oriented design page states the rule
  directly instead of delegating to the deprecated page, and its checklist row
  reads "no polymorphic payload fields" rather than "polymorphism is closed and
  versioned".
- The story carries the replacement design: struct-of-arrays at the leaf level,
  generated, joined by trade id, one round trip, and the discriminator taken
  from the trade type the anchor already carries.

The sweep found no other code to change: =instrument_payload= is the only
untyped payload in the tree and no protocol header declares a =std::variant=
field. The =std::string type= fields in ores.iam are tenant and account
classifications, which are business data rather than tags.

No code changed on this task.
