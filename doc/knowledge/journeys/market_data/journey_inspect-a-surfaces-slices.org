This page documents a [[id:F44766FA-1E9C-416F-849A-E131101B2ECA][user journey]]: A trader or quant inspects a candidate surface's smiles across tenors and its mesh, and checks the calendar and butterfly arbitrage between them.

A user journey describes what one person is trying to do, before anything is
built. It is a design document that outlives any sprint, so it names the
screens, the entity fields each screen shows, and the operations and messages
each step needs.

* Goal

"I want to see the smiles this surface produces at each tenor, the mesh they
make together, and whether the surface has an arbitrage in it." The journey is
done when the person has read the smile at each tenor of interest, seen the
mesh, and read the calendar and butterfly arbitrage checks. The person does not
publish here.

* Actor and trigger

The actor is the trader or the quant. The trader owns the candidate surface;
the quant owns the model and reads the same screen. Neither decides to publish
here: an approver does that in a different journey. The trigger is a calibration
just run, a smile that looks odd at one tenor, a mesh that folds, or a
pricing question about an expiry and a strike. The journey starts at a
candidate surface and ends when the person leaves it, still a candidate.

This journey reads and diagnoses; it changes nothing. It is the surface-shaped
check the maker runs before the candidate goes to a second pair of eyes.

* Steps

1. *Open the candidate surface.* The person opens *Curves and surfaces* and
   selects the candidate surface. The system reads the =market_series= row and
   its =derivation_kind=/config/version, which says the surface is derived and
   by which recipe.
2. *Read the surface.* The person chooses *Read surface*. There is no surface
   read: =marketdata.v1.curve_snapshot.get= is keyed by an =oresmd_uri= and
   reconstructs curve or grid points, and it has no surface shape. The system
   lists the surface series row and the quotes it holds, and marks the surface
   itself as not served. See /What is missing/.
3. *Read the smiles across tenors.* The person picks a set of tenors and reads
   the smile at each. Each smile is a slice: the vol at a fixed expiry across
   strikes, from the low-strike put wing through the at-the-money point to the
   high-strike call wing. There is no slice read, so no smile can be drawn. See
   /What is missing/.
4. *Overlay the quotes on a smile.* The person reads each raw quote of that
   tenor against the fitted smile. The quotes come from
   =marketdata.v1.market_observations.list_by_series_id=, one row per
   =oresmd_uri=, but the fitted smile does not exist to overlay them on.
5. *Read the mesh.* The person reads the mesh: the vol across expiry and
   strike at once, either as a grid or as a shaded total-variance view. The
   mesh needs the surface read of step 2 and a mesh read, and neither exists.
   See /What is missing/.
6. *Check calendar arbitrage.* The person checks that total variance does not
   fall as expiry grows, at a fixed strike. No operation returns total
   variance along an expiry axis and no check runs, so the screen states the
   check it needs and marks it as absent. See /What is missing/.
7. *Check butterfly arbitrage.* The person checks that the risk-neutral
   density implied by each smile is non-negative, which fails when a smile is
   too concave. No operation returns a density or a butterfly check, so the
   screen states the check it needs and marks it as absent. See /What is
   missing/.
8. *Leave.* The person returns to the list. The candidate surface is unchanged;
   the person did not write or publish anything.

Every read here would be server-side and synchronous, so the journey carries no
state of its own.

* Screens and wireframes

One screen. The surface and the tenor picker sit at the top, the smiles and the
mesh sit in the middle, and the two arbitrage checks sit at the foot, so the
person reads the slices, then the whole surface, then its faults.

There is no surface model and no slice model, so the smiles, the mesh and the
two arbitrage checks have no entity to compose. The screen can read the series
row and its quotes only; see /What is missing/.

* Operations and messages

| Step | Operation | Subject | Status |
|------+-----------+---------+--------|

| Open the candidate surface | Read one series | =marketdata.v1.market_series.get= | exists |
| Read the surface | Read the surface | =marketdata.v1.surface.get= | missing |
| Read the smiles | Read one smile (slice) | =marketdata.v1.surface-slice.get= | missing |
| Overlay the quotes | List the observations of one series | =marketdata.v1.market_observations.list_by_series_id= | exists |
| Read the mesh | Read the surface mesh | =marketdata.v1.surface-mesh.get= | missing |
| Check calendar arbitrage | Read the calendar-arbitrage check | =marketdata.v1.surface.arbitrage= | missing |
| Check butterfly arbitrage | Read the butterfly-arbitrage check | =marketdata.v1.surface.arbitrage= | missing |
| Read the recipe lineage | Read the lineage of a derived point | =marketdata.v1.observation_lineages.list= | exists |
| Check the change trail | Read the series' history | =marketdata.v1.history.get= | exists |

No surface subject exists. Every surface read above is a candidate subject, not
a subject the server serves today. Only the series and observation reads, the
lineage read and the generic history read are real. The generic history read is
=marketdata.v1.history.get= with =entity_type= =ores.marketdata.market_series=;
=market_series= and =observation_lineage= register history providers.

* What is missing

1. *There is no surface read.* The subject =marketdata.v1.surface.get= would
   have to be added to return the fitted surface for an =oresmd_uri= and an
   as-of. Until then the screen shows the series row and marks the surface
   panel as not served. A surface model is the precondition: there is no table
   that carries the surface's axes, its quoting convention or its
   parameterisation.
2. *There is no slice read.* The subject =marketdata.v1.surface-slice.get=
   would have to be added to return the smile at one expiry: the vol at each
   strike, with the strike labels. Until then no smile can be drawn and the
   screen shows the raw quotes of that tenor alone. A strike-label vocabulary
   does not exist, so a strike axis cannot yet be labelled.
3. *There is no mesh read.* The subject =marketdata.v1.surface-mesh.get= would
   have to be added, or the surface read extended, to return the vol across
   expiry and strike as a grid. Until then the mesh panel is empty and
   labelled.
4. *There is no arbitrage check of any kind.* No operation exposes a residual,
   a slice, a total variance or a density, so neither the calendar check nor
   the butterfly check can run. The candidate is a subject such as
   =marketdata.v1.surface.arbitrage=, returning the total variance along an
   expiry axis and the implied density along a smile. Until then the screen
   names both checks and marks them as absent, and it does not compute them on
   the client, because the client has no fitted surface to check.
5. *The surface has no typed identity at the wire.* Every read is keyed by the
   =oresmd_uri= string, so a caller cannot ask for "the USD swaption surface"
   without knowing the URI spelling, and a slice cannot be addressed by expiry
   and strike. The candidate is a typed surface and slice read,
   =marketdata.v1.surfaces.*=. Until then the screen shows the URI as the
   identity.
6. *A read cannot be taken as of a time in the past.* The snapshot read always
   reads now, so the person cannot inspect the surface as of the instant it
   was calibrated. The candidate is an =as_of= parameter on the surface read.
   Until then the screen reads the current surface only.
