This page is a [[id:671F18E4-E09C-4B3B-BD24-D33DF8AE38A6][capture]] in the *next* bucket of the product backlog — a pre-sprint idea, not yet pulled into a sprint as a story.

* What

Count ticks at every stage boundary and check that the counts balance. The
stages are synthetic =published= per feed, then marketdata =received=,
=dropped= by reason (=unmapped=, =stale=, =overflow=, =no_route=),
=stored_source_ticks=, =stored_rows= and =republished=. Each counter is
monotonic. marketdata and synthetic publish a timestamped sample of them once a
minute, and telemetry stores it as it stores =nats_samples=, so a minute costs a
few rows and a long history is cheap. Rates come from the difference between
two samples.

Each interval must balance: =received= equals =dropped= plus =stored= plus the
change in the queue, and =published= is about =received=. A stage that does not
balance names where ticks go missing. An =ores.shell= command,
=marketdata flow --since <duration>=, prints the funnel per minute, the balance
of each interval and the stage that is off.

* Why

On 2026-10-26 the machine went down overnight. Two defects together caused it: the shared Hawkes heat ran the synthetic feeds at about 300 times their configured rate, and the NATS client kept every message it received. Finding both took ad hoc sampling of systemd and /proc, parsing marketdata's once-a-minute INGEST STATUS log line, NATS monitoring endpoints, Postgres row counts and a core dump. Most of that time went on two questions: is the volume what was
configured, and where do the ticks go? A funnel answers both at a glance. The
largest anomaly that night, about 6,400 unmapped ticks a second dropped before
the store, never reached any table, so no query over stored data could have
shown it.

Today the counters exist only in marketdata's memory and in a log line, and
synthetic logs its publish count every hundred ticks. Nothing is kept, so after
the crash no record said what the tick rate had been.
