* Summary

The evolution prototype argues that one instant and a range are the same view at
different zoom. The object and its time control sit at the top, the aligned grid
of instants by nodes in the middle, and the added, dropped and two-instant
comparison at the bottom.

It is throwaway: not production code, reading no service, every value mock. It
exists because the [[id:28D1545F-1BE8-4C14-8B69-292DC12BC45F][journey]] settles
what the evolution view reads but not how it is shown. The alignment is the view,
and the nodes an instant added or dropped are what the range adds to the instant.

Open the [[file:watch-a-curve-or-surface-evolve/][evolution view]]; it opens on
variant *A*, market =churn= and the range zoom.

* Detail

** The question

The journey's Goal is to see how a curve or surface moved, to read one instant
and a range at the zoom the person needs, and to name the nodes that appeared or
dropped between two instants, and it leaves open whether those readings are two
screens or one. A term structure is drawn from points that rarely share one
observation instant, so a curve stitched across market horizons draws as the same
shape as a curve read from one, and the ages are what tell them apart.

** The variants

Switch with the *Layout* buttons in the bottom bar, or =?variant=. The variant is
the reading, which the journey records as the central design question.

| Variant | Name       | The structural argument |
|---------+------------+-------------------------|
| =a=     | matrix     | The matrix of instants by nodes: one row per node, one column per instant, the value and the age of its point in every cell. The alignment drawn at its plainest, and the shortest read of what changed where. |
| =b=     | sparklines | A per-node row with a sparkline of the range, its first and last value, its move, its gaps and its age at the read. Reads one node's shape, and gives the node set only as gaps. |
| =c=     | compare    | Two chosen instants side by side, with the signed difference per node and the node set labelled *added*, *dropped* or *both*. The journey's comparison step, and the narrowest of the three. |

The variants disagree about the reading, not colour: *A* reads the whole grid,
*B* one node across the range, *C* two columns and their difference.

** The states

Switch with the *Market* buttons, or =?state=. The state is the mock market the
page reads.

| State     | What it shows |
|-----------+---------------|
| =stable=  | The same six tenors at every instant, each published within two minutes of its instant. Read from one instant, the spread stays under the threshold, and the screen says *one market state*. |
| =churn=   | The 9M tenor is carried only by the first instant and the 2Y only from the third, with publication lags from 20 seconds to two and a half hours. Nodes appear and drop across the range, and the spread crosses the threshold, so the screen says *stitched across market horizons*. |
| =surface= | A swaption surface keyed by expiry and delta and built horizon by horizon, with the 2Y ATM appearing at the third instant and the 30Y ATM carried only at the first. Draws the change from the first instant to the last as a heat map of the grid, not as a line. |

The threshold is the argument in one number: the half hour of spread the journey
states, at or over which the panel is flagged stale and the curve is called
stitched across market horizons, and under which it is called one market state.

The zoom is the second switch, =?show=: =instant= draws one column and =range= all
five, the same grid at one column or five, which is the prototype's whole claim.

** Driving it

The bottom bar switches layout, zoom and market, and every button rewrites the
query string rather than hiding panels in place, so any combination is a URL. The
compared instants are =?a= and =?b=, set by the two selects in the compare variant,
and the series URI opens the oresmd URL editor in an overlay. *Apply* is inert: it
names the missing subject, that the range always ends at now.

** What is fake

Everything. The series URI, its subclass, its asset classes and its derivation;
the five instants; the six tenors of the curve markets and the seven expiries
and deltas of the surface; every value; every node's publication lag and
therefore every age; and the read's own instant, the mock's last column. There is
no server, no session and no build step, and nothing is stored. The three plots
are drawn on the page by a dependency-free SVG helper in the same file, and the
half-hour threshold is the journey's own number.

** Where the code lives

=doc/prototypes/market_data/watch-a-curve-or-surface-evolve/=. One file,
=index.html=, holding its markup, styles and script, with a plotting helper =PC=
in the same file, and no framework and no build step; there is no =prototype.css=
and no =prototype.js=. The site copies the directory as it stands through
=ores-deploy-web-app= and adds nothing to it. The URI button opens
=../oresmd-url-editor/index.html= in an overlay, borrowing the editor prototype
beside it.

** The verdict

The group accepted the argument rather than one layout: one instant and a range
are the same view at different zoom, the alignment is the view, and the nodes an
instant added or dropped are what the range adds. It accepted the readings the
page insists on: every value carries the age of the point behind it, the read
states the instant the ages are relative to, and a spread at or over half an hour
is drawn as a stitched curve.

What stayed on the page as the record: the three layouts and the three markets,
because the churn and surface cases are what produce the added and dropped nodes
and the warning; the two zooms; and the surface heat map. No Gemini review was
raised.

** What it does not settle

Which layout wins. The matrix, the sparkline list and the compare are all drawn
and none is chosen; the choice belongs to the reads the server will answer.

The as-of read and the range. =marketdata.v1.curve_snapshot.get= answers for now
and carries no time, and =marketdata.v1.ops.get_curve_snapshot_buckets= ends at
now, so the range always ends at the read's own instant and the past is not shown.

The set of instants and the difference. No request names two or more instants and
returns one snapshot per instant, so the comparison and its difference are
computed on the page from two reads.

The alignment and the shape. The reply is unaligned with text values, so the grid
is aligned in the browser on the =oresmd_uri= and a hole in a bucket is a gap,
not a typed null; and with no declared node set, a node that should be there and
is not cannot be told from a node not part of the curve at all.
