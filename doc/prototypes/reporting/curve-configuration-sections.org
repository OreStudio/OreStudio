* Summary

The curve configuration page was drawn with one tab per section, and it does
not work. A =curveconfig.xml= declares nineteen sections, one tab each does not
fit a screen, and most of them are empty in any real document.

This prototype asks a narrower question: does the shape the product pricing
screen already uses for its products tab read here? That screen holds a
document with a hundred product types and draws it as one tab, a searchable rail
of the products grouped by asset class, and the chosen product's settings in a
workbench beside it. The same shape for curves is one *Curves* tab, a rail of
the curves grouped by the section that declares them, and the chosen curve's
parameters beside it.

It answers to the story
[[id:18A29061-FBAD-4C5A-90A1-F02039F791BF][Standardise the curve configuration's parameters on the one contract]],
and to the page it argues about,
[[id:FCD8EE04-D549-45B2-AE29-8C5FBBC66B74][Draw the curve configuration page with the shared leaf parts]].
It is throwaway: it reads no service, writes nothing to one, and states what it
does not draw.

* Detail

** The question the screen answers

A person opens a curve configuration to read or check a curve: its currency, its
discount curve, its interpolation, its tolerance, and the elements it holds. The
section a curve belongs to matters, because the entry's table and its leaves
differ per section, but it is not what the person came for. A tab per section
asks them to find the section first; a rail of the curves asks them to find the
curve.

** The fixtures

Three documents, behind =?sections=1|4|16=, because the navigation has to hold
at both ends of what ORE ships:

| Fixture | Sections that hold curves | Curves |
|---------+---------------------------+--------|
| 1       | YieldCurves               | 4      |
| 4       | YieldCurves, DefaultCurves, FXVolatilities, SwaptionVolatilities | 45 |
| 16      | all but three of the nineteen | 113 |

The counts come from measuring the 89 =curveconfig*.xml= files ORE ships: the
median document populates 3 of the 19 sections, the smallest 1, the largest 16,
and 59 of the 89 populate five or fewer. A navigation that only works for a
small document is a navigation for the wrong screen.

** What the prototype asks

1. *Is one tab for the curves right, with the sections in the rail?* The rail
   keeps the section as the group a curve sits in, so the person still sees which
   family a curve belongs to, and never opens a tab that holds nothing.
2. *Do the sections that hold nothing earn a row?* They sit behind one
   disclosure that names the count, so the document is complete and the rail is
   short. The alternative is a rail that lists nineteen rows of which fifteen say
   "empty".
3. *Does the workbench carry the screen?* The chosen curve's parameters, their
   documentation, the Simple and Advanced switch and the elements the curve
   holds are all in the workbench, as a product's settings are.
4. *Does the group and the curve read as one list?* The rail is a tree:
   a section is a group, a curve is a row. A person who does not know the
   section can type a curve or a currency and reach it, because the filter
   matches both.

** States the page draws

- A document with one section, where the rail is a single group.
- A document with sixteen, where the rail scrolls rather than overflows the page,
  and the empty three are behind the disclosure.
- A filter that matches no curve, which says so.
- No curve chosen, which names what the workbench will hold.
- A curve with a parameter the contract does not document, which shows the ORE
  name and a note and never hides the value.

** What is fake

Every value. The sections and their order are =curveconfig.xsd='s own, and the
descriptions are the seeded ones. The curves, the counts, the values and the
documentation are fixtures written for this page. The save does not exist: the
page is read only, as the screen is until the write path lands.

** What the page does not draw

The nested collections of an entry — Segments, Quotes, BootstrapConfig, Report —
which are named with whether the entry states them and are not drawn. The
proposed screen still owes them, and they are the task
[[id:894F3A64-F564-44B2-A6F1-AB00FF30A6B9][Draw the nested collections of a curve entry]].

** Where the code lives

=doc/prototypes/reporting/curve-configuration-sections/index.html= and
=prototype.js= beside it. Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript, the design tokens
copied from =projects/ores.web/packages/web/src/styles.css=, no framework, no
build and no backend.

** What it argues

That the section is the group and the curve is the row, and that the screen
should read as the product pricing screen does rather than as a document with
nineteen chapters. If that is agreed, the page keeps =Details=, replays the
sections as groups in one *Curves* tab, and the standard's "one tab per section"
is amended to "one tab for the collection, and the section as the group within
it".
