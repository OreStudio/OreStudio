* Goal

The installation can name every store it keeps, in the words a person reads,
before anybody writes a policy against one. A store is a row: its code, the name
and description the screen shows instead of the physical target, the component
that writes it, whether the platform or a tenant owns the rows, the shape and name
of its target, the column a deletion compares, how its size is measured, and the
bound it ships with. The sweep, the screen and the alerts all read the catalogue
rather than carrying their own list of tables.

* Acceptance

- The catalogue is an entity with generated operations, delivered as platform data, so a store is described without a rebuild
- A row states its code, its display name, its description, the component that writes it, the scope that owns it (platform or tenant), the kind and name of its target, the column a delete compares, and how its size is measured
- A row states its default bound: a policy with an action, a period and a cadence, a bound by content keying that needs no policy, or no bound at all where none can be expressed as an age
- The SQL refuses an unknown target kind, an unknown scope, an unknown measure, a policy with no period and a period with no policy
- The rows for the nine stores measured in the investigation are seeded, each with the period the story's decision states, and a second publication leaves one row per store

* Plan

- Model the entity in the scheduler, beside the retention policy, because the
  sweep, the screen and the alerts all read it and the scheduler already owns
  the firing they ride on. Follow the policy's model: =code= is the key, the
  audit tail comes from the entity generator, and the row is the platform's
  statement of a fact about the software.
- State the bound as a kind rather than always as an action and a period. Three
  kinds cover what the investigation found: =policy= (with an action, a period
  and a cadence), =keying= (the package cache, bounded by its digest), and
  =none= (the calendar dates, whose bound is a range and not an age). The SQL
  refuses a policy with no period and a period with no policy, so the two cannot
  disagree.
- Deliver the rows as platform data, like the policies: a DQ dataset,
  =scheduler.retained_stores=, with an artefact table the generator produces, a
  publish function that reconciles the whole row, a bundle member beside the
  policies, and the seed rows for the ten stores the investigation names.
- The whole row is reconciled and nothing is carried from the installation,
  because the catalogue states what the platform keeps and the policy records
  what an installation decided. A changed default therefore never rewrites a
  policy that already exists; that decision belongs to the task that creates the
  policies from the defaults.
- Prove it with the generated codec tests, the publish subject plan test, a new
  pgTAP test for the publish and the entity's checks, and the gates for the
  classes the change touches.
