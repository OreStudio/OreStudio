#+title: Official rate adapters go stale together
#+description: Every official rate adapter misses its heartbeat at the same moments, about fifteen times an hour, so the dashboard reports demanded ids not served while the adapters still run.
#+type: capture
#+level: cross
#+filetags: :marketdata:official:adapters:heartbeat:
#+created: 2026-11-23
#+updated: 2026-11-23

This page is a [[id:671F18E4-E09C-4B3B-BD24-D33DF8AE38A6][capture]] in the *inbox* bucket of the product backlog — a pre-sprint idea, not yet pulled into a sprint as a story.

* What

The market data service marks every official rate adapter stale at the same
moment, about fifteen times an hour, and each recovers seconds later. In
environment =eager_maxwell= on 2026-11-23, over one hour of
=ores.marketdata.service=, =rba-aonia= went stale 16 times and =nyfed-sofr=,
=ecb-frankfurter=, =imf-frankfurter=, =mas-sora=, =norgesbank-nowa=,
=riksbank-swestr= and =tcmb-frankfurter= 15 times each, every adapter at the
same timestamps, such as 21:40:32. Each stale is followed by the adapters
announcing again: =ecb-estr= announced 38 times since its service started at
15:39. While they are stale, the dashboard reports the demanded ids as not
served (153 at one reading), although nothing is wrong with the rates.

* Why

An operator reads a warning that is false most of the time, and a real outage
of an official source looks the same as the flapping.

Hypothesis, not yet proved: the cause is the adapter's single loop
(=official_adapter::loop= in =projects/ores.marketdata/official/src/app/official_adapter.cpp=).
Each pass sends every source's heartbeat, then lets one source read its
publisher through a blocking =refresh=. One read that takes longer than the
supervisor's 15-second timeout (=adapter_registry::default_heartbeat_timeout=)
holds back every heartbeat at once. That fits all the adapters going stale
together. The service's NATS log supports it but does not prove it: it is
silent from 21:40:04 to 21:42:34. The log does not say which publisher read
was slow, because a refresh logs only what it sent.

To settle it: log each refresh's duration with its source, then line the slow
reads up with the stale timestamps. If they match, beat from a thread of its
own, or read the publishers off the loop, so a slow read never delays a
heartbeat.
