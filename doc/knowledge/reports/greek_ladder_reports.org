#+title: Greek ladder reports
#+description: The .1 family and the wider greek ladders: what a .1 report is in ORE terms, the full ladder family across delta, gamma, vega, rho, theta and the cross terms, what each needs, and what the store holds
#+type: knowledge
#+level: cross
#+filetags: :knowledge:reporting:risk:greeks:sensitivity:ore:
#+created: 2026-11-03
#+updated: 2026-11-03

* Summary

ORE has no concept of a ".1"; it has a /shift/ — a shift type, a shift size,
a shift scheme and the tenors, expiries or strikes to shift — and it writes
the resulting value change to one =Delta= column. A .1 report is therefore a
sensitivity report bound to a configuration whose shift size is 0.1 in that
factor's own quote unit, and the family is as wide as the risk classes the
engine shifts, not just volatility. Two of the family the product owner asked
for — the risk reversal and the strangle — are not ORE factors at all: ORE
shifts an ATM-only grid or a strike-by-strike surface, so =RR= and =STR= must
be derived from the surface points by the smile identity that
[[id:5B513D53-5DF7-4540-99C0-60D909678873][FX Volatility Surface]] already states. The store names the sensitivity
document and switches the analytic on for nine seeded risk configurations, but
no configuration binds one, so today a sensitivity run shifts nothing. What
follows fixes the vocabulary, the family, what each ladder needs, and what the
store must grow before a single .1 report can be seeded.

* What a .1 report is in ORE terms

** ORE has a shift, not a .1

The sensitivity configuration is one XML document whose root is
=SensitivityAnalysis=, validated against =external/ore/xsd/sensitivity.xsd=.
It carries one block for each group of risk factors — discount curves, index
curves, yield curves, FX spots, FX volatilities, swaption volatilities, cap and
floor volatilities, CDS volatilities, default and credit curves, equity spots
and volatilities, both inflation curve families, both inflation cap/floor
families, dividend yields, base correlations, security spreads, commodity
curves and volatilities, intraday power curves, correlations and bond future
volatilities — plus four switches that are not factors: =ComputeGamma=,
=CrossGammaFilter=, =ThetaPeriod= and the par conversion group.

Every factor block states the same four things:

| Element | Meaning | Values |
|---------+---------+--------|
| =ShiftType= | how the number moves | =Relative=, =Absolute=, =EqualTo= |
| =ShiftSize= | by how much | a real, with an optional =key= for an alternative size |
| =ShiftScheme= | which direction the difference reads | =Forward=, =Backward=, =Central= |
| the bucket list | what to bump | =ShiftTenors=, =ShiftExpiries=, =ShiftStrikes=, =ShiftLossLevels=, =ShiftTerms= |

=ShiftType= and =ShiftScheme= are enums in =external/ore/xsd/ore_types.xsd=.
The shipped =external/ore/examples/MarketRisk/Input/sensitivity.xml= shows the
conventional numbers: curves at =Absolute= =0.0001= (one basis point) with a
=Central= =0.001= override keyed =IR_FD=; FX spots at =Relative= =0.01=; FX
volatilities at =Relative= =0.01= with an expiry list and an empty
=ShiftStrikes=; and =ThetaPeriod= =1D=.

No shipped example states a =ShiftSize= of 0.1, and the schema has no element
for one. A ".1 report" is a name for a shift size, and the size is meaningless
without the type and the unit it is quoted in.

** The Delta column is a value change, not a derivative

This is the load-bearing fact for the whole family, and it is worth stating
precisely because the prose catalogue on
[[id:04C68B55-E0B5-41CD-B90E-D670C17530B6][Risk Reporting]] writes the Greek as
a quotient.

ORE writes =sensitivity.csv= with =Delta= and =Gamma= columns
([[id:34C68112-7A34-4557-835C-BDCAA7BB9C2A][ORE output formats]], family
=sensitivity_report=). Both are money. Neither is divided by the shift size.
Measured on the shipped MarketRisk sample at commit =deb15641d0=:

#+begin_src python
import csv
sens = {}
with open('sensitivity.csv') as f:
    r = csv.reader(f); next(r)
    for row in r:
        if len(row) >= 10:
            sens[(row[0], row[2])] = (float(row[3]), float(row[8]))
with open('scenario.csv') as f:
    r = csv.reader(f); next(r)
    for row in r:
        if len(row) >= 8 and row[2] == 'Up':
            shift, delta = sens[(row[0], row[1])]
            print(row[1], shift, delta, float(row[7]))
#+end_src

Run it from =external/ore/examples/MarketRisk/ExpectedOutput/Sensi=. It prints
the factor, its shift size, =Delta= from the sensitivity report and =Difference=
from the scenario report.

| Trade | Factor | ShiftSize_1 | =Delta= | =Difference= in scenario.csv |
|-------+--------+-------------+---------+------------------------------|
| CAP_EUR | IndexCurve/EUR-EURIBOR-6M/11/10Y | 0.0001 | −137.61 | −137.61 |
| CAP_EUR | OptionletVolatility/EUR/8/2Y/0.04 | 0.0001 | −2.25 | −2.25 |
| CAP_EUR | OptionletVolatility/EUR/18/5Y/0.04 | 0.0001 | −59.14 | −59.14 |
| CAP_EUR | OptionletVolatility/EUR/23/7Y/0.04 | 0.0001 | −137.56 | −137.56 |

Every pair matches to the printed precision. So =Delta= is
$PV(\text{shifted}) - PV(\text{base})$, and the shift size is already inside
it. Two consequences follow, and they decide the whole design:

1. A .1 ladder is /not/ a comparison of a Vega against a spot move. It is the
   =Delta= column of a run configured with =ShiftSize= = 0.1. The number the
   trader reads is money per 0.1, and it is already in that unit.
2. Two reports over the same book that differ only in shift size differ only in
   their sensitivity configuration. Nothing in the report definition, the
   analytics or the reader has to know what ".1" means.

** The ATM-only switch is =ShiftStrikes=

ORE distinguishes "shift the smile" from "shift the ATM level" with the same
element. The two shipped configs are otherwise identical:
=external/ore/examples/MarketRisk/Input/SensiSmile/sensitivity_atmOnly.xml=
carries =<ShiftStrikes/>= for the swaption block, while
=sensitivity_fullSurface.xml= carries a strike list. Their run documents,
=ore_sensismile_atm.xml= and =ore_sensismile_surface.xml=, differ only in which
=marketConfigFile= and =sensitivityConfigFile= they name.

That is the direct ORE analogue of the .1 ATM and .1 surface reports, and it
means the ATM ladder is native while the full-surface ladder is native too —
provided the surface's strikes are nameable in the configuration.

** What ".1" means in the shift's own unit

The shift size is a bare real. Its unit comes from the factor:

| Factor class | Unit of the shift | 0.1 means |
|--------------+-------------------+-----------|
| Curve rate (discount, index, yield, credit, inflation) | a rate, decimal | 10 basis points |
| Volatility (FX, swaption, cap/floor, equity, commodity, CDS) | a volatility, decimal | 10 volatility points, if the surface is decimal |
| FX spot, equity spot | a ratio, if =Relative= | 10 per cent, if =Relative= |
| Theta | a period, not a size | the period itself |

The shipped example quotes FX volatilities at =Relative= =0.01=, which is one
per cent /of/ the volatility, not one volatility point. So "0.1 vol" could
mean a =Relative= shift of 0.1 (a tenth of the level — rarely intended) or an
=Absolute= shift of 0.1 on a surface stored in vol points. This ambiguity is
the first open question for the product owner, and it is the only one that
blocks every other decision.

* The ladder family

** Two axes, not one

A ladder is a Cartesian product: a *risk class* (which market data it bumps and
so which buckets it has) times an *order* (what it measures about that bump).

The risk class is fixed by which block of =sensitivity.xsd= the factor belongs
to. The order is fixed by four switches:

| Ladder order | What it is | What produces it |
|--------------+------------+------------------|
| Delta (first order) | the value change for one shifted factor | the =ShiftSize= and the bucket list, one factor at a time |
| Gamma (second order) | the curvature in one factor | =ComputeGamma= = true, or a =Central= shift scheme |
| Cross-gamma | the curvature between two factors | =CrossGammaFilter= pairs, and two shift sizes in one row |
| Theta | the value change for the passage of a period | =ThetaPeriod= |

** The full family across the risk classes

Each row is a ladder the engine can produce. The *bucket* column is the set of
columns a reader needs to place a figure on the ladder.

** The four vol ladders the trader asked for, and how they relate

The prose catalogue defines .1 ATM, .1 RR, .1 STR, .1 Triple and .1 Quad as
"the PV change from a 0.1 bump" to one or more smile components. On an FX
surface quoted as ATM, risk reversal and butterfly, that family is a linear
change of basis over the surface points, and
[[id:5B513D53-5DF7-4540-99C0-60D909678873][FX Volatility Surface]] already
states the identity:

#+begin_src text
sigma_call = sigma_ATM + sigma_BF + 0.5 * sigma_RR
sigma_put  = sigma_ATM + sigma_BF - 0.5 * sigma_RR
#+end_src

Read backwards, the three components are:

#+begin_src text
sigma_ATM = sigma_ATM
sigma_RR  = sigma_call - sigma_put
sigma_BF  = 0.5 * (sigma_call + sigma_put) - sigma_ATM
#+end_src

So, writing $D_x$ for the =Delta= column at surface point $x$:

| Report | What it is | Native in ORE? |
|--------+------------+----------------|
| .1 ATM | one shift on the ATM point of each expiry | Yes: =FxVolatilities= with an empty =ShiftStrikes= |
| .1 surface (the catalogue's Triple) | the same shift on every strike of every expiry | Yes: =FxVolatilities= with =ShiftStrikes= populated |
| .1 RR | $D_{call} - D_{put}$ at each expiry | No: a combination of two native runs |
| .1 STR | $0.5 (D_{call} + D_{put}) - D_{ATM}$ | No: a combination of three native runs |
| .1 Quad | a fourth surface component, unnamed in the catalogue | No: the catalogue does not say what the fourth component is |

A caution on the STR row. The identity above is the *smile* butterfly. A broker
quoting a strangle is quoting the price of the strangle structure, and turning
that into a smile butterfly takes the calculation
[[id:5B513D53-5DF7-4540-99C0-60D909678873][FX Volatility Surface]] describes,
because the strikes implied by the two quotes are not the same strikes. The
strangle ladder must therefore read the butterfly style the pair is stored with,
and it cannot be read as a plain average.

Two things follow. First, the Triple is not a special configuration — it is the
full-surface shift, and it already exists as a shipped example. Second, RR and
STR are bookkeeping over native deltas, which is exactly what ORE's own par
conversion does for rates: it converts zero-rate deltas into par-rate deltas
through a change of basis rather than by re-running the engine
([[https://github.com/OpenSourceRisk/Engine/blob/master/Docs/methods.pdf][ORE
Methods]], "Sensitivity/Par-Sensitivity Analysis"). The store should treat the
RR and STR ladders the same way.

Whether =ShiftStrikes= on an FX volatility block names a delta or an absolute
strike decides whether the call and put points are addressable at all. The
schema does not say, the examples leave the element empty for FX, and the store
records the surface's =deltas= rather than its strikes
(=ores_refdata_fx_volatility_configs_tbl=). This is the second open question,
and it decides whether RR and STR can be computed from one full-surface run or
need a configuration per delta.

* What each ladder needs

Three things, and the boundary between them is what the store's configuration
model already draws: market data, the sensitivity configuration, and the engine
settings.

** Market data

A ladder can only be as wide as the quotes behind it. The bucket list in the
configuration must be a subset of the pillars the run's curves and surfaces
actually carry, because the engine shifts the factors it is told to shift and
silently writes nothing for a factor it cannot find.

| Ladder group | Quotes the run must hold |
|--------------+--------------------------|
| Rates (1–4) | IR swap, deposit, FRA, OIS and basis quotes per currency, and the conventions to bootstrap them |
| Inflation (5) | Zero and year-on-year inflation swap quotes per index |
| FX spot (6) | One spot per currency pair in the book |
| FX vol (7–10) | =FX_OPTION/RATE_LNVOL/<base>/<quote>/<expiry>/<strike>= at =ATM=, =25RR= and =25BF= per expiry |
| Swaption vol (11–12) | Swaption ATM quotes per currency, expiry and term, and a smile if the smile ladder is wanted |
| Cap and floor vol (13) | Caplet or optionlet quotes per currency, expiry and strike |
| Equity (15–16) | Equity spots, and a vol surface per equity |
| Credit (17–19) | CDS spreads per name and tenor, a recovery rate, and index tranche quotes for base correlation |
| Commodity, dividend, correlation, bond future, power (21–26) | The respective curve or surface per underlier |

The FX vol key spellings are the sample's own: the shipped
=external/ore/examples/Input/market_20160205.txt= writes
=FX_OPTION/RATE_LNVOL/EUR/USD/1Y/ATM= alongside =…/1Y/25RR= and =…/1Y/25BF=.
That the market data carries the three components as separate keys while the
sensitivity configuration addresses surface points is the gap this design has
to bridge.

** The sensitivity configuration**

The same document serves every ladder. A report's ladder family is therefore a
property of which document it binds, and the differences a trader cares about —
0.1 against 1 basis point, ATM only against the whole smile, one currency pair
against all — are differences between documents, not between reports.

The example configs show what a full document costs to write: the shipped
=sensitivity.xml= is 517 lines for three currencies, one swaption set, one
equity set and six credit names, with a =ParConversion= block per curve. A
configuration per ladder per shift family would multiply that. The design
should therefore hold one document per *risk class* and let the shift size vary
through the keyed =ShiftSize= override the schema already provides, rather than
one document per report.

** Engine and analytic settings**

Beyond the configuration, a run needs:

- the =sensitivity= analytic switched on, which the seeds already do;
- =sensitivityConfigFile= naming the document, which no seed does;
- =marketConfigFile= naming the risk-factor description, which the shipped
  =ore_sensi.xml= sets to =simulation.xml= and =ore_sensismile_atm.xml= sets to
  =simulation_atmOnly.xml=;
- =recalibrateModels=, =parSensitivity= and =outputSensitivityThreshold=, which
  the seeds already set to =Y=, =Y= and =0.000001=;
- for par ladders, the conventions the =ParConversion= instruments name, and the
  =ParConversionExcludes= list for curves that must stay in the zero domain.

** What the cross terms need, and what 1.8.17.0 does not write**

Cross-gamma needs =ComputeGamma= and a =CrossGammaFilter= listing the pairs as
substrings of factor keys. The shipped filter names three pairs:

#+begin_src xml
<CrossGammaFilter>
  <Pair>DiscountCurve/EUR,DiscountCurve/EUR</Pair>
  <Pair>IndexCurve/EUR,IndexCurve/EUR</Pair>
  <Pair>DiscountCurve/EUR,IndexCurve/EUR</Pair>
</CrossGammaFilter>
#+end_src

The engine writes cross gammas into =sensitivity.csv= as rows with both
=Factor_1= and =Factor_2= populated, not into a file of their own: at v1.8.17.0
no writer reads =sensitivity.crossGammaOutputFile=, so =crossgamma.csv= never
appears ([[id:34C68112-7A34-4557-835C-BDCAA7BB9C2A][ORE output formats]]).
Measured on the sample at commit =deb15641d0=, =sensitivity.csv= holds 968 data
rows of which 484 carry a second factor, and =scenario.csv= holds 17,225 rows,
the shifted values behind both. The ladder is therefore buildable; it just does
not arrive in the file its name suggests.

* What the store lacks

Each of these blocks at least one ladder.

1. *No configuration binds a sensitivity document.* The ACME seeds switch the
   =sensitivity= analytic on in nine risk report configurations and set three
   parameters — =outputSensitivityThreshold=, =parSensitivity=,
   =recalibrateModels= — and set =sensitivityConfigFile= in none of them. A run
   today asks the engine for sensitivities with no factors to shift.
2. *No mapper for the document.* =grep -rln SensitivityAnalysis projects/=
   finds only seed rows and a description file; no C++ mapper reads or writes
   =SensitivityAnalysis=. The document is a name in a lookup and a row in the
   file library, not a modelled entity. The round trip is backlogged as
   [[id:190620B4-2F86-44C0-B4B7-31EE2C07D3F9][Round trip the sensitivity
   configuration]] in
   [[id:A93B2CA2-66AB-4AD2-84EB-B41964980C57][Complete the ORE report
   configuration]].
3. *No volatility market data.* The ACME seed's 420 curve quotes are all
   =IR_SWAP/RATE/…=; there is no =FX_OPTION/RATE_LNVOL= quote and no FX vol
   surface config in the dataset. Every vol ladder in the family table — rows 7
   to 14 and row 16 — needs this, and it is what
   [[id:3D2A2BD4-4126-4E54-9046-A9E00966D898][Support volatility surfaces]]
   is for.
4. *No FX spot market data in the seed.* Row 6 needs at least one spot per pair,
   and the seed holds curve quotes only.
5. *No reading of the ladder into a stored shape.* =sensitivity.csv= is
   catalogued as a family with a =TradeId= key and its figures become stored
   values, but nothing states which bucketed shape a ladder report presents. The
   prose catalogue does not settle it either: its "Spot Ladder" is
   =audit-derived= and its "Spot Greeks Report" is =numeric-bump ·
   risk-predict=, so neither reads a sensitivity run. The ladder needs a
   definition of its own grid, not a copy of the engine's row order.
6. *No cross-gamma ingestion path.* The figures exist in =sensitivity.csv= and in
   =scenario.csv=, but no stored shape has a pair of factors as its key, and
   =crossgamma.csv= cannot be relied on at v1.8.17.0.
7. *No shift-size vocabulary.* Nothing in the store names 0.1, 1 basis point or
   1 per cent as a choice. The risk report configuration holds the analytics and
   their parameters and a book and portfolio scope, and nothing that says how
   far to move a factor. This is the one gap the store cannot close without the
   product owner's answer.

* Reports to build

The list is ordered so that each row's dependencies are met by an earlier row.
*Cycle* uses the axes on [[id:04C68B55-E0B5-41CD-B90E-D670C17530B6][Risk Reporting]]:
=intraday= reads live market data and is indicative, =eod= reads an authorised
cut and is authoritative.

| # | Report | Cycle | Configuration it binds | Analytics | Depends on |
|---+--------+-------+------------------------+-----------+------------|

| 2 | Rates gamma ladder | eod | The same document with =ComputeGamma= on | =sensitivity=, =npv= | Report 1 |

| 4 | Rates cross-gamma ladder | eod | The same document plus =CrossGammaFilter= pairs | =sensitivity=, =npv= | Report 2 |
| 5 | .1 ATM vol ladder | eod and intraday | An FX vol block with an expiry list and an empty =ShiftStrikes=, =ShiftSize= 0.1 in vol points | =sensitivity=, =npv= | FX vol market data |
| 6 | .1 surface vol ladder | eod and intraday | The same block with =ShiftStrikes= populated for every configured delta | =sensitivity=, =npv= | Report 5 |
| 7 | .1 risk-reversal ladder | eod and intraday | No configuration of its own: it reads reports 5 and 6 | =sensitivity= plus the change of basis | Reports 5 and 6 |
| 8 | .1 strangle ladder | eod and intraday | As report 7 | =sensitivity= plus the change of basis | Reports 5 and 6 |
| 9 | Par-rate DV01 ladder | eod | Report 1's document with =parSensitivity= on and conventions on every par instrument | =sensitivity= | Report 1, conventions |
| 10 | FX spot delta ladder | eod | An =FxSpots= block per currency pair, =Relative= 0.01 or the chosen spot size | =sensitivity=, =npv= | FX spot market data |
| 11 | Swaption vol ladder | eod | A =SwaptionVolatilities= block per currency | =sensitivity=, =npv= | Swaption vol market data |
| 12 | Cap and floor vol ladder | eod | A =CapFloorVolatilities= block per currency | =sensitivity=, =npv= | Cap/floor vol market data |
| 13 | Credit spread ladder | eod | A =CreditCurves= block per name | =sensitivity=, =npv= | Credit market data |
| 14 | Equity vol ladder | eod | An =EquityVolatilities= block per equity | =sensitivity=, =npv= | Equity vol market data |

Rows 1–10 are the .1 family and the greek ladders the product owner asked for,
restated across every risk class the store's data reaches. Rows 11–14 name the
wider ladder for completeness; they are backlog until their market data exists,
and each is the same report over a different block.

Three reports from the existing EOD set in
[[id:04C68B55-E0B5-41CD-B90E-D670C17530B6][Risk Reporting]] are not ladders and
this design does not claim them: the "Rho/Vol Matrix", the "Spot/Vol Matrix" and
the "Spot/Rho Matrix" are two-factor scenario grids, not bucketed deltas. They
are built with the =stress= analytic and a =stresstest.xml=, and the sensitivity
analytic with two shift sizes produces the same numbers as scenario rows
(=scenario.csv= writes a =Cross= direction for them). They are the natural
next step after row 4 and they are out of scope here.

* Decisions

** D1. A .1 report is a report bound to a shift size, not a report type

The store gains no =point_one= report type, no =point_one= analytic and no
per-report shift column. A .1 report differs from its 1-basis-point sibling only
in the sensitivity configuration it binds. This keeps the report definition
what [[id:D4938956-1A5E-4F67-BCE8-7A951CE957E9][Report definition and
schedule]] says it is — the description of a report, with the engine parameters
in a shared configuration — and it keeps the family open: a 0.5 report and a 2
basis point report cost nothing.

** D2. One sensitivity document per risk class, with keyed shift overrides

The schema gives =ShiftSize=, =ShiftType= and =ShiftScheme= entries an optional
=key= attribute, so one document can carry a named size family alongside the
default and a run selects the family by key, without duplication. The store
should seed one document per risk class and select the shift family through the
key, rather than seed a document per report.

** D3. RR and STR are derived in the store, not asked of the engine

The engine has no risk-reversal factor. The store therefore computes the RR and
STR ladders from the surface-point deltas of one full-surface run, using the
identity on [[id:5B513D53-5DF7-4540-99C0-60D909678873][FX Volatility Surface]].
This mirrors what =ParConversion= already does for rates and keeps the derived
figures reproducible from one stored run.

** D4. The Triple is the full-surface shift

The catalogue's ".1 Triple" and the engine's full-surface shift are the same
computation. The store should name it once, prefer the engine's words, and
record the catalogue's name as an alias rather than as a second report.

** D5. Cross-gamma is read from =sensitivity.csv=

Until =crossgamma.csv= has a writer, the cross ladder reads the =Factor_1= and
=Factor_2= rows of =sensitivity.csv= and keeps =scenario.csv= as the evidence
behind them. No new output file is expected.

** D6. Theta is its own ladder, not a rates ladder

=ThetaPeriod= produces a risk factor key of its own (=Theta//0/1D=) and a
bucket list of its own (the period). It is a report in its own right because
the number means something different: the value change from moving the clock,
not from moving a market.

* Open questions for the product owner

** Q1. What is the .1?

A .1 in vol points (=Absolute= 0.001 on a decimal surface, or =Absolute= 0.1 on
a vol-point surface) and a .1 as a per-cent relative shift (=Relative= 0.1) are
two different reports and differ by orders of magnitude on a 10-vol book. The
catalogue says "0.1 bump", "0.1-unit shift" and "0.1% change in interest rates"
in three neighbouring paragraphs, so the unit is stated inconsistently in the
source. *Which one is the desk's .1, and is it the same number for rates and
for volatility?*

** Q2. Which greeks, and which buckets?

The catalogue names Delta, Gamma, Vega, Rega, Sega, Theta and Rho. The engine
shifts 26 risk classes and reports delta, gamma, cross-gamma and theta for each.
Rega and Sega are the risk reversal and strangle, which the engine does not
have (D3). *Does the .1 family mean the five vol ladders only, or the whole
family in the reports table? And for each ladder, which bucket list is the
desk's — the shipped sixteen-tenor curve list, the shipped four-expiry vol list,
or something shorter?*

** Q3. Which market data must the store hold for the ladders to be honest?

Rows 7 to 10 of the family table need FX vol surfaces quoted as ATM, RR and BF;
rows 11 to 14 need swaption, cap/floor, credit and equity data. *Which of these is
in scope for the book the .1 reports will run on, and is the vol surface a
requirement for the first .1 report or a later one?*

** Q4. Which ORE version is the design against?

Two versions publish today, 1.8.16.0-4-g3b62ba248 and 1.8.17.0
(=external/ore/packages/manifest.json=). The cross-gamma finding, the
=crossgamma.csv= gap and the analytic parameter list are all measured at
1.8.17.0. *Is 1.8.17.0 the version the .1 reports must run on, and may the store
stop publishing the older package?*

** Q5. Is the .1 report an intraday report, an EOD report, or both?

The catalogue gives the .1 family =intraday · eod= for cycle and =on-demand ·
scheduled= for frequency, and the EOD set then lists ".1 Rho" and "Trade and
Book Level .1 Triple Vol" as =scheduled= EOD members. A dual-cycle report needs
two definitions with two market-data types, which
[[id:EA59C7E0-CFDF-4BBE-815C-8AF53BFC16E1][Light the intraday reports on the
LIVE cut]] is already doing for the headline reports. *Which of the .1 family
runs intraday, and which runs only on the authorised cut?*
