This page documents a [[id:F44766FA-1E9C-416F-849A-E131101B2ECA][user journey]]: A market-risk owner gives a report its cut on create or update, reads what the selection would resolve to now, runs the report, and follows the run that resolved the selection to one body and recorded it, with the badge and the run's card stating what the report prices from.

A user journey describes what one person is trying to do, before anything is
built. It is a design document that outlives any sprint, so it names the
screens, the entity fields each screen shows, and the operations and messages
each step needs.

The decision behind it is
[[id:F6362451-D569-4609-97A3-F1C785AAAF02][The cut is the body a run prices
from]]: a report carries a cut selection, the run resolves it to a concrete cut,
and the cut is the body the run prices from. The journey replaces an earlier one
that imagined a pricing screen choosing a cut: there is no two-way pricing in the
product, reporting is the only surface, and a cut is selected where a report is
defined rather than where a price is looked at. It also replaces the market
bindings the earlier
[[id:FB8F701C-E56A-49A9-AA90-1B6BD0B02092][binding decision]] put on the run
settings, which that decision now records as superseded in part.

* Goal

"Reports in this list all look the same, and I cannot tell which of them prices
from yesterday's close and which from the moment I press Run. I want to say what a
report prices from, once, where the report is defined; to see on the report that I
have; to be told before I run it when the cut it wants does not exist or is not
signed; and to have the run's own record name the body it priced from, so that the
number in the report and the cut on the cuts screen are one object and not two."

The journey is done when the report's badge states its selection, the run has
resolved that selection to one cut and recorded it, and the run's card names the
body, its timepoint and what the selection resolved from.

* Actor and trigger

A market-risk owner runs this journey, or a tenant administrator acting for the
party. Both act inside their own tenant, on a report definition their party owns.

Three permissions decide what the person sees. Reading the cuts a selection may
name needs =reporting::cuts:read=. Setting the selection needs
=reporting::report_definitions:write=, because the selection is a property of the
definition and changes with it. Running the report is
=reporting::report_instances:write=, through
=reporting.v1.ops.trigger_report_instance=, and that same permission is what lets a
LIVE selection mint a cut. An action the person may not take is not drawn, as the
[[id:2BF7F03F-6BF4-45FF-8E26-4D3B4E2D943D][Record screen standard]] requires.

The trigger is a result nobody can account for, or a report whose numbers are not
the ones the desk means. A run completed and the question is which body it priced
from; the answer available today is a valuation date, and the store answers that
date differently a week later. The person opens the reports list, sees a badge that
says nothing about the body, opens the report, and the journey ends on the run's
page with the cut named.

This journey does not take a cut by hand: that is
[[id:E840111E-AA2F-4746-8D49-A1D73742925E][Take a cut]]. It does not approve a
published curve, and it does not read what a run produced, which is
[[id:969C14D2-4280-4D7C-8F51-1D395B521C83][Read what a run produced]].

* Steps

** Step 1: read what the reports price from

1. *Open Reports.* The reports list is
   [[id:891DE3CB-2374-4FD9-B7FB-D8892F5174A1][See the reports a party runs]]'s,
   with one addition: a *Cut* column carrying the selection's badge.

   | Column | What it holds |
   |--------+---------------|
   | Report | The definition's name, and its type under it |
   | Cut | The selection's kind as a badge, and under it the fact that qualifies it: *no cut selected*, *pinned* for a specific cut, *indicative* for a kind whose values are |
   | Cycle | =eod= or =intraday=, as the report's page states |
   | Latest run | The health word the list already draws |
   | Last run | The instant, relative |

2. *Read the badge.* A person who reads thirty-one reports sees which of them price
   from the close, which from the open and which from the run itself, without
   opening one. The badge is a classification of the report and not a status, so it
   borrows no green, amber or red; the health of the last run stands where it
   already stands.
3. *Open the report and read the selection beside the runs.* The report's record
   carries the *Definition* tab with its selection, and the *Market* tab with the
   selection beside the cut the last run resolved. The two are different facts and
   the tab says so: the selection is what the report will resolve next time, and the
   resolved cut is what the last run used.

** Step 2: give the report its cut

4. *Set the selection on create.* The create flow's step that names the report
   gains one field: the cut selection, with the kinds the party may select and the
   cuts already taken. The default is the end of day kind, because most reports
   cover a day, and a report created without a choice says *no cut selected* on its
   badge and is refused at its first run with the sentence that names what it
   lacks.
5. *Set the selection on update.* The *Definition* tab's *Edit* opens the record
   dialog with the selection among the definition's own fields. It is written with
   the rest of the definition, in one version, with a change reason and a
   commentary; a change to the selection is a material change, because it moves what
   the report prices from.
6. *Read what each kind resolves to.* The field offers the kinds the party's cut
   type list holds, each with the sentence that says what it resolves against:
   *End of day — the close of the date this report covers*, *Month end — the close of
   the date's month's last business day*, *Year end — the close of the year's last
   business day*, *Start of day — the open of that date*, *Independent verification —
   the signed verification cut of that date*, *Live — the instant you press Run, and
   the body it fixes is indicative*. The kinds are read from the type list rather
   than named here, so a firm that adds a kind adds it once. A kind the party has
   taken no cut for is still offered, because the party may take one; the preview
   says what it would need.
6a. *Read the family's fallback.* A report that selects the ordinary close prices
   from the body fixed on the date, whether that body was taken as an ordinary close,
   a month end or a year end, because the family fixes the same four inputs at the
   same centre's close. A report that selects the month end is stricter: it needs the
   month end cut, because the longer retention is the reason the occasion exists and a
   plain close of that day was not kept as one.

** Step 3: read the preview, which is the resolution

7. *Read the preview.* Beside the field, the dialog draws what the current choice
   would resolve to now, computed by the run's own operation: the cut's label, its
   kind, its timepoint, its state, its authoriser and the counts of its body, or the
   sentence that would refuse the run. *EOD would resolve to
   EOD.ACME-LONDON.2026-10-06, authorised by a.reyes, 1,284 deals and 3,912 values*
   is the common reading; *EOD would resolve to nothing: no end of day cut of 06
   October exists for these books* is the other.
8. *Read the preview's refusals.* The preview refuses what the run would refuse, in
   the run's words, and each names what would make the selection usable.

   | What the preview finds | What it says |
   |------------------------+--------------|
   | No cut of the kind and date exists | That no cut of that kind covers the date, for these books and this centre, and that somebody must take one. For a month end or year end selection it names the last business day it looked at |
   | A cut that is taken and unsigned | That the cut is waiting on its kind's authorisation, and the request and account it waits on |
   | A cut whose body is incomplete | That its body has gaps, and where they are read |
   | A cut that was refused | That its authorisation was refused, and that the remedy is a new take |
   | A pinned cut that has been superseded | That a later cut replaced it, and that a rerun may still name it on purpose |
   | A scope spanning two centres | That no one cut values books that read two centres, and that the report's scope must be narrowed or split |

9. *Select a specific cut where the kind is not enough.* A person who means one
   body — to reproduce an earlier result, or to pin a report to the body a client
   was shown — selects the cut itself from the list of the party's cuts, filtered by
   kind and date. The badge then reads the kind with *pinned* under it, and the
   selection resolves to that cut whatever the date, which is the one way a report
   can price from a body that is not of its own date.

** Step 4: run the report, and follow the resolution

10. *Run the report.* *Run now* is
    [[id:76431668-8656-4276-9F9B-F292FE45B2CA][Run a report and watch it
    finish]]'s press. The dialog names the cut the selection resolves to before the
    press, in the same words the preview used, so the person is not asked to trust a
    tab they read an hour ago.
11. *Let the run resolve and gather.* The run resolves the selection first, records
    the cut and its timepoint, and then gathers: the deals from the cut's population
    and the market data from the cut's fixed snapshot, in one body and not two halves
    at two instants. A resolution that yields nothing refuses the run before the
    engine is reached, and the run's output message carries the same sentence.
12. *Read the run's record.* The run's page carries the card that names the body: the
    cut's label, kind, timepoint and scope, the counts of its parts, its basis — the
    authoriser where the kind requires one, and that the kind requires none where it
    does not — and the selection it resolved from, which may have moved since. The
    run's `priced_at` is the cut's timepoint, so the date the report states and the
    body it used are one fact.

** Step 5: the LIVE report, which has no cut until it runs

13. *Select Live and read what it promises.* The preview cannot name a cut, and says
    so instead: *LIVE will fix a body at the instant you press Run, and every result
    it prices is marked indicative.* The badge reads *LIVE* with *no body until a
    run* under it.
14. *Run it.* The run resolves the selection to a cut it mints at its own instant:
    the four inputs at that instant, the taking identity being the run acting for
    the account that pressed it, and no authorisation, because the run mints and
    consumes the body in one act. The cut appears in the cuts list with its instant
    in its label, in the *In use* state, marked indicative.
15. *Read why it is different.* The run's card states that the cut was minted by this
    run, that its values are indicative, and that the result is reproducible only by
    naming that cut. A rerun of the same LIVE report tomorrow is a new body: the
    comparison verdict says *cannot be compared* for the retake reason, which is the
    same verdict a retaken daily cut draws.

** The audit of the selection**

16. *Read the definition's history.* The selection is a field of the definition, so
    its changes merge into the report's *History* tab: the selection before and
    after, the account, the instant and the reason. A run already produced keeps the
    cut it recorded, so the history explains the difference between two runs rather
    than relabelling them.
17. *Read the cut's consumers.* The cut's *Runs* tab lists the runs that priced from
    it, and the *Market* tab of each report names the selection that resolved to it.
    That is the question a person asks before a cut is superseded — *what still
    rests on this body?* — and it is the same fact read from each side.

* Screens and wireframes

Three surfaces, all of them reporting's. The reports list gains a *Cut* column. The
report's record gains the selection on its *Definition* tab and the resolved cut on
its *Market* tab. The run's page keeps the binding decision's card, read through the
cut, and gains the sentence that names the selection it resolved from.

No surface selects a cut while pricing, because there is no pricing surface: a cut
is selected where a report is defined, and a price is read where a run is recorded.

* Prototype

The [[file:../../prototypes/cuts/the-cut/][the cut prototype]] draws this
journey on four of its surfaces: the reports list with the badge, the report's
record, the selection dialog with the preview, and the run that resolved. Its
[[file:../../prototypes/cuts/the-cut.org][design note]] records the states and
what is fake.

It must draw a report with no selection, one that selects the end of day kind, one
that selects the start of day kind, one that selects LIVE, one whose selection
resolves to nothing, one pinned to a specific cut, one whose cut is taken and
unsigned, a run that resolved and recorded its cut, and a LIVE run whose resolved
cut exists only because the run minted it. Every value is a fixture.

The resolution is one call and it is the same call for a preview and for a run.
That is the point of it: a preview computed by a second code path is a preview of
that path and not of the run, which is how a person comes to trust a screen that
lies.

* What is missing

1. *No cut exists to select.* Everything this journey draws waits on the entity the
   decision note describes. Until it exists the preview has nothing to resolve and
   the card has nothing to name.
2. *The definition has no selection.* =report_definition= carries no field for a cut
   kind or a cut, so the list cannot draw a badge and the dialog cannot set one.
3. *The run records a date and not a body.* =record_run_pricing= carries =priced_at=
   and =price_note=. A cut is the answer that survives a correction to the store, and
   the run needs the field and the timepoint.
4. *No resolution exists.* Nothing reads a selection and answers a cut. The rule is
   stated here and on the decision note; the operation, its refusals and its one
   sentence are missing.
5. *The gathering steps re-read the store.* Neither step reads a fixed body, so a
   run of a resolved cut would price from whatever the store holds when the step
   runs. The [[id:19630B5D-A163-42B5-B9C4-61A87BAE8F5F][Trade cut]] page states the
   defect in the population and the
   [[id:DD59B04C-DAD2-458C-B35E-3F6B0B3D6DC6][Market data cut]] page states it in
   the market data.
6. *The preview is a new part.* No rule draws a resolution preview, and the shape it
   needs — a panel that states what a choice resolves to and what would refuse it —
   is the prototype's one addition to the standards.
7. *The picker over cuts is not a standard part.* =RecordPicker= reads its whole kind
   once and draws no state, and a cut is filtered on the server and carries a derived
   state. The screen needs its own picker or the shared one needs a filtered form.
8. *No market card that reads through the cut.* The binding decision's card reads the
   publish records the run recorded. Read through a cut it reads the cut's market data
   half, and the two must not disagree: the run records the cut, and the card reads
   the sets from it.
9. *The retired row still exists.* =configuration_market_binding=, its tab and its
   picker are in the schema and in the signed-off screens, and nothing yet says in
   the schema that a run ignores them. The decision to retire them is recorded; the
   removal is work.
10. *No route and no screen for reporting.* The reports list, the report page and its
    tabs do not exist in the web application, so this journey's surfaces are new with
    the reporting group's.
