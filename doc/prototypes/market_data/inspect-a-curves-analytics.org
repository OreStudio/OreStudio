* Summary

A curve is read twice: as what the market sent, and as what the curve makes of
it. The prototype puts them on the same rows - the market rate and its provider,
then the zero rate, the implied forward, the discount factor and the shift -
under a strip of conventions that say what those numbers mean. The shift column
is the argument a desk needs: it is the distance between the bootstrapped curve
and the market, stated, not assumed.

It is ported from hedgr's =prototypes/aud_aonia_curve_dialog_prototype.html=.
This is not the candidate check and not the publish record: those screens exist
and answer different questions.

* Detail

** The question

Do the inputs and the outputs share one row, or are they read apart? A person
checking a curve reads across a pillar; a person quoting a discount factor reads
one column. The variant decides which is the default.

** The variants

Switch with the bar at the foot, or =?variant=.

| Variant | Name              | The structural argument |
|---------+-------------------+-------------------------|
| =a=     | Market pillars    | What the market sent: tenor, maturity, year fraction, rate, provider, age. |
| =b=     | Bootstrapped curve | What the curve makes: zero rate, implied forward, discount factor, shift. |
| =c=     | All columns       | Both, side by side, which is what a person checks a curve against. |

The default is =b=, because the curve's own numbers are what a person cannot
compute elsewhere.

** The states

- =?state=stale= — the whole curve is stale: the publish is older than the
  threshold, and the badge says how old.
- =?state=live= — the curve is within the threshold.
- =?state=none= — the party holds no published curve. The page says so and draws
  nothing.
- =?compare=1= — the prior close, as a dashed line over the current curve and as
  a prior and change column.

** What is fake

The pillars are hedgr's AUD-AONIA discount factors, and every rate is derived
from them in the page so the mock cannot disagree with itself: the zero rate is
=-ln(DF)/τ=, the forward is the rate the segment implies, the shift is the zero
rate less the market rate. Nothing reads a service.

The chart draws the zero curve by interpolating log-linearly in the discount
factor between pillars, which is what =LOG_LINEAR_DISCOUNT= means. That is
deliberate: the page shows why the curve's mathematics must live on the server,
because a second implementation would smooth differently.

** Where the code lives

=doc/prototypes/market_data/inspect-a-curves-analytics/index.html=,
self-contained.

** The verdict

Take it, with three changes, each forced by how ORE Studio stores a fact:

1. *One value per observation.* =ores_marketdata_market_observations_tbl= holds
   one =value=, so hedgr's Bid, Ask and Spread columns become one market rate
   and the provider that sent it.
2. *A page, not a dialog.* The screen is a route with the product's header and
   cards; its collapse and drag affordances belong to a desktop terminal, not to
   the web app.
3. *Two different shifts.* hedgr's =Shift (bps)= is the move against the prior
   close. Here the shift is curve-less-market, and the move has its own column
   under the comparison. The journey records why.

** What changed from hedgr

| Item | hedgr | Here | Why |
|------+-------+------+-----|
| Pillar columns | Bid, Ask, Mid, Spread | One market rate and its provider | An observation holds one value |
| Shift | Zero less prior zero | Zero less market, with the prior move as its own column | Two different facts wore one name |
| Forward | Segment forward | Segment forward, stated as such | A chart needs a value at every pillar |
| Chrome | A collapse/expand dialog with a feed toggle | A page with a staleness badge | Prototypes read as the product |
| Refresh | A pulsing button | A Refresh in the header | The standard's refresh, RS-LIST-03 |
