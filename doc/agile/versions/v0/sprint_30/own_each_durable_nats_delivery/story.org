* Goal

A service that reads a durable NATS subject never crashes on, loses, or
answers twice a delivery it was handed. Each delivery has one owner. The owner
settles it once: commit, rollback, rollback after a delay, or reject. A
delivery its owner drops without settling rolls back, so the server delivers
it again. The type makes the wrong uses fail to compile, not fail at run time.

The story comes from a crash. With its provisioning steps on several workers,
the IAM service freed one NATS message twice and died in the cnats garbage
collector. The subscription let cnats acknowledge and free each delivery when
the callback returned, while the client's own handle also owned it. A
delivery passed to a worker outlived the callback, so both owners freed it.

* Acceptance

- A durable subscription takes a handler of a move-only =delivery=. A copy of
  a delivery does not compile.
- Commit, rollback, rollback after a delay and reject consume the delivery. A
  delivery dropped unsettled rolls back.
- The transport asks cnats for manual acks in one place, so cnats never frees
  a delivery the client owns. A test keeps a delivery past its callback,
  settles it on another thread, and proves the server delivered it once.
- A delivery's keep-alive thread belongs to the delivery and ends before it is
  settled or freed.
- A subscription is not destroyed while a delivery it made is open.
- A core message carries no acknowledgement, and =ack_guard= is gone.
- The pattern page /Acknowledge What Was Handled/ shows the new type and states
  the ownership rule.

* Decisions

- A delivery is a transaction, like a database one: one owner, one answer,
  rollback on drop. The alternative, a shared handle with a guard, is what
  allowed two owners and an answer whose timing depended on the last copy.
- The answers are rvalue-qualified, so a second answer is a use after move,
  which clang-tidy's bugprone-use-after-move reports.

* Out of scope

- Running steps on several workers. That is the story /Run a service's steps
  concurrently/, which is rebased on this one.
