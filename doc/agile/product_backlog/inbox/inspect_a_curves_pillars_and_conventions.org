This page is a [[id:671F18E4-E09C-4B3B-BD24-D33DF8AE38A6][capture]] in the *inbox* bucket of the product backlog — a pre-sprint idea, not yet pulled into a sprint as a story.

* What

A dialog that opens one curve from its coverage row: the conventions it was
built on (index, day count, interpolation, tenor convention), one row per
pillar with its start and end tenor, instrument role, the series it reads,
its discount factor and the rate it implies, and the as-of of the publish it
came from, with the movement since the previous publish. The dialog is the
drill-in of the coverage row, not a second place to look.

Gemini produced a prototype at =~/Downloads/aud_aonia_curve_dialog_prototype.html=
on 2026-11-13. Read it as a design sketch, not as a contract.

* Why

The coverage screen answers "is this currency covered". The next question is
"what does its curve say", and today that means the curves topic's tables and
a SQL read. A person wants the pillars and the conventions in one place, next
to the coverage row they were already reading.

The prototype's strengths are its density, the table-and-chart hover sync, and
the strip that states the conventions. Its weaknesses are that it is mocked: one
currency and index (AUD-AONIA), one as-of, seven fixed pillars, a fabricated par
rate and bid/ask spread, fixed chart axes, and a LIVE/STALE label with no
source. It also invents a "Continuous" compounding field the store does not
hold, and a per-row age that is one constant. None of that survives contact
with the API.

Before it is built, the server contract must state: the config and its version,
the output series and its latest publish (id, as-of, superseded-by), the pillar
set with each pillar's input series and role, and the previous publish the
movement is measured against. The inspector adds no new computation: the zero
rate and the forward are the pricer's, and the UI reads them or states that it
cannot.
