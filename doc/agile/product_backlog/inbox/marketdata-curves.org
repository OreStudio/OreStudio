It captures the goal, current status, acceptance criteria, and the tasks that compose it.

* Goal

Make a curve a thing ores.marketdata owns. Today a curve exists only as a
side effect of one service: a `market_series` row stamped
=derivation_kind='IR_CURVE_BOOTSTRAP'= with one =market_observation= per
pillar. Nothing gives it identity, nothing records what it was built from,
nothing tells a build apart from an observation, and nothing reads it back.

The outcome this story delivers: a curve can be created, identified, read
and asked about its provenance, without the reader having to know that it is
stored as a series.

* What exists today

The findings below came from a read-only investigation. Each is grounded in
code; the paths are the evidence.

**A curve is a derived series, and nothing else.** A curve is created by
=curve_republish_service::republish()= in
=projects/ores.marketdata/service/src/app/curve_republish_service.cpp= (note
=service/=, not =core/=). It stamps the pre-minted output series
=derivation_kind = "IR_CURVE_BOOTSTRAP"= (:182), calls
=quant::curve_bootstrap_engine::bootstrap()= (:224), and writes one
=market_observation= per pillar (:296) plus =observation_lineage= rows
(:299). Derivation kinds are seeded in
=projects/ores.sql/populate/refdata/refdata_derivation_kinds_populate.sql=:
=OBSERVED=, =IR_CURVE_BOOTSTRAP=, =CRM_DERIVATION=.

**There is no curve table, no curve entity and no curve type.** The
component's seven tables are series, the asset-class junction, observations,
fixings, observation lineages, feed bindings and classification rules.
=market_series= has no curve column: =marketdata_market_series_create.sql=
carries only the key columns, =series_subclass=, and the derivation markers.
=oresmd= declares =instrument_type::curve=, and no line of code references
it.

**A build is indistinguishable from an observation by shape.** There is no
=reference_date= and no =build_kind= anywhere. The only markers are
=derivation_kind= on the *series* and a =source= string of the form
="ir_curve_bootstrap:<config id>"= on the *observation*. The seeded example
=marketdata_market_series_fomc_populate.sql= shows the intended pairing: a
=RATES=/=YIELD= input grid and a =YieldCurve=/=DISCOUNT= output series for
the same qualifier.

**The recipe lives in refdata, not marketdata.**
=ir_curve_bootstrap_config= plus =ir_curve_bootstrap_pillar= hold the
interpolation method, day-count convention, split tenor, curve family role
(FUNDING or PROJECTION) and the ordered pillar list.
=source_series_id= and =output_series_id= are *soft* references into
marketdata's =market_series= — no foreign key, because the tables are in
different components. A database check requires
=source_series_id <> output_series_id=, so the first republish cannot
reclassify the raw feed's own series and overwrite live data.

**Nothing consumes a curve.** =curve_health_checker= has no production
callers at all. =interpolate_discount_factor= is called only from inside the
engine and its tests. The curve_snapshot reads
(=marketdata.v1.curve-snapshot.get= and =.buckets=) are thin wrappers over
=read_as_of= on one series' observations, so they work for any series and do
not know a curve when they read one. The bootstrapped output series is
write-only today.

**A curve is reachable only by hand-crafted NATS.** The two requests
=republish_curve_request= and =compute_curve_request= are registered at
=application.cpp:202,208=, with permissions
=marketdata::curve_bootstrap:republish= and =:compute=. There is no shell
verb, no HTTP route, no web client and no scheduler job that publishes on
either subject. The recipe table has no editing UI either: the model records
that its real editor, =CurveBuilderWorkbench=, lived in the removed desktop
client.

**The design for doing this properly exists and was abandoned.** Sprint 25's
=adopt-oresmd-native-market-data-identity= story proposed =curve_build= and
=curve_point= tables — a build is one observation of a curve entity, and its
points hang off it. That story was abandoned at sprint close on 2026-09-23
and nothing implements it. The classes survive in
=ores.marketdata.puml= under a "Target model" legend, marked =<<new>>=.

**Three vocabularies are called a role, and two of them are called
=curve_role=.** refdata's =curve_role= is DEPOSIT/FRA/SWAP/NONE and
classifies an *instrument code*, not a curve; oresmd's =curve_role= is
discount/projection/self_discounting; and
=ir_curve_bootstrap_config.curve_family_role= is FUNDING/PROJECTION, where
FUNDING does not mean discount. oresmd's =curve_role= is inert — parsed,
carried, re-serialised, and branched on nowhere. Its one behavioural
consumer in the whole tree is =feeds_conflict()= in
=ores.synthetic/service/src/feed_controller.hpp=.

**Nothing crosses the ORE boundary in either direction.** ORE builds a curve
in memory for the duration of a run from =curveconfig.xml= (which quote keys,
conventions, interpolation), =market.txt= (the values) and =todaysmarket.xml=
(which to build). None of that configuration is stored here: the only reader
of ORE's =curveconfiguration= model is a round-trip fidelity test. The
quote-to-curve wiring itself — ORE's =Simple=, =AverageOIS=, =TenorBasis=,
=CrossCurrency=, =ZeroSpread= segtypes — has no counterpart in this
repository. Going the other way, =report_package_handler::prepare_package=
describes itself as a stub that packs raw MsgPack rather than an ORE input
package, =collect_results= is a pass-through stub, and
=ores.ore/core/src/xml/document_kind.cpp= does not even classify a
=curveconfig.xml= dropped into an import.

**Where ORE's own source is, and where curves live in it.** The vendored copy
under =external/ore/= ships examples, XSDs and prebuilt engine packages but
*no* engine source, so none of the above can be answered from it — and an
earlier pass at this investigation wrongly concluded the source was
unavailable. It is a separate checkout:

* Deeper analysis, 2026-10-06

A second read-only pass, at =d02032a18c=. It answers the open questions far
enough to choose a direction. It is a snapshot, not a description of the
system. Two facts the first investigation stated are corrected here, because
they change the shape of the answer.

** What a curve is made of

Four things, and the tree already holds three of them under other names.

- *Identity*: which curve this is. =market_series= is already the identity of
  a term structure; the oresmd URI is the name. The seeded example is
  =oresmd://ir/USD?type=series&instrument=discount&quote=rate&curve_id=USD-SOFR-FOMC=
  (=(marketdata_market_series_fomc_populate.sql)=). There is no
  =instrument_type::curve= in code: the only =instrument_type= enum is ORE's
  own MarketDatum list (=api/include/ores.marketdata.api/datum/ore_types.hpp=),
  and the ={fixing, curve, quote, vol}= enum is a design-file fiction. A curve
  is named by =curve_id= in the URI today, not by an instrument type.
- *Points*: the pillar-to-value map. These are =market_observation= rows. The
  point identity is the =oresmd_uri=; **the observation table has no
  =point_id= column** (the design file draws one; the model does not). The
  pillar's tenor lives inside the URI.
- *Build*: when the curve was built against which source, and by which recipe.
  This is the part that is only half present. =market_series= carries
  =derivation_kind= and =derivation_config_id/version=; =observation_lineage=
  carries, per point, =derivation_config_id/version=, =source_as_of= and
  =source_series_ids=. What is missing is a *reference date* and a *build
  kind*, and a build identity that groups the points of one build.
- *Recipe*: how to build it. This lives in =ores.refdata= as
  =ir_curve_bootstrap_config= (family role, discount curve for a projection,
  interpolation, day count, tenor convention, split tenor) plus
  =ir_curve_bootstrap_pillar= rows. Its =output_series_id= and the pillars'
  tenor codes are *soft* references into other components.

** The correction that matters: lineage is most of the build

The first investigation read the build as entirely absent. It is not:
=observation_lineage= is written by =republish()= beside every output
observation (=curve_republish_service.cpp:365-366=) and already answers "what
was this built from, by which recipe, and against what as-of". A build header
therefore does not have to invent provenance; it has to *group* the points of
one build and add the two facts lineage lacks, =reference_date= and
=build_kind=. That is a much smaller table than the sprint 25 design assumed.

** The second correction: the point identity is the URI

The sprint 25 =curve_point= design draws a =point_id= column. The shipped
observation table has none; the point identity is the whole =oresmd_uri=. Any
curve design either reads the pillar identity out of the URI or adds a real
=point_id=/tenor column. The first is free; the second must be justified.

** How each part maps onto the existing infrastructure

| Curve part | Existing home | State |
|------------+---------------+-------|
| Identity | =market_series= + oresmd URI =curve_id= | Exists; the curve is not *marked* as a curve beyond =derivation_kind= and =series_subclass= |
| Points | =market_observation= (=oresmd_uri=, =value=, =observation_datetime=) | Exists, bitemporal, versioned, RLS, evented |
| Point provenance | =observation_lineage= | Exists per point |
| Build identity and reference date | none | Missing |
| Build kind (bootstrap / derived / imported) | none | Missing (=IR_CURVE_BOOTSTRAP= sits on the series, not the build) |
| Recipe | =ir_curve_bootstrap_config= + =_pillar= in =ores.refdata= | Exists, cross-component soft refs |
| Recipe editing | refdata shell CRUD units | Exists for the recipe; no curve verb |
| Compute / publish | =marketdata.v1.curve_bootstrap.compute= / =.republish= | Exists server-side; no sender anywhere |
| Read back | =marketdata.v1.curve-snapshot.get= / =.buckets= | Exists; the handler checks no permission and no code is seeded |
| Consumer | none | Missing |

** The modelling decision

Three shapes are available. The recommendation is the first, with the third
held open until a journey proves it.

1. *Keep the series as the identity, add a build header, keep the points as
   observations.* A =curve_build= row names the output series, the
   =reference_date=, the =build_kind=, the recipe (=config id + version=) and
   the =source_as_of=; the points stay =market_observation= rows whose
   =observation_datetime= is the reference date and whose =oresmd_uri= is the
   pillar. This reuses bitemporality, versioning, row-level security,
   eventing, shell units, the protocol and the TypeScript twin, and adds one
   small table. It matches what =republish()= already writes, so no data has
   to move.
2. *A =curve_point= table beside =curve_build=.* The sprint 25 design. It buys
   an explicit point-to-build link and a place to hang point-level
   trustworthiness fields, and it pays for it with a second bitemporal,
   versioned, evented, shell-served table that duplicates the observation
   stack. Justified only if a consumer needs point sets that are independent
   of the series' own history, such as two builds compared point by point.
3. *A =curve= entity distinct from =market_series=.* Rejected as the starting
   point: it duplicates the identity, versioning and history the series
   already owns. The series is the term structure; a curve is a series whose
   points are pillars.

The deciding input is *what a consumer does with a curve*, which is what the
journeys are for. The design deliberately stops before =curve_point= and lets
the journey work decide whether it is earned.

** Curves and tenors

The relation is real but undeclared today, in three places.

- *The recipe side.* =ir_curve_bootstrap_pillar.start_tenor_code= and
  =.end_tenor_code= are undeclared soft references to =refdata_tenors=: no
  foreign key, and the only database check validates =curve_role_code=, not
  the tenors. The engine resolves a pillar by
  =end_tenor_code -> tenors_by_code -> resolution(convention) -> anchor +
  offset [+ schedule steps] -> date= (=tenor_resolution.cpp:151-222=), and the
  end tenor doubles as the =point_id= the resolver reads the quote under
  (=curve_republish_resolver.cpp:90-92=). A typo in a tenor code is a
  runtime throw, not a save-time refusal.
- *The point side.* The observation's =oresmd_uri= carries the term
  coordinate (=datum::term=: period, date, fx_tenor or continuation), and the
  oresmd =term= field is the canonical datum-side name for a tenor. The two
  vocabularies join only by string at the pillar bridge; nothing checks that
  the URI's term is the pillar's tenor.
- *The gaps.* Tenor resolution never rolls for a business day (the
  conventions carry no calendar column), so a pillar that lands on a holiday
  is not adjusted; =split_tenor_code= is stored but read by no consumer logic;
  and =dataset_family_role=FUNDING/PROJECTION= is the one role vocabulary that
  is live, while refdata's =curve_role= is hard-coded in the engine and
  oresmd's =curve_role= is inert outside the synthetic feed's conflict check.

The recommendation is to make the pillar's tenor a checked reference to
=refdata_tenors=, and to carry that same tenor code on the curve's points so a
reader can group by tenor without parsing the URI. The business-day gap and
the split tenor are separate defects, not part of the representation.

** The user journeys are the input that decides this

The tree has no market-data journey group and no journey that owns a curve;
the closest analogues are =journey_keep_a_holiday_calendar_right= (rules, then
a derived artefact consumers read) and =journey_add_or_correct_a_currency=
(entity with owned child panels and history). Curve creation, review and
consumption have no owner, and the journey-extraction task for marketdata is
still =BACKLOG=. The candidates a journey pass should settle, one person and
one goal each, are:

| Candidate journey | Person | First consumer it creates |
|-------------------+--------+---------------------------|
| Build a curve for a currency and a date | Rates quant or market data engineer | The =republish= sender the server has never had |
| Keep a curve recipe right | Market data engineer | The recipe editor the desktop client took away |
| Read a curve and trust it | Rates quant | The curve read-back: points, shape, provenance |
| Correct a curve point | Market data engineer | Point-level override and re-publish |
| See how a curve changed | Risk reviewer or auditor | Build history and build-to-build comparison |
| Take a curve to ORE, and bring one back | Platform | The lossless =curveconfig= / =market= round trip |

They settle the model because each names an operation the server must serve:
a build journey needs a build, a comparison journey is what earns
=curve_point=, and a provenance journey decides which of
=curve_point_provenance='s fields are worth storing. They also force the
vocabulary question, because the actor classes in the journeys are
=TenantAdmin= and =Operations= today and neither is a rates quant.

** Defects found on the way, independent of the representation

- The curve-snapshot reads are unauthenticated: =curve_snapshot_handler= checks
  no permission and no =curve-snapshot= code is seeded
  (=iam_permissions_populate.sql=). This is a W02-class defect and belongs in
  its own capture.
- The recipe's tenors are unvalidated at save time, as above.
- =CUBIC_SPLINE= is recognised by the engine and throws; =IMM_ROLL= is
  superseded and throws.

* Acceptance

- A curve is identifiable as a curve without knowing which strings to
  combine. Whatever the representation, "is this a curve" and "which curve is
  this" are answerable from the data.
- A build records when it was built and against what, so two builds of the
  same curve are distinguishable and a build is distinguishable from an
  observation.
- A curve's provenance is readable: what it was built from, by which recipe,
  and when.
- At least one production consumer reads a curve, or the story says in the
  record why none does.
- The three role vocabularies are either reconciled or documented where a
  reader meets them, because two of them share a name.
- No new name collision between this work and the oresmd layer's existing
  =curve= vocabulary.

* Decisions

**Why this is a story and not a task under the clean-up.** The clean-up
brings ores.marketdata up to the Component Clean Standard. This changes the
data model, adds an entity or a shaped representation, and needs a consumer
that does not exist. Folding it into the clean-up would put a modelling
decision inside a mechanical PR.

**Why it does not start yet.** The clean-up is still moving the models,
projections and import boundary. Landing a curve representation on top of a
model that is still in motion would mean doing it twice.

* Out of scope

- The ORE handoff — writing =curveconfig.xml=, =market.txt=, =todaysmarket.xml=
  and reading =curves.csv= back. It is a prerequisite for a lossless curve
  round trip and it is large enough to be its own story, but it is not this
  one.
- Changing how ORE itself builds curves, and any ORE-side configuration
  format.
- The synthetic generator's curve feeds (=ir_curve_feed=,
  =ir_curve_generation_config=). They publish curve *points* as ticks and are
  a producer, not a representation.

* Open questions for the deeper analysis

Answered by the =Deeper analysis, 2026-10-06= section above; the questions
are kept as the record of what was asked.

- **How does a curve read its points?** The grammar story keeps the 45-way
  variation in the datum identity, which is cold, and leaves the hot side
  here: a series' points as parallel arrays per coordinate shape (about six:
  none, term, contract, expiry by strike or delta, maturity by strike, and the
  shape-profile slot), batched, with each point referring to its series by
  index. Curve and surface building should read that shape, per the
  [[id:BBD43BF2-9FEE-42B0-A667-9468AF759DC2][data-oriented design]] criteria
  for kernels.
- **An entity or a shaped series?** Sprint 25 proposed =curve_build= and
  =curve_point= tables. The shipped implementation reused the observation
  tables instead. Which is right, given that a curve's points must be
  bitemporal and versioned the way observations already are?
- **Does a curve need its own table when it is one series plus points?** If
  not, what is the minimal thing that gives it identity — a marker column, a
  reserved =series_type=, a view, or the oresmd URI?
- **What consumes a curve?** Nothing does today. Until something does, every
  representation decision is speculative, so the analysis should establish the
  first real consumer before choosing a shape.
- **Does the oresmd =curve= vocabulary become load-bearing here?**
  =instrument_type::curve= is dead and =to_curve_key()= is gated on
  =type=fixing=. If a curve gains identity, is the URI its identity, and does
  =to_curve_key() then mean what its name says?
- **Is =IR_CURVE_BOOTSTRAP= the whole story?** =CRM_DERIVATION= is seeded and
  unused, so there is at least one other derived kind waiting. Does the shape
  chosen generalise to it?
- **Where does the recipe belong?** It is in refdata today behind soft
  references. Should a curve and its recipe live in one component?
- **How does a curve relate to =feed_binding= and =observation_lineage=?=
  Lineage is documented as deliberately generic, and feed bindings map a raw
  producer channel to a series. A curve's inputs are a series, not a channel.
