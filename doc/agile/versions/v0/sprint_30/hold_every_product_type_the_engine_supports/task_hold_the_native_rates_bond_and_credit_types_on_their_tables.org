* Goal

rates, bond and credit families. A CBO holds its investment; a structure

* Acceptance

- each of the three types routes to a rates, bond or credit table (new, or an
  existing one extended), whose columns hold every scalar term the
  element states; lists, baskets and schedules are rows of child tables or of
  the shared rows (options, barriers, underlyings, schedules)
- the projection reads and writes the element from those rows, value for value,
  and a round-trip test reads ORE's or the user guide's example and writes it back
- the builder states the rows, and the generator books the element form
- the class-read entry for the type is removed: the scripted instrument table
  holds no element form of it, only the generic ScriptedTrade form where ORE's
  library carries one
- the catalogue names both forms of the type where ORE carries both
- the ORE engine test reads and builds each, with no error of the trade kind
- every fact the element states is a column or a row: nothing is held as text

* Plan

Each type moves to the family whose facts it states, and the class-read entry
that held it as parameter rows is deleted, so the scripted table keeps only the
generic =ScriptedTrade= form.

- =RateDigitalOption= gains a fact table of its own under the rates header, with
  its option block in the shared option rows the family already uses.
- =CBO= gains a credit fact table holding its investment. A structure stated
  inline is refused, with the message naming the split task, because its bond
  basket holds whole bond definitions.
- =BondFutureOption= needs no new table: the bond family already states a future
  and an option, so the type holds one row of each under the bond header.

The builders move to the family files, the planner branches with them, and the
tests follow, so each family's code sits together.

* Notes

The type was first held on the scripted instrument table through the
class-read path, as parameter rows. That held every value but gave the element
no table of its own, against the story's rule that a product ORE carries in
both forms is held in both, and that the forms can be compared. ORE builds all three natively. The CBO structure was captured
separately as "Hold a CBO structure stated inline". Its bond basket holds full
bond definitions, so it is split out again as [[id:70A950DD-F516-44D3-8859-6FEA30766589][a task of its own]]; this
task holds the investment and refuses a structure stated inline.

* Result

=RateDigitalOption= holds its facts on a rates table of its own, under the rates
header, with its option block in the shared option rows. =CBO= holds its
investment on a credit fact table under the credit header, which names the
structure as an internal reference; a structure stated inline is refused with
the message pointing at [[id:70A950DD-F516-44D3-8859-6FEA30766589][the task that holds it]].
=BondFutureOption= needed no new table: it states one bond future row and one
bond option row under the bond header, and the bond reader now asks for both by
type rather than one per trade.

The class-read entries for the three are gone, so the scripted table holds only
the generic =ScriptedTrade= form. The builders, planner branches and tests moved
to their families.

The suites the change touches pass on the merged tree — 520,655 assertions over
the ORE core's 564 cases, 18,390 over the trading API's 4,874, 1,671 over the
trading core's 258, 4,044 over the shell's 1,432, and 4,289 over the planner's
147 — and the ORE engine reads and builds every recorded trade with no error of
the trade kind.

Two defects the paused run left are fixed: the rate digital option's writer
stated the option block and not the exercise dates the reader keeps as schedule
rows, and the CBO refusal test's fixture was not schema-valid, so it tested
binding rather than the refusal. The bond future option's recorded portfolio
gains the settlement its option row now states, and the rebook test records one
shape difference: a bond's rows come back in its carrier, and the flat arrays
beside it are the write-time shape.
