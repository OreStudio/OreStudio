* Summary

A feed's ticks used to be one number. They are now a regime: a mode (periodic,
Poisson or Hawkes), a branching ratio and a decay that say how a cluster of
ticks feeds itself, a sensitivity that says how hard the feed follows the
market, and — one level up — a run-wide market state that a person can pin.

The controls exist in three surfaces that do not know about each other. This
prototype argues how they should be managed before a fourth is added.

* What we have

| Surface | What it offers | What it cannot say |
|---------+----------------+--------------------|
| Feed editor (=fxFeeds=, =irFeeds=) | the four columns as fields beside the rate | whether the numbers produce a burst, and what the market is doing to them |
| Ticks panel, per feed | the process and its mixture, the rate as one line | anything about arrival: no mode, no clustering, no sensitivity |
| Shell | =synthetic regime get= and =pin= | the same, but readable and drivable by a person who knows the verbs |
| Prototype =synthetic-feeds= | the tree, hero, summary, chart | no arrival vocabulary at all |

Three gaps follow.

1. *A number without a consequence.* A branching ratio of 0.9 is a legal value
   that produces almost no clustering, and one of 0.99 produces bursts that the
   pipeline cannot keep up with. The editor shows neither.
2. *Two levels, one place.* The market state is a property of the run and the
   sensitivity is a property of the row, but the only place both are visible
   together is a terminal.
3. *The screen's own rule.* The screen standard says a node carries running,
   stopped or partial, and the editors behind it author one feed. It has no
   vocabulary for "this feed follows a market that is currently busy".

* The journeys

A journey is one screen organised around a goal, and its steps draw the views
they need; the rail is data. Three journeys cover this work.

** J1: "See what a feed's ticks will look like"

Author opens a feed from the tree, and the editor's arrival section shows a
sparkline of the gaps the current numbers produce — the same seeds the service
would use — beside the fields. No state is committed to judge it.

*** Steps
1. *The feed*, its pair and its rate.
2. *The regime*, four fields and the gap preview.
3. *The market*, where this feed sits against the run's state: its sensitivity
   and what the state does to it.

** J2: "Set how the whole market behaves"

Operator opens the Market Simulator with no feed selected. The right-hand side
is the run: the state in force, the multiplier, and a switch of auto, calm and
active, with the time the pin was set and by whom.

*** Steps
1. *The run*, its collections and how many feeds are live.
2. *The state*, what is in force and whether a scenario pinned it.
3. *The consequence*, the multiplier each feed is now scaled by, listed by
   asset class so a curve at 0.4 and a pair at 1.3 read side by side.

** J3: "Test a fast market without waiting for one"

Tester pins the state, watches two feeds whose counts used to be independent
move together, and releases the pin.

*** Steps
1. *Pin*, from the panel, with the reason recorded like any other change.
2. *Observe*, the run's counts per feed over a window, against the same window
   before the pin.
3. *Release*, back to auto, with the same chart showing the fall back.

* Three prototypes

** A: inline, in the editor's arrival section

The four fields sit in the feed editor, and under them a small "what this
produces" panel: a gap sparkline, the long-run rate, and the Fano factor of the
gaps. The market state appears as one read-only line — "market: active ×2" —
with a link to the panel.

/For:/ one screen per feed, nothing new to learn, the consequence is where the
choice is.
/Against:/ the run-wide state has no home; the same read-only line repeats in
every editor.

** B: a Market panel beside the tree

The Market Simulator gains a docked panel on the right: state, multiplier, the
auto/calm/active switch, and the list of feeds by sensitivity. The feed editor
keeps its four fields and gains nothing else.

/For:/ the two levels are finally adjacent, and the run's state has one home.
/Against:/ a person editing one feed's clustering must look at two places to
understand it.

** C: one "Arrivals" journey screen

A single screen with a rail: *the run* (state, pin, multiplier), *the feeds* (a
table of every feed's mode, ratio, decay, sensitivity and live rate), *the
preview* (pick one row, see its gaps). The editor's four fields become read-only
with "open in Arrivals".

/For:/ every control in one place, and a table is the only shape that compares
twenty feeds.
/Against:/ it puts the choice away from the row it belongs to, and adds a
second place a rate is edited.

* Decided: B, rebuilt

The panel is the chosen shape, and the first build of it was wrong twice over.

**It ignored the Simple and Advanced convention.** The screen standard already
sets how a screen shows an ORE element's settings: one switch, Simple for the
value in force and Advanced for the raw entries behind it. The panel follows it
— Simple gives the state, the multiplier, who pinned it and the raster; Advanced
opens the chain's own rates and the shared heat's jump and decay, which are the
raw entries behind the same fact.

**A per-feed sparkline cannot show a market-wide factor.** Every argument for the
endogenous half of the factor is about *several* feeds at once: a burst in one
row raising the others, and the rows densifying together when the market turns.
A neuron-spiking raster is the figure that says that — one row per feed, time
across, a tick as a mark — and it makes the pin visible as a vertical mark with a
denser, more correlated field to its right.

* Recommendation

B for the run, A for the row, and the table from C as the *panel's* lower half
rather than a screen of its own: the Market panel lists feeds by sensitivity, so
"who follows the market hardest" is one glance, and the feed's own editor keeps
its four fields with the consequence preview under them.

The argument is the standard's own: a node carries a state and the editors
author one feed. The market is not a feed, so it is not the editor's business;
the clustering is one feed's, so it is not the panel's.

* What to prototype next

Built. The page carries the three shapes, and B is the one that now shows the
market: the tree on the left, the market in the middle with its Simple and
Advanced switch and the raster of every feed's ticks, and the by-sensitivity list
on the right.

The raster is drawn from the same model the service uses: a shared heat that
every event feeds and that fades, each feed's intensity
=sensitivity × (mean × S + heat)=, and its own Hawkes excess on top, which is the
branching ratio's job. The chain turns before the pin as well, so the field to the
left of the mark is the market moving on its own and the field to the right is
the same market held active.

The panel keeps the screen standard's Simple and Advanced switch as a pair, and
Advanced adds the raw rows rather than hiding anything: the chain's rates are in
Simple because they are the value in force, and Advanced adds the shared heat and
the ORE XML path behind the multiplier.
