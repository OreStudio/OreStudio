* Goal

One page says which parts of the path a surface takes are generic, which are
parameterised by asset class, and which are FX only, so the rest of the story knows
what it replaces and what it adds.

* Acceptance

- Every stage is named and given a verdict: the datum and the key grammar, the ingest, the series identity and its classification, the blueprint and its convention, the calibration, the checks, the publish, the withdrawal, the approval kinds, the export and the package, the simulator, and the page.
- A part named generic names the evidence that it is: the test, the table or the call site. A part named FX only names what a second asset class needs instead.
- The verdicts are checked against the claim the kind table makes: that a kind is a row and a declaration of its four parts.
- The page states which of the story's tasks the verdicts confirm, and any the verdicts change.

* Notes

- The datum schema already reads a swaption and a cap/floor key, in ATM and Smile form, with normal, lognormal and shifted-lognormal quotes and the shift that goes with them (=schema.hpp=, rows 20 and 21; =ore_key_volatility.cpp=).
- The classification rules place both in =interest_rates= as volatility (=marketdata_series_classification_rules_populate.sql=).
- The kind table holds three rows, and its comment claims a fourth is a row and a declaration beside it (=derived_market_object_kind.hpp=).
- The calibration record carries the smile model's own columns (rho, eta, gamma) and an FX forward publish id, so it is not asset-class neutral (=fx_vol_surface_calibration.hpp=).
- The export writes an FX option quote per surface point and an FX volatility configuration (=ore_export_service.cpp=).

* Result

The survey is [[file:investigation_what_the_surface_path_assumes_about_fx.org][What the surface path assumes about FX]], written at =819a1245ee=.

- *The quote layer is ready.* The datum, the key grammar, the URI, the classification, the ingest,
  the publish record and the approval mechanics take a swaption quote today, unchanged. The missing
  pieces are two seeds: an =ir.volatility= identity shape and the derived inventory's interest rate
  rows.
- *Five parts are FX only.* The blueprint, the calibration and the checks are FX shapes and take
  their own tables; the export and the simulator are dispatch, one path per kind. The calibration
  record and the blueprint name their FX assumptions in columns, so they cannot be parameterised by
  a nullable asset class.
- *The kind mechanism is closed in code.* A new kind costs an enumerator, a switch case and an
  implementation. The header comment that calls a kind a row and a declaration understates it, and
  the comment is worth correcting.
- *The derivation is the only part that must be lifted rather than copied*, because its measurement
  names no asset class and only its transform, coordinates and smile model do.
- *The story's tasks stand.* The survey sizes the simulator and the export as the risk, not the
  market data tables.

A durable rule the survey produced: a part of the path is per kind when it holds a coordinate, a
model or a convention of the asset class, and shared otherwise. That rule belongs on
[[id:53D14F31-EE32-493E-BBEB-140AA4906BE4][Constructing a volatility surface from quotes to a
published set]], which is not this page's to edit.
