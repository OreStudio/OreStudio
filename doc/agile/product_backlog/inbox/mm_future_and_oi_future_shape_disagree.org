#+title: The shape table gives MM_FUTURE and OI_FUTURE the same corpus shape at two depths
#+description: Both futures types are written ccy/contract-month/exchange:code/tenor, but the shape table qualifies MM_FUTURE at depth 2 and OI_FUTURE at depth 1. Which segments identify the series is unsettled, and it decides what a stored series is.

* What

ORE writes the two listed-futures families in the same shape, and the shape
table describes them two different ways:

| Type | Corpus key | Shape table |
|------+------------+-------------|
| MM_FUTURE | =MM_FUTURE/PRICE/EUR/2025-10/XICE:FEI/3M= | =ccy/expiry/tenor=, qualifier depth 2 |
| OI_FUTURE | =OI_FUTURE/PRICE/USD/2025-08/XCME:SRA/3M= | =ccy/contract_month/exchange:code/tenor=, qualifier depth 1 |

Both keys are six segments and identical in shape: currency, contract month,
exchange and code, then the reference tenor. The depths differ, so the two
types store different things.

At depth 2 the qualifier is =currency/contract-month= and the point is
=exchange:code/tenor=, which gives one series per contract with exactly one
point each -- a series per observation.

At depth 1 the qualifier is the currency alone and the point is
=contract-month/exchange:code/tenor=, which gives one series per currency
holding every contract as a point -- a strip as a curve.

Neither is obviously right, and a third reading is available: the series is the
contract itself, =currency/contract-month/exchange:code= at depth 3, with the
reference tenor as its point.

The corpus cannot settle it either, because it makes the question moot. The
ordering puts the contract month between the currency and the exchange, so a
single qualifier depth cannot separate "the contract month identifies the
series" from "the exchange and tenor do". Every depth is a compromise.

The market data catalogue does not settle it either, and its silence is the
clearest evidence that the shape changed. It documents both as five segments --
=MM_FUTURE/PRICE/USD/2025-09/3M= and =OI_FUTURE/PRICE/USD/2025-09/SOFR=, currency,
contract month, then a tenor or an index -- with no exchange at all. The corpus
writes six. So the exchange was added to the key after the catalogue was
written, and neither our shape table nor the catalogue has been reconciled with
that.

* Why

The depth is not a display choice. It decides what a stored =market_series= row
is, how many observations each holds, and what the classification rules
classify. Changing it after data is imported changes the identity of every row,
and while this project recreates the database from scratch -- so the change is
recoverable -- the decision should still be made rather than absorbed.

It also blocks oresmd. Both types are among the eleven ORE series types with no
oresmd representation, and the oresmd identifier needs to know which segments
are the series' identity before it can carry them. Guessing would put a wrong
identity in the model and, from there, in every stored series.

The other two registry rows that state a depth for a futures-like key agree with
neither, so the correction is not a one-line edit to one row: the shape table
needs a decision about what a listed future's series is, then both rows
rewritten to say it.
