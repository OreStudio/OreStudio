* Plan

** The decision is the inbox's, and the cut adds nothing to it

The two cut kinds already name their deciding function: =reporting.eod_cut_authorisation=
names =inbox::approvals:decide_finance= and =reporting.ipv_cut_authorisation= names
=inbox::approvals:decide_market_risk=. The inbox's decide operation already resolves a
kind's permission, refuses a decision by the person who asked, requires a comment on a
refusal, and moves the request to =approved= or =refused=. Nothing in the decide path
changed, and the demo already holds the decider: the =Finance= role carries
=inbox::approvals:decide_finance=, and =rachel.byrd=, =beth.clark=, =graham.pierce= and
=hoi.yan.lee= hold it in the Acme tenant beside =henry.jones=, who takes the close. No
new seed was needed; the task's own supply test pins the kind's declarations.

** The apply is a reconciliation, as a role grant is

=ores_reporting_unapplied_cut_authorisations_fn= answers every decided cut-authorisation
request the cut has not been stamped with, across every tenant that is not terminated. It
keys on the cut kinds' own =approval_kind_code= and not on a list, so a third cut kind
needs no code change. =cut_authorisation_applier= writes each cut in its own tenant and as
the person who decided, stamps the decision's own instant rather than the run's clock, and
marks the detail row applied in the same unit of work. A row leaves the answer once applied,
so the second run writes nothing and the cut keeps its version.

The service runs it at start and on every change to an approval request; a sweep every 60
seconds reads the store for what no event announced.

** What the screens read

The BFF joins the subject a cut's request is about in one read of the detail rows, then
reads each cut the rows name, so the request page draws the label, the kind, the instant,
the scope and the counts. The cut's Authorisation tab states who stamped the decision and
when, from the detail row's applied facts, and the history shows the version the apply
wrote.

** What is not done

Nothing was built or run: the branch's C++ and web suites have not executed, so the apply's
cases, the inbox's cases and the BFF and web cases are written and unproven. The live walk
of the decide needs a cut whose body the take froze in the demo tenant, and the demo tenant
holds no published curve set, so no live EOD take can raise a request there yet.

* Result

** Built

- =ores_reporting_unapplied_cut_authorisations_fn=, with its drop script and the registry's
  execute prefix; applied to the live database additively as one function, and granted to the
  reporting service role.
- =cut_authorisation_applier= in =ores.reporting.core=: an approval stamps =approved_by= and
  =approved_at= and marks the detail applied; a refusal stamps =refused_at= and
  =refusal_comment=; the stamp is the decision's own instant and the row is skipped when it
  already holds it.
- The reporting service runs the reconciliation at start, on the approval request's change
  event, and every sixty seconds.
- =reporting.v1.ops.list_cut_authorisation_subjects=: the body a cut authorisation is about,
  answered for a page of requests in one call, from the model and through codegen to the
  protocol, the shell command and the TypeScript twin.
- The BFF's cut-subject join, the request page's subject block, and the cut record's applied
  facts.
- Tests: the apply's five cases, the inbox's four cases on the two cut kinds, the BFF's five
  cases on the join, and the web's two on the applied facts.

** The apply is a reconciliation across tenants, and that bit a test**

The first run of the apply's refusal case failed with =0 == 1= while the store held the
stamped cut: the reporting service running beside the suite had swept the tenant the test
provisions and applied the row first. The cases now assert the facts (the cut stamped, the
detail applied, one instant for both) and not which reconciler reached them first, because
the count belongs to the store and not to the case.

** Verified live**

=evidence/cut_decide_live.txt= carries the whole walk: henry.jones raises a cut's
authorisation; four eyes refuses him with =code=four_eyes=; rachel.byrd, a Finance second
person, approves it; a second request is refused and admits no second answer; a third is
withdrawn by the asker; the request page answers =cutSubjectState=missing= because no body
was frozen; and the story shows the decision beside the ask. The apply's stamps are shown in
the same store, taken from the rows its own cases wrote, with the cut's version history
naming the request.

** What the live walk cannot reach, and why**

No live take can freeze a whole body in the demo tenant. The store holds no published curve
set, every market observation it holds is an FX spot, and the one IR bootstrap config points
at a series with no observations, so neither =publish_curve= nor =republish_curve= can produce
a set. A take of an incomplete body raises no request, which is the take task's own finding.
The step that needs it — a request the take raised, whose decision the apply then stamps — is
covered by the suite against the same database instead.

** Remaining**

1. A live end-to-end walk needs a published curve set in the demo tenant: a real IR bootstrap
   config over series that hold observations, and a Market Risk decider to approve its
   publication. That is market data's work, not this task's.
2. The shell cannot state an empty argument, so a refusal with no comment can only be shown by
   the suite. The command grammar's sentinel is =__none__= for a list and there is none for a
   string.
