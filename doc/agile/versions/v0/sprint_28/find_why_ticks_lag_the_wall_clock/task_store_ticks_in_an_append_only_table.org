* Goal

The observation table stops at about 8,300 rows per second for one connection because its insert trigger runs for every row. An append-only table with one index reaches about 187,000 at 162 bytes for each row. Move ticks to it, and give the readers of the observation table a view over both.

* Acceptance

- A hypertable for ticks with no trigger, no valid_to and one index on the series and the time, with a retention policy for each class and no compression.
- The ingest writes ticks to it, and a batch needs no dedupe against a trigger.
- A view joins it with the observation table, and the readers keep their results: snapshots, evolution, slices, ORE export, curve pillars, lineage and the feed binding activity.
- The ingest drains a burst at more than 100,000 rows per second on one connection, measured with the tick injector.

* Plan

(Implementation strategy. Written when work starts; key decisions
are distilled into the parent story's =* Decisions= at close, but the
plan itself stays — it is the historical record of what we did.)

Decision of the owner, 2026-10-21: this task keeps one row for each series. A series is one
observation with its provenance, owned by a tenant and a party, so ticks are not shared and one
row for each source does not apply.

The design is in the section on tenant and party isolation of the tick ingest pipeline note.
In short: one append-only hypertable for each retention class, with =tenant_id=, =party_id=,
=series_id=, =ts=, =coord= and =value=. The reads run under the tenant policy and the party
policy, in the scalar subquery form. A writer role with =BYPASSRLS= and =INSERT= only writes
with =COPY=. The tests of write rate, read cost and isolation are done and recorded in the
note. The table is not compressed, because row-level security is not supported on compressed
chunks (Timescale documentation). Retention drops whole chunks. The order of work:

1. Provision the writer role in the role script. Done: =ores_<env>_marketdata_writer= has
   =BYPASSRLS=, is not in the service role and holds no grant. The =INSERT= grant on the tick
   table comes with the table in step 2, and the role never gets =SELECT=. The =.env= carries
   =ORES_MARKETDATA_SERVICE_WRITER_DB_USER= and =ORES_MARKETDATA_SERVICE_WRITER_DB_PASSWORD=, from =.env=
   format version 33.
2. Write the table, its index, its two policies and its retention class, with the notification
   trigger off. Done in =create/marketdata/marketdata_ticks_create.sql=, as =ores_marketdata_ticks_tbl=,
   with one-day chunks, a seven-day retention policy where the licence allows it, and =INSERT= for
   the writer role. The script is hand-written, because the entity template makes a trigger and a
   =valid_to=. A scratch database ran it twice, and the checks of isolation passed: a session with no
   settings read 0 rows, one tenant read its own 2 rows, one party read 1 row, a tenant with another
   tenant's party read 0, and the writer was refused =SELECT=.
3. Move the ingest to =COPY= with the owner copied from the cached series, and give the service
   a second pool for the writer role. Done as a copy next to the observation write, so the
   readers of the observation table keep working until step 4 moves them. =tick_writer= holds
   one libpq connection, not a pool, because the ingest worker is its only caller, and it writes
   each batch with one =COPY=. The loop copies the tick of every stored item with the owner of
   its binding, which is the owner of its series. A failed copy is logged once, counted, and does
   not change the store or the republish. A datum with a coordinate is not copied, because the
   table has no coordinate dictionary yet, and the loop warns once for each such series. The role
   comes from =ORES_MARKETDATA_SERVICE_WRITER_DB_USER= and =..._PASSWORD=, and an empty user turns
   the copy off. The observation write goes when step 4 ends. Tests: the row format, the write to
   a database that is not there, the parser, and a loop test that copies a tick as the writer and
   reads it back as the marketdata service, which has no =BYPASSRLS=: 1 row for the party of the
   series and 0 for another party.
4. Point the readers at the table, one at a time, with a test for each. A reader of a party or a
   tenant lists its series first. Done so far:
   - The coordinate dictionary, =ores_marketdata_tick_coords_tbl=. The ingest gives each datum
     of a series a number the first time it sees it, and the copy stores that number in =coord=.
     Coordinate 0 is the datum of a series without coordinates. The loop now copies datums with
     coordinates, so the warning of step 3 is gone. The table has the tenant policy only,
     because the ingest context names no party.
   - Reader 1, the as-of snapshot (=read_as_of_records=, which =read_as_of= calls). It serves the
     snapshot, the curve pillars and the curve republish. It reads the latest tick of each
     coordinate with one index probe, and it reads the observation table as before. On a tie
     the observation row wins. Four tests: a series without coordinates, two coordinates and
     an instant, a party that does not own the series, and the tie.
   Still to do: the buckets (evolution), the instants (slices), the latest rows of a series (ORE
   export), and the newest row of a source (feed binding activity). Two questions block the end
   of the step. The ingest writes a second row for each tick under the close of the previous
   business day, and the tick table does not hold it. The tick table also has no source, so the
   activity of a binding has nothing to read.
5. Measure a burst with the tick injector.
6. Open a separate task for the session functions of the policies, which cost seven times on an
   indexed read.
