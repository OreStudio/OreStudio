* What

`index_family` lists 22 benchmarks. `tiie` is there; `ftiie` is not. The corpus
writes both forms:

| Key | Count |
|-----+-------|
| =IR_SWAP/RATE/MXN/FTIIE/1D/1D/130M= and 41 sibling maturities | 42 |
| =MM/RATE/MXN/FTIIE/0D/1D= | 4 |

These are the whole of what remains unnamed in those two types: after the
seven-segment swap form and the ESTER spelling were carried, `IR_SWAP` stands at
13,841 of 13,883 and `MM` at 1,672 of 1,676.

F-TIIE is the *TIIE de Fondeo*, Mexico's overnight funding rate. It is not a
spelling of =tiie=, which is the 28-day interbank rate; it is a second Mexican
benchmark, and ORE quotes curves on both. So it belongs in the family list on
its merits rather than as an alias.

* Why it is not a one-line change

The family list is mirrored by a CHECK constraint in
=projects/ores.sql/create/synthetic/synthetic_ir_curve_generation_configs_create.sql=,
in two places: the membership list and the tenor-combo rule that splits term
families (libor, euribor) from overnight ones. A family the enum holds and the
constraint does not is a drift the codegen gates exist to catch, so the change
is:

1. =ftiie= in the Index family table of =ir_quote_type.org=, which regenerates
   =oresmd_enums.hpp=.
2. =ftiie= in both constraint lists, classified as overnight.
3. A one-shot migration widening the constraint, following
   =synthetic_ir_curve_generation_configs_tenor_combo_relax_migration.sql=.
4. The two template comments and =oresmd_index_family_utils.hpp= that state the
   family count and name the constraint.

That is six files in two components, to name 46 keys.

* Why it was deferred rather than done

The open question is not mechanical: it is whether ores models F-TIIE curve
configurations at all. The constraint governs
=ores_synthetic_ir_curve_generation_configs_tbl=, which is an ores concept, and
adding a family there is a claim that ores seeds and bootstraps MXN F-TIIE
curves. Nothing in the corpus or the seeds says that. Adding the family to
satisfy the key parser would widen an ores domain contract to fit an ORE file
naming, which is the wrong direction of fit.

Until that is answered, the gap is named and counted rather than closed.
=oresmd= names every ORE series *type*; this is one missing member of a closed
enumerated vocabulary, which is a different kind of gap.

The same walk turns up one more such token: three
=IR_SWAP/RATE/.../NoDiscount/...= keys, where the index slot holds a curve
marker rather than a benchmark. Those want the index slot to accept an arbitrary
name, which is a larger change than either of the two above and is not sized
here.
