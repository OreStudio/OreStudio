* Goal

Every synthetic volatility surface is struck against the forward of the run's own latent curves. A surface's quotes are then the pair's surface at the moneyness its own rate quotes imply, a curve tick moves the surface's quotes without a restart, and a party's calibration reads them at the strikes its bootstrap recovers.

The design is [[id:675E89FC-B5AA-4250-8B86-78062F0F22BC][The forward a synthetic surface is struck against]], reached by [[id:4825DEE0-D3E4-4E38-B46F-0C5EA838F1E0][the investigation]]. The vendor publishes raw quotes and never a curve.

What has already landed on this task's branch, and is not work again: the one market resolution (=math::pair_market_at=) shared with the calibration, the calibration's adoption of it, one market per expiry in the surface feed's build input, and the proof that a surface's quotes are invariant to the spot level at =1e-12= even when its two discounts differ.

* Acceptance

- A curve feed publishes its dated discount factors to the run's board on every tick, and a board entry's factor at a pillar date equals that process's own =discount_factor= at the pillar's horizon to =1e-12=.
- A spot feed publishes its level to the board on every tick, and the level read back is the process's own current value.
- A surface feed builds one market per expiry from the board on every tick and re-solves its strikes, so a curve tick reaches the surface's quotes with no restart and no reconfiguration.
- A surface whose pair the run does not carry publishes nothing, and names the member it could not read. It never quotes against a flat forward.
- A pair whose currencies have no configured curve feed is refused when the start verb builds it, with the missing currency named.
- A two-percent discount differential moves a surface's quoted volatilities by the amount the strike solve implies, and the test states that amount rather than a direction.
- With the curve unchanged and the SSVI state unchanged, two consecutive ticks emit identical quotes, so the board adds no drift of its own.
- A party's calibration of the clean quotes lands on the same strikes within a tolerance the test states, and the number is measured once and recorded in Measurements with its command and commit.
- No curve is published as market data, no marketdata operation is added, and no schema changes.

* Design

Settled, because the stages depend on the shapes:

- *The board.* =market_board=, in =ores.analytics.quant= beside =latent_market=: one instance per run, owned by the application next to =run_market=, written and read only on the scheduler's loop thread. It holds named members of two shapes -- a currency's dated discount series and a pair's spot -- keyed by a kind and a code, in flat storage that a republish reuses, so a tick allocates nothing.
- *The publishers.* The IR curve feed writes =curve:<CCY>= from its resolved entries' end dates and =discount_factor(ticks_ahead_end)=, with the spot date at one; the FX spot feed writes =spot:<PAIR>= with its current level. Both take an optional board, and a feed built without one publishes nothing, which is what every existing test wants.
- *The read.* The surface feed holds a =const market_board*=, parses its expiry tenors into periods once (the parser and the period addition the calibration already uses), and each tick builds one =fx_market= per expiry through =math::pair_market_at=.
- *The date rule.* The period-to-date addition moves out of the calibration into =ores.analytics.quant=, so both sides place an expiry on a date by one rule. The calibration adopts it and its tests stay as they are.
- *The emitter.* =fx_vol_surface_emitter::step= takes the per-expiry markets; =make= stops taking them. The markets are positional in the emitter's own expiry order and checked by size, so a tick does no string work.
- *Two gates, deliberately different.* A configured pair whose currencies have no configured curve feed is refused when the start verb builds the feed: that is a configuration error, static, and it names the currency. A board member missing at tick time publishes nothing and says which: that is not an error but an ordering, because a surface may tick before any curve has, and a curve may be stopped.

* Notes

Why the spot is a member at all, when the quotes provably do not need it: the strike the emitter solves is scaled by the spot, so a published strike or a delta hedge would need the real one, and the spot's covered-interest-parity drift -- later work -- needs it too. Leaving it at one would be a second deliberate lie in a task whose subject is removing the first.

Why the markets move from =make= to =step=: with a board the market stops being a constant of the feed. Keeping =make='s markets would mean a surface struck once against whatever the board held at start, which is the defect at lower frequency.

The ordering risk is real and is the point of the tick-time gate: the scheduler's own ordering means a surface may tick before any curve has. That tick publishes nothing and says "no market: curve:EUR" rather than stepping an empty market, which is why the gate is on the tick and not only on the start verb.
