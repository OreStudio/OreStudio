#+title: The publication lifecycle of derived market objects
#+description: Every curve, FX forward set and volatility surface becomes authoritative the same way: compute a candidate, raise, decide, apply, and later supersede or withdraw, implemented once and parameterised by the kind.
#+type: knowledge
#+level: cross
#+filetags: :architecture:marketdata:publish:approval:four_eyes:
#+created: 2026-11-11
#+updated: 2026-11-24

* Summary

A curve, an FX forward set, a volatility surface and any kind added later go
through one lifecycle, implemented once in =ores.marketdata= and parameterised by
the kind. A kind supplies only what
differs in finance: its subject, its compute, its checks and its approval kinds.
Everything else, the record of a publish, the states, the raise, the apply, the
supersession, the withdrawal, the notices, the manual marks and the read, is the
same for every kind, so the same act behaves the same way whatever is published.

Two objects make up the lifecycle and not one. A *request* asks, and a *set* is
what the request makes authoritative. The two have their own states, and the acts of
the lifecycle are the transitions of both: a raise opens a request, a decision
closes it, an apply publishes a set, and a withdrawal ends one.

* Detail

** The two objects

| Object | What it is | Its states | Its record |
|--------+------------+------------+------------|
| The request | The approval that authorises one candidate version of one subject at one as-of | Waiting, held, approved, refused, expired, withdrawn | The inbox's approval request |
| The set | The published rows of the subject's output series, with the authority over them | Candidate, raised, published, superseded, withdrawn, closed | The publish record ([[id:EEE9DBF4-F190-4FCD-8193-40B583184BD4][The lifecycle record of a derived market object]]) |

The distinction keeps the two questions apart. The request answers who authorised the
act and on what grounds; the set answers which numbers are in use and until
when. A set outlives its request, and a supersession and a withdrawal act on the
set after the request that created it has closed.

** The states of a set

#+caption: The states of a published set, from the candidate to its supersession or its withdrawal.
#+attr_html: :width 100%
[[proj:doc/knowledge/architecture/publish_lifecycle_states.png]]

Source: [[proj:doc/knowledge/architecture/publish_lifecycle_states.puml][=publish_lifecycle_states.puml=]]. Regenerate with
=plantuml -tpng doc/knowledge/architecture/publish_lifecycle_states.puml=. The
states are those of a set: a candidate, the request raised for it, and the
published set that comes of it.

| State | Meaning |
|-------+---------|
| Candidate | Computed and stored as a version, with its lineage, its inputs and its checks. Not consumable. |
| Raised | A request names the candidate's version and waits on a decision. |
| Approved | The request is approved by a person and waits for the trader to apply it, within the kind's apply window. |
| Published | Applied. The newest published set of the series that is not withdrawn is the live set. |
| Superseded | A set at a newer or equal as-of was applied after it. It is still the set for an as-of before its successor's. |
| Withdrawn | Taken out of use under an approved withdrawal. No read by as-of takes it. |
| Closed | The request was refused, withdrawn, expired or lapsed, or its apply was refused, so nothing was published. |

A request closed under a stated rule is applied in the transaction that raised it,
so it passes from Raised to Published at once and is never left approved.

** The states of a request

#+caption: The states of the approval request that authorises a set, and the apply that publishes it.
#+attr_html: :width 100%
[[proj:doc/knowledge/architecture/publish_request_states.png]]

Source: [[proj:doc/knowledge/architecture/publish_request_states.puml][=publish_request_states.puml=]]. Regenerate with
=plantuml -tpng doc/knowledge/architecture/publish_request_states.puml=. The
request has the inbox's states for every kind, and the apply is an act over it
rather than a state of it.

A request publishes once. The apply refuses a request that has published
already, and the next publish is a new request, because an approval is a decision
on one candidate version and a second publish decides different numbers.

** The acts

Six acts make up the lifecycle, and each is documented once for every kind.

| Act | What it does | The page that states it |
|-----+--------------+-------------------------|
| Compute | Writes the candidate and its checks as a version, before any person is asked | [[id:24950B8E-7FC4-4F0A-8AA4-9603B344F970][Candidate computation and storage before publication]] |
| Raise | Asks for one candidate version to become the set of its series at its as-of | [[id:BF14C932-D699-4224-98DA-0481E49FA394][Raising a publish request for a candidate version]] |
| Decide | A second person answers, or a stated rule closes the request | [[id:71146F3F-B884-4E61-BA8B-A8A0731703C6][Rules for the automatic approval of requests]], [[id:0FE6BDD9-EB24-4B07-8B0C-D816AAF1BEBF][Approval requests]] |
| Apply | Publishes the set, supersedes the sets it replaces and writes the notice, in one transaction | [[id:86D8986F-5247-4138-85C7-F8899C029F24][The trader applies an approved publish, which supersedes the live set and tells its readers]] |
| Withdraw | Takes a published set out of use, with its reason kept | [[id:E474FB93-2ACE-4981-9761-33E214B3D268][Withdrawal of published market data sets]] |
| Read | Answers with the set the as-of rule selects, and never with a candidate | [[id:86158AAD-423A-421A-8BE2-CC5123109297][As-of resolution of published market data sets]] |

Two further subjects run across the acts, and both are documented once for every
kind: a person may correct a candidate by hand before the apply, and every act
that moves a published set tells its readers
([[id:C1C37A35-7DE8-4FD2-8A2F-E7E2CF08EED4][Manual marks on a derived market object]],
[[id:B3F03DBB-EBD1-4649-94E5-6C5C1B67A084][Notification of a change to a published market data set]]).

** What a kind supplies

A kind is a declaration and never a copy of the lifecycle. It states four things.

| Part | What the kind states |
|------+----------------------|
| Subject | What one set is a set of, and the party that owns it: a bootstrap configuration, a currency pair, a surface blueprint. The subject names the output series the set is written to. |
| Compute | The operation that computes a candidate for a subject at an as-of. |
| Checks | What a candidate is checked against, which checks block a raise, and which a decider reads. |
| Approval kinds | One kind for a publish and one for a withdrawal, each with the permission that raises it, the permission that decides it, its auto-approval code and its apply window. |

The two approval kinds are part of the declaration and not an afterthought. A
kind that names a publish kind and no withdrawal kind cannot take a wrong set out
of use, and a kind that names neither cannot be published at all.

** The kinds

Three kinds exist in the tree, and each supplies the declaration above. A row
states the whole of what a kind owns.

| Kind | Subject | Compute | Checks | Publish kind | Withdrawal kind |
|------+---------+---------+--------+------------------+-----------------|
| IR curve | a bootstrap configuration | the bootstrap | one, on the recipe | =marketdata.curve_publish= | =marketdata.curve_withdrawal= |
| FX forward set | a currency pair | the forward compute, by covered interest parity from two published curves | the recipe and the inputs' state | =marketdata.fx_forward_publish= | none |
| FX volatility surface | a surface blueprint | the calibration | two: the recipe, and the result's freedom from arbitrage | =marketdata.fx_vol_surface_publish= | none |

** The record

A publish is one record, whatever its kind, with the columns every kind shares.
The subject is a part of its own, one table per kind, keyed by the publish. A
reader of any kind reads one shape. The record holds no values and no checks,
because those belong to the candidate version the decision covered
([[id:EEE9DBF4-F190-4FCD-8193-40B583184BD4][The lifecycle record of a derived market object]]).

** Where it lives

=ores.marketdata.service= holds the one implementation. A kind is a declaration
beside it, never a copy of it: its subject part, its compute operation, its
checks and its approval kinds. The messages of every kind name the same steps:
compute, raise, apply, withdraw and read. The approval record is written by the
inbox's library inside the same database transaction, because the inbox and
market data share one database.

** Where the kinds stand

The lifecycle is one implementation in the target state and three in the tree.
=curve_publish_gate.cpp=, =fx_forward_publish_service.cpp= and
=fx_vol_surface_publish_service.cpp= each carry their own raise, apply,
supersession and notice, and each copy has fallen behind the others in a
different place.

| Capability | IR curve | FX forward set | FX volatility surface |
|------------+----------+----------------+-----------------------|
| Compute into a candidate | yes | yes | yes |
| Publish under an approval | yes | yes | yes |
| Supersede the set it replaces | yes | no | yes |
| Withdraw a published set | yes | no | no |
| Publish notice | yes | no | yes |
| Withdrawal notice | yes | no | no |
| List the party's sets | yes | no | no |
| Publish on a schedule | yes | no | no |

A fourth kind written today would be a fourth copy, and the copies would diverge
as these three have. The correction is one implementation over the declaration
above, so that a kind is a declaration and never a copy.
