* What

Find every column that holds an oresmd URI, and see that each either carries the
validation or records why it does not. Where a field names something, pin what it
is allowed to name, not only that the URI parses.

The vocabulary appears in more places than the one that prompted this. The market
series `oresmd_uri`. The synthetic feed configs' target URI. The vintage series URI
on the IR curve generation config. The reference curve on a bond issue, which now
has a validator. Any curve or index reference on trades, credit, volatility and the
rest. Search the models and the schema rather than working from memory, because the
list above is what one person happened to notice.

* Why

`ores_marketdata_validate_curve_series_fn` was written for a single column,
`bond_issue.reference_curve_id`, and wired into that model's generated insert
trigger. One column is not the class.

The defect that started this was possible because the ORE key parser takes the
curve segment of `ZERO/RATE/ccy/curve/daycounter/tenor` verbatim and nothing checks
what it may contain, so a producer that spelled it as an internal identifier and a
producer that spelled it as a symbolic name both succeeded. A validator that only
proves the URI parses would have accepted the bad one, which is why the grammar has
to say what each field names.

Two properties found while fixing that one column will shape every validator here.
Market series are party-scoped, so a key that ignores the party can match another
party's series. And retiring a tenant leaves its market data current, so a stale
tenant's rows stay visible to anything that reads series without scoping on a
current tenant.

* References

- The bond issue field and its validator, added while pricing a report run's bonds.
- `ores_marketdata_validate_curve_series_fn`, which reads the series URI rather
  than joining the identity projection, because a series inserted by SQL has no
  projection row until a backfill runs.
