* Goal

The sign-in screen offers the providers the deployment enables beside the
password, and a person who chooses one knows what will happen before they leave
ORE Studio. A first sign-in says what the registration policy will give them,
and says so plainly when their account must wait for an approval.

A person then sees what is linked to their account and removes it if they wish.
Every session and every sign-in says how it happened, so a person can tell their
own sign-in from an agent's and can see one they do not recognise. A client that
asks for access to ORE shows a consent screen that states what it asks for.

The screens tell the story the platform now makes true: a person can arrive
without a password, and can leave without losing their way back in.

* Acceptance

- The sign-in screen offers each provider the deployment enables, and none it
  does not, without a build change.
- A first sign-in states what the policy will give the person, including the
  state where an approval is pending, before the provider is chosen.
- Every refusal reaches the screen with the reason it happened, in the person's
  language, and never as a raw code.
- Security shows each link with its provider, the address the provider stated
  and the date, links another after the password is proven, and removes one,
  refusing when it is the last way in.
- The session list and the sign-in history name how each session was signed in,
  and an agent's sessions stay shown apart from its person's.
- A client is approved or refused on a consent screen that states the client,
  the person, the party, the permissions and the term, and approving creates the
  grant the person can see and revoke.
- The screens are pinned by tests, and every string is translated.
- The gates cover the new code.

* Decisions

- *Prototype before building* (2026-11-25). The identity screens are a design
  question before they are an implementation one: a person meets ORE here
  before they trust it with anything, and the platform has no precedent for a
  door that leaves the application. The first task prototypes variants and
  chooses one with a person using it.
- *The consent screen is part of this story* (2026-11-25). The consent a client
  shows is the only screen an agent's person meets at the moment an agent gains
  power, so it is designed with the doors and not left to the client.
