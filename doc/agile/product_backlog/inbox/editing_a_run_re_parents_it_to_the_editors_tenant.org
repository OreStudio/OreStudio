This page is a [[id:671F18E4-E09C-4B3B-BD24-D33DF8AE38A6][capture]] in the *inbox* bucket of the product backlog — a pre-sprint idea, not yet pulled into a sprint as a story.

* What

Make an edit through the generated verbs keep a row in the tenant that owns it.
Today `tenant_id` is server-owned and stamped from the session, so a caller in
the system tenant editing another tenant's run writes the row into its own
tenant. The result is two open rows for one id, one in each tenant, and whichever
the reader picks depends on the reader.

Decide whether a caller may edit across tenants at all. If it may, the owning
tenant has to survive the write. If it may not, the verb should refuse rather
than silently re-parent.

* Why

Met while fixing the erase-on-set timestamps, and unrelated to them. It is
pre-existing and reachable, and it produces exactly the shape the bitemporal
machinery exists to prevent: two current rows for one identity.

The reason it has stayed invisible is the same reason the other tenant defects
have: nothing reads the row by id alone, so both rows answer and the reader
takes whichever it finds first.
