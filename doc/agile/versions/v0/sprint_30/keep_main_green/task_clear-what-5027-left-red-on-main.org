* Goal

The merge that put the C++ server in front of the web app left two workflows red
on =main=:

- =Compass check=: the route inventory page had five exception rows with four
  cells where the table needs six, which made the whole page unreadable, and two
  rows of its Rules table had been pasted into Views. Three types had grown past
  the wide-type ceiling as well.
- =TypeScript=: two sign-in tests assert the switch between the servers, and the
  ports they name are the ones the build defines, which the tests never set.

Neither is a design problem; both are the kind of breakage a merge leaves and a
gate catches.

* Plan

The repairs, in the order the gate reports them:

- =compass/checks/route_inventory.org=: the five rows in =* Exceptions= that
  carried =| POST | /api/market/… | POST | /api/market/… |= are deleted; they
  belong to =* Pairs=, whose four columns are the BFF method and path and the
  C++ method and path, so they move there. The two rows of =* Rules= that had
  been pasted into =* Views= — the four market-data desk reads and the
  authorisation queue, whose =bff= counterparts already sit in Rules — move
  back beside them.
- The inventory document is regenerated from the repaired tables with
  =compass generate=.
- =wide_types=: the three types the switch grew or added are excused with a
  =compass:except= comment above each, expiring in two weeks, naming what each
  holds and that splitting it is the next step. The check's own mechanism for
  debt.
- =ServerSwitch= takes its ports as a prop, defaulting to the compile-time
  =__ORE_SERVER_PORTS__=, so the two sign-in tests state =20401=/=20402= instead
  of depending on the .env of whoever runs them.

The tests that failed for the missing global are the ones that already existed:
they passed before only when the build's ports happened to be the ones they
assert.

* Notes

Two of the three repairs are the same mistake in different tables: a merge took
rows that belong to one table and appended them to another, so the page said
both that a row had the wrong number of cells and that it could not be read at
all. The cell count against each header is what finds it, and it is worth a check
of its own rather than a reader's eye.

The TypeScript failure is the more interesting one. The two tests were not
broken by the switch; they had always depended on the build's own ports, which
are =20401= and =20402= in the environment file and =21601= and =8080= by
default. They passed locally and failed on a runner without the file. The
component now takes the ports it reads the page against, so the test states them
and the bundle still gets the compile-time ones.

The three wide types are debt, not repairs: the exception says so, names what
each type holds, and expires in two weeks.

* Result

Both workflows that the merge left red are answered. =compass check= reports 0
diagnostics: the five malformed exception rows are gone from the table that
could not hold them, the two rows that had been pasted into Views are back in
Rules beside their =bff= counterparts, the five route pairs are in Pairs, and the
inventory is regenerated from the repaired page. The three wide types are
excused with a date and a reason rather than left as warnings the gate reports
on every run. The ores.web suites pass, 1335 tests in the web package among
them, and the two sign-in tests now state the ports they assert instead of
inheriting them from whichever environment built the bundle.
