#+title: Demand drives what an adapter publishes
#+description: How the service computes the set of ids each adapter must serve, when it sends the set, and why demand also feeds metering.
#+type: knowledge
#+level: cross
#+filetags: :marketdata:demand:adapters:metering:
#+created: 2026-10-22
#+updated: 2026-10-22

* Summary

An adapter publishes only the ids that parties need. The service computes the
demand set of each adapter from the subscriptions and the routes of its
parties, and sends the set by request and reply. A live adapter then pays the
vendor for those ids only. The same demand intervals feed metering.

* Detail

** How the set is computed

The demand set of an adapter is the union, over all parties routed to the
adapter, of the external ids that the parties' items map to. A party's items
come from its explicit requirements and from the composites it uses. A
composite adds the ids of all its inputs.

** When the service sends it

- When an adapter becomes =ready=, and after it recovers from =stale=.
- When a route, a subscription or a requirement changes.
- The set always replaces the previous set. The service does not send deltas,
  so a lost message cannot leave the adapter with a wrong set.

** The reply

The adapter answers with the ids it accepts and the ids it rejects. A reason
goes with each rejection, such as =unknown_id= or =not_entitled=. The service
shows the rejected ids on the source's screen.

** Demand intervals

The service records when each (party, instrument) pair enters and leaves the
set. See [[id:3E51B0BD-D22B-4790-821F-E2FED44D152C][Usage is recorded as events and priced later]]. Demand is therefore both a control signal and a billing
fact.
