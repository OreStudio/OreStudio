* Goal

The publishing model is settled, documented and implemented, and the design of
what a person sees is signed off. Nothing in ores.web shows any of it: no screen
names a dataset, says whether it is published, or offers the publish action. This
story builds that place, and the server operations it reads.

The design is the record: the five journeys in [[id:50550498-38ED-4A07-AF28-209BAFF378F0][DQ journeys]] and their
signed-off prototypes. The screens follow those journeys, and they are written
from the shared parts rather than from the prototype markup.

* Acceptance

- ores.web shows each dataset, its artefacts, whether it is published, and
  offers the publish action.
- Each of the five journeys can be walked against a freshly recreated database,
  on screens built from the shared parts.
- Every read the screens make is served by ores.http. No route is added to the
  BFF.

* Decisions

- *The single screens task is decomposed into four operations and four screens*
  (2026-11-25, marco). One task covering five screens and five server
  operations could not close on anything: the coverage screen landed inside it
  and the task still read as unstarted work. The operations are their own tasks
  because each unblocks more than one screen, and the screens follow them in the
  order the journeys imply.
- *The reads are served by ores.http, not the BFF* (2026-11-22, marco). The BFF
  is being decommissioned, so a screen wired to it would spend its life on a path
  with no future.
- *The screens are written from the shared parts* (2026-11-22, marco). Neither
  the prototypes nor the Qt client is the source: the prototypes are the design
  record and the Qt inventory is evidence of what the old client offered.
- *Split from the story that settled the model* (2026-11-25, marco). The
  publishers, the levels and the guarantees belong to
  [[id:7D2BBBBC-36A3-4BFB-9673-0F9005991D94][Take the DQ pipeline end to end, from a seeded artefact table to a published system]],
  which closed at sprint 30. Keeping the build inside it would have left a
  closed sprint owning work no task of that sprint covered.

* Out of scope
