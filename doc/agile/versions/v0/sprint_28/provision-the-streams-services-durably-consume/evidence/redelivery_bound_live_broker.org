#+title: Evidence: what bounds a durable step command
#+description: Live-broker measurements of the delivery bound, the ack wait, and the point at which a consumer gives a command up.

This is the evidence for [[id:B5475503-B59E-42F8-B022-A961D7C281C0][Settle the
redelivery bound]]. Every number below comes from a running =nats-server=, not
from reading the client code. The server reports its own consumer state.

* Settings as the server stores them

The running fleet, read with =nats consumer info=:

| Consumer | AckPolicy | AckWait | MaxDeliver | MaxAckPending | Backoff |
|----------+-----------+---------+------------+---------------+---------|
| =ores_dev_festive_dijkstra_refdata_steps_consumer= | explicit | 30 s | 10 | 1000 | none |
| =workflow-engine-start= | explicit | 30 s | 10 | 1000 | none |
| =workflow-engine-step-completed= | explicit | 30 s | 10 | 1000 | none |
| a consumer created with no =AckWait= and no =MaxDeliver= | explicit | 30 s | -1 (unlimited) | 1000 | none |

The last row is the control: the server's own default is a 30-second ack wait
and an unlimited delivery count. ORE Studio passes 10 and 30 s explicitly. The
step streams store =interest= retention and =max_age= 86400000000000 ns (one
day); the =workflow= stream stores =limits= retention and seven days.

* The delivery bound is not a downtime window

Scratch =nats-server= 2.14.6, a push consumer with =MaxDeliver= 3 and
=AckWait= 2 s, no subscriber bound. The command is published, then the
consumer state is read after seven seconds, which is more than three ack
waits:

#+begin_example
published with no subscriber
after 7s:
  delivered=0 ack_floor=0 num_ack_pending=0 num_redelivered=0 num_pending=1 push_bound=None
  stream messages=1 retention=interest max_age_ns=86400000000000
bind a core queue subscriber for 4s:
  delivered=1 ack_floor=0 num_ack_pending=1 num_redelivered=1 num_pending=0 push_bound=True
subscriber saw the command: 3 time(s)
#+end_example

No delivery was spent during the seven seconds. The command was delivered when
a subscriber bound. The bound counts deliveries, and an unbound push consumer
makes none.

The same holds when a member dies holding an unacknowledged message. The
message stays pending through ten seconds of no subscriber, with
=num_redelivered= 0, and the next subscriber receives it.

* When the bound is spent

Scratch =nats-server= 2.14.6, a subscriber bound but never acknowledging,
=MaxDeliver= 3 and =AckWait= 2 s. Sampled once per second:

#+begin_example
t=1s  delivered=1 ack_floor=0 num_ack_pending=1 num_redelivered=0
t=2s  delivered=1 ack_floor=0 num_ack_pending=1 num_redelivered=1
t=5s  delivered=1 ack_floor=0 num_ack_pending=1 num_redelivered=1
t=6s  delivered=1 ack_floor=0 num_ack_pending=0 num_redelivered=1
t=10s delivered=1 ack_floor=0 num_ack_pending=0 num_redelivered=1
stream messages=1 retention=interest max_age_ns=86400000000000
the never-acking subscriber saw the command 3 times
rebind the same consumer: the subscriber saw the command 0 times
a different consumer from DeliverAll: received the command
#+end_example

When the last delivery goes unanswered the server removes the message from the
consumer's pending set (=NumAckPending= returns to 0) but does not acknowledge
it. The message stays in the stream. That consumer never delivers it again,
even after a rebind. A new consumer reading from =DeliverAll= still receives it.
So the message is left outstanding in the stream, not acked and not parked.
This matches nats-server PR #6575 and its test
=TestJetStreamInterestMaxDeliveryReached=.

* The fleet, with the production numbers

A throwaway stream on the running fleet, =interest= retention, one-day age, and
a push consumer created with the production settings =MaxDeliver= 10 and
=AckWait= 30 s. The stream and consumer names carried a unique suffix, and the
stream was deleted at the end.

#+begin_example
configuration: ack_policy=explicit ack_wait_ns=30000000000 max_deliver=10 max_ack_pending=1000 backoff=None
published at 04:17:59 with no subscriber bound
after 330s with no subscriber (longer than the old 300s bound):
  delivered=0 ack_floor=0 num_ack_pending=0 num_redelivered=0 num_pending=1 push_bound=None
  stream messages=1 retention=interest max_age_ns=86400000000000
bind a core queue subscriber on the delivery subject for 10s:
  delivered=1 ack_floor=0 num_ack_pending=1 num_redelivered=0 num_pending=0 push_bound=True
subscriber received the command: 1 time(s)
#+end_example

The command survived a wait longer than =MaxDeliver x AckWait=, because the wait
was downtime, not a bound and failing consumer. This is the property the
documents now state.

* What is left unsatisfied

The measurements do not cover a full day of downtime; the fleet probe waits
330 s. They do not cover a change of =MaxDeliver= on a live consumer either.
The =ores.nats= suite covers the in-place reconciliation of the stored consumer
with a further test: the creation time and the acknowledged position survive an
update of the delivery bound and the ack wait.
