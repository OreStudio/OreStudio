* Goal

A tenant whose setup left work undone is one click from finishing it: the tenant administrator sees an alert on the tenant dashboard while any setup step stands with warnings, follows it to the setup report, and re-runs the step there or in the shell. A re-run repeats only the work the step did not finish, because every step that may end with warnings is idempotent.

* Acceptance

- Each setup step that is not essential leaves the tenant as it found it when run a second time over finished work, shown by a re-run of every such step on a provisioned tenant.
- The engine re-runs one step of a finished run that ended with warnings, under a new idempotency key, and records the new outcome on that step; an essential step, a step that completed cleanly, or a run that is still going is refused with its reason.
- The setup report, in the web and in the shell (setup-report rerun STEP), offers the re-run beside each step with warnings and shows its progress and outcome.
- While any setup step stands with warnings the tenant dashboard shows an alert that links to the setup report; the alert goes once no step has warnings, and dismissing the notice does not hide it.

* Decisions

- *Ride PR #4790.* At the person's request the story lands on the branch that made the setup
  steps end with warnings, so the warnings and the way out of them ship together.
- *Each step is already written to skip finished work.* Read, not yet run: the curve, forward
  and surface steps skip what a party has published; the blueprint step plans from what a party
  holds and says a rerun is safe; photos skip an account or party that has an image; reports
  skip a definition that holds a scheduler job; trades count what is already present; the
  subscription step skips a subscription or route the party holds. The re-run task proves each.
- *A re-run needs a new idempotency key.* The step handlers answer a step id they hold a
  terminal record of from that record, and retry_instance re-sends under the same id, so it
  would replay the warned answer. It also resumes only a stopped run.
