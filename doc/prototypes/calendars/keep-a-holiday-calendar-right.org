* Summary

This prototype draws the screens of [[id:4C3A8F3E-655F-4DB8-9F94-A25EBA9C4E8A][Keep a holiday calendar right]] as the
[[id:2BF7F03F-6BF4-45FF-8E26-4D3B4E2D943D][Record screen standard]] settled them on 2026-10-25, so the review is about
the calendars topic and not about the language. It is the first prototype of
the [[id:C0D1A833-3E57-489A-A4D3-8C44266D3D6D][screen pipeline]]: the web already draws a calendars screen, and this
page says where that screen moves to meet the standard and the owner's two
decisions on the journey, and defines the one view the standard has no rule
for, the Business days.

It is throwaway. It reads no service: the 61 QuantLib calendars are the Acme
seed as the server returned them on 2026-10-25, and the three user calendars,
their rules, exceptions, events and history are invented. The business days are
computed in the page from the rules, so the view is real for the bespoke
calendar; the QuantLib holidays of the two bases are typed in for 2026.

Open [[file:keep-a-holiday-calendar-right/][the prototype]]. Every state has an address, for example
=?screen=calendar&code=Acme.Desk&tab=days= or
=?screen=calendar&code=TARGET&dialog=derive&who=reader=.

* Detail

** What it asks

Three questions the journey judgement left to the prototype:

1. Where do the two own actions stand? The owner decided that /Rebuild
   business days/ moves from the tab to the header, beside /Derive a calendar/
   (RS-PAGE-03). The prototype draws both there and turns the rebuild into an
   operation dialog, so the header holds buttons and the tab holds the view.
2. How is the Business days view drawn? No rule of the standard draws a
   derived, read-only view. The prototype defines one, below, for the standard
   to take.
3. What does a reader see? A person who may not write sees the mark /Read
   only/ and no action, on the page and in every panel (RS-FEEDBACK-01,
   RS-FEEDBACK-02).

** The screens and the rules each follows

- *The list.* Breadcrumb, plural title and one sentence (RS-LIST-01); Refresh
  with its load time, then Add, at the right (RS-LIST-03, ES-REFRESH-06,
  ES-REFRESH-16); a search box with Clear (RS-LIST-04); sortable columns Code,
  Name, Calendar type, Country, and /Holidays from/, which says in words what
  the calendar is made of; the pager with the count first and 15 rows
  (RS-LIST-17); search, page, size and order in the address (RS-LIST-20); the
  code and the country in monospace with their flags (RS-ICON-03); a click on
  a row opens the page (RS-LIST-10). The nothing-matches state offers /Clear
  the search/ (RS-LIST-21).
- *The record page.* The breadcrumb ends at the code and the title is the name
  with the flag (RS-PAGE-01); the lead reads code and version (RS-PAGE-02);
  the actions stand in this order: Refresh with its load time, the access
  mark, /Derive a calendar/, /Rebuild business days/, /Edit/, /Delete/
  (RS-PAGE-03, RS-FEEDBACK-02). A QuantLib calendar is read only, so it draws
  Derive and nothing else, and its Details end in the notice that says why
  (RS-WRITE-33). The tabs follow the shape: Details, Rules, Exceptions, Diary
  events, Business days, History; a tab that does not apply is not drawn
  (RS-PAGE-09). The details stand in four columns in the field table's order,
  with the fixed reason under the code and /Holidays from/ as a link to the
  base (RS-PAGE-05, RS-PAGE-06, RS-PAGE-08); the last-changed line ends them
  (RS-PAGE-10).
- *The parts.* Rules, exceptions and events are parts panels: the columns the
  kind declares, History, Edit and Delete on each row, Add under the table
  (RS-RELATED-04, RS-RELATED-05); each opens the record dialog with the parent
  given, the history in a wide dialog, or the remove dialog (RS-RELATED-06). A
  rule is shown in words, "Last Monday of May", because a rule has no name
  (the journey's gap 6). The rule dialog's fields follow the kind (RS-WRITE-07).
- *The dialogs.* Edit is titled /Edit/ and the page's title, and says under
  its title when the record was loaded (RS-WRITE-20, RS-WRITE-16); the key is
  fixed with its reason (RS-WRITE-03, ES-PAGE-08); the fields stand in two
  columns in the field table's order (RS-WRITE-11); one line of help stands
  under a control (RS-WRITE-12); Why and Commentary close the form
  (ES-EDIT-02); the footer holds Cancel then Save, each with its icon, and no
  Delete (RS-WRITE-14); a create states its reason in a note and lists what is
  still to fill (RS-WRITE-17, RS-WRITE-13); the ✕ asks /You have unsaved
  changes. Close anyway?/ (RS-WRITE-24). Delete confirms, names the record,
  says what is lost and asks for a reason (RS-WRITE-25, RS-WRITE-30).
- *Derive a calendar.* The record dialog titled /Derive a calendar from
  <name>/, with the base shown as a fixed field and the type and the country
  given from the base; a create, so no reason (RS-WRITE-17). After it the
  screen opens the new calendar (RS-WRITE-19).
- *Rebuild business days.* An operation dialog: one field, /Build up to the
  end of/, the note that a rebuild adds the days not built yet, and the button
  /Rebuild/. The reply is a notice with the count of days written. This is the
  one dialog that is not a record, remove or revert dialog, and the standard's
  RS-WRITE-31 gains the operation dialog as a fourth shared shape, or names
  the exception.
- *History.* The timeline of versions at the left with the current emblem on
  the newest, the compare at the right with the old value on a red line and
  the new on a green line, and Revert on an older version (ES-HIST-11,
  ES-HIST-18, RS-HIST-02).

** The Business days view

The rule the standard gains, in words. A derived view is a tab of the record
page that shows what the record's parts produce. It is read only and has no
Add. It:

- names the period it shows and lets the person step it, here a year with
  Previous and Next, and keeps the period in the address;
- shows one block per natural division, here one per month, in the same grid
  the details use: three across, then two, then one;
- shows each entry with its date, its words and what produced it: a rule, an
  exception, the base, in a faint column at the right, so the person reads
  cause beside effect;
- draws a business day an exception opened in green, because it is the one
  entry that adds a day rather than takes one;
- offers one comparison, /Compare with/, and draws the other calendar's
  holidays in italics with the words /Holiday in <code> only/, because the
  union is the definition a pair settles on;
- says in one line what it does not show: a change to a rule or an exception
  shows once the year is rebuilt;
- has an empty state that names the action: /No business days are built for
  2026. Rebuild the calendar to build them/, with the link for a writer and
  the words for a reader.

** What is fake

The three user calendars and everything under them. The history of
=Acme.Desk=. The QuantLib holidays of the bases, typed in for 2026 only. The
count of days a rebuild writes. The flags, which are the country code in a box.
Nothing is written: Save, Add, Rebuild and Delete show a notice and go back.

** Where the code lives

=index.html= is the shell, =prototype.css= the look from the web app's tokens,
and =prototype.js= the data, the rule engine that turns rules into dates, and
the screens. The query string is the state; every link changes it.

** The verdict

Open. The owner signs it off before the build task starts. The build then
moves the web's calendars screen to this: Rebuild into the header as an
operation dialog, Refresh and the load time on the page, the dialog titles
and load times, the Business days view as drawn here, and the Read only mark.

** What it does not settle

- The rebuild that rewrites a built day, and the QuantLib holidays the server
  does not read: the view draws the intended screen, and the journey's gaps 1
  and 2 carry the server work.
- The ORE export, which has no screen until the server returns the document.
- The role below the tenant administrator; the reader here is any person who
  may not write.
