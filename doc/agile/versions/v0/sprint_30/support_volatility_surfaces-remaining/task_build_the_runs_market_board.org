* Goal

Every synthetic volatility surface is struck against the forward of the run's own latent curves. A surface's quotes are then the pair's surface at the moneyness its own rate quotes imply, a curve tick moves the surface's quotes without a restart, and a party's calibration reads them at the strikes its bootstrap recovers.

The design is [[id:675E89FC-B5AA-4250-8B86-78062F0F22BC][The forward a synthetic surface is struck against]]; the shared market resolution and the per-expiry markets landed under [[id:CFFA13BE-A386-4E72-B06C-60976FA34DF3][Resolve a pair's market once, shared by the calibration and the surface]]. The vendor publishes raw quotes and never a curve.

* Acceptance

- A curve feed publishes its dated discount factors to the run's board on every tick, and a board entry's factor at a pillar date equals that process's own =discount_factor= at the pillar's horizon to =1e-12=.
- A spot feed publishes its level to the board on every tick, and the level read back is the process's own current value.
- A surface feed builds one market per expiry from the board on every tick and re-solves its strikes, so a curve tick reaches the surface's quotes with no restart and no reconfiguration.
- A surface whose pair the run does not carry publishes nothing, and names the member it could not read. It never quotes against a flat forward.
- A pair whose currencies have no configured curve feed is refused when the start verb builds it, with the missing currency named.
- An expiry's value date is the spot date plus its period, computed by =calendar_arithmetic::add_period= on both the feed's side and the calibration's, with no calendar consulted.
- A two-percent discount differential moves a surface's quoted volatilities by the amount the strike solve implies, and the test states that amount rather than a direction.
- With the curve unchanged and the SSVI state unchanged, two consecutive ticks emit identical quotes, so the board adds no drift of its own.
- A party's calibration of the clean quotes lands on the same strikes within a tolerance the test states, and the number is measured once and recorded in Measurements with its command and commit.
- No curve is published as market data, no marketdata operation is added, and no schema changes.
- Each member records when it was last published, so
  [[id:28B21DE8-6352-4121-95CE-0473144061D1][Show the run's market board on the Market tab]] can tell a quiet market from a stopped feed.

* Design

Settled, because the stages depend on the shapes:

- *The board.* =market_board=, in =ores.analytics.quant= beside =latent_market=: one instance per run, owned by the application next to =run_market=, written and read only on the scheduler's loop thread. It holds named members of two shapes -- a currency's dated discount series and a pair's spot -- keyed by a kind and a code, in flat storage that a republish reuses, so a tick allocates nothing.
- *The publishers.* The IR curve feed writes =curve:<CCY>= from its resolved entries' end dates and =discount_factor(ticks_ahead_end)=, with the spot date at one; the FX spot feed writes =spot:<PAIR>= with its current level. Both take an optional board, and a feed built without one publishes nothing, which is what every existing test wants.
- *The read.* The surface feed holds a =const market_board*=, parses its expiry tenors into periods once (the parser and the period addition the calibration already uses), and each tick builds one =fx_market= per expiry through =math::pair_market_at=.
- *The date rule.* Nothing is written: =ores.analytics.quant= already has =calendar_arithmetic::add_period(date, period)=, QuantLib's =Date::advance= semantics with a month-end clamp and no calendar consulted, with its own tests. The calibration's private =add_period= is deleted and calls it, and the feed calls the same function. The value date is the spot date plus the expiry's period -- what the calibration discounts to, not the expiry date -- so the two sides discount to one date by one rule.
- *No business-day adjustment.* The value date is unadjusted and may be a weekend. That is the calibration's present behaviour, and matching it is the point; whether ORE's own expiry should be the yardstick is a separate question, already noted for the IR tenor resolution as a Phase-1 simplification, and it is not in this task.
- *The emitter.* =fx_vol_surface_emitter::step= takes the per-expiry markets; =make= stops taking them. The markets are positional in the emitter's own expiry order and checked by size, so a tick does no string work.
- *Two gates, deliberately different.* A configured pair whose currencies have no configured curve feed is refused when the start verb builds the feed: that is a configuration error, static, and it names the currency. A board member missing at tick time publishes nothing and says which: that is not an error but an ordering, because a surface may tick before any curve has, and a curve may be stopped.

* Architecture review

Taken when the board was wired into the scheduler, because that placement was a
change to the shape of the run and not a detail of the board.

** What each class is responsible for today

| Class | Says it is | Actually owns | Depends on |
|-------+------------+---------------+------------|
| =tick_scheduler= | "the one loop of a run: it owns the market and decides which series ticks next" | the clock, the heap and arrival laws, the pace and the heat and the regime, the history and the trace, the series groups and names, a driver hook, a published hook and a failure hook | =i_run_clock=, =market_config= |
| =latent_market= | the run's shared noise | factors, keyed walks, per-series loadings, the driver values a series reads | nothing |
| =run_market= (service) | "the one latent market a run owns, and where each feed's drivers are" | the latent market, its blocks, the series-to-drivers map, and it installs the driver hook on a scheduler it is handed | =latent_market=, =tick_scheduler= |
| =market_board= | the values the run's feeds share | a curve per currency and a spot per pair, each with the time it was last published | nothing |

** Three smells, one of them this task's

1. *Cohesion.* The scheduler already owns more than timing: the market's pace, its
   heat, its regime, and the run's whole observability. Its own doc calls it "the one
   loop of a run: it owns the market". So the name is the first problem, not the
   board. Putting the board there adds a second axis -- values published by feeds --
   to a class that already mixes time, market state and observability. That is how a
   god object begins.
2. *Visibility, and this is the load-bearing one.* A feed that reads market values
   through the scheduler has an invisible dependency: at the call site
   =scheduler_.board()= nothing says "this feed consumes another feed's output". The
   dependencies between feeds are exactly what the architecture exists to make
   explicit -- a surface reads two curves and a spot, an equity option will read an
   equity spot, the capfloor shift reads an IR curve -- and a dependency reached
   through an unrelated object hides the graph.
3. *Lifetime, which is older than this task.* The driver hook is a lambda over
   =run_market=, so the application must declare =run_market= before the scheduler and
   rely on a comment saying the scheduler's hook points at it. Ownership that exists to
   satisfy a callback's capture is a footgun, and it is invisible at the type level.

** The direction that is right, and the one that is not

"A market board owning a scheduler", taken literally, is wrong: the board is a store of
values, and giving it a loop makes data own time and inverts the dependency. What the
instinct is right about is that the object owning both should be a *market*, not the
loop: the loop is one part of a run's market, not its container.

** Target

#+begin_example
quant::service::run_market          one run's market: composition, no policy of its own
  tick_scheduler  loop              when: the clock, the heap, the laws, pace, heat, regime, trace
  latent_market   noise             why prices co-move: factors, loadings, drivers
  market_board    values            what the feeds share: curves, spots, when each was published
#+end_example

The rules the shape is worth having for:

- *The loop knows nothing of noise or values.* It calls one driver hook and nothing
  else. A scheduler that can hand out market data is a scheduler that will.
- *Noise knows nothing of the loop or of values*, as today.
- *Values know nothing of either*: the board is a table with ids, not a service.
- *=run_market= wires them.* It builds its own loop, so it installs the driver hook on
  the object it owns and no declaration-order rule survives. It also keeps the
  block-to-series map, which is noise wiring, not service policy.
- *A feed depends on =run_market= and reaches =loop()= and =values()=.* A feed's
  dependency on another asset class is then a line in its constructor, and no feed
  reaches values through the loop.

It belongs in =ores.analytics.quant=, not in the service: the feeds live in
=ores.synthetic.api=, which must not depend on =ores.synthetic.service=. The aggregate
has to sit below both, beside the three things it wires. That also retires the
service's own =run_market=, which duplicates what the aggregate should be.

** How a new asset class joins, which is the test of the shape

- A new feed kind registers in the factory and the registry, as today.
- A feed that publishes a value another feed reads defines a member kind and a code and
  writes it to =values()=: no change to the loop, no change to the other feeds.
- A feed that is a block -- a volatility surface today, an equity or commodity surface
  later -- calls =add_block(anchor_series, name, ...)=, which is already generic and
  only named for FX at its call site.
- A feed that needs an input holds the aggregate and reads =values()= in its
  constructor, so the dependency is visible where it is created.
- Nothing in the aggregate is FX-specific: no =fx_vol_surface_*= type appears in it, and
  a new asset class adds no field to it.

** Performance and data orientation

- *No interface is introduced.* The aggregate is concrete and a feed holds a reference
  to it, so the tick path gains no indirection and no type erasure. The driver hook
  stays a =std::function= whose capture is one pointer, set once, which fits its small
  buffer and allocates nothing.
- *The board is already shaped for the many case*: capacity fixed when the run is built,
  a member id resolved once and used by index afterwards, a member's points in its own
  reused buffer, no lock on the tick path, and the one lock only when a member is added
  off the loop. A tick allocates nothing for a curve whose size has not changed, which
  its tests pin by checking the buffer's address across publishes.
- *A budget, so nobody optimises the wrong thing*: per tick the loop costs one
  =std::function= call and one =std::string= from the body, against a wire encode and a
  publish which are orders of magnitude larger. At ten thousand ticks a second the
  dispatch is well under a millisecond a second. The rule this review proposes is the
  one that matters: *nothing on the tick path may allocate per lookup*, which is why the
  board deals in ids.

** Migration, each step green

1. =quant::service::run_market=: move the service's aggregate down into quant and give
   it the loop and the board. It installs its own driver hook in =build()=. Delete the
   service's copy and move its tests.
2. Take the board out of =tick_scheduler=, and the local variable that feed ordering
   leaned on.
3. The feeds take =run_market=& instead of =tick_scheduler=&, and the factory's build
   context carries the aggregate.
4. The application constructs one aggregate; the declaration-order comment goes.
5. Stage 4 then reads the values through =market.values()=.

What this review does not change: the loop's =std::string=-returning body and its
=std::function= driver hook stay, having been measured as noise against a publish. If a
run ever costs more in dispatch than in work, that is the next thing to look at, and it
is a change inside the loop, not to this shape.

* Notes

Why the date rule is a deletion and not a port: the calendar machinery already exists in quant -- =business_day_calendar_set= holds materialised holidays as one flat sorted array built from refdata rows, =business_day_convention= is QuantLib's set of conventions, and =calendar_arithmetic= offers =add_period=, =adjust= and =advance= over them. The surface work needs none of it: an FX surface's expiry is a tenor added to a date, and the calibration has always done that without a calendar. Deleting the duplicate keeps that true while making both sides share one function. If a later stage decides the value date should be a business day, it is one call to =calendar_arithmetic::advance= with the pair's convention and calendar, in that one function's caller, and no new date code.

Why the spot is a member at all, when the quotes provably do not need it: the strike the emitter solves is scaled by the spot, so a published strike or a delta hedge would need the real one, and the spot's covered-interest-parity drift -- later work -- needs it too. Leaving it at one would be a second deliberate lie in a task whose subject is removing the first.

Why the markets move from =make= to =step=: with a board the market stops being a constant of the feed. Keeping =make='s markets would mean a surface struck once against whatever the board held at start, which is the defect at lower frequency.

The ordering risk is real and is the point of the tick-time gate: the scheduler's own ordering means a surface may tick before any curve has. That tick publishes nothing and says "no market: curve:EUR" rather than stepping an empty market, which is why the gate is on the tick and not only on the start verb.

* Result

The run's market is one aggregate in =ores.analytics.quant= that owns its loop, its noise
and its values; the loop is handed a driver hook and knows nothing of the other two. The
board holds a curve per currency and a spot per pair, id-indexed, sized once, with no lock on
the tick path and no allocation after a curve's first publish. Curves and spots publish to it,
the surface reads its pair's three values every tick and strikes each expiry against the
forward they imply, and the emitter takes those markets as an argument of its step.

Two gates, deliberately different: the start verb refuses a pair the run carries no curve feed
for, naming the currency, and a tick whose member is not published yet publishes nothing and
names it.

The round trip is measured rather than asserted: a calibration of the run's own quotes,
against the same market, recovers them to 1.25e-09 at ATM and 3.81e-09 at the wings, and the
ATM strike it solves matches to 5.3e-12 relative. The test asserts 1e-7 and 1e-8.

Architecture: the board was put in the scheduler first and taken out again. The review on this
page records why -- cohesion, the visibility of one generator's dependency on another, and a
lifetime footgun -- and the aggregate is the answer it recommended. The publishable statement
is [[id:1F404EE8-215D-4E62-AB09-B2D34BE9C12F][The components of the market data generator]], which now describes the code as it is.

What is not here: a party's bootstrap tolerance. Its margin is the bootstrap's and moves with
it, so it belongs beside the bootstrap rather than in this arc.
