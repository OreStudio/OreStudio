This page documents a [[id:F44766FA-1E9C-416F-849A-E131101B2ECA][user journey]]: A trader reads a published volatility surface - its axes in draw order, the grid of vols, and one expiry as a smile, with the model, the strike convention and the delta type that make the numbers mean something.

A user journey describes what one person is trying to do, before anything is
built. It is a design document that outlives any sprint, so it names the
screens, the entity fields each screen shows, and the operations and messages
each step needs.

* Goal

"I want the vol surface my party published for this pair: the picture, the grid
of numbers behind it, and one expiry's smile, knowing what a strike column means
and what it was calibrated with." The journey is done when the person can read
the vol at an expiry and a strike, and say what those two words mean.

* Actor and trigger

The actor is the trader, the FX option trader or the quant. The trigger is an
option to price, a smile that moved, a delta quote to check, or a surface a
colleague published. The journey starts at *Market data / Vol surface*, reached
from the instrument search, and ends when the person leaves the page.

The journey reads and changes nothing. It reads a published surface. It is not
the calibration (that writes a candidate) and not the candidate's inspection
(that is *Inspect a surface's slices*, which reads the smiles, the mesh and the
arbitrage of a candidate).

* Steps

1. *Open the surface.* The person opens *Market data / Vol surface* on
   =EURUSD_SSVI*. The system states the party, the pair, the model, the strike
   convention, the delta type, the grid size, the range and when it was
   published.
2. *Read the axes.* The system states the two axes and their values in draw
   order: expiries and strikes, with the strike codes the grid is indexed by
   (=DEL/Spot/Put/25=) and the display labels a person reads.
3. *Read the surface.* The person reads the surface as a picture. The picture is
   the grid and cannot disagree with it, because both are drawn from the same
   response.
4. *Read the grid.* The person reads the numbers: one row per expiry, one column
   per strike, with the ATM mid stated. The column headers are the axis values,
   and the code behind each header is available rather than guessed.
5. *Read one smile.* The person picks an expiry and reads the smile across the
   strikes: the put wing, the ATM point and the call wing. A second operation is
   not needed; the smile is the grid's row.
6. *Read what a strike is.* The person reads the strike convention and the delta
   type. A column called =25D put= is a delta, quoted spot, premium-adjusted or
   not; without those three facts the number is not a vol.
7. *Meet a stale verdict.* The system marks the surface stale against the
   server's threshold for its kind, and says when it was published.
8. *Meet nothing published.* The party holds the blueprint but has published no
   surface. The system says so and draws no grid: an unpublished surface has no
   numbers a person may price from.
9. *Leave.* The address holds the blueprint, the as-of, the expiry and the view.

* Screens and wireframes

One read-only page: the header facts, the surface, the grid and the smile, in
that order, because a person reads the picture, then the numbers, then one slice.

* Operations and messages

#+ATTR_HTML: :class hug-leading
| Step | Operation | Subject | Status |
|------+-----------+---------+--------|
| Open the surface | the published surface of a blueprint at an as-of | =marketdata.v1.ops.get_published_fx_vol_surface= | partial |
| Read the axes | the axes in draw order, with their values | =marketdata.v1.ops.get_published_fx_vol_surface= | partial |
| Read the grid | expiries, strikes and the row-major grid | =marketdata.v1.ops.get_published_fx_vol_surface= | exists |
| Read one smile | one row of the grid, or a slice by expiry | =marketdata.v1.ops.get_series_slice= | exists, unreachable |
| Read what a strike is | model, strike convention, delta type, conventions | =marketdata.v1.ops.get_published_fx_vol_surface= | missing |

=marketdata.v1.ops.get_published_fx_vol_surface= already returns the expiries,
the strikes and a row-major grid. It does not return the model, the strike
convention, the delta type, the conventions, the axes in a stated order, the
publish instant or the staleness verdict.

* What is missing

- The surface read must state =model=, =strike_convention=, =delta_type=,
  =conventions=, =axes= (in draw order), =published_at= and =stale=. Without the
  first three, the screen shows numbers whose meaning it cannot state.
- The surface as an instrument. The smile needs the surface's =instrument_id= and
  its axis field names, which =list_instruments= must expose; today a client has
  no identity to slice by.
- The staleness verdict, so the screen does not compute its own threshold.
