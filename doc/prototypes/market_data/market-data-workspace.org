* Summary

The prototype answers one question: what is the market data screen set now
that a feed is never started or stopped? The answer keeps the tree and the
feed's full property panel, because that is where a person manages what a feed
is. It separates the operator's sources board, the party's subscriptions and
the series history into their own screens, reached from the same tree.

The tree is the navigation. A feed is still a record with its stochastic
process and parameters, its arrival process, its tick clock, its vintage, its
GMM components and its collections. What is gone is the run control. Demand is
computed from the subscriptions and the routes, and the demand is what starts
the generator.

* Detail

** The question

The old screen was a synthetic tree with a feed roster and a start and a stop.
It showed every feed's properties, including the process parameters, because a
person tuned them there. The first attempt at the new world replaced it with a
sources coverage board. That board answers the operator's question, not the
feed author's, and on its own it loses most of what a person could manage.

** The screens

Reached from one navigation, =?screen=.

- *Workspace.* The default. The synthetic tree on the left: provider, then
  asset class, then feed. A feed's panel on the right holds everything the old
  screen held: name, provider, kind, series URI, the process type and its
  parameters, the arrival process and its three fields, the tick clock, the
  vintage source and date, the GMM components, the collections, the parties in
  demand, and the last tick. The Edit button opens the editor.
- *Feed editor.* The process type, its parameter rows, the arrival fields and a
  sample-path preview. This is the same authoring the old FX and IR screens
  did; only the frame is new.
- *Sources.* The operator's board, kept short because it has its own prototype
  under Operations. Adapter state, routes, demand, accepted and rejected ids,
  ticks and the last tick.
- *Subscriptions.* The party's rights by provider, and the sources it routes to.
  This is where the party administrator works, not the operator.
- *History.* One series over a span, with the party and the coordinates. The
  read is refused without display or non-display.

** What is kept

Every property the old feed screens showed. A feed that offered a stochastic
process is still edited as a process with parameters. The GMM components and
the collections are still rows on the feed. The tree still groups the feeds by
provider and asset class, so a person who knew where a feed lived finds it
where it was.

** What changed

- The run control is gone. A feed's state is =serving=, =ready= or =stale=, and
  =no demand= is the state that matters: offered, routed, and demanded nothing.
- The parties' demand is on the feed, read-only. It is a consequence of the
  subscriptions and the routes, not a switch.
- The operator's board is a separate screen. A quiet source is a demand or
  mapping question there, not a start button here.

** States

=screen= picks the screen. =feed= picks the tree leaf shown in the workspace,
the editor and the history. =party= picks the party on the subscriptions screen.
The mock data covers serving, ready, no demand and stale, so every screen can
be read against the failure it exists for.

** What is fake

All data is mock. There is no service, no session and no build step. The
numbers follow the live shape measured on the provisioned tenant.

** Where the code lives

[[file:market-data-workspace/index.html][The prototype]] is one plain HTML file with its CSS and JavaScript embedded.
The site publishes it as it stands.

** Verdict

Awaiting sign-off. My recommendation is this set: the workspace with the tree
and the full feed panel is the home, the editor is the same panel in edit mode,
and sources, subscriptions and history are separate screens reached from the
tree. The old [[file:browse-the-feed-tree.org][Browse the feed tree]] prototype
stays as the record of the screen this replaces.
