#+title: The cron expression
#+description: When a job runs: the five fields of a cron expression, the value forms, the validation at the boundary, and the condition that makes a job due.
#+type: knowledge
#+level: cross
#+filetags: :knowledge:architecture:scheduling:
#+created: 2026-09-28
#+updated: 2026-09-28

A cron expression defines the instants at which a job runs. It has five
fields, read left to right: minute, hour, day of month, month, and day of
week. The scheduler validates an expression at the API boundary, so it
never stores a job whose expression it cannot evaluate. Every expression
resolves to a whole minute, so the minimum interval between two firings
is one minute. This page defines the fields, the value forms, the
validation rule, and the condition under which a job is due.

* Detail

** The five fields

An expression has five fields, in this order.

| position | field        | values                          |
|----------+--------------+---------------------------------|
| first    | minute       | the minute of the hour, 0 to 59 |
| second   | hour         | the hour of the day, 0 to 23    |
| third    | day of month | the day of the month, 1 to 31   |
| fourth   | month        | the month of the year, 1 to 12  |
| fifth    | day of week  | the day of the week             |

** The value forms

A field takes one of five forms.

- =*= — any value.
- a single value, such as =6=.
- a range, such as =1-5=.
- a list, such as =1,15=.
- a step over a range, such as =*/15=.

The forms compose within a field, so one expression can select the first
minute of the first working day of a month.

| expression    | it defines                              |
|---------------+-----------------------------------------|
| =* * * * *=   | every minute                            |
| =0 * * * *=   | the first minute of every hour          |
| =*/15 * * * *=| every fifteen minutes                   |
| =0 6 * * 1-5= | six in the morning, on a working day    |
| =0 0 1 * *=   | midnight on the first day of each month |

There is no seconds field: an expression resolves to whole minutes, so the
shortest interval between two firings is one minute and a firing is never due
at a stated second. A recurrence more frequent than once a minute cannot be
expressed.

** Validation at the API boundary

The boundary that accepts a job validates its expression, and a malformed
expression is rejected there, before it reaches a row. Validation happens
once, on the way in: the scheduler stores only expressions it can
evaluate, and the stored text is the text that passed validation. A
rejected expression is returned to the caller as an error, because the
caller asked for a schedule and got none.

The scheduler never parses an expression into fields of its own. It asks
the expression for the next occurrence after a given instant. Adding a
value form to the notation therefore adds it to every stored expression.

** When a job is due

A job is due when both of these hold.

1. The job's active flag is true.
2. The next occurrence after the job's last recorded firing has already
   passed.

Condition two fixes the last firing as the reference point, and three
properties follow from it.

- /A new job runs at its next occurrence./ A job with no recorded firing
  is evaluated from the current minute, so its first execution is the
  next occurrence its expression defines, not the moment of registration.
- /Missed occurrences are not replayed./ A job whose occurrence passed
  while the scheduler was down runs once when the scheduler restarts.
  The reference is the last recorded firing, so the scheduler builds no
  backlog and performs no work for the occurrences it missed.
- /An inactive job is never due./ The active flag is condition one, so an
  inactive job does not fire. It keeps its row and its execution history,
  and activating it applies the same rule as before.

The loop evaluates this condition for every job it holds at each minute.
[[id:172812F0-17F0-47BC-9933-7A70848105BC][The scheduler loop]]
describes the evaluation and what follows from it.

** The expression defines when, and nothing else

An expression defines no property of the action: not the action type, not
the payload, not the tenant or party the job runs for. Those are separate
fields of the job definition, read at the firing. The separation is why an
expression can be changed without changing what a job does, and why the
action can be replaced without moving the schedule.
