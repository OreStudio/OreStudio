* Result

Drew the four-eyes controls in the counterparty, party, convention and tenor
prototypes, on the pattern the signed-off book prototype set.

*How.* Each prototype carries a copy of =four-eyes.js= beside its own script,
because a prototype is self-contained. The file draws the three screens every
gated write needs: =waiting=, =decide= and =declined=. A prototype supplies its
changes and who decides each, and a check. The book prototype keeps its own inline
copy of the same logic, which was signed off before the shared file existed.

*The rule per journey* is in the controls note, under "The controls on the other
reference-data journeys", and is the working rule for the product owner to confirm:
Operations decides a counterparty and Market Risk its agreements and collateral;
Finance decides a party's identity and Operations its memberships and links; Market
Risk decides a convention and a tenor. A contact is not gated.

*What each shows.* The review marks every change as =needs <function>= or
=no approval=, and the confirm becomes =Raise request= when any is gated. The decide
screen names who is acting, shows the delta and a check, refuses the person who
asked, needs a comment, and applies the request only when every named function has
approved. A decline leaves the record alone. Each takes =?actor=, =?maker= and
=?check=.

*Verification.* For each prototype I rendered the review and the new states in
headless Chrome and read them, and ran a scripted flow in headless Chrome: raise,
open as the checker, approve as each named function in turn, and read the applied
outcome. All four applied only after the last approval. =node= parses each script.
No automated test covers a prototype.

*Not done.* Nothing holds or applies a pending change, and Acme has no Finance
account. Both gaps are stated in each journey document.
