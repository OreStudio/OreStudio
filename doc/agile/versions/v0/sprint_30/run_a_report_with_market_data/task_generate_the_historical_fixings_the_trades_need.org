* Acceptance

- ores.synthetic generates the daily fixings of an index back from the as-of, derived from the curve of that index and tenor, and the same seed gives the same fixings.
- The fixings are written in the series the convention's oresmd URI names, under the party that runs the report.
- After provisioning, the count of indices the generated trades use with no fixing series holding data is zero.
- Where a public fixing exists for a date, the public value is used and the synthetic one is not written.

* Plan

(Implementation strategy. Written when work starts; key decisions
are distilled into the parent story's =* Decisions= at close, but the
plan itself stays — it is the historical record of what we did.)

1. *Measure the gap.* Recreate the database, provision the =acme_demo=
   profile, and read the fixing addresses the population names with
   =ores_trading_market_references_fn=, joined to the fixing series and
   their rows. Record the count of addresses with no series holding a
   point at or before the run's as-of, by asset class.
2. *Carry a fixing on the direct tick path.* A synthetic feed publishes
   ticks that name their oresmd address and carry the source. The
   ingest reads such an address as a datum and drops an address that is
   no datum. Teach it to read that address as an index instead, and to
   send it to the fixing path with the date of the tick. The fixing then
   lands in the series of each consumer of the source, under that
   consumer's party, exactly as an adapter's fixing does. No mapping row
   and no vendor id are needed, because the tick names the item itself.
3. *A simulated source fills a gap and never overwrites.* A fixing from
   a source on the =synthetic-local= session is written only on a date
   the series does not hold, so a run republished after a restart adds
   nothing and a public fixing is never replaced. A fixing from any
   other source keeps the rule it has: a correction replaces the value
   of its own date.
4. *Derive the history from the curve.* A curve's index rate is the
   price of the deposit entry at the index's own tenor, which the feed
   already computes for its first pillar. The feed states the fixing of
   the last $n$ business days from that rate: a mean-reverting walk
   drawn from the run's seed and the index's address, shifted so the
   newest fixing equals the curve's rate on the as-of. Two runs with one
   seed state the same fixings, and the history and the curve agree on
   the day the run starts.
5. *Publish it once per start.* The feed publishes the history on its
   producer subject before it ticks. The walk is a function of the seed
   and the address, so a restart republishes the same values, which the
   store drops as already held.
6. *Prove it.* Re-provision, and show the fixing addresses of the
   interest rate legs the population books now answer with a point at or
   before the as-of. Record what is still empty, and which story owns it.

Scope. This task states the fixings of the interest rate indices, which
is what a generated floating leg resets on. The FX fixing addresses a
generated trade names are a second kind: they are barrier and averaging
observations, and the FX asset class is proved in the same sign-off, so
they are measured here and taken next.

* Result

A curve states the fixings of its own index, and a fresh provision holds them.
The acme_demo provision of 2026-11-19 wrote 41 interest rate fixing series and
21,320 fixings, all of them in the system tenant beside whatever a publisher
fixes for the same index, and 6 of the 21 interest rate fixing addresses the
808 trades name answer where none did before.

What landed:

- =ores.synthetic.api/feeds/index_fixings=: the address of an index's fixings,
  written by the same codec the market reads, and the 520 business days of its
  history at a level the curve states, drawn from the run's seed and shifted so
  the newest day is the curve's own rate. Eleven cases hold it: the address of a
  term and of an overnight index, the newest day at the level, the same seed
  giving the same days, one index stating its own days, a held day left out
  without moving the rest, a longer window keeping the days it shares with a
  shorter one, and a level that is no rate.
- =ir_curve_feed= states its index's rate: the deposit to the index's tenor, the
  agreement that ends on it when the tenor is past the last deposit, and the
  short rate the process carries when the curve starts past the overnight tenor.
  Four cases hold the choice of entry.
- =fixing_history_writer=, in the synthetic service, writes a plan's days into
  the series the index already has, skipping the days that series holds, and
  =generator_runner= tells it every generator a run starts.
- =market_data_client::put_fixings= writes fixings through
  =marketdata.v1.market_fixings.put_many=, and the SyntheticService role is
  granted =marketdata::market_fixings:write=.

What is not met, and why:

- The third acceptance is not reached: 15 interest rate fixing addresses still
  answer with nothing, because no generated curve states their index at their
  tenor. A fixing is derived from the curve of its index and tenor, so the curve
  is the gap. The addresses are listed in =evidence/fixing_coverage.txt=, and
  =product_backlog/inbox/the_generated_population_floats_on_indices_no_gene.org=
  records them with the two refused rate configs of the same provision.
- The second acceptance is met in effect and not in the letter: the fixings are
  kept under the system party, which is what a party's export falls back to for
  an index it does not hold, so every party reads them; they are not copied into
  each party's own series. A party that holds its own series of one of these
  indices shadows the run's history for that index, which is how a published
  fixing wins.
- The fourth acceptance is met by construction and not yet observed: the writer
  reads the days the series holds and leaves them, so a published fixing is
  never replaced by a run. The public sources adapter was not running for this
  provision, so the path is untested live.
- No unit test covers =fixing_history_writer=: it needs a database and a live
  market data service, and the proof it has is the provision above.
