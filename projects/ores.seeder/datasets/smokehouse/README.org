#+title: Smokehouse dataset generator
#+description: Why a third dataset set exists, what it carries, and how its populate SQL is generated.

Smokehouse is the smallest tenant that exercises the publishing lifecycle. It
exists so a gate check can watch a curve, a forward set and a volatility surface
publish, be superseded and announce, and then watch three trades price against
them, without paying for a demonstration tenant. ACME Corporation does all of
that, and takes a quarter of an hour to do it because it also loads four legal
entities, forty-five staff, eleven hundred trades and a photograph per person.

* What the tenant holds

| Row | Count | Why it is there |
|-----+-------+-----------------|
| Legal entities | 1 | One party, so the books and their approval matrix hang from a company and not from the tenant's administrative System Party |
| Business units | 1 | A book's approval inherits through its desk, so the chain from the party down has every link |
| Portfolios | 1 | Books hang from a portfolio |
| Books | 3 | One per product: a forward, an option and a swap |
| Book product types | 3 | The approval matrix, stated on the book that permits it |
| Counterparties | 4 | A trade picks one, and they are the GLEIF LEIs every deployment publishes |

The books are =GBP FX Book=, =GBP FX Options Book= and =GBP Rates Book=. A book
permits one product type and refuses the others, so one book cannot carry both
the forward and the option: the approval is the book's, and the planner refuses
a product the book's matrix does not permit. That is why the count is three and
not one.

* The legal entity

The company is fictional, so its LEI is synthetic: the =9695= test prefix ACME
uses, with ISO 17442 mod-97 check digits computed by the generator. A fictional
company has no entry in the public register, and the party has to come from
somewhere. The party is created by publishing the company's LEI entities, which
is the same road every tenant's legal entities travel.

The counterparties are the other way round. They are real GLEIF records, read
from the Acme dataset rather than restated here, so the two sets cannot drift
apart. Only four are linked: one trade per book draws from them, and a longer
list would make the dataset harder to read without making the check stronger.

* What the profile adds, and what this directory does not state

This directory states the reference data. It states no market data, no trades
and no reports: those are the working of a running system, and the seed profile
that provisions the tenant orders the steps that make them. The profile is
hand-written, in =projects/ores.sql/populate/iam/iam_seed_profiles_populate.sql=,
and it is the place to look for what the tenant does rather than what it holds.

In short: the profile imports the legal entity, gives the party its conventions
and this directory's datasets, subscribes it to one vendor for the rates ladders
and the G4 spots and to the derived source for the volatility quotes, copies the
system's curve and surface blueprints into the party, and then publishes the
curves, the forward sets and the surfaces. Last, it books one trade per book,
each stating the one product its book permits.

* Regenerating

#+begin_src sh
cd projects/ores.seeder/datasets/smokehouse
python3 generate_sql.py
#+end_src

The generator rewrites every file in =projects/ores.sql/populate/smokehouse/=
and nothing else. Do not edit that directory by hand.
