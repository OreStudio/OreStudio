#+title: Calibrating a synthetic feed: from fixings to a reviewed run
#+description: How the class calibration run sets a synthetic FX feed's level, spread and GJR-GARCH variance from public fixings, how a person reviews the variance, and how a feed runs only the approved calibration, with every step stored and traceable.
#+type: knowledge
#+level: cross
#+filetags: :synthetic:calibration:garch:marketdata:knowledge:
#+created: 2026-11-03
#+updated: 2026-11-03

* Summary

A synthetic FX feed takes its starting level, the spread of its mixture and its
GJR-GARCH daily variance from public fixings, through one calibration run of its
asset class. The run resolves each feed's fixing item under the class policy,
measures the level and the spread, and, for a GJR-GARCH feed, captures the
variance window of the same item and fits it. The variance is never used as
fitted: the fit is stored as a proposed calibration, a person approves or rejects
it with a reason, and a feed runs only the approved calibration of its pair, which
its configuration names. Every step is a stored, versioned record, so a run can be
traced back through its calibration and the input it was fitted from to the
fixings that made it.

* Detail

** One run calibrates the class

The calibration run, =synthetic.v1.ops.calibrate_feeds=, is the one action that
calibrates an asset class, from the Calibration screen or the API. It reads the
class's calibration policy, then walks every feed of the class, or the one feed
it is asked for. A preview reports what it finds and stores nothing; an applied
run, which only the system tenant may make, writes what it finds.

#+caption: The calibration run of an asset class, for one feed and for its daily variance.
#+attr_html: :width 100%
#+attr_latex: :width \textwidth
[[proj:doc/knowledge/domain/variance_calibration_run.png]]

For each feed the run resolves the fixing item the feed starts from, ordered by the
policy's provider order and allowed to be read inverted when the policy says so;
see [[id:3F9D6EAE-9FB8-4A0D-9BAF-1EA3EA93AB4F][Synthetic prices are calibrated from free public sources]].
It measures the level and the spread of the mixture over the policy's window, and
an applied run writes them as a new version of the feed.

A feed whose variance follows a GJR-GARCH is calibrated a second time from the same
item. The run reads the policy's variance window, five years by default, and
captures it as an /input/: every fixing date of the window is a /point/, holding
the fixing as stored, its daily log return in the pair's market convention, and,
when the point is left out, why. The fit reads only those points, by variance
targeting and Gaussian quasi maximum likelihood, as
[[id:351CB816-28F5-428A-A289-414B6F4B9F36][Volatility clustering and GARCH models]]
describes. An input with fewer than 250 returns, or one no admissible shape fits,
gives a /rejected/ calibration with the reason; any other fit gives a /proposed/
one, with the evidence a reviewer judges it by: the shape, its persistence under
the feed's mixture, the long-run and starting variance, the likelihood, and
whether every start of the simplex reached the same optimum.

An applied run stores the input, its points and the calibration once each, under
an id named by what identifies it: the input by its party, item and window, the
calibration by its input, the method's version and the feed's mixture. A run made
again finds the records it made and writes nothing, and it never reopens a
calibration a person has decided. A preview reports a stored calibration as
stored, with its id, so the screen reviews the record an earlier run kept.

** A person decides the variance

#+caption: The states of a variance calibration.
[[proj:doc/knowledge/domain/variance_calibration_states.png]]

A proposed calibration waits for a person with the review permission, which the
Trading role holds. Approving or rejecting it, with a reason, is
=synthetic.v1.ops.review_variance_calibration=; each decision is a new version of
the calibration under the change reason =system.review=, so its history shows who
decided what, when and why.

#+caption: What an approval does, and what a feed reads when it starts.
#+attr_html: :width 100%
#+attr_latex: :width \textwidth
[[proj:doc/knowledge/domain/variance_calibration_approval.png]]

A pair has at most one approved calibration at a time. Approving one supersedes the
pair's earlier approval, approves the new one, and names it on every GJR-GARCH feed
of the pair, each as a new version of the feed. Each step reads what is stored
before it writes, so an approval that stopped part way is completed by sending it
again. Rejecting moves no feed.

** A feed runs only what was approved

A GJR-GARCH feed's configuration names the calibration it runs. When it starts, it
reads that calibration and fits nothing. A calibration that is missing, of another
pair, not approved or without a shape refuses the start, and the person who
started the feed reads why. A run's start record names the calibration and the
version it read, and states the persistence under the feed's own mixture.

A fresh installation runs reviewed shapes from its first start: publishing the
system feeds installs, for each pair that has none, the authored shape of the feed
data as an approved, authored calibration, and names it on the pair's feeds. A
later approval replaces it, and publishing again keeps the later one.

** Every number traces to its fixings

#+caption: The audit chain of a feed's daily variance.
[[proj:doc/knowledge/domain/variance_calibration_chain.png]]

The chain reads from a run to the calibration it names, to the input that
calibration was fitted from, to the points of that input, to the fixings they
were read from, and to the policy and its version they were read under; and from a
calibration back to the feeds that name it. On screen, a feed's /Calibration/ tab holds
the run in four cards: the start level in the pair's market convention, with the
fixing, its provider and its orientation; what the fixing window measured; the
daily variance, as a table of the GJR-GARCH parameters; and the calibration the run
used, whose id links to the feed's calibration page. When the config names a newer
calibration than the run used, the card says so and a restart runs it. A run that
took no fixing records the level it kept, so the card states the level the run began
at even after a later calibration moves the config's level.

The history reads the whole process before and after approval. A feed's /History/
tab holds the feed's versions and its mixture's, and also every variance input and
variance calibration of its pair: the input a run captured, the fit it proposed,
the reviewer's decision, and the calibration an approval superseded. Each entry
names who made it, when, under which change reason, and its commentary. The
commentary states the outcome: the input names the fixings it captured and how
many are usable as returns; the calibration names its shape and says that it waits
for review, or why the fit was rejected; and the feed's own calibration entry
names the GJR-GARCH outcome beside the level it moved.

Approving a fit also closes the pair's proposals that are older than it: they are
superseded with a reason that names the approved fit, because approving one of them
later would replace a fit of later fixings. A proposal made after the approved fit
stays open.

A proposed calibration changes no feed until a person approves it, so every screen
that shows the pair says when a fit waits for review: the class list counts the
fits that wait, and the feed's /Calibration/ tab and its calibration page state the
calibration that waits and lead to its review. The review is attached to the
stored calibration, not to a preview, so a newer fixing that moves the preview
leaves the stored fit reviewable.

The calibration page draws two charts. The first is the daily volatility a fit
expected over its input, beside the size of each day's move and the long-run
level. It is computed in the browser from the stored points and the stored shape
with the fit's own recursion, and the variance after the last return over the
long-run level must equal the stored first-day ratio; a series that does not is
reported, not drawn as if it matched. The second is the pair's calibrations over
time, one small chart for each of alpha (α), gamma (γ), beta (β) and persistence,
beside a table of every calibration with its state, method and reason. On the
Calibration screen the class list shows each feed's variance calibration and its
state, and a feed's /Variance/ tab shows the whole calibration, its input and the
review.

The script =projects/ores.sql/utility/audit_variance_calibrations.py= checks the
stored chain against what it should hold: every input's points and counts, every
return against its fixing, every calibration's evidence against its method and
state, one approval per pair, and every GJR-GARCH feed on the approved calibration
of its own pair.
