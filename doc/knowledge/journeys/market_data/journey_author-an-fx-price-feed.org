This page documents a [[id:F44766FA-1E9C-416F-849A-E131101B2ECA][user journey]]: A quant composes one FX feed: the pair it quotes, the process that drives its ticks, the mixture of Gaussians it draws from, and the sample paths that say whether the choice is a price feed at all.

A user journey describes what one person is trying to do, before anything is
built. It is a design document that outlives any sprint, so it names the
screens, the entity fields each screen shows, and the operations and messages
each step needs.

* Goal

"I want this feed to produce the pair I mean, at a believable price, so the
desk can test against something that behaves like the market." The journey is
done when the config and its mixture are saved and read back with the pair, the
derived ORE key, the tick rate and the process the person chose, and when the
sample paths the screen drew look like a price rather than a number.

* Actor and trigger

The actor is a quant or a trader who holds the synthetic configuration
permission. The quant is a member acting on their own party's records: the
config carries =party_id=, and another party's feeds stay out of reach. The
trigger is a pair the desk must generate and has no feed for, a price behaviour
that has to change, or a mixture that no longer fits the pair. The journey
starts at the feed tree the Market Simulator draws and ends when the person
leaves the saved feed.

A saved config is a candidate. Nothing it describes is produced until the feed
is enabled and started, and the tick rate it carries is the one a running feed
honours. The model calls this record the FX spot generation config, and the
screen calls it the feed.

* Steps

1. *Find the feed.* The person opens *Market data*, then *Synthetic*, and reads
   the folder tree. The system lists the party's FX feeds under their folder:
   the base and quote codes, the ORE key, the source name, the tick rate, the
   engine and the enabled and auto-start flags. The person filters the list by
   pair or by source name.
2. *Open or start a feed.* The person selects a feed and the system shows the
   editor for it, or the person chooses *New FX Rate* and the system shows the
   same editor empty. Opening a feed reads its mixture of =gmm_component= rows
   beside the config, because the two are one answer.
3. *Name the instrument.* The person enters the base and quote currency codes.
   The system derives the ORE key, =FX/RATE/EUR/USD=, from the pair, and
   suggests a source name from it. The person may change the source name and
   add a description. The system offers the currency codes from the reference
   data and the pairs from the pair catalogue, and it states the pair is not
   quoted when the catalogue does not hold it.
4. *Set the price behaviour.* The person chooses the engine, which is the
   =process_type= column: =geometric=, =arithmetic= or =ornstein_uhlenbeck=.
   The person sets how often a new price is produced, as ticks per hour, and
   enters the initial price. For the mean-reverting engine the screen adds the
   long-term mean and the reversion speed and shows the half-life they imply.
5. *Edit the mixture.* The person adds, changes and removes the Gaussians the
   process draws from. Each row carries a zero-based index, a description, a
   mean log-return, a standard deviation and a weight. The system shows the raw
   sum of the weights and says that the save normalises them; a sum that is not
   one is a warning and not a refusal.
6. *Say where the initial price comes from.* The person chooses =fixed=, where
   the initial price is the one entered, or =vintage=, where the feed reads the
   opening price from a named market-data source and date. The system shows the
   vintage it would read and refuses a source or a date it cannot reach.
7. *Look at the process before it is saved.* The person reads the return
   distribution the mixture implies and a batch of sample price paths. The
   system draws both from the values on the screen and adds a seed and a path
   count; *Reseed* draws a new batch from the same mixture. Nothing the preview
   computes is stored and nothing reaches the feed.
8. *Validate and save.* The person chooses *Save feed*. The screen holds the
   save while a rule fails: both currency codes are three letters and differ,
   the price source has what it needs, the mixture holds at least one component
   and the weights sum above zero. The system then writes the config, writes
   the mixture, and replies with the version it wrote. The feed is not started
   by the save; it stays disabled until the person enables it and a running
   feed is asked for.
9. *Check the history.* The person opens the history of the config or of one
   component. The system shows every version with the field-level difference
   from the one before. A revert is a new version, not a deletion.
10. *Delete a feed that is no longer wanted.* The person chooses *Delete*. The
    system removes the mixture first and the config after it, and asks for a
    reason. A feed that is running is stopped before it can be removed.
11. *Leave.* The person goes back to the feed tree. Nothing has been published,
    and no tick has been produced.

Steps 8 and 10 are each two writes, not one: the config and its mixture are
separate resources, so a failure between them leaves a feed whose mixture is
partly updated, or orphaned component rows; see /What is missing/.

* Screens and wireframes

One screen. The feed's identity and the pair sit at the top, the price
behaviour, the mixture and the rules sit in the middle in the order the save
reads them, and the two previews sit beside them, so the person reads the
choice and what it does at once. Save sits at the end of the form it commits.

* Entities composed

| =gmm_component= | The mixture the process draws from: one weighted Gaussian per row, with its index, description, mean, standard deviation and weight | [[id:0F0FB279-A707-4BA5-A1A2-40F46E591EB6][ores.synthetic.gmm_component]] |
| =market_data_generation_config= | The named container the feed belongs to, which supplies the collection the tree groups the feed under | [[id:8771702C-0BC4-4955-8D7E-DDBE2497D5B3][ores.synthetic.market_data_generation_config]] |
| =folder= | The hierarchy node the feed hangs from, so a person finds it in the tree rather than by its source name | [[id:5455BC0F-59E3-4EA4-ABA2-6C4BB6CA00C7][ores.synthetic.folder]] |

The pair is two soft references: =base_currency_code= and
=quote_currency_code= are text and point at =ores.refdata.currency= without a
declared foreign key. The ticks a saved feed produces land in a =market_series=
whose oresmd URI is derived from the ORE key, and the feed references that
series only through the key. Both configs and components are versioned, so each
has a history, and the mixture is stored as rows and never as a blob.

The history request is the generic one, with =entity_type=
=ores.synthetic.fx_spot_generation_config= or =ores.synthetic.gmm_component=;
both register a history provider. The mixture is its own resource, so saving a
feed and saving its weights are separate calls, and no operation writes both
together. The FX simulate takes the mixture as request parameters rather than
reading the config, so it previews the values on the screen and never the saved
row.

* What is missing

1. *No screen exists.* No web page, HTTP route or shell verb reaches these
   operations from a browser. The generated detail dialog for this config went
   with the removed desktop client, and the shell files are codegen commands a
   person runs by hand. The candidate is a web page and a route. Until then the
   journey runs from the shell, with the previews unavailable.
2. *The typed weight is not kept.* The save normalises the weights of a feed,
   but nothing stores the values as typed, so the raw sum and the normalised
   weights cannot be told apart after the save, and the screen that shows both
   cannot be rebuilt from the row. The candidate is a decision on whether the
   raw weight is stored beside the normalised one. Until then the mixture reads
   back as the numbers the draw uses.
3. *The engine picker does not select the mixture.* The screen presents three
   engines, while =process_type= holds =geometric=, =arithmetic= or
   =ornstein_uhlenbeck=, and the =gmm_component= rows are what the draw
   actually mixes. Nothing in the row says the mixture is the engine, so a
   saved feed does not reproduce what was previewed. The candidate is a process
   type that names the mixture, or a rule that a mixture is always the source
   of the returns. Until then the screen says which engine the config holds and
   draws the mixture regardless.
4. *The preview does not name its feed.* The simulate request carries the
   mixture, the price and the seed, and no config id, so the paths a person
   judges are the screen's numbers and not the stored feed's. The candidate is
   a preview that reads a saved config by id. Until then the screen states that
   the preview uses the unsaved values.
5. *A new feed has no folder.* =folder_id= is optional and only the
   publish-from-dq path fills it, so a feed made by hand has no place in the
   tree and the person cannot find it where they left it. The candidate is a
   folder the editor resolves or creates on save. Until then the screen lists
   the feed under the collection that owns it.
6. *Saving is not atomic.* The config and its mixture are separate generated
   resources with separate =put= subjects, and no operation writes both. A
   failure between the two writes leaves a feed with a partly updated mixture,
   and the save is a transaction the caller must compose. The candidate is an
   apply operation that writes the config and its mixture together. Until then
   the screen saves the config first, then the mixture, and says what it wrote.
7. *The pair is not validated against a declared reference.* The base and quote
   codes are text, the pair catalogue is read on the page, and no operation
   answers "is this pair quoted". The candidate is a declared reference or a
   validation call. Until then the screen marks a pair the catalogue does not
   hold and lets the save through.
8. *No approval kind covers a feed.* A change to a feed that a running producer
   honours is written with no second pair of eyes and no comment. The candidate
   is an approval kind for the feed, with its decide permission. Until then a
   save takes effect at once and the history names the writer alone.
