#+description: How a package travels: a content-addressed artefact in object storage with a modelled manifest that names its bundles and their parts, a coordinator that reads only the manifest and sends one message per bundle, a per-bundle transaction in its owner's unit_of_work, sequencing and a sandbox in place of global atomicity, per-bundle progress and retry, and an as_of instant that makes a stale import detectable.

* Summary

A package is an artefact that carries one or more bundles between
systems as bytes. It has no relatedness constraint: the bundles it
carries need not be related to one another, may belong to different
owners, and the package as a whole is never atomic. Relatedness is what
makes a bundle a bundle, and the absence of relatedness is what makes a
package merely a package.

The atomic unit inside a package is the bundle: a set of related
families, belonging to one owner, applied as one unit. The families are
related when they share either a data invariant that must hold at one
instant, or a single operation whose intent is all-or-nothing. The test
is one sentence, completed without naming a file, a screen, a workflow
step or a boundary format: "these families may not be written apart
because ___ must hold at one instant, or because one operation must
apply all of them or none". If the blank needs a file, a screen, a step
or a format, the families are not related, and the set is a package's
contents. A bundle never spans two owners, because no transaction does.

A package is carried by three artefacts. The *manifest* is a generated,
modelled operation type naming every bundle and every part. A *part* is
one bundle's rows of one kind, encoded as a msgpack stream. The
*package archive* is a content-addressed, zstd-compressed ZIP that holds
one entry per (owner, kind) and embeds the manifest. The bytes live in
object storage. The manifest and the part references are kilobytes; the
rows are never on the wire.

A coordinator reads only the manifest and dispatches one message per
bundle, to that bundle's owner. Each message carries the archive
URI and that bundle's part records, never the data. Each owner fetches
only its own entries, verifies their checksums, streams and validates
the rows, and applies its bundle atomically in its own =unit_of_work=.
A package is never atomic across bundles or owners: no transaction
spans two owners, global atomicity does not exist on this platform, and
it must not be promised. Consistency comes from sequencing, a stated
=as_of= instant, and a sandbox for examine-before-live. Progress and
retry are per bundle.

* Detail

** What a bundle is, the test for one, and what a package carries

A bundle is a classification, not a file format and not a large message.
It is sanctioned by
[[id:B2D74E96-0353-4A3D-8CE5-3FC3C30D864C][the Bundle classification]]
when its families are related and one owner holds them all. The families
are open in number: a structure with its trades, its instruments and its
legs is one bundle over trading's tables, and the trading owner applies
it in one =unit_of_work=.

The test is the sentence above. Complete it without naming a file, a
screen, a workflow step or a boundary format:

#+begin_quote
These families may not be written apart because __________ must hold at
one instant, or because one operation must apply all of them or none.
#+end_quote

- If the blank needs a file, a screen, a step or a format to be true,
  the families are not related. The set is a package's contents, and each
  family is its own bundle. Split it.
- If the invariant covers only some of the families, the set is two or
  more bundles. Split it at the invariant.
- If the invariant covers every family and one owner holds them, the
  set is a *bundle*, and one transaction applies it.
- If the families are related only by provenance and several owners
  hold them, no bundle spans them. The set is a *package* carrying one
  bundle per owner, and this page is its transport.

A file's contents are never an invariant. A document lists conventions
because the format lists them; that is a fact about ORE XML, not about
conventions. An export that must be converted to a boundary format is a
package when its bundles came from one system at one instant; the
conversion needs every part at one instant, and the stated =as_of= is
what gives it that.

The design constraint is the heterogeneity, not the size. Size makes the
transport worth designing; heterogeneity across owners is why the
transport cannot name the types in one message, and why the atomic unit
is the bundle rather than the package.

** Anatomy: manifest, part, package archive

A package has exactly three artefacts, and the bundles it carries are
records in the manifest. Each artefact has one writer, one place to
live, and a fixed set of readers.

#+ATTR_HTML: :class hug-leading
| Artefact | What it is | Where it lives | Who may read it |
|----------+------------+----------------+-----------------|
| *Manifest* | A generated operation object: the package format version, the producer, the =as_of= instant, the target, one record per bundle, and one record per part inside its bundle. It is encoded with the platform wire codec, like any other message. | The object =packages/{package_id}/manifest.msgpack=. The same bytes are the first entry of the archive, named =manifest.msgpack=, so the archive describes itself. | The coordinator, the producer, and a boundary converter such as the ORE XML writer. An owner receives its own bundle and part records in its message and does not need the whole manifest. |
| *Part* | One bundle's rows of one kind, encoded as a msgpack stream of one generated type. It is a logical member of the archive, not a separate object. | An entry in =package.zip.zst= or in the tar container, selected by its entry name or by its byte range. | Its owner, and only its owner, during an apply. A converter may read every part during an export. |
| *Package archive* | A content-addressed ZIP whose entries are zstd-compressed, with one entry per (owner, kind) and the manifest embedded. It is the only object that holds rows. | The object =packages/{package_id}/package.zip.zst=, or =package.tar.zst= when the container is a tar. | An owner reads one entry. A converter reads every entry. The coordinator does not open it. |

The archive is assembled from staging objects written by the owners.
Each owner encodes the parts of its bundle, compresses each entry with
zstd, writes the compressed entry to a staging object, and returns a
part record with the checksum and both sizes. The producer writes the
manifest, then asks object storage to copy each staging entry into the
archive under its entry name, at ZIP method 93, without recompressing
it, and to add the manifest as the =manifest.msgpack= entry. It never
decodes an entry. The producer deletes the staging objects once the
archive's part checksums have been verified. No component ever encodes
or compresses another owner's rows.

The =package_id= is SHA-256 over the canonical manifest bytes with every
=part.uri= set to the empty string, encoded by the same codec in
declaration order. The URI is the only field blanked, because it is
derived from the id. The digest therefore covers =as_of=, the target,
the producer, the bundles, every part checksum and the compressed
layout. A re-encoding changes the id and therefore the key; the part
checksums do not change, so semantic identity survives at the part
level. A key is never reused for different semantic content.

** Why nothing bulk is on the wire

The manifest and the part references are kilobytes. The data is in
object storage. Efficiency comes from that structure, not from the
encoding: encoding a package into a message would still move the same
bytes onto the bus, and would add a decode step before any owner could
start.

The reason a package does not travel as a message is validation,
routing, per-bundle retry and the prohibition on an encoded value in a
message field — not size.

- *Validation.* A message is validated before it is accepted. A part's
  rows cannot be validated until they are decoded against the owner's
  model, which is the owner's job at its own boundary.
- *Routing.* One message has one subject and one handler. A package has
  one bundle per owner and one owner per bundle, and the coordinator must
  dispatch each bundle to the owner that may write it.
- *Per-bundle retry.* A failed part must cost one bundle, not a whole
  package. A message that carries the archive forces a re-send of every
  bundle to retry one.
- *The encoded-value rule.* A message field that holds an encoded value
  has no static type at either end; see
  [[id:C951B5F1-B51C-4274-BA4D-6297AD46A4FB][Polymorphic payloads over NATS]].

A message also has a practical bound, and a package does not. That is a
limit on the message, not the reason for the design.

** The manifest

The manifest is a *generated, modelled operation type*. It is declared
in =ores.codegen.operation=, generated with a schema and a codec, and
encoded with the platform wire codec like any other message. It is never
free-form JSON, never a =std::string= field holding JSON, and never
inferred from the payload or from the archive. It is versioned, so a
reader refuses a layout it does not know instead of guessing. It is the
document that states which bundles the package carries, and the parts of
each.

The target is not decoration. An apply package with an empty
=target.sandbox= is refused by the coordinator. A package aimed at a
sandbox applies inside that sandbox's isolation; the anchor decides who
may open the sandbox and who may then see the result, per
[[id:4AB0BC63-D73A-4FC3-B9AF-16C1BB90653F][Sandbox]].

=bundles= is ordered by owner dependency: every bundle is contiguous,
and the bundles appear in dependency order. The grouping is what lets
one message per bundle preserve the global application order. A manifest
that interleaves two bundles of one owner is refused, because a bundle
is one atomic unit and no message can apply half of it before the other
half.

=bundles= is O(number of bundles), and each bundle's parts are
O(number of parts), never O(number of rows). A part record holds a
=row_count=, not the rows. That bound is what makes the manifest
validatable before any bytes move: its size is set by the number of
owners, bundles and kinds, not by the size of the portfolio, so a
portfolio of a hundred million rows still has a manifest of kilobytes.
A manifest that grew with rows would be the message the package exists
to avoid, and the coordinator would have to read the data to validate
its own routing table.

** The container and the encoding

The archive is a ZIP with one zstd-compressed entry per (owner, kind),
named ={owner}/{kind}=, using ZIP compression method 93, plus the
embedded =manifest.msgpack= entry. The ZIP central directory is the
index: it gives every entry's name, compression method, compressed size
and offset. The archive is content-addressed: a key is never reused for a
different semantic content. A put replaces the whole object; a different
compression is a new =package_id=. A =tar_zstd= container is allowed
only when it carries a seekable zstd frame index that gives each entry's
byte range; a plain =tar.zst= has no index, cannot give random access,
and is refused.

*Random access is the decisive point.* A container with an index gives
random access to a single part. An owner seeks to its entry, reads that
entry and nothing else. A single large msgpack document cannot do this,
because a msgpack array has no index: to reach the owner's part, a reader
must decode every row of every earlier part. That would force one owner
to read and parse bytes another owner produced, which is exactly what
[[id:D5F77B10-D498-4B56-ADF7-7E5CEFA44FA0][Database per Service]]
forbids. The index is therefore a boundary control, not a performance
detail.

The row encoding has four rules:

1. *One entry, one type, one stream.* An entry is a stream of msgpack
   maps, one map per row, and every map is the owner's generated type
   for that kind. The entry does not hold one array of all rows.
2. *Named fields, never positional arrays.* A row is a map with string
   keys. A later field addition adds a key and does not shift an
   existing value, so an archived package still decodes after the type
   gains a field. A positional array binds meaning to position; adding
   a field in the middle silently reinterprets every archived row.
3. *Row-by-row streaming decode with bounded memory.* The owner
   decompresses the entry and decodes one map at a time, validates it
   and writes it inside its transaction. Memory is the size of one row
   plus the transaction state, not the size of the package. This is what
   makes a portfolio spanning every instrument type tractable: the
   number of kinds changes the number of parts, not the memory an owner
   needs to apply one of them.
4. *The part format version is independent of the package format
   version.* =part.format_version= gates the row encoding;
   =package_format_version= gates only the manifest layout and the
   container rules. A version 1 manifest may contain a version 2 part,
   and the owner selects the decoder from the part record. A new row
   field or a new part encoding does not force a new manifest layout.

** Checksums and compression

Every part is checksummed *uncompressed*: SHA-256 over the concatenated
msgpack maps exactly as the owner will decode them. The checksum is
therefore a statement about the data, not about the compressor. A later
zstd version, a different compression level or a re-encoding of the
archive leaves the checksum unchanged, so the part's semantic identity
survives a compressor upgrade. The compressed size is recorded
separately in =part.compressed_size=; it describes the object, not the
data. A re-encoding produces a new =package_id=, because the compressed
layout is part of the manifest, and the recomputed checksum still
matches.

The owner decompresses the entry, hashes the uncompressed stream, and
compares the digest with the manifest before it applies the first row.
Verification is a first pass over the entry and decoding is a second
pass, so memory stays bounded and no row is applied before the digest
matches. A mismatch is a failure of the part, never a warning.

Compress the archive, not the messages. The wire codec already gzips a
message payload above 4096 bytes, so compressing a manifest or a
part-reference message again is compressing compressed data. Do not wrap
the archive in gzip either: its entries are already zstd-compressed.
Compression belongs to the object-storage layer, where the archive and
its entries live.

** The coordinator

The coordinator is the apply operation's front end. It reads only the
manifest. It validates the version, the target, the required fields,
that every bundle names a known owner, that every (owner, kind) pair is
unique, and that the bundle order is the declared sequence. It may HEAD
the archive object to confirm that it exists; it does not GET, decompress
or decode a part, and it does not compute a part checksum. That check
belongs to the owner, which reads the bytes anyway.

The coordinator must never parse another owner's data, or database per
service is broken. It has no schema for another service's rows, so any
parse it performs is a second model that can only drift. The same rule
is recorded in
[[id:8866D24E-D95B-43C6-A65B-546807E049DD][No service reads or writes
another component's tables]] and in
[[id:D5F77B10-D498-4B56-ADF7-7E5CEFA44FA0][Database per Service]].

It dispatches one message per bundle, carrying the package id, the
bundle id, the correlation id, the archive URI, the manifest's =as_of=
and =target=, and that bundle's part records. A reference, never the
data. When a bundle holds several parts, the one message carries all of
that bundle's records; the message is still O(parts this owner owns),
and the owner applies them in manifest order and reports each part
separately. No message carries rows, a document or an archive. The
coordinator never merges two bundles into one message, because that
would make one message carry two atomic units and two owners' rows.

The part message is a request with a deadline, per
[[id:49F0A1B2-C3D4-4E5F-8A71-8293A4B5C6D7][Bounded Request]]. The
reply names the part and reports applied, rejected or failed, with the
reason and the row count. The bundle's reply is the set of its parts'
outcomes.

** The owner

The owner's step is the only step that writes rows, and it writes only
its own. It receives one message per bundle:

1. Fetch only its bundle's parts, by entry name or byte range, from the
   archive the message names. It does not fetch the whole archive.
2. Decompress each entry and verify the SHA-256 of the uncompressed
   stream against =part.checksum=.
3. Look up the idempotency key (package id, bundle id, kind, checksum).
   If this part already committed, reply applied with its recorded counts
   and stop.
4. Compare =as_of= with the owner's heads. A stale bundle is refused, as
   *Progress, retry, and staleness* states.
5. Decode the stream with the codec =part.format_version= names, one
   row at a time, and validate each row against the owner's own model:
   required fields, keys, and references the owner holds.
6. Write each validated row through the generated repository for its
   kind, inside one =unit_of_work= for the bundle, in manifest order.
   Never write a kind's table with a hand-written statement in a part
   handler; see
   [[id:5724BDE2-7FA4-4667-9129-2D9DD7783AB2][Entity-table writes go
   through the repositories]].
7. Commit only after the last row, and only if the decoded count equals
   =part.row_count= for every part. A row that fails validation, a count
   that differs and any write error roll back the whole bundle. The
   bundle is all or nothing.
8. Do not read between the writes of the unit of work. In unpatched
   sqlgen a read commits the transaction, so the rule that keeps the
   bundle atomic is the one in
   [[id:9562C34E-5790-422D-8E31-774691995860][A read inside a unit of
   work commits it]].
9. Stamp the session's tenant and party, never a value from the payload.
10. Reply with the counts per part and, on failure, the reason.

The transaction is the owner's, so the bundle is the unit of atomicity. A
failure halfway rolls back that bundle alone. No other owner sees a
partial write, and no other owner is asked to compensate. A package with
several bundles is therefore never atomic as a whole.

** Transactionality

A package serves two cases, and the two do not carry the same guarantee.
One is a package that carries a single bundle, and that bundle is
written in one transaction. The other is a package whose bundles span
several services, and no single transaction covers it. Which case a
package is decides the mechanism, and the mechanism decides the
guarantee. A package is never atomic across bundles or owners in either
case.

*** A package that carries one bundle, held by one service

A structure with its trades, its instruments and its legs is a set of
related families, and every family of it is held by one owner. That is
[[id:B2D74E96-0353-4A3D-8CE5-3FC3C30D864C][the Bundle
classification]]: one bundle. It is written in one =unit_of_work=: one
transaction, all or nothing, over the central row and every part the
write names. No partially saved structure can ever be observed, because
the transaction either commits every row or none of them.

Each family is the generated =ores.codegen.family= model, whose
put writes the central row and that kind's parts in one transaction, per
[[id:87E3F021-1C69-48DE-9139-32C44C99B3B9][Codegen patterns: choosing a
model shape]]. The unit of work is bounded by the database connection,
and the rule that keeps a read from ending it is the unit-of-work rule
in [[id:9562C34E-5790-422D-8E31-774691995860][A read inside a unit of
work commits it]].

*** A package whose bundles span several services

An ORE import touches reference data, trades and market data, and those
rows belong to three services. That set cannot be one transaction. It is
a package carrying three bundles, one per owner.

The platform does not support distributed transactions. There is no
two-phase commit, no XA, and no transaction that spans services. The
=unit_of_work= is function-local to one connection: it begins and ends on
the connection the calling service holds, and no mechanism extends it
across a call to another service.

This is a deliberate position, not an omission:

- *Contention and lock lifetime.* A transaction held across owners holds
  its row locks for the whole of every remote call. One bundle that takes
  minutes would block every writer of every table it had touched for
  those minutes, and the wait would grow with the size of the bundle
  rather than with the row being written.
- *A pooled connection cannot be held across messages.* The service's
  connection comes from a pool, and a message is handled between other
  messages. Keeping the transaction open while another owner applies its
  bundle would pin one pooled connection and one open transaction for an
  unbounded time, and the pool would run out.
- *Availability.* Two-phase commit couples every owner's availability to
  the coordinator's and to the slowest participant's. An owner that
  cannot answer leaves every other owner's prepared transaction open and
  locked. The component boundaries forbid that coupling.
- *Ownership.* A service reads and writes only its own tables. A
  transaction over two services needs one transaction manager over both
  databases, and it gives one service the power to commit rows it does
  not own, which
  [[id:D5F77B10-D498-4B56-ADF7-7E5CEFA44FA0][Database per Service]]
  forbids.

Do not promise global atomicity to a caller, and do not describe a
package as all-or-nothing across bundles or owners.

*** What replaces the distributed transaction

Six things carry the consistency the transaction cannot, in the order a
bundle passes through them.

1. *Per-bundle atomicity.* Each owner applies its bundle in one
   transaction. A bundle is all or nothing, and the manifest names
   exactly which bundles and parts exist. A package that is complete
   when every bundle reports applied is a package whose bundles all
   resolve at the same instant.
2. *Sequencing by the workflow engine.* The coordinator is the front end
   of a [[id:4F95A407-AD43-4CBD-952E-1CCACAA90080][workflow run]]: each
   bundle dispatch is a step, and the steps of the run are the unit of
   progress, never the archive. The engine holds the state of the run and
   decides each next step from the results so far, which is the
   [[id:98177604-4092-4A95-B2C3-EE6F82A3185E][Process Manager]]. The
   order in =bundles= is the application order, and a bundle starts only
   after its predecessor's reply where the invariant has a direction. An
   environment export imports reference data before the configuration
   that names it, and trades before the structures that group them. A
   failed bundle blocks only its successors, which stay pending and are
   not dispatched; independent bundles continue. Where no bundle
   constrains another, they apply independently, and =as_of= is what
   makes the set coherent rather than the sequence.
3. *A declared failure policy.* Each step states what a failure does:
   stop, or compensate, per
   [[id:353DC9B3-65BC-4801-B6D8-08ADD2647F24][Failure, compensation
   and retry]]. An import into a sandbox declares stop, because the
   sandbox is the compensating control and a retry of the failed bundle is
   safe. A definition whose completed steps are a liability may declare
   compensate instead, and the engine walks the completed steps in
   reverse. That is the [[id:E8DAA8F6-9A41-44A7-A79C-58F7FDB5ADB2][Saga]],
   and a compensation is a new action, not an erasure: the other owners
   saw the intermediate state, so a compensation writes something new
   and is not a rollback of what was already committed. A bundle that
   fails inside its own transaction is rolled back locally and asks
   nothing of anyone; compensation is for the completed steps a run has
   to give back.
4. *An idempotency ledger per owner.* The owner records the key (package
   id, bundle id, kind, checksum) in the same transaction as the rows it
   applies. A second delivery of the same part finds the key and replies
   applied without writing again, so a retry is safe.
5. *A sandbox for examine-before-live.* An import applies into a sandbox,
   so a wrong bundle is discarded with the whole sandbox rather than
   repaired row by row in live data. Promotion from the sandbox to live
   data is a separate, smaller operation with its own review; it is not a
   retry of the import. See
   [[id:4AB0BC63-D73A-4FC3-B9AF-16C1BB90653F][Sandbox]].
6. *An honest report of a partial outcome.* The reply names each part and
   its outcome, and the package's progress is the set of its bundles. A
   partial result is reported as a partial result.

A package whose bundles span owners is therefore eventually consistent:
its bundles resolve at one declared instant, =as_of=, and not at one
commit. A partial result is a supported outcome, reported as such, and
not a failure of the mechanism.

A package that needs global rollback is a modelling error. If the
invariant truly needs every owner to commit together, the data belongs
under one owner, and the set is then a bundle, not a package. Split the
invariant until each bundle holds locally, or use the sandbox above.

| Case | Scope of atomicity | Mechanism | What the user sees on failure | Guaranteed | Not guaranteed |
|------+--------------------+-----------+-------------------------------+------------+----------------|
| A package carrying one bundle held by one service | one service, every family of the bundle | one =unit_of_work= over the central rows and their parts | nothing was written: the transaction rolled back | all or nothing for the bundle; no partially saved structure is ever observed | atomicity across a second service |
| A package whose bundles span several services | per bundle, which is per owner | one transaction per bundle, sequenced by a workflow run | the bundles that applied stay applied, and the failed bundle is named | per-bundle atomicity, and eventual consistency at =as_of= | global atomicity across bundles, all-or-nothing, global rollback |
| One part of a bundle | one owner's rows of one kind | the checksum, the bundle's transaction and the idempotency key | the part applied nothing; the bundle rolled back | the part is applied at most once | that the other bundles agree with it |
| A bundle that is retried | the same one bundle | the ledger key (package id, bundle id, kind, checksum) | applied, with the counts the first delivery recorded | a repeat writes nothing | a new instant; the retry keeps the package's =as_of= |
| A package in a sandbox | the sandbox | discard the whole sandbox | the sandbox is discarded as a whole, and no official row was touched | a wrong import costs no live row | promotion to live data, which is a separate reviewed operation |

*** How a part fails

#+ATTR_HTML: :class hug-leading
| Failure | What the coordinator does | What the user sees |
|---------+---------------------------+--------------------|
| Part missing | The owner's fetch of the entry fails. The coordinator marks that part =failed= and does not re-send the same immutable archive; the other bundles keep their own state. | The part's owner, kind and entry name, and =missing=. The bundle is incomplete, and no rows from that part were applied. |
| Checksum mismatch | The owner refuses before applying, because the bytes are not the bytes the manifest names. The coordinator marks the part =failed= and reports both digests; a retry of the same bytes cannot help. | =checksum_mismatch=, with the expected and actual SHA-256. The bundle applied nothing; the other bundles are unchanged. |
| Kind unsupported | The coordinator refuses the manifest at dispatch when no known owner claims the bundle. If the owner knows the kind but not the format version, it replies =unsupported= and the coordinator marks the part =failed=. | The kind and =part.format_version=, and the component that must be upgraded. No message is sent, or no row is applied. |
| Apply refused | The owner validates or applies and rejects the bundle; it rolls the whole bundle back and replies =rejected= with the row or key that failed. The coordinator records the reason and does not retry a data finding. | The reason and the failing row or key. The bundle applied nothing; the other bundles are not rolled back. |
| Owner unreachable | No responder is available, or the request cannot be delivered. The coordinator retries with bounded backoff until the package deadline, then marks the bundle =failed=. | =unreachable=, with the owner's name and the attempts made. The bundle stays pending until the deadline, and other bundles proceed. |
| Deadline exceeded | The bundle's bounded request expires. The coordinator marks it =timed_out= and re-offers it only through the idempotent retry below; the owner may still commit after the reply was lost. | =timed_out=, with the deadline and the bundle's last known state. The coordinator reports the bundle as unknown until the retry or a reconciliation read confirms it. |

** Progress, retry, and staleness

Progress and retry are per bundle and per part, never per archive:

- *Progress.* The coordinator records each part as =pending=,
  =dispatched=, =applied=, =rejected=, =failed= or =timed_out=, with its
  row count and reason, and reports each bundle as the set of its parts.
  The package's progress is the set of its bundles, not a percentage of a
  blob.
- *Retry.* A retry re-sends the bundle reference. The archive's content
  is immutable, so the retry reads the same uncompressed bytes. The apply
  is idempotent
  on the key (package id, bundle id, kind, checksum): the owner records
  that key in its own part-apply ledger in the same transaction as the
  rows, and a second delivery of the same part finds the key and replies
  applied without writing again. The coordinator does not re-upload the
  archive and does not restart the whole package to fix one bundle.
- *Rejection.* A rejected bundle is a finding about the data, not a
  transport failure. It is reported with the row or key that failed, and
  the other bundles are not rolled back.
- *Restart.* Restarting the coordinator re-offers the outstanding bundles
  only.

=as_of= makes a stale package detectable rather than last-writer-wins.
It is the bitemporal instant the package is a view of, per
[[id:4AAD3581-CFA7-4B44-A2DD-0236784E939F][Time and Timestamps]]. A
bundle is *stale* when the owner's heads have moved since that instant:
any row the bundle would write has a version whose transaction time is
later than =as_of=, or a key the bundle references in this owner's
database was deleted or replaced after =as_of=. A bundle whose =as_of= is
later than or equal to every relevant change is not stale, and it
applies.

The owner *refuses* a stale bundle. It does not rebase it. A rebase is an
explicit new export at a newer =as_of=, which produces a new manifest and
a new =package_id=; the producer mints it, and no owner edits the
manifest or silently applies an older bundle against newer rows.

Refusal is the chosen behaviour for three reasons. A silent rebase would
make each bundle a different instant, which destroys the one property the
package exists to provide. The archived package is the evidence of what
was imported and when; a rebase that changed its meaning would destroy
that evidence. And a refusal is detectable: the user sees the stale
bundle and can ask for a fresh package, while a rebase reports success
and leaves the mixture invisible.

** Worked example: a portfolio exported for ORE XML

A portfolio spans currencies, calendars, conventions, curves, trades and
every instrument type the book holds. The export must give the ORE XML
converter one instant, or the document mixes two books. The artefact is
a package; the atomic units inside it are one bundle per owner.

*The bundles and their parts.* One bundle per owner, and one part per
(owner, kind), in application order:

- the =refdata= bundle: =refdata/currency=, =refdata/calendar=,
  =refdata/day_count= and =refdata/convention=;
- the =marketdata= bundle: =marketdata/curve= and
  =marketdata/vol_surface=;
- the =trading= bundle: =trading/portfolio=, =trading/book=,
  =trading/trade= and =trading/instrument/*=, one part per instrument
  kind.

This is exactly the case a closed message type cannot carry: the last
group gains a member whenever the book gains an instrument kind.

*The manifest in outline.*

#+begin_example
package_format_version  1
container               zip_zstd
producer                {trading-export, 1.0.0, production}
as_of                   2026-10-17T09:00:00.000000Z
target                  {sandbox: empty (export only),
                         anchor: desk-42, party: portfolio-owner}
bundles                 refdata, marketdata, trading (in that order)
                        one part record per (owner, kind) in its bundle
#+end_example

*The sequence.*

1. The converter fixes =as_of= and asks the export operation for the
   portfolio. No owner reads outside that instant.
2. Each owner reads its rows in one transaction, encodes and
   zstd-compresses its entries, writes a staging object per part, and
   returns a part record with the checksum, row count and both sizes.
3. The producer writes the manifest, then asks storage to assemble the
   staging entries into one archive and to add the manifest entry. It
   verifies every part checksum.
4. The converter reads the manifest, then streams each entry into the
   ORE XML writer. It writes to a temporary file and renames it only
   after every part has been read and checked.

*The atomicity actually obtained.* There is no global transaction. Each
owner's bundle is one consistent read at =as_of=, and the conversion is
all or nothing at its own boundary: if any part is missing or fails its
checksum, the converter writes no ORE XML at all. A failure does not
roll back a read, because there is nothing to roll back; it withholds
the output. That is consistency by the instant plus an atomic output
file, not distributed atomicity, and not package-wide atomicity.

** Worked example: an environment export imported into development

A developer needs a production defect reproduced from one snapshot of
the environment: identity, reference data, market data and trades
together. The snapshot is a package carrying four bundles.

*The bundles and their parts.* The =iam= bundle: =iam/tenant= and
=iam/party=. The =refdata= bundle: =refdata/currency=,
=refdata/calendar= and =refdata/convention=. The =marketdata= bundle:
=marketdata/curve=. The =trading= bundle: =trading/book=,
=trading/portfolio=, =trading/trade= and =trading/instrument/*=. Owners:
=ores.iam=, =ores.refdata=, =ores.marketdata= and =ores.trading=.

The four bundles are not related to one another: iam rows and marketdata
rows share no invariant and no operation. They are in one package
because one export produced them, which is provenance and not
relatedness. The package is therefore never atomic as a whole.

*The manifest in outline.*

#+begin_example
package_format_version  1
container               zip_zstd
producer                {environment-export, 1.0.0, production}
as_of                   2026-10-17T09:00:00.000000Z
target                  {sandbox: 7f3c..., anchor: desk-42,
                         party: 8a21...}
bundles                 iam, refdata, marketdata, trading
                        ordered by reference, not alphabetical
#+end_example

*The sequence.*

1. The source environment exports the package and stores the archive and
   manifest, as in the previous example.
2. The development coordinator reads the manifest, checks that the
   sandbox is open, that its anchor matches =target.anchor=, and that
   every bundle names a known owner.
3. It sends one message per bundle in =bundles= order: =ores.iam= first,
   then =ores.refdata=, then =ores.marketdata=, then =ores.trading=.
4. Each owner fetches only its bundle's entries, verifies the checksums,
   streams the rows, validates them, and applies them in one
   =unit_of_work= per bundle.
5. A person examines the sandbox. Promotion to live data is a separate
   operation with its own review.

*The atomicity actually obtained.* Each bundle is atomic in its owner's
database. If =trading= rejects a trade after =refdata= applied, the
=refdata= rows stay in the sandbox and the =trading= bundle applied
nothing; the sandbox is then discarded as a whole, and no official row
was touched. The package does not provide global rollback, and the
sandbox is what makes that limit safe.

** What a package must not become

- *An opaque archive inside a message field.* That is the artefact
  anti-pattern in
  [[id:B2D74E96-0353-4A3D-8CE5-3FC3C30D864C][Data classifications]]:
  the receiver must unpack before it can validate, no owner can be
  routed to, and one failed row costs the whole archive. It also breaks
  the encoded-value rule in
  [[id:C951B5F1-B51C-4274-BA4D-6297AD46A4FB][Polymorphic payloads over
  NATS]].
- *A union of unrelated entities presented as one struct.* That is the
  anti-type in
  [[id:B2D74E96-0353-4A3D-8CE5-3FC3C30D864C][Data classifications]]:
  one typed array per kind, named after a file or a screen, with no
  manifest, no owner boundary and no instant.
- *A payload the coordinator parses.* A coordinator that decodes another
  owner's rows is a second model. It is the composite scope the
  classification page refuses, in
  [[id:B2D74E96-0353-4A3D-8CE5-3FC3C30D864C][Data classifications]],
  and it breaks
  [[id:D5F77B10-D498-4B56-ADF7-7E5CEFA44FA0][Database per Service]].
  The manifest is the only thing the coordinator reads.
- *A package treated as one atomic set.* The package is the carriage, not
  the unit of consistency. A caller that needs all-or-nothing is asking
  for a bundle, and a bundle belongs to one owner. A package that
  promises all-or-nothing across owners promises what no transaction can
  give.
- *A substitute for a bundle when one owner suffices.* If one owner
  writes every family and one transaction can hold the invariant, the
  set is a bundle; use =ores.codegen.family= for its families, not this
  transport. The classification page owns that decision, in
  [[id:B2D74E96-0353-4A3D-8CE5-3FC3C30D864C][Data classifications]],
  and the checklist records it, in
  [[id:56AD6F46-99E0-482E-B7B5-C9FDEC8A7641][the data classification
  checklist]].

* Patterns used

- [[id:E8DAA8F6-9A41-44A7-A79C-58F7FDB5ADB2][Saga]] — the declared
  failure policy: the completed bundles are local steps whose
  compensations run in reverse, and a compensation is a new action rather
  than a rollback.
- [[id:98177604-4092-4A95-B2C3-EE6F82A3185E][Process Manager]] — the
  coordinator holds the run's state and decides each next step from the
  bundles' replies.
- [[id:D5F77B10-D498-4B56-ADF7-7E5CEFA44FA0][Database per Service]] — one
  owner per bundle, and a coordinator that reads only the manifest and
  never another owner's rows.
