#+title: ORE Studio computes FX forwards from published curves and stores none
#+description: The design decision: forwards are a pure function of one spot and two published funding curves, and what that costs.
#+type: knowledge
#+level: cross
#+filetags: :domain:finance:fx:fx_forward:architecture:
#+created: 2026-11-09
#+updated: 2026-11-09

* Summary

ORE Studio derives each FX forward from one spot and two published funding curves. It stores no forward and accepts no keyed points. The result is a parity forward, so it holds no cross-currency basis.

* Detail

** Why

A forward is a pure function of its inputs, so a stored copy can only go stale. The curves are already bootstrapped per currency and pass through a publish. Computing from them keeps one source of truth.

The calculation uses dates and discount factors, so different day-count conventions do not enter it. See [[id:9CDD445B-964A-5C7E-8E65-DB9967E99F55][FX forward calculations run on discount factors and not quoted rates]].

** What this costs

- A market forward holds the basis, and the BIS documents a persistent basis since 2007. A quoted forward and an ORE price under a cross-currency curve will differ.
- Points are outputs. See [[id:F1F326CE-5384-559E-AF4A-88DBBC4EC557][Swap points and interest rate differentials are two views of one number]].
- A computed forward has no end-of-day record. [[id:75B40B64-FF31-5381-922B-0EB1F521BC38][An end-of-day FX rate set must be complete and independently verified]] needs one, so that requirement will bring stored forwards.

** Scope

USD-hub pairs come first. A pair's spot days are the larger of its two currencies, as [[id:267DD5E8-F095-47C1-B42B-733565306776][FX spot date and settlement]] states.
