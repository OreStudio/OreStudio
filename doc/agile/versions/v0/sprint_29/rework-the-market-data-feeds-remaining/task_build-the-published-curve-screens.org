* Plan

Three units, each committed and verified on its own.

1. *The reads and the model.* The end facts go on the publish record: a
   withdrawal is a new version carrying the account, the instant, the reason and
   the request that authorised it, and a supersession links the successor's
   request and its as-of. The state is read off those facts and stored nowhere.
   Two new operations serve the screens: the tenant's published sets with the
   filters and the pager, and one series' last sets with the pillars each holds.
2. *The web.* The list at the market data area, the record page with Details,
   Published points, Compare as of and History, the routing and the words in
   three languages.
3. *The queue subject.* The BFF joins the market data component's own subject
   into the requests it holds, and the Requests row and the opened request draw
   it.

*The transitions, and why.* A withdrawal is an operation of its own
(=marketdata.v1.ops.withdraw_curve=): it names a withdrawal request, not a
publish request, so the publish gate's single-use rule is untouched. A
supersession is not an operation at all, because the journey states that a
replacement is a publish whose subject is the successor: the successor's own
apply ends the sets it replaces, in the same transaction, so a series never
holds two live sets.

*What is deliberately not here.* The zero-approval publish path, the publish
notice and the maker and checker pair on the kind are a later task. The
withdrawal's own raise, and the withdrawal kind's approval count, are the other
journey's screens: the operation records whether a request authorised the act and
enforces that a request ends one set, and it applies the act at once when no
request is named.

** What the demo data cannot show, and why

- *A move between publishes.* The observations are keyed by (series, as-of,
  pillar) and the demo holds one as-of, so every set of the curve reads the same
  two pillars. The overlay therefore draws identical curves, and a comparison of
  two sets on this data is the "nothing changed" state and never a changed,
  added or dropped pillar. A set whose pillars differ needs a second as-of, which
  needs a bootstrap run at that instant, which needs market quotes there. The
  three states are asserted in the reference suite instead.
- *The decision beside the versions for one person.* Reading a decision needs
  =inbox::approval_decisions:read=, which the seeded Market Risk decider role
  does not hold, and no account holds both that and
  =marketdata::curve_publishes:read=. The History tab states the decision it
  cannot read as a gap of the stream. The merge itself is asserted in the
  reference suite, and the two halves were verified live separately: the subject
  as =mindy.adams=, the decision as =graham.pierce=.

** The reads the screens needed that did not exist

The list, the last-N sets with their pillars, and the withdrawal. The comparison
is drawn from the sets, as the prototype argues, and needs no read of its own:
the snapshot read serves the market at the caller's clock, so a set built at an
earlier as-of travels with its points.

** Fixes the live run forced

The reads asked for =marketdata::curve_snapshots:read=, which a Market Risk
decider does not hold, so the decider could not read the subject of the request
it was deciding; they now ask for =marketdata::curve_publishes:read=. The BFF's
history allow-list did not name the publish record, so the History tab answered
"No history is served" even though the service registers the provider.
