#+title: System-level fixing observations and party-level fixings
#+description: A fixing observation is a system fact a source publishes; a party fixing is one party's adoption of it for its own trades.

* Summary

A fixing exists at two levels, and the two must not be conflated. A /fixing
observation/ is a system-level fact: the value a source publishes for one index
on one fixing date, stored once for the whole installation and visible to the
super administrator across every source. A /party fixing/ is one party's
adoption of that observation for its own trades: the party's fixings manager
records that its trades require it, its operations authorise or override it,
and its product services apply it to the trades and reverse it on undo. Only a
party that holds trades with fixings has party fixings, and authorisation,
overrides, status and application exist only at that level.

** Why the levels are separate

An observation is a public fact about a source. Two parties that trade on the
same index read the same value, and neither may change it for the other; a
correction is the publisher's act and reaches every party alike. This is why
ORE Studio stores fixings once under the system party; see
[[id:F4BBEF34-D84C-4AE6-A3AA-DC47C3EE06EE][Public publishers publish daily fixings through a supplier session]]
and [[id:3EF67CE4-6AF2-45FC-AA9B-D543550EF8C2][Generators belong to the system tenant and the system party]].

Authorisation is a control over a party's books. Each party's operations
decide, on their own schedule, that a value is fit to settle their trades; a
party may override a value it has agreed differently with a client, and that
override applies to its own trades only. Two parties can therefore hold one
observation as official and unofficial at the same moment. If authorisation
were a property of the observation, one party's control would decide another
party's settlements.

** What each level offers

The super administrator has a system view of the observations: every source, every
definition and every date, with each value's
publisher and versions. It is a view of facts and holds no authorisation; see
[[id:DC6F4D69-5E3E-4562-9503-88C74329FF1E][Show the FX and IR fixings on a fixings screen]].

A party with trades has a fixings manager: the fixings its live trades require,
their status, the published value beside the value the party uses, and the
actions of the fixing process; see
[[id:B5CA0D8F-D9C5-4F09-ABD7-7ED6F0FEF1A5][Fixing management and its controls]] and
[[id:CA61B445-B6DC-41D3-ACF9-D3FB32D0D342][The fixing process: observation, authorisation, application and reversal]].
A party with no trades that fix has nothing to manage, and its fixings manager
is empty rather than a view of the system's observations. The surfaces themselves, and which of them may act, are set out in
[[id:5D2A8C41-6E3B-47F0-9A28-1C4B7E9D3F62][The fixing screens and what each one is for]].

** Storage

The observation lives in the fixing series of its index under the system
party. The party fixing lives with the party: the observation it refers to,
its status, the value used, its origin and its history. Neither is written on
the trades; see
[[id:C6A619F3-B2B6-42D5-9C41-5269A84BC96C][Storage of fixing observations apart from trades]].
