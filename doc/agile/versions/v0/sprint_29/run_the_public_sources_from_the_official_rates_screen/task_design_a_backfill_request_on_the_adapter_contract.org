* Goal

The backfill window lives in the service's command line today: the adapter fetches the last --backfill-days for an item the first time it is demanded, then polls. A person-chosen range and a scheduled refresh are one request on the adapter contract, which carries announce, demand, tick and heartbeat and nothing else. This task designs that request: who may ask, how a range is stated, what the adapter answers while it fetches and when it is done, how the store's own duplicate rule keeps a repeated range free of duplicates, and which of the two windows wins when a range is asked for. It also decides whether the service's window becomes a default the request may narrow, or whether the service keeps its window and the request adds to it.

* Notes

The review of 2026-10-28 widened this task's premise. The platform's scheduler
runs the periodic refresh; the screen's Fetch tab sends the same request on
demand. One request serves both, so the design settles the scopes they share: a
day, a range, one publisher or all, one pair or every pair.


- The design states the request and response on the adapter contract, with who may send it and what happens when the adapter is not running.
- The design states the window rule: how a chosen range and the service's --backfill-days relate, and which one wins.
- The design states what a person sees while a backfill runs and when it ends, and that a repeated range adds nothing because the store skips what it holds.
- The design names the fetcher change and the protocol model change, or says why no model change is needed.
