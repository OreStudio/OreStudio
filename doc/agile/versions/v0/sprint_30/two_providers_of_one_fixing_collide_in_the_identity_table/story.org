* Goal

The series table keys a series by party, URI and provider, so one party may hold
the EURIBOR 6M fixing from Bloomberg and from LSEG. The identity table refuses
that: its unique index over a fixing's decomposed fields is per tenant and party
and does not hold the provider. The second series is stored, and its identity
write then fails on the duplicate key. A caller without a transaction keeps a
series that no identity reader can find.

Decide which of the two is the rule. Either the identity row carries the
provider, or one identity row serves every provider's series of that fixing and
the series table's key loses the provider for fixings.

* Decisions

- The identity follows the series key. The series table keys a series by party,
  URI and provider, so the identity row carries the provider and the fixing
  index holds it. The series key does not lose the provider for fixings.
- A check's column lists are data. The identity check's context columns live in
  the codec's row of =.compass.org=, which compass-ng and the Python gate read.

** Why

Found while making the test generator write real fixings. The repository case
=two_providers_keep_two_series_for_one_item_and_party= holds a Bloomberg and an
LSEG series of one item for one party. It passes only because its fixture writes an =oresmd://generic/= URI that no codec reads, so no
fixing identity row is written. With a fixing URI, the second write fails.
