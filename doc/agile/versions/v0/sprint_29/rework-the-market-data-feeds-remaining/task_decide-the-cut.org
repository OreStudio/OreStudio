* Plan

** The decision is the inbox's, and the cut adds nothing to it

The two cut kinds already name their deciding function: =reporting.eod_cut_authorisation=
names =inbox::approvals:decide_finance= and =reporting.ipv_cut_authorisation= names
=inbox::approvals:decide_market_risk=. The inbox's decide operation already resolves a
kind's permission, refuses a decision by the person who asked, requires a comment on a
refusal, and moves the request to =approved= or =refused=. Nothing in the decide path
changed, and the demo already holds the decider: the =Finance= role carries
=inbox::approvals:decide_finance=, and =rachel.byrd=, =beth.clark=, =graham.pierce= and
=hoi.yan.lee= hold it in the Acme tenant beside =henry.jones=, who takes the close. No
new seed was needed; the task's own supply test pins the kind's declarations.

** The apply is a reconciliation, as a role grant is

=ores_reporting_unapplied_cut_authorisations_fn= answers every decided cut-authorisation
request the cut has not been stamped with, across every tenant that is not terminated. It
keys on the cut kinds' own =approval_kind_code= and not on a list, so a third cut kind
needs no code change. =cut_authorisation_applier= writes each cut in its own tenant and as
the person who decided, stamps the decision's own instant rather than the run's clock, and
marks the detail row applied in the same unit of work. A row leaves the answer once applied,
so the second run writes nothing and the cut keeps its version.

The service runs it at start and on every change to an approval request; a sweep every 60
seconds reads the store for what no event announced.

** What the screens read

The BFF joins the subject a cut's request is about in one read of the detail rows, then
reads each cut the rows name, so the request page draws the label, the kind, the instant,
the scope and the counts. The cut's Authorisation tab states who stamped the decision and
when, from the detail row's applied facts, and the history shows the version the apply
wrote.

* Result

** Built

- =ores_reporting_unapplied_cut_authorisations_fn=, with its drop script and the registry's
  execute prefix, applied to the live database additively as one function.
- =cut_authorisation_applier= in =ores.reporting.core=: an approval stamps =approved_by= and
  =approved_at= and marks the detail applied; a refusal stamps =refused_at= and
  =refusal_comment=; the stamp is the decision's own instant and the row is skipped when it
  already holds it.
- The reporting service runs the reconciliation at start, on the approval request's change
  event, and every 60 seconds.
- The BFF's cut-subject join, the request page's subject block, and the cut record's applied
  facts.
- Three suites: the apply's five cases, the inbox's four cases on the two cut kinds, and the
  BFF's five cases on the join, plus the web's two on the applied facts.

** Verified

The function was applied to the live database and answers zero rows:

#+begin_example
  select request_id, cut_id, approval_kind_code, decision_code, decided_by, decided_at,
         left(comment,40)
  from ores_reporting_unapplied_cut_authorisations_fn();
  -- 0 rows
#+end_example

That is the right answer for the store as it stands, and it is also the reason the live walk
did not happen: every waiting =reporting.eod_cut_authorisation= request in the database,
including =01a1945d-8bbe-76c7-ae2f-9512e37bade1=, belongs to a tenant the integration suite
provisioned and then terminated (=ores_reporting_core_tests_20261031_234737_3_3676=). A
terminated tenant admits nobody's sign-in, and the function leaves its rows out, so that
request cannot be decided and could not be applied if it were.
