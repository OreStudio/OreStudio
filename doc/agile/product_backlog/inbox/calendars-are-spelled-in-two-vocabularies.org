#+title: Conventions spell calendars in a vocabulary ORE refuses
#+description: The convention seed holds refdata.calendars codes such as UnitedStates.Settlement, which ORE's parseCalendar refuses; ORE reads its own calendar names, such as US or US-SOFR, and the tree has no translation between the two vocabularies.

This page is a [[id:671F18E4-E09C-4B3B-BD24-D33DF8AE38A6][capture]] in the *inbox* bucket of the product backlog — a pre-sprint idea, not yet pulled into a sprint as a story.

* What

A convention row holds a calendar, and two vocabularies spell one: refdata.calendars
holds codes such as =UnitedStates.Settlement=, =UnitedStates.SOFR= and
=UnitedKingdom.Settlement=, while ORE reads its own calendar names, =US=,
=US-SOFR=, =TARGET=, and refuses everything else. The convention populate
scripts use the first vocabulary. ORE's =parseCalendar= refuses all three of
those dotted spellings, so a convention exported as it stands names a calendar
the engine cannot build.

* Why

The index conventions were mined from the engine and now carry ORE's calendar
names, which is what =ores_refdata_validate_calendar_fn= reads and the parser
accepts. The currency pair conventions were not:
=refdata_currency_pair_convention_calendars_populate.sql= and its seed twin
still hold =UnitedStates.Settlement= and =UnitedKingdom.Settlement=.

Two more files still hold the first vocabulary for the same reason:
the OreStudio rows of the refdata.conventions dataset
(=projects/ores.seeder/datasets/conventions/conventions.tsv=), whose seven
rows keep the synthetic feeds' families and their =refdata.calendars= codes
(=TARGET=, =Japan=), and =refdata_currency_pair_convention_calendars_seed_populate.sql=.

Nothing translates between the two vocabularies today. Either the seed moves
onto ORE's names, as the index conventions did, or the export translates, and
that choice is what this capture is for. The oracle is cheap: the
=code_catalogue= harness tool reads a code and prints the name ORE reports for
it, so the inverse table can be derived rather than written by hand.
