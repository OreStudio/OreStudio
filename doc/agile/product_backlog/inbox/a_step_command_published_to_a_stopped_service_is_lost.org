#+title: A step command published while its service is stopped is lost
#+description: The engine publishes a step command fire-and-forget to the subject its definition names. If the service that serves that subject is not subscribed at that instant, the command is gone: nothing retries it, nothing reports it, and the run waits for ever on a step that will never answer. Recovery re-dispatches an in-progress step, but only when the workflow service restarts, so the run needs an operator to notice and restart the engine. A step's command must be held until the far end acknowledges it, or a step whose handler has not answered within its budget must be re-dispatched.

This page is a [[id:671F18E4-E09C-4B3B-BD24-D33DF8AE38A6][capture]] in the *inbox* bucket of the product backlog — a pre-sprint idea, not yet pulled into a sprint as a story.

* What

Hold a step command until the far end has answered for it, or re-dispatch a
step whose handler has not answered within the step's budget. The engine
publishes a step command fire-and-forget to the subject its definition names.
A subject with no subscriber at that instant is a command that is gone:
nothing retries it, nothing reports it, and the run waits for ever on a step
that will never answer.

* Why

Found on 2026-09-28 while proving the retry verb on the live deployment. Two
services were stopped in turn to make a provisioning step fail, and neither
stopping produced the clean failure it was meant to:

- With the marketdata service stopped, a run's =provision_party= step
  dispatched the CRM-topology dataset to a service that was not there. The run
  rested in =in_progress= with that step =in_progress= for ten minutes, and
  the engine never re-dispatched it. What moved it was restarting the
  workflow service, whose recovery pass re-dispatches the in-progress steps of
  in-progress runs.
- With the assets service stopped, the base bundle's country-flags step was
  lost the same way, and the run stalled on its *first* step.

The recovery pass runs once, at service start. So a lost command leaves a run
that stops making progress with no error, no timeout and no step the progress
read can show as failed — the read shows a step that is still running. The
only actor who can end it is an operator who notices and restarts the engine.

The engine's own waiting contract already argues where a step's budget
belongs: a step that takes minutes is a step with a deadline, and a deadline
that expires is a failure the run records. A command acknowledged by its
handler, or a step re-dispatched when its deadline passes, closes this without
inventing a second recovery path — the retry verb re-dispatches a step under
its own identity, and a timeout could use the same machinery.
