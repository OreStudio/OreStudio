* Plan

Measure first, then read, then challenge the readings. The numbers come from
four sources: =git= for commits, =gh= for pull requests, a parse of the 159
story documents and their 900 task documents, and =compass sprint audit= for
state-sync violations. Nothing is taken from a status table that the documents
themselves can answer.

The two readings that most shape the verdict are the ones a status table
hides: how many stories are in flight, and how many of those are already
finished but still open. The first is a strategy failure; the second makes the
in-flight count wrong in both directions at once.

** Review on 2026-11-24 (day 17 of 7)

*** Goal alignment

The mission is "Finish moving off sqlgen and reflect-cpp, with a limit on the
stories in flight". It has two halves and they are not comparable: one is a
deliverable, the other is a constraint on how work is taken.

| Goal | Coverage | Stories | Verdict |
|------+----------+---------+---------|
| Finish moving off sqlgen and reflect-cpp | Direct | =Replace sqlgen and reflect-cpp with generated code= DONE with 61 of 63 tasks done and 2 abandoned; =Break the large compilation dependencies= DONE, 11 of 11; =Move codegen and the gates to compass-ng= STARTED, 19 of 21 | MET |
| Hold a limit on the stories in flight | None | 29 stories STARTED; no number is stated in the sprint, so no story can be tested against it | NOT MET |

The first half is delivered: the two dependencies the sprint named are gone,
and the compilation-coupling work that made the migration worth finishing is
closed with it. The second half never became a constraint. A limit that exists
only in a sentence cannot fail a story, stop a start, or be counted at close,
and the count it was meant to bound is 29, roughly ten times the 1 to 3 the
review skill treats as healthy.

Twelve stories started after the planned end date. Five of them are still
STARTED today. That is the sharper reading of "finish": the sprint did not
only overrun, it kept opening work while overrunning.

Challenge: is the met half enough to rescue the sprint? On its own it is worth
what the sprint claimed. It does not excuse a second goal that was adopted as
words and dropped as a mechanism, because the next sprint inherits the same
sentence.

*** What this review did not check

- Whether each of the 24 "task open, all its PRs merged" findings is really
  finished work. The PR named may be a partial step, and each needs its own
  reading; the review records the count and the shape, not a verdict per task.
- The quality of what merged. A fast queue and a high count say nothing about
  correctness; the local gate and the suites own that.
- Whether the sprint's mission was still the right mission on day 17. The
  version's goals are not restated here, and a review that rearranges the
  targets is a different document.
- The charts the sprint template expects. =compass sprint charts= writes them,
  and this review did not run it.

Sprint 30 is RED. It delivered the thing it was named for: the sqlgen and
reflect-cpp migration is gone, and the compilation coupling that blocked
further work is closed. It failed the constraint in its own mission, which was
never written as a number and so could not be enforced: 29 stories were in
flight on day 17, of which 10 were already finished. It ran 3.9 times its
commit ceiling, 2.3 times its duration and 4.9 times its PR band, while
keeping the fastest merge flow of any sprint reviewed so far. The most
important concern is not the volume; it is that this is the third consecutive
review to recommend a real work-in-progress limit, and the second sprint to
adopt that recommendation as a phrase in its mission rather than as a rule in
the tool. The one change that would most improve the next sprint is to make
the limit executable: refuse a story start that would exceed a stated number
of in-flight stories, and state that number in the sprint document where the
close can measure it.

Before the sprint closes, three mechanical steps follow from this review:
close the ten finished stories, return =Make the build faster= to the backlog,
and sync the sprint table to the documents. Each is cheap, and each inflates
what the closure reports until it is done.
