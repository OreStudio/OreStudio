This page defines the [[id:68FAC606-569C-4B9B-932B-3B3A0181AC8B][service architecture pattern]]
Bulkhead in the [[id:EA249899-6611-407F-A43A-B37019EBD1A7][pattern language]].
Resources are split into isolated pools, so one overloaded caller, tenant or
dependency cannot take all of them. In ORE Studio the reporting design gives
each tenant its own share of a step service, and compute routes work by platform.

* Also known as

- /Isolation pools/.
- /Partitioned capacity/.

* Context

A service shares threads, connections and workers between many callers and many
dependencies. One of them can become slow or demanding.

* Problem

How does a service stop one source of load from exhausting what every other
source needs?

* Forces

- Shared pools use capacity well until one user takes all of it.
- Fixed pools waste capacity when idle.
- A slow dependency holds its callers' resources while it waits.

* Solution

Divide the resources into pools and bound each one: per tenant, per dependency,
per platform. A call takes a slot from its own pool, and waits or is refused when
the pool is full. A slow dependency then holds only its own pool, and a busy
tenant only its own share.

* Failure handling

#+ATTR_HTML: :class hug-leading
| Concern | What the pattern does |
|---------+-----------------------|
| Errors | A full pool refuses or queues a call; the error names the pool. |
| Timeouts | A slot held past its timeout is released by the timeout. |
| Retries | A refused call may be retried later, within the caller's budget. |
| Idempotency | Does not apply: the pool decides capacity, not repetition. |
| Observability | Each pool's use and queue length are measured. |

* Pitfalls

- *Pools too small.* A tenant waits while capacity sits idle.
- *One pool for everything.* The bulkhead does not exist.
- *A wait that counts against a deadline.* A step queued for its pool can expire before it runs; size pools inside the budget.

* Security

Availability: one tenant cannot deny service to another by load.

* Resulting context

- Benefits :: One slow dependency or busy tenant cannot take down the rest.
- Liabilities :: Idle capacity in quiet pools, and limits to size and tune.
