* Goal

A person who schedules a report definition gives IAM their consent for its
runs, and unscheduling it withdraws that consent. The ReportRun role exists in
every tenant.

* Acceptance

- Scheduling creates a run grant on behalf of the person, before the job, and
  records its id on the definition; a refused grant schedules nothing.
- A definition the scheduler cannot take gets no grant: an invalid cron fails
  before the grant, and a refused job revokes the grant it was given.
- A session acting for another party than the definition's cannot schedule it.
- Unscheduling revokes the grant; a revoke the person may not make leaves the
  grant recorded and logs why.
- The =ReportRun= role holds the permissions the run's steps are checked for,
  and every tenant receives it.

* Plan

Of the six moments the design gives a grant, schedule and unschedule are the
ones reporting can act on now, because both already relay the person's token.
A manual run's grant, suspension on lapse, revoke on delete and re-grant on a
change of party each need the exchange or a person's token where reporting has
none; they move to the exchange task.

1. =run_grant_id= on the report definition, regenerated on every surface.
2. =schedule_one= asks IAM for the grant before it puts the job; =unschedule_one=
   revokes it after it deletes the job. Both call IAM with the relayed client.
3. The schedule handler refuses a session that acts for another party.
4. Seed =ReportRun= with the codes the run's steps check: the run setup, the
   curve configuration, conventions, pricing model and today's market documents,
   storage objects, and the compute batch and workunits.

* Notes

- The first live test scheduled /Intercompany Exposure Matrix/, whose cron is
  invalid. IAM made the grant, then the cron check failed, and the grant stayed
  active with no job behind it. =schedule_one= now checks the cron before it
  asks for the grant, and revokes the grant when the scheduler refuses the job.
- The cron =0 6 * * 1,15= in =reporting_report_definitions_populate.sql= puts
  15 in the day-of-week field, which takes 0 to 7. It most likely means days 1
  and 15 of the month, =0 6 1,15 * *=. That is a seed data defect outside this
  task.
- Reconciliation schedules every unscheduled definition at service start, as
  the service account, so those jobs have no grant. Under the run token design
  such a run cannot act in its party. The exchange task decides whether
  reconciliation only re-creates jobs for definitions that hold a grant.
