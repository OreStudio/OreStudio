* Result

The capacity sampler runs every fifteen minutes. One firing costs about eleven
milliseconds of database time, one statvfs and one insert, so the density is
bought for nothing: the shortest horizon on the screens now has four points an
hour instead of one, and a disk that fills between two firings is seen inside a
quarter of an hour.

Applied to this environment and observed: a row at 14:00:04 with 2634 MB held and
494 GB free, after the hourly 13:05 row. The seed carries the new cadence, so a
recreated database gets it, and the definition stays editable for a deployment
that wants another.

The first hours of the series already earned their keep: the database grew from
1.85 GB to 2.63 GB between 12:42 and 14:00 on this freshly provisioned
environment, with the top relations unchanged — so the growth is spread across
versioned rows the provisioning wrote, not one store. Nothing bounds most of
those tables yet, which is the catalogue's gap and its capture.
