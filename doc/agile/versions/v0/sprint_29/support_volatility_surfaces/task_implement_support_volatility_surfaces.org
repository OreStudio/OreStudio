Decisions agreed with the user on 2026-11-03:

- The MDN route is abandoned. A surface moves through its own parameter
  processes (ATM level, skew, curvature per tenor), with shocks correlated with
  spot but not driven by it.
- GARCH drives FX spot returns, not the surface (agreed 2026-11-03). Proposed:
  the GMM stays the shape of each shock and GJR-GARCH scales it by the day's
  volatility.
- A surface follows the IR curve approach: state processes, a template ladder
  of pillars, one quote tick per pillar on an oresmd series, a blueprint that
  builds the surface with the smile model the tenant chooses (SSVI, SABR, a
  spline or Vanna-Volga) and static no-arbitrage checks, then a
  candidate and a published set. Quotes are published, not model parameters.
- Agreed on 2026-11-03: a *derived source* of public data. It is a publisher
  of its own. Its session is an engine that reads the stored spot fixings and
  backs out plausible ATM, RR and BF values (realised volatility for ATM,
  skewness for RR, excess kurtosis for BF, through a Gram-Charlier smile, the
  higher moments shrunk to authored priors). It publishes them as ticks
  through the same adapter contract as any source, and provenance says they
  are derived. A real vendor, such as LSEG, is another provider of the same
  items, so replacing the derived source is a change of route or rank and
  nothing else, as in
  [[id:FA280E9B-7E00-4747-8D29-42B3D4258873][A source switch changes one route and nothing else]].
- Agreed on 2026-11-03: the surface is SSVI (or eSSVI). The state
  processes are its parameters (ATM total variance per tenor, skew, curvature),
  each held inside the no-arbitrage region, so every surface is free of static
  arbitrage by construction. The generator prices ATM, RR and BF quotes from
  the SSVI state. SSVI is the generator's law of motion only: it never leaves
  the simulator, and a tenant's blueprint fits whatever smile model it chooses.
- Agreed on 2026-11-03: an *observation layer* between the surface state and
  the quotes, inside the simulator adapter. The SSVI state is the latent "true
  market". Synthetic contributors observe it, each a provider of its own, and
  publish what a real broker feed carries: the pair's full delta ladder (5D,
  10D, 15D, 25D, 35D as the pair quotes them), mids rounded to the quote
  step, measurement noise that grows on the wings and at long tenors,
  stale and missing points, rare outliers, and a persistent bias per
  contributor. Noise touches only what is published, never the state, so it
  does not feed back into the dynamics. Each contributor has a noise profile;
  the profile =none= publishes the clean quotes, for tests, and a fixed seed
  makes a noisy run repeatable. Everything stays behind the adapter seam: the
  ingest, the store and the tenant's marking see contributor quotes on the same
  four messages as a vendor's. Because the truth is known, a tenant's marked
  surface can be measured against it, which no real feed allows. The truth needs
  nothing special: as for IR, where the clean pillar quotes and their bootstrap
  are the truth, the contributor whose profile is =none= is a provider like the
  others, and its quotes, constructed by the same build, are the true surface.
  A tenant sees it only if the system routes that provider to it. Every quote
  is a mid. Two-way pricing is a later level of work across the whole system,
  once mid valuation is reliable for every product (agreed 2026-11-03); the
  noise scale per point stands in for the width of the market until then. The
  observation layer is where two-way pricing will attach: a contributor will
  publish a bid and an ask around the mid it already publishes, so the mid
  path does not change.
- Agreed on 2026-11-03: *surface construction* follows curve construction
  exactly. A surface blueprint names its output series; its build fits the
  tenant's chosen smile model to the quotes, checks it, and writes the fitted
  vols on the blueprint's grid as observations of that series, each with a
  lineage record naming the source series and the blueprint version. That is
  the candidate, and the same four-eyes publish makes it a published set that
  reports bind. The blueprint is the series' only writer, and the series'
  identity says its points are inferred, as a discount series with a
  =curve_id= does. oresmd needs a new instrument type for that series, with a
  surface identity in place of =curve_id=. Naming: a curve's method is a
  /bootstrap/, a sequential solve pillar by pillar; a surface's method is a
  /calibration/, all points at once, the idiomatic market term (agreed with
  the user). Both are /construction/. Calibrating a smile model to quotes and
  calibrating a generator to public fixings are one meaning: fitting a model's
  parameters to data.
  The one true bootstrap among vol surfaces, stripping caplet vols from cap
  vols, keeps that name when cap/floor surfaces arrive.

** Checks of the analysis's claims (2026-11-03)

1. *FX implied vols on DBnomics, from the BIS or the ECB.* Not found. A
   DBnomics API search for "implied volatility" returns no dataset, and a
   search for "volatility" returns only the ECB's systemic stress indicator
   (CISS) and unrelated OECD data. No free source of ATM, RR or BF quotes was
   found; the daily FX vol surfaces on offer are commercial (LSEG, GFI,
   Exchange Data International). Consequence: the starting surface has no free
   public source. Its shape is authored, as the GMM components are.
   https://api.db.nomics.world/v22/search?q=volatility
2. *The MOVE index on FRED.* Not found on FRED. The ICE BofA series that FRED
   does carry state that reproduction in any form is prohibited without ICE
   Data's written permission, and from April 2026 keep only three years of
   observations, so they cannot be stored as fixings in any case. FRED carries
   VIX as =VIXCLS= (daily close, Cboe), marked "Copyrighted: Citation
   Required"; its storage terms are unread. VIX is an equity vol index and does
   not set an FX surface.
   https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/BAMLC0A0CMEY ,
   https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/VIXCLS
3. *The RR and BF formulas.* σ_call = σ_ATM + σ_BF + σ_RR/2 and
   σ_put = σ_ATM + σ_BF − σ_RR/2 hold only for the smile convention. The broker
   convention quotes a market strangle at one volatility and at strikes of its
   own, and turning it into call and put volatilities needs a numerical solve
   against a smile. Healy (2025), "Counterexamples for FX Options Interpolations
   - Part II", arXiv:2512.19625, eq. 1 and 5–12, citing Clark (2011) and
   Reiswich and Wystup (2012). The same paper notes that FX SABR usually fixes
   β = 1. FX Volatility Surface states the smile definition without the broker
   one, so that note is extended.
4. *"SABR and SVI keep the surface free of static arbitrage."* False in
   general. Gatheral and Jacquier (2014), "Arbitrage-free SVI volatility
   surfaces", arXiv:1204.0646: SVI smiles may be arbitrageable, a slice is free
   of butterfly arbitrage only if its density function g(k) ≥ 0, and a
   restricted class (SSVI) is free of static arbitrage in closed form. Hagan's
   SABR expansion can give a negative density at low strikes; arbitrage-free
   SABR (Hagan et al. 2014) addresses it. Consequence: the surface blueprint
   checks static arbitrage, as a curve blueprint checks its curve.
