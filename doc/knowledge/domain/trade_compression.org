#+title: Trade compression
#+description: Replacing a portfolio of offsetting trades with fewer trades of the same net risk: the kinds, the cycle, what it ends and creates, and why each run has an identity.
#+type: knowledge
#+level: cross
#+filetags: :knowledge:domain:finance:trading:compression:
#+created: 2026-10-02
#+updated: 2026-10-02

* Summary

*Compression* finds trades whose risks offset one another and replaces
them with fewer trades, or none, that carry the same net risk. The
position is unchanged; the gross notional and the number of trades
fall. That lowers operational cost, counterparty exposure and the
capital the gross figures drive.

A compression ends trades in full, reduces others, and sometimes
creates replacement trades to keep each participant's risk where it
was. Every one of those changes happens because of one run. The sets
of trades the run ends and creates are
[[id:2AF58405-4F3D-4B74-AAD0-6A97E7DA215F][trade groups]], each carrying a foreign key to the
run, and the run records when it ran, who ran it, and the provider's own
reference. Each member records the role the trade played.

Overall P&L is unchanged: compression realises value that the trades
already carried. But it is an economic event for each trade it
touches, so it is confirmed, settled and reported.

* Detail

** Kinds of compression

| Kind | Who takes part | Who proposes the result |
|------+----------------+-------------------------|
| Bilateral | Two counterparties | The two parties, comparing their portfolios |
| Multilateral | Many counterparties at once | A third-party service that runs compression cycles |
| Cleared | The members of a central counterparty, for their cleared trades | The central counterparty |
| Portfolio restructure (tear-up) | The firm and one counterparty, for trades on one value date | The firm's own process |

The first three differ in scale; the mechanics are the same. A
multilateral cycle finds offsets that no single pair of counterparties
can see, which is why most volume runs through multilateral services.

The /portfolio restructure/ is the firm's own form, described in the
analysis notes for FX. Where a set of deals with the same value date
nets to zero, or to an amount in one currency, the firm generates an
equal-and-opposite deal with today's value date for each
future-dated deal, and present-values any outstanding cash and pays it
out. The equal-and-opposite deals are called /tear-ups/.

"Tear-up" also names the cancellation of a trade with no net P&L
effect, which happens in migrations and book moves. The two uses
agree: a tear-up is a cancellation that leaves the firm's value where
it was.

** The cycle

A multilateral run, the most structured kind, proceeds in steps:

1. /Submission./ Each participant submits the trades it offers for
   compression, with its tolerances: how far its risk, its exposure to
   each counterparty and its cash flows may move.
2. /Proposal./ The provider computes a set of terminations, reductions
   and replacement trades that lowers gross notional while keeping
   every participant inside its tolerances.
3. /Acceptance./ Every participant reviews the proposal. It becomes
   binding only when all of them accept it; a single rejection voids
   the run.
4. /Execution./ The terminations and replacements take effect together
   at an agreed time, and each participant books them.

Only the last step changes trades. The earlier steps are the provider's
workflow, and a firm that records them records them against the run,
not against the trades.

** What a run does to a trade

| Role | What happens to the trade |
|------+---------------------------|
| Terminated | Ends in full. Its state moves to terminated, with compression as the cause. |
| Partially terminated | Stays live with a reduced notional. Its economics are amended, which cuts a version. |
| Replacement | A new trade, created by the run to keep a participant inside its tolerances. |

A partial termination is an amendment of the same trade, not a new
trade, and the run records the notional before and after.

The run therefore reads one set of trades and writes another, and the two
overlap. A terminated trade is in the read set only, a reduced trade is in
both, and a replacement is in the write set only.
[[id:D6CF158A-69D1-4C1A-8FF3-0F1F4836B16B][Trade group]] states why the two roles are not a
partition.

** Effects

The analysis notes record compression as one of the few activities
that reaches every downstream area, with a P&L effect of zero.

| Area | Effect |
|------+--------|
| P&L | Net zero. The run turns unrealised value into realised value; the P&L attribution notes file it under early termination, valued at the mark at the termination date. |
| Risk | Unchanged in total, within the tolerances. |
| Settlement | Termination payments settle. |
| Confirmation | Terminations and replacements are confirmed. |
| Credit | Exposure falls, which is the point. |
| Regulatory reporting | Terminations are reported as compression events, and replacement trades are reported as new trades with identifiers of their own. |

Compression is therefore a trade event in the sense of
[[id:2AAC92EB-2320-4385-999E-947E9E8FE5C5][trade links]]: it changes the trades it touches, cuts their
versions and may require the counterparty to agree again.

** Why a run has an identity

A run is an operation that attaches to a set of trades as a whole.
Under [[id:2AF58405-4F3D-4B74-AAD0-6A97E7DA215F][trade sets and trade groups]] the sets it reads and
writes are trade groups, and the run is the identity they hang from. In
practice the business refers to the run in its own right:

- /When and who./ The execution time, the person or process that
  accepted it, and any notes the desk attaches.
- /The provider's reference./ The cycle or run identifier the provider
  assigned, which is what the provider's statements and the
  counterparty's records quote.
- /What it did./ The trades it ended, reduced and created, so a report
  can answer "what did the March cycle do to this book".
- /Traceability./ A terminated trade's history must say why it ended,
  and a replacement trade's history must say where it came from. Both
  answer by naming the run.

Recording the run only as links between pairs of trades would lose all
four: a multilateral run is many-to-many, and no pair carries the run's
time, owner or reference.

** What follows for the model

This section is analysis rather than reported practice.

- /The run./ One row per run: its kind, its provider and the provider's
  reference, the execution time, who accepted it, and notes.
- /Participation./ One row per trade the run touched: the trade, its
  role, and for a partial termination the notional before and after.
  A trade takes part in a given run at most once; it may take part in
  several runs over its life.
- /The trades./ A terminated trade moves to its terminated state with
  compression as the activity, and the activity references the run. A
  replacement trade records the run as its origin.
- /Created together./ The run, its participation rows, the state
  changes, the amendments and the new trades are written in one
  transaction.
- /Undo./ An executed run is legally binding and is not undone. A run
  booked in error is reversed by a corrective act that references it.

** Open questions

- /Do we record proposals?/ Recording submitted and proposed runs
  supports reconciliation against the provider; recording only executed
  runs is enough for the trade history.
- /Tolerances./ Whether the firm stores its submitted tolerances with
  the run, or only the result.
- /Cross-entity runs./ The analysis notes describe compression as
  identifying offsetting positions across entities. Whether a run may
  span the firm's own legal entities, and how each entity's books see
  it, is not settled.
