This page documents a [[id:F44766FA-1E9C-416F-849A-E131101B2ECA][user journey]]: A trader builds or refreshes a candidate curve from the day's market, reads its points and its health, and leaves it unpublished.

A user journey describes what one person is trying to do, before anything is
built. It is a design document that outlives any sprint, so it names the
screens, the entity fields each screen shows, and the operations and messages
each step needs.

* Goal

"I want to turn today's market into a curve I can check before anyone
publishes it." The journey is done when the candidate's points answer every
pillar of the blueprint and the person can see which market points it read, and
nothing has been published.

* Actor and trigger

The actor is a trader who holds the market-data management role. The trigger is
the start of a trading day, a material market move, or a correction to the
curve's inputs. The journey starts at a blueprint and an as-of time, and ends
with a candidate curve the person has read and not published.

The candidate is a proposal, not an object. It is what the person reads and
tests before the publish journey asks a checker to approve it. Nothing the
person sees here reaches pricing: pricing reads only a published curve.

* Steps

1. *Find the blueprint.* The person opens *Market data*, then *Curves*, chooses
   a currency and selects a blueprint. The system shows the blueprint: its
   family role, its output series, its discount curve and its pillars in order.
2. *Choose the as-of.* The person names the snapshot time the build reads, with
   now as the default. The system shows what the market holds for the
   blueprint's pillars at that time: which points exist, their age and their
   source. It reads them by the as-of the person names. An observation may come
   from a market feed or from the synthetic generator; the build treats both as
   a point with a source, so a generated rate is marked generated rather than
   observed, and the same bootstrap serves either.
3. *Run the bootstrap.* The person chooses *Compute candidate*. The system calls
   =marketdata.v1.ops.compute_curve=, which bootstraps the config's
   pillars from the observations at that as-of and replies with the computed
   points: a point id, a date and a discount factor. Nothing is written.
4. *Read the points.* The system shows the computed pillars in short-to-long
   order with their discount factors, beside the market value each came from.
   The person checks the shape of the curve and the ends.
5. *Read the health.* The system shows a health panel: how many pillars the
   build computed, which input points were missing, which were stale, and
   whether the discount curve a projection needs was present. The server returns
   no diagnostics, so the screen derives all of this from the point list and the
   pillars it read.
6. *Refresh.* The person changes the as-of, or corrects an input and runs
   *Compute candidate* again. The compute is stateless, so a re-run replaces
   the points on the screen and writes nothing.
7. *Leave the candidate unpublished.* The person leaves the screen without
   pressing anything that writes. The candidate exists on the screen only; see
   /What is missing/.
8. *The publish act, in a later journey.* A publish is a named act with an
   owner and, where the desk configures it, an approval. The operation that
   exists today, =marketdata.v1.ops.republish_curve=, writes the
   bootstrap's observations and lineage straight into the output series and
   stamps it. It has no candidate step and no approval gate, so this journey
   does not offer it.

* Screens and wireframes

Three screens, one build. *Result* shows the observations the build read and
the pillars it solved; *Curves* shows the input rate, the zero rate, the
discount factor and the forward; *Checks* shows the reprice residual, the
convergence, and the missing or stale inputs. All three read the same build,
so the curve never differs between them — only the question the reader is
asking does. The blueprint and the as-of sit above all three.

the only curve the data can hold is the one a republish writes.

The two curve operations exist server-side: the handlers and the subjects are
in the tree. No caller sends them. The history request is the component's
generic one, =marketdata.v1.history.get=, with =entity_type=
=ores.marketdata.market_series= or =ores.marketdata.observation_lineage=; both
register a history provider. =market_observation= registers none, because it
carries no audit columns, so a point has no readable trail.

* What is missing

1. *Nothing calls the curve operations.* =marketdata.v1.ops.compute_curve=,
   =.republish=, =marketdata.v1.curve_snapshot.get= and =.buckets= have subjects
   and handlers, and no caller sends any of them. There is no web page, HTTP
   route or shell verb for a curve operation. The candidate is a page and a
   route, and a verb for the shell. Until then the whole journey runs only from
   the shell, and today no command reaches it.
2. *There is no candidate curve.* =compute= echoes its points in the reply and
   touches no table; =republish= writes the results straight to
   =market_observation=. Nothing sits between them: no candidate entity and no
   candidate state. So a candidate that is "not yet published" exists only on
   the screen, and the person cannot return to it, compare it, or hand it to a
   checker. The candidate is a candidate object, or a candidate state on the
   output series. Until then the screen holds the points for its own session and
   says so.
3. *=republish= publishes with no approval and no named publisher.* Its own
   model comment calls the review and approval gate "a separate, later layer on
   top of this always-auto-publishing mechanism". No approval kind covers a
   curve publish: the only concrete kind is =iam.role_grant=. Publishing is
   meant to be a named, attributable act; today the operation is anonymous and
   immediate. The candidate is a market-data approval kind, with how many
   approvals it needs, the group that decides and the comment it requires, and a
   publish that waits for it. Until then a screen must not offer =republish=.
4. *A candidate has no marker.* =market_series= carries
   =derivation_kind=, =derivation_config_id= and =derivation_config_version=,
   and a republish stamps them; before that the series reads =OBSERVED=, exactly
   like a series the market sent. Two series that differ only by being proposed
   or observed look the same. The candidate is a stamp at candidate creation,
   not at publish. Until then the screen labels the candidate itself.
5. *The build reports no health.* =compute= returns success, a message and the
   points. It does not say which pillar failed, which input was missing or
   stale, or where it interpolated across a gap. The candidate is a diagnostic
   per pillar in the response. Until then the screen derives the health panel
   from the points it received and the pillars it read, and guesses at the
   causes.
6. *The reads cannot name a time.* =curve-snapshot.get= is always now: it takes
   no as-of. =curve-snapshot.buckets= is fixed at five half-hour buckets ending
   now, unaligned to any observation time. So the person can name a time on the
   compute and cannot read that time back. The candidate is an as-of parameter
   and a range on the read. Until then the screen shows only the compute's
   reply and the series' latest state.
7. *The curve has no typed identity at the wire.* The build is keyed by
   =bootstrap_config_id= and the reads are keyed by the oresmd URI. Nothing
   names the curve itself. The candidate is a typed curve identity. Until then
   the screen joins the config to its output series by the id it read.
