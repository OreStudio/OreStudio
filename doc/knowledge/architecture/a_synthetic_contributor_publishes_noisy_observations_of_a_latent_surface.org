#+title: A synthetic contributor publishes noisy observations of a latent surface
#+description: Inside the simulator adapter a surface's state is a latent market that synthetic contributors observe, each a provider with its own noise, so a tenant meets a broker-like feed and the clean contributor is the truth tests compare against.
#+type: knowledge

* Summary

A synthetic volatility surface never leaves the simulator as itself. Its state is
a latent market, and synthetic contributors observe it: each contributor is a
provider of its own and publishes the pair's delta ladder with noise, gaps and a
bias of its own, as several brokers quoting one market do. A tenant therefore
marks its surface from contributor quotes, as a desk marks from broker quotes.
The noise touches only what is published, so the dynamics do not depend on it,
and a contributor whose noise profile is =none= publishes the clean quotes,
which are the truth a test compares a marked surface against. The whole layer is
behind the adapter seam.

* Detail

** The layer

| Part | What it is |
|------+------------|
| Latent market | The surface generator's state. No tick carries it. |
| Contributor | A provider of the simulator with vendor ids of its own, observing the latent market at its own update times |
| Noise profile | Per contributor: measurement noise per point, larger on the wings and at long expiries; rounding to the quote step; the chance a point is stale or missing; outliers; a persistent bias |
| Clean profile | =none=: the quotes as the latent market gives them |
| Seed | Fixed per run, so a noisy run repeats exactly |

The generator prices the clean quotes from its state once a step, as
[[id:BB2B7C4C-FD4E-4DCA-BF1D-00F929622B87][Synthetic market data generation: approach]]
describes, and each contributor turns them into its own. An observation is
computed and published, and nothing of it returns to the state.

** The generator

A surface generator joins the simulator by the edits of
[[id:B7A03C80-6566-4E97-9D45-14A69726C381][Adding an asset class]]: a codegen
entity model for its config, a builder registered under the kind
="fx_vol_surface"= in the generator factory, a permission and its grant, and
catalogue rows for each contributor's vendor ids. The runner, the subject
builders, the adapter contract and the ingest loop need no edit
([[id:D67C930B-261F-480B-B5EF-020C6D617333][The generator factory and the kind seam]]).

Its configuration is four tables, each holding typed facts and nothing derived:

| Table | One row per | Holds |
|-------+-------------+-------|
| =fx_vol_surface_feed= | pair | the tick rate, the skew and curvature drivers' reversion and volatility, and three correlations |
| =fx_vol_surface_feed_expiry= | feed and quoted expiry | the tenor, and the reversion and volatility of the forward variance of the segment ending there |
| =fx_vol_contributor= | contributor | the provider it publishes as, and its noise profile |
| =fx_vol_surface_feed_contributor= | feed and contributor | which contributors quote which pair |

The expiries are ordered by length, so no row stores a position. A contributor's
noise is the contributor's, whatever pair it quotes, so its profile is held once.
The correlation of the shocks is three numbers: spot with every forward variance,
spot with the skew, and the correlation of neighbouring forward variances, which
falls by that factor for each segment between two of them. The feed builds the
matrix from them and refuses one that is not a correlation, so no stored matrix
can drift out of shape. The points a feed quotes are its expiries by the 25 and
10 delta wings, so they are not stored either. Its starting quotes come by rank
from a real vol source or from
[[id:099697A6-8526-48E0-A63D-DB3932C06468][the derived source]].

** Why the layer exists

A curve's quotes are its pillars, and the IR curve producer publishes them
directly. A surface's quotes are not the surface: in a real market several
contributors quote a fuller ladder than any one model needs, at different times,
with points missing and numbers that disagree, and a desk constructs its own
surface from them. A generator that published its clean surface would test none
of that construction. The layer gives a tenant the inputs a desk receives and
leaves the construction to the tenant's surface blueprint, as
[[id:53D14F31-EE32-493E-BBEB-140AA4906BE4][Constructing a volatility surface: from quotes to a published set]]
describes.

** Every quote is a mid

Valuation runs on mids, so a contributor publishes a mid for every point. The
noise scale per point stands in for the width of the market. Two-way prices, a
bid and an ask around the mid, are an extension of the contributor and leave the
mid path unchanged.

** The truth is a contributor

The clean contributor is a provider like the others, routed like the others. Its
quotes, constructed by the same calibration as any tenant's, give the true surface, as
the clean pillar quotes and their bootstrap give the true curve. A tenant sees
that provider only if the system routes it to the tenant. Because the truth is
known, the distance between a tenant's marked surface and the true one can be
measured, which no real feed allows.

** Behind the seam

The layer is inside the simulator adapter, which
[[id:69287DA4-3558-4EAE-972B-82BC7F9296E9][stops at the adapter seam]]:
contributors publish through the four messages of
[[id:5DFA094B-4F9F-4190-8251-8D4D0B77A5C7][An adapter and the service meet at four messages]],
and their quotes are stored as series keyed by item, provider and party. Past
the seam a contributor's quote is a vendor's quote, as
[[id:F64DB34E-084D-4437-B536-392AA26D8B77][No code path may behave differently because a source is simulated]]
requires, so a real vendor replaces a contributor by a change of route.
