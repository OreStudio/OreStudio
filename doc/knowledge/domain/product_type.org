#+title: Product Type
#+description: What a product type is, the three classifications it carries, the markets it references and how the pricing engine types join it.

#+filetags: :trading:refdata:taxonomy:product_type:

* Summary

A *product type* is one kind of instrument a desk can book: a single row in the
catalogue, named by the code ORE uses, such as =BondOption= or =CrossCurrencySwap=.
It is the unit the whole system names when it classifies a trade, prices it,
allows a book to hold it, or reports on it.

A product type carries three independent classifications — the *markets* it
references, the *product family* that names its contract shape, and its
*construction method* — and it declares the instrument table that holds a trade
of its kind. It is not a risk taxonomy: the risk classes of a product are a
different set, and a larger one, because discounting reaches every product.

* Detail

** The catalogue

=ores_trading_product_types_tbl= holds one row per kind. The row states the code,
the name a desk uses, a description of the product, the source that description
came from, the family, the construction, whether a trade of the kind carries an
option block or an extension block, the instrument table that holds it, its
display order and the knowledge note that describes it.

The catalogue is declared once, in codegen, and seeded into the system tenant.
Every tenant reads those rows. A tenant does not hold a product type of its own,
and no other table states a product type: this one is the golden source.

A type with no instrument table routes nowhere, so nothing can hold a trade of
it. That is a fact to state, not a gap to hide.

** The markets a product type references

The markets are a set, not a column, because a kind may reference more than one.
A =CrossCurrencySwap= references interest rates and foreign exchange; an
=InflationSwap= references inflation and interest rates; a =ConvertibleBond=
references bonds and equity.

The junction =ores_trading_product_type_asset_classes_tbl= holds the set. One
row is marked as the *home market*: the market the catalogue groups the kind
under, and the answer to "what is this product". The rest are the markets it also
references.

Two invariants hold:

- A product type has *exactly one* home market when it has any rows at all, and
  *no rows* when the markets are decided per trade. A =CompositeTrade= is a
  container whose markets come from its legs, and a =ScriptedTrade= states its
  markets in its script, so neither carries rows.
- A type with rows but no home is not representable. The seed writes the home row
  with the secondaries, and a check refuses a type that has rows and no home.

The values are the codes of [[id:4043DE4C-5CDB-455E-B208-5AFF69AA76AE][ores.refdata.asset_class_catalogue]], which is the
one taxonomy to reference. The external schemes — FpML's, ORE's, ISDA's — stay
registered as schemes and are mapped to it, never adopted in its place.

** The product family

The family names the shape of the contract, independent of the market it
references: Option, Swap, Forward, Position, Swaption, Future, Repo, Security.
It exists so that every option can be seen across markets, which neither the
market nor the product type permits on its own.

A kind whose shape is unsettled carries no family, and the page draws the gap.
[[id:936ACDC4-830B-434E-82E7-A92276869869][Product Family]] holds the analysis behind the values.

** The construction method

Construction says how the payoff is expressed: =native= for a product the engine
implements directly, =scripted= for one whose payoff is a script, and =container=
for one whose legs determine it. It is independent of the market and of the
family, and it is the attribute that the older eight-value =product_type_t= enum
was trying to express when it also carried markets and a shape.

** How the pricing engine types join

The pricing configuration names its own products — 108 of them, in
=ores_analytics_pricing_engine_types_tbl= — and they are not a second product
catalogue. Each either names the product type it prices, is finer than one, or is
a building block with no product at all:

- *A product type's own code.* The pricing product is the product.
- *Finer than a product type.* =EuropeanSwaption=, =BermudanSwaption= and
  =AmericanSwaption= all price =Swaption=; the exercise style is a pricing
  decision, not a different instrument.
- *A building block.* A capped leg or a CMS index is a part of a product, not one.
- *A product the catalogue lacks.* The pricing configuration names a few products
  the catalogue has no code for, and the gap is stated rather than papered over.

The join is a column on the pricing engine type, because no pricing product names
more than one product type. The column is nullable and a =kind= of =product= or
=block= says whether a row without a column value is a gap or a building block.

The instrument description belongs to the product type. A pricing product that is
its own code carries no prose of its own; one that is finer keeps a sentence
saying how it differs; a building block keeps the prose that describes the part.

** What the product type is not

- It is not the risk class. A product's risk classes are the asset classes its
  value is sensitive to, and discounting makes that set nearly always include
  rates. [[id:327EFD17-5708-4A54-AF4B-52CF60E87C01][Risk Classes]] holds that relation, and it is a different one.
- It is not the desk-facing product. A desk treats several configurations of one
  =Swap= as several products, which is a finer classification many-to-one onto
  this one, and a separate subject.
- It is not a construction method, a family or a market. Those are the three
  attributes the row carries.
