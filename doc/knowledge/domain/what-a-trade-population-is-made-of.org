#+title: What a trade population is made of
#+description: The model behind the synthetic trade population: what the generator owns, what the domain owns, and why a per-family "terms" bag is the wrong seam.
#+filetags: :knowledge:domain:synthetic:

* The question

The synthetic population carries its generated values in a per-asset-class bag:
=trade_terms= is a variant of =bond_terms=, =commodity_terms=, =rate_terms=,
=fx_terms=, =equity_terms= and =credit_terms=. A planner fills the bag and a
writer reads it and constructs the trading domain's rows from it.

That shape produced eight database refusals in one family, each found by a
provisioning run. This note asks what the bag is, and what the model should be.

* What the bag actually holds

Not one thing. Three different kinds of thing are mixed in it:

1. *The generator's own facts.* Which book, which counterparty, which trader,
   the trade date, the netting set, the trade identifier. These are facts about
   the act of generating, and they belong to the generator.
2. *Drawing parameters.* The seed, the tenor band, the notional band, the
   catalogue the underlying is drawn from. These are the =how= of generation.
3. *The product's economic content.* A strike, an expiry, a barrier type, a
   basket of names. These are facts about a trade, which exist whether or not a
   generator produced them.

Only the first two are the generator's language. The third is the trading
domain's, and the bag is a second, weaker description of it.

* Why the bag is not a boundary

An anti-corruption layer translates once, at a boundary, in one direction,
against a contract that a test can hold still. The bag is the opposite:

- Both sides interpret it independently. The planner decides what a field means
  and the writer decides again.
- It is per asset class, not per product, so it states the *union* of what nine
  tables hold. An =equity_terms= can carry a barrier, a variance strike and a
  cliquet frequency at once, and no product does.
- The product's identity is a string beside it. The writer branches on that
  string to decide what to read, so nothing in the type system connects a
  product to the fields it is allowed to have.

The consequence is not stylistic. A target on an accumulator, an option type on
a touch, a forward with no quantity and a swap returning =PriceReturn= were all
representable, all written, and all refused by the database, one run at a time.
Had the planner constructed the domain's own types, most of them would have
been compile errors.

* What the domain says a trade is

The trading model already answers this. A trade is a =trade= row — party, book,
counterparty, trade date, netting set — and an =instrument_batch= carrying the
product's rows: a header per family and the product's facts in the table the
routing table names. The batch is the aggregate the repository writes in one
transaction, which is why the routing table exists at all.

So the bag's third kind of thing has a home. It is the instrument batch.

* The defect underneath

The trading model states a product's identity as data: a =trade_type_code=
column plus check constraints that say which columns are legal for which code.
=equity_digital_option_instruments_tbl= serves two products and its check
decides which columns each may fill; =equity_option_instruments_tbl= serves
three.

That is a storage normalisation, and it is a reasonable one: two products whose
facts coincide share a table. But it means the *type* of a product is only ever
half in the type system and half in SQL. A generator that wants to be correct
has to satisfy invariants it cannot see.

* The model this implies

Three parts, with different owners:

| Part | Owner | What it is |
|------+-------+------------|
| =trade_generation_config= | generator | A specification: asset class, book, bands, seed, counts. |
| =population_inputs= (today's scope) | generator | The resolved drawing context: party, books, counterparties, catalogues, as-of date, seed. |
| The population itself | domain | A list of trades, each a trade envelope and its instrument batch. |

The planner's job becomes: draw the product, then *construct the domain rows*.
The writer's job becomes: persist them. There is no translation left, and no
second description of a trade.

The generator keeps the language it is entitled to — specification and drawing —
and stops describing products. A product description exists once, in the domain.

* What makes the couplings enforceable

Constructing the domain rows directly is necessary and not sufficient: the
domain's rows are table-shaped, so a coded product still has to be assembled
correctly. The couplings belong in named constructors beside the types —
=equity_option_instrument make_equity_cliquet_option(...)= — one per product,
each taking the product's own facts and filling exactly the columns that
product states.

Then the rule becomes: a product is constructed, never assembled. The SQL check
stays as the backstop it should always have been, and a new family cannot add a
product without writing the constructor that says what that product is.

* What this costs and what it buys

Cost: the per-family planners and the writer are rewritten, and a constructor
per routed product appears. That is real work, and it is bounded: the routing
table already enumerates the products.

Buys: one description of a trade instead of two; illegal states unrepresentable
where the compiler can see them; the writer reduced to persistence; and the
machinery that the next tenant needs, since Northwind will book through the same
planners and a bag with Northwind-specific quirks would be a second bag.

* Order of work

1. Introduce the product constructors beside the domain types, one per routed
   product, with the couplings the checks state.
2. Change the plan to carry a trade envelope and an instrument batch.
3. Move each family's planner onto the constructors, one family at a time, and
   delete that family's =*_terms=.
4. Reduce the writer to a persister.
5. Keep =trade_generation_config= and the scope as they are; they are the
   generator's own language.
