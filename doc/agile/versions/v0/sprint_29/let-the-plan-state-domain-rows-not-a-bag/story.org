#+title: Story: Let the plan state domain rows, not a bag
#+description: The trade population describes each trade twice: once in a per-asset-class *_terms bag and once in the domain rows the writer builds from it. The bag mixes the generator's drawing parameters with the product's economic content, so a product can be assembled from whichever fields happen to be present and eight SQL refusals police the difference. Let the plan carry the domain rows, construct products by name so an illegal combination cannot be stated, and reduce the writer to a persister.

* Goal

A trade has one description, in the domain. The planner draws a product and constructs the domain's own rows; the writer persists them. The per-asset-class =*_terms= bag disappears, and a product can no longer be assembled from whichever fields happen to be present.

- No =*_terms= bag remains, and the plan states the domain's own rows.
- Every routed product is constructed by a named constructor that states what that product is, and that constructor cannot fill a column the product does not have.
- The writer persists and no longer translates.
- The families that book today still book, proved by a provisioning run and not by a unit test alone.

* Decisions

- *Constructors beside the types, not in the synthetic component.* The
  couplings are facts about the products, so they live with the products. The
  synthetic component then depends on the trading domain the way any producer
  does, which it already does through the writer.
- *One family at a time.* Each family's move deletes that family's bag and
  proves itself with a run, so there is never a state where nothing books.
- *The config and the scope stay.* They are the generator's own language and
  they do not describe trades.

* Out of scope

- The trading model's own normalisation, where several products share a table
  and a check constraint says which columns each may fill. That stays as the
  backstop; this story stops a generator from having to guess at it.

* Why

The synthetic population carries each trade's values in a per-asset-class bag.
=trade_terms= is a =std::variant= of =bond_terms=, =commodity_terms=,
=rate_terms=, =fx_terms=, =equity_terms= and now =credit_terms=, and
=equity_terms= alone is about forty members. A planner fills the bag and a
writer reads it and constructs the trading domain's rows from it.

The cost is measured. In one family the bag let eight values through that the
schema refuses, and each one was found by a provisioning run: a target on an
accumulator, an option type on a touch, a forward with no quantity, a swap
returning a value nobody defined, a kind that named a classification rather than
a vocabulary. None of them was visible by reading the schema, because the
invariant is not in the schema a reader reads; it is in the check constraints.

* What the bag is

Three kinds of thing mixed together:

| Kind | Examples | Owner |
|------+----------+-------|
| The generator's own facts | book, counterparty, trader, trade date, netting set, trade id | the generator |
| Drawing parameters | seed, tenor band, notional band, which catalogue | the generator |
| The product's economic content | strike, expiry, barrier, basket | the domain |

Only the first two are the generator's language. The third belongs to the
domain, and the bag is a second, weaker description of it.

It is not an anti-corruption layer: an ACL translates once, at a boundary, in
one direction, against a contract a test can hold still. This bag is interpreted
independently by both ends, is per asset class rather than per product, and
leaves the product's identity as a string that the writer branches on.

* The defect underneath

The trading model states a product's identity as data: a =trade_type_code=
column plus check constraints naming the columns each code may fill. That is a
legitimate storage normalisation -- two products whose facts coincide share a
table -- but it puts half of a product's type in SQL, where a generator cannot
see it.

So this story is not only about the bag. It is about giving a product one
description, in the domain, and a way to construct it that cannot state an
illegal combination.

* The model

| Part | Owner | What it is |
|------+-------+------------|
| =trade_generation_config= | generator | A specification: asset class, book, bands, seed, counts. |
| the scope | generator | The resolved drawing context: party, books, counterparties, catalogues, as-of date, seed. |
| the population | domain | A list of trades: a trade envelope and its instrument batch. |

The planner draws the product and constructs the domain rows; the writer
persists them. The generator keeps specification and drawing and stops
describing products.

The couplings move into named constructors beside the types, one per routed
product, each taking that product's own facts and filling exactly the columns it
states. The rule becomes: *a product is constructed, never assembled*. The SQL
check stays as the backstop it should always have been.

* The flip: what it changes, and the order

Every family now constructs its products, but the population still carries each
trade as a bag of drawn fields and the writer still translates one. The last
step removes both: a slot states the domain rows, and the writer persists them.

**1. The slot carries an instrument batch.** =trade_population_slot.terms= is a
=std::variant= of eight bags; it becomes one =instrument_batch=, the shape the
booking request already takes. The envelope the slot already carries -- book,
counterparty, trader, trade date, structure -- is untouched, because it belongs
to the trade and not to the product.

**2. Construction moves into the planner.** A planner already resolves the
scope; it will call the constructors the last four tasks produced and put the
rows in the batch. A planner that cannot construct a product will fail where the
drawing happens, with the product named, instead of failing in the writer after
the trade has been half-built.

**3. The writer stops translating.** Its per-family arms go. It stamps the audit
columns on every row in the batch, builds the booking request's anchor and
booking from the envelope, and sends. Stamping becomes one walk over the batch
rather than six hand-written ones.

**4. The bag dies last, and only when empty.** =trade_terms= and the eight
=*_terms= structs are deleted when the last family stops filling them. Until
then they stay and one family uses each, which is what makes the move safe: a
family that has flipped cannot regress, and a family that has not keeps booking.

* Order of families

Bond, commodity, rates, fx, equity, credit. Smallest first, and bond first
because it draws one product, so the seam is proved where there is least to
change. Each family flips in its own task with its own provisioning run, and the
population it books must match what it booked before the flip: the run is the
proof, and a changed count is a defect in the move rather than a decision.

* What must not change

- The rows the population writes. A flip is a move, not a redesign; the eight
  families book the same trades afterwards.
- The envelope. Book, counterparty, trader and dates stay the generator's.
- The config and the scope. They are the generator's own language.

* What this does not fix

Products the planner does not draw. Bonds draw one of ten, and their fact rows
are empty; credit draws five of six. Those are population decisions recorded as
their own tasks, and the flip does not hide them: a family that draws one
product still books one product.
