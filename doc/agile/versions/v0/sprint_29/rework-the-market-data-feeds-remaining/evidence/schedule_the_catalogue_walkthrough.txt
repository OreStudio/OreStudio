================================================================================
6. A second schedule attempt creates nothing
================================================================================

The provisioner refuses a tenant code that exists ("the tenant code and hostname
are free" is the first check), so a second provision of the same tenant is not a
path the product offers yet; the backlog holds it as "a provisioning run cannot
be resumed". The re-provision path was therefore exercised at the two layers a
re-provision would reach, in order.

(a) The step's selection, as a unit test: a definition that already holds a
    scheduler job is asked for nothing. See
    projects/ores.iam/core/tests/provision_schedule_reports_tests.cpp,
    "a second provision asks for nothing the first already scheduled".

(b) The operation the step calls, live, re-issued for three definitions that
    already hold jobs, as the tenant's own administrator:

        ./compass.sh shell --no-login -l <script>
        > login tenant_admin@acme_corporation Secure-Password-123
        ✓ Login successful!
          User: tenant_admin
          Tenant: Acme Corporation (482df22b-ee2c-4482-b741-ea937154a2f7)
          Party: Acme Corporation Plc
        > report_operations schedule-report-definitions 083454f1-...,5c6e5ffd-...,1bdff2f3-...
        {"success":true,"message":"","scheduled_count":0,"failed_ids":[]}

    and the counts are the same afterwards:

         jobs | grants | scheduled_defs
        ------+--------+----------------
           92 |     92 |             92

(c) The party check the operation applies, seen from the same session when it
    names another party's definition:

        {"success":false,"message":"Act for the definition's party to schedule it: its runs act
         in that party, with your consent.","scheduled_count":0,
         "failed_ids":[ three ids ]}

    which is why the step mints one client per party rather than acting as the
    administrator's own party for all four.
