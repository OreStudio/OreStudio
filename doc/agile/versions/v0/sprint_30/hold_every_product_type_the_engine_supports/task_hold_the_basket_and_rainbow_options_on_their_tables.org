* Goal

The fixed elements of these products are held on tables of their own, with
their terms as columns, beside the generic ScriptedTrade form the scripted
table holds.

* Acceptance

- each of the four types routes to a table of its own (one table may serve an equity
  and an fx pair that state the same element), whose columns hold every scalar term the
  element states; lists, baskets and schedules are rows of child tables or of
  the shared rows (options, barriers, underlyings, schedules)
- the projection reads and writes the element from those rows, value for value,
  and a round-trip test reads ORE's or the user guide's example and writes it back
- the builder states the rows, and the generator books the element form
- the class-read entry for the type is removed: the scripted instrument table
  holds no element form of it, only the generic ScriptedTrade form where ORE's
  library carries one
- the catalogue names both forms of the type where ORE carries both
- the ORE engine test reads and builds each, with no error of the trade kind
- every fact the element states is a column or a row: nothing is held as text

* Plan

ORE states the four elements in one shape: =Currency=, =Notional=, =Strike=
(optional on a basket, required on a rainbow), =Underlyings=, =OptionData=,
=Settlement=, and on a basket the optional =ObservationDates=. The commodity
family already holds the same shape natively.

- Two tables, one per family, each serving the basket and the rainbow of that
  family: =equity_basket_option_instrument= (EquityBasketOption,
  EquityRainbowOption) and =fx_basket_option_instrument= (FxBasketOption,
  FxRainbowOption). The columns are the element's scalars: =currency=,
  =notional=, =strike= and =settlement_date=. A check requires the strike on a
  rainbow.
- The lists are shared rows, read and written by the families' generic tables:
  the =OptionData= block and its exercise dates are the instrument option and
  schedule rows; the members are instrument underlying rows under the role
  =underlying=, each with its weight and currency; the =ObservationDates= are
  a schedule row under the role =observation_schedule=.
- The builder keeps =basket_option_builder<Product>= and its terms, which move
  to a header of their own because the basket variance swap shares the member;
  it now returns the family's result instead of the scripted rows.
- The class-read entries leave the scripted projection. The catalogue routes
  the four codes to the new tables and names their scripted forms:
  =VanillaBasketOption= for a basket and =MaxRainbowOption= for a rainbow,
  the payoffs the generator books.

* Notes

The type was first held on the scripted instrument table through the
class-read path, as parameter rows. That held every value but gave the element
no table of its own, against the story's rule that a product ORE carries in
both forms is held in both, and that the forms can be compared.
