#+title: Roll the market day at the close
#+description: Nothing moves the system from one market day to the next: curves, forwards and surfaces are built once in provisioning, the derived FX vol source refreshes on an hourly timer, and the synthetic feeds read their fixings once at start.
#+type: capture
#+level: cross
#+filetags: :marketdata:synthetic:cut:eod:
#+created: 2026-11-12
#+updated: 2026-11-12

This page is a [[id:671F18E4-E09C-4B3B-BD24-D33DF8AE38A6][capture]] in the *inbox* bucket of the product backlog — a pre-sprint idea, not yet pulled into a sprint as a story.

* What

Give the system one market-day roll: at a stated close, the market moves from
one business day to the next as a single, ordered, visible event. The roll
closes the day, rebuilds what depends on the new date in dependency order, and
restarts the synthetic feeds on the new day's fixings. Today "today" moves in
several places on separate timers, and nothing moves the market data together.

The roll is the mechanism behind the cut model the knowledge base already
states. [[id:33E879B7-496A-406D-B3F2-72637379AD02][Kinds of cut]] defines the end
of day cut, the complete market body fixed at the rates centre's close, and the
start of day cut, which opens the next day by carrying that body forward. No
document says how the system gets from one to the other, and no code does it.

** Proposed shape

1. *Close the day.* A configured close per rates centre; for a start, one
   system-wide close such as 17:00 UTC. At the close the market clock moves to
   the new as-of date and announces it once, on one subject, with the old and
   the new date. Every consumer that today computes "today" for itself reads
   this instead.
2. *Roll in dependency order.* The same order provisioning uses, each step
   reading at the new as-of: public fixings, then the derived FX vol quotes,
   then each party's curves, then its FX forwards, then its surfaces. The roll
   is a workflow, so a step that fails is named, and a rerun repeats only the
   missing work, as the provisioning steps already do.
3. *Restart the feeds.* Each synthetic FX spot feed and IR curve feed of price
   source =fixing= starts again from the newest fixing, so its start record
   names the new day's rate.
4. *Take the cut.* The roll records the end of day cut of the closing day, so a
   report can select it, and the start of day cut that opens the next.
5. *Make it visible.* The screen shows the last completed as-of, when the roll
   ran, each step's outcome, and any party or item it left out.

* Why

On 2026-11-12 a fresh Acme provisioning failed twice in a row at
=publish_party_surfaces=. All 40 surfaces of the four Acme parties were refused,
because no pillar had a quote. The cause, traced with the bright_faraday
session, was the end of day:

- The derived FX vol source stamps each quote at 17:00 UTC of its day
  (=derived_fx_vol.cpp=, =sys_days{day} + hours(17)=). Once the ECB fixings of
  today were stored in the afternoon, the latest day was today, so the quotes
  sent at 15:46 and 15:47 UTC were dated 17:00, after their send.
- =publish_party_surfaces= calibrates at its as-of and reads the quotes at or
  before it, so at 15:53 it saw none. The same run before the day's fixings, or
  after 17:00, passes: a run at about 04:40 UTC published all 40 surfaces.
- The stamp fix on =feature/calibrate-synthetic-ir-feeds-from-public-rates=
  dates a live quote no later than its send. That removes the symptom, not the
  gap: the 17:00 stamp is an unnamed stand-in for a close nothing else knows.

The same session found the wider gap. Each part of the market keeps its own
"today":

| Part | When it moves to a new day | Evidence |
|------+----------------------------+----------|
| Public fixings | each official adapter polls every 60 minutes and stores what is new | =poll_minutes= 60 in the seeded sessions |
| Derived FX vol quotes | the source refreshes on a fixed one-hour timer, stamping 17:00 UTC | =refresh_interval{3600000}= in =derived_fx_vol_adapter.hpp= |
| Party curves | built once by =publish_party_curves= in provisioning; the curve republish service runs only when asked | =curve_republish_service.hpp=: on demand, from a request, the shell or a scheduler job |
| FX forwards and surfaces | built once by =publish_party_forwards= and =publish_party_surfaces= in provisioning | the provisioning profile |
| Synthetic FX and IR feeds | read their fixings once, when the feed starts | =resolve_fx_start=, =resolve_ir_start=; a running feed never sees a later fixing |
| Scheduler | one job definition, none of them a market-day roll | =scheduler_populate.sql= |

Three effects follow, all seen this session:

- *Feeds start on stale or no fixings.* On a fresh environment the synthetic
  feeds start before any fixing is stored, so every FX feed and every IR curve
  keeps its fixed level. After a restart of the synthetic service, the 13
  currencies with a publisher started from their fixings (USD 0.0389 from SOFR,
  EUR 0.02439 from €STR, SGD 0.015992 from SORA, and so on). A feed that keeps
  running keeps the day it started on.
- *Curves and surfaces never move.* Nothing rebuilds a party's curves, forwards
  or surfaces after provisioning, so a report run on a later day prices from
  the provisioning day's market.
- *Ordering by accident.* Whether a step sees today's data depends on which
  timer fired first: the fixings poll, the derived refresh, or a provisioning
  step. The surfaces failure is that race made visible.

The synthetic design already states the intent:
[[id:BB2B7C4C-FD4E-4DCA-BF1D-00F929622B87][Synthetic market data generation: approach]]
says interest rate curves update daily at standard market close times, and a
surface used for daily pricing is regenerated once per business day.

** Questions to settle

- One system-wide close, or one per rates centre as the cut documents state?
  A per-centre close means a party's roll waits for every centre it reads.
- Does the roll wait for the day's fixings, or take what is stored at the close
  and record what was missing? A publisher can be late; the ECB publishes at
  about 16:00 CET.
- Weekends and holidays: does the roll skip a day that no centre trades, by
  which calendar, and what does a feed read on such a day?
- Does a running feed restart, or take a new start level in place? A restart
  breaks the price path; a level change in place makes a jump.
- How does the start of day cut relate to the existing cut entities in
  reporting, and who approves the end of day cut's rates?
- What replaces the derived source's fixed 17:00 stamp: the close of the
  centre that owns the pair?
