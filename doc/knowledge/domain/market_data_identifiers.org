#+description: How market data series are identified externally (RIC, Bloomberg) and by ORE's own canonical key, compared side by side with pros, cons, and limitations of each.

* Summary

Market data series are identified differently across data vendors and internal
systems. Reuters uses RICs (e.g. =EUR==), Bloomberg uses its own ticker syntax (e.g.
=EURUSD BGN Curncy=), and ORE uses a slash-delimited canonical key
(e.g. =FX/RATE/EUR/USD=). ORE Studio currently follows the *ORE canonical key* as the
closest thing it has to an internal identifier for market data series: it is the
native format of every ORE input file. This ORE canonical key alone cannot express
every case ORE Studio needs (e.g. discount vs. projection curves for the same
currency); designing the correct ORE Studio market data identifier — one that
covers all these use cases — is the job of the follow-up canonical URN design task,
which will carry its own gap analysis against ORE Studio's current identifiers.

* Identifier scheme comparison

Three schemes address the same instruments differently: RIC (Reuters/Refinitiv,
now LSEG) is instrument-type-specific and not fully systematic; Bloomberg uses a
space-separated =<ticker> <yellow-key>= composite; ORE uses a slash-delimited
=<series_type>/<metric>/<qualifier>[/<point_id>]= key consumed directly by the ORE
pricing engine (=marketdata.csv=). RIC and Bloomberg cells below are
compiled from publicly visible instrument examples, not a primary vendor spec — see
[[*Sources][Sources]]. "—" marks instruments with no worked ORE example in this
document.

** FX

| Instrument | RIC | Bloomberg ticker | ORE canonical key |
|------------+-----+-------------------+--------------------|
| EUR/USD spot | =EUR== | =EURUSD BGN Curncy= | =FX/RATE/EUR/USD= |
| GBP/USD spot | =GBP== | =GBPUSD BGN Curncy= | =FX/RATE/GBP/USD= |
| USD/JPY spot | =JPY== | — | — |
| EUR/GBP cross | =EURGBP== | — | =FX/RATE/EUR/GBP= |
| EUR/USD 1M forward | =EURUSD1MF== | =EURUSD1M BGN Curncy= | — |

Notes: RIC major pairs use base currency code + ==; crosses use the 6-char pair +
==; outright forwards append tenor + =F=. Bloomberg =BGN= = Bloomberg Generic
fixing. FX spot is a scalar ORE series — no =point_id= segment.

** Interest rates

| Instrument | RIC | Bloomberg ticker | ORE canonical key |
|------------+-----+-------------------+--------------------|
| USD overnight rate (SOFR) | =USDSOFR== | =SOFRRATE Index= | — |
| EUR 6M EURIBOR | =EUR6MFSR== | =EUR006M Index= | — |
| USD 5Y IRS par rate | =USDSB5Y=ICAP= | =USSA5 Curncy= | — |
| EUR 10Y IRS par rate | =EURABS10Y=ICAP= | =EUSA10 Curncy= | — |
| 10Y US Treasury yield | =US10YT=RR= | =USGG10YR Index= | — |
| 10Y German Bund yield | =DE10YT=RR= | =GDBR10 Index= | — |
| EUR discount curve, 2Y point | — | — | =DISCOUNT/RATE/EUR/2Y= |
| USD discount curve, 5Y point | — | — | =DISCOUNT/RATE/USD/5Y= |

Notes: RIC =ICAP= / =GFI= suffixes are broker-specific; =RR= = Reuters composite.
No RIC/Bloomberg row above lines up with an ORE key and vice versa — the RIC/
Bloomberg rows quote par rates for specific indices/tenors, while the ORE rows are
term-structure series (=point_id= carries the tenor); yield curves are
term-structure series, unlike FX spot.

** Swaptions

| Instrument | RIC | Bloomberg ticker | ORE canonical key |
|------------+-----+-------------------+--------------------|
| EUR swaption vol, 5Y expiry / 2Y tenor, ATM | — | — | =SWAPTION/RATE_LNVOL/EUR/5Y/2Y/ATM= |

Notes: swaption surfaces carry a two-dimensional =point_id= (expiry/tenor/strike).

** ORE canonical key structure

| Segment | Meaning | Example |
|---------+---------+---------|
| =series_type= | Asset class and curve family | =FX=, =DISCOUNT=, =SWAPTION=, =CAPFLOOR= |
| =metric= | What is being quoted | =RATE=, =RATE_LNVOL=, =PRICE=, =SPREAD= |
| =qualifier= | Series-specific identifier (CCY, CCY pair, index name, credit name) | =EUR/USD=, =EUR=, =CPTY_A/SR/USD= |
| =point_id= | Tenor or surface coordinate (absent for scalar series) | =1Y=, =5Y/2Y/ATM=, =6M/25RR= |

RIC and Bloomberg identifiers are *not* used as internal identifiers in ORE Studio
today.

* Pros, cons, and limitations

| Scheme | Pros | Cons / limitations |
|--------+------+---------------------|
| RIC | Widely deployed across sell-side systems and data terminals; per-asset-class conventions are well understood in the industry. | Proprietary, licence-gated (no open spec); instrument-type-specific and not fully systematic — conventions differ by asset class; contains characters (==, =.=) that many downstream systems (e.g. hierarchical pub/sub subjects) cannot use unescaped; broker-specific suffixes (=ICAP=, =GFI=) leak vendor/broker identity into the instrument key; no native concept of curve family or discount-vs-projection role. |
| Bloomberg ticker | Widely deployed in buy-side and sell-side systems; yellow key gives an explicit, human-readable asset-class tag. | Proprietary, licence-gated (no open spec); space-separated composite is awkward for systems that expect a single token or path-like key; mixed case and spaces conflict with many downstream systems; like RIC, no native concept of curve family or discount-vs-projection role. |
| ORE canonical key | Open source and openly documented; already the native format of every ORE pricing-engine input file; slash-delimited structure decomposes cleanly into =series_type=/=metric=/=qualifier=/=point_id=; free of vendor licensing. | No worked example in ORE's own documentation covers every instrument class ORE Studio needs (equities, indices, credit, commodities have no ORE canonical key precedent in this document); as used today it has no structured field for curve family or discount-vs-projection role. |
