#+title: Feed screen standard
#+description: The one shape and behaviour of every ores.web screen that authors and runs a synthetic feed, for every asset class: the feed list as a dashboard, the feed page, the process editor and its previews, the engine and its conversion, saving a process, runs, ticks, history and the quant help.

#+level: cross
#+filetags: :web:ux:standards:synthetic:
#+created: 2026-10-11
#+updated: 2026-10-11

* Summary

A feed is a record that produces market data: it holds a process, and while it
runs, the process makes a tick at a fixed rate. The FX price feed screen is
the first feed screen, and its shape is the standard for every asset class
that follows: IR curves, and any other kind of synthetic feed.

A feed is still a record. Everything this page does not state follows the
[[id:2BF7F03F-6BF4-45FF-8E26-4D3B4E2D943D][Record screen standard]]: the list
and its paging, the header, the edit dialog, change reasons, delete, the
states and the words. This page adds what a feed has and a plain record does
not: a run state, a process edited as one set, an engine that decides what the
numbers mean, previews, live ticks, and the quant help that makes the numbers
readable.

Each rule names the shared part that carries it, so a new feed screen gets the
rule by using the part.

Feeds have two scopes. A /system feed/ simulates a real provider, such as
Bloomberg, Refinitiv or an exchange; it is run once for the installation and a
tenant consumes it through a feed binding. A /test feed/ is one a tenant or a
party creates for itself. Today every feed is a party's test feed, and the FX
price feed screen lists one party's FX feeds. In the organisation that
[[id:0A32284A-5F8C-4A99-9539-34923631DED3][Organise feeds by provider and scope]]
brings, a feed browser organises system feeds by provider and kind and holds
the per-kind screens of this page as its leaves, at a scale of many thousands
of feeds.

* Detail

** Where this standard applies

A /feed screen/ is a screen whose subject is the feeds of one kind: it lists
them, starts and stops them, opens one, and edits its process. The FX price
feed screens and the IR curve feed screens under *Market data* are feed
screens.

These are not feed screens:

- The collection screen. A collection holds feeds and starts and stops them as
  one; it is a record screen with its own task.
- The model catalogue. Process types are reference data.
- The Qt market simulator, which
  [[id:5779B40E-1C82-478D-951E-C81C13F7DFEA][The market simulator screens]]
  describes.

** Words

| Word | Meaning |
|------+---------|
| /Feed/ | The record: what it simulates, its rate, its engine and its run settings |
| /Process/ | The parts the engine draws from, such as an FX feed's Gaussian components |
| /Engine/ | The price process that reads the parts: geometric, arithmetic or mean reverting for FX |
| /Draft/ | The process as it stands on the screen, before Save |
| /Run/ | The feed producing ticks. A feed is /running/ or /stopped/ |
| /Tick/ | One value the running feed produced, with its time |
| /Share/ | A part's weight over the sum of the weights, as the engine normalises it |

The tab that edits the process carries the Qt editor's name for it. For FX it
is /Price behaviour/.

** What a feed kind declares

A kind of feed declares, once, in a =FeedKind= (=marketdata/feedKind.ts=):

- its record resource, its entity type, the address of its screens and the
  block of words it speaks in;
- its fields, the fields its edit dialog offers, what a new feed starts with, the
  fields that follow from the others, and the rules across fields;
- how the feed is named in a title, such as a pair or a curve, and the line
  under the name;
- its columns after the status, and the facts its Details tab adds;
- the rows a feed owns, with the resource of each, so the screens read them,
  delete them with the feed and draw their history;
- its own tabs, between Details and History, each a function of the feed, whether
  it runs, whether the person may write it, and the rows it owns;
- the tab a new feed opens on.

The list, the page, the add, edit and delete dialogs, the run controls and the
History tab are then drawn by =FeedsPage= and =FeedPage=
(=marketdata/FeedScreens.tsx=), the same for every kind. A kind also states the
engines it offers, what each number of a part means, the server operations its
tabs call, the subject its ticks are published on and the permission that
gates it.

** The list is the dashboard

The list follows the record standard, and it is also where feeds are run.

- A *Status* column shows *Running* or *Stopped* as a tag. The screen reads
  the running feeds every five seconds, because a feed can start or stop from
  anywhere.
- A writer gets a choice box on each row, and *Start chosen (n)* and *Stop
  chosen (n)* in the list header.
- Each chosen feed is its own call, so one refusal does not stop the others.
  The answer counts what started or stopped and names each refusal with the
  server's reason. A feed is named by its source name, never by its id.
- Starting a whole collection belongs to the collection screen.

** The feed page

*** Header

- The flag of what the feed simulates, such as the pair, then the feed's name.
- The line under the name: the key, the version and the engine, such as
  "EUR/USD · version 3 · Geometric".
- The status tag, then *Start* or *Stop*, then *Edit* and *Delete*.
- No access mark. The actions offered already say what the person may do.

*** Tabs

| Tab | What it holds |
|-----+---------------|
| /Details/ | The feed's fields, as the record standard draws them |
| /The process tab/ | The process, the engine, and the previews. For FX: /Price behaviour/ |
| /Ticks/ | The last ticks of the feed |
| /History/ | One stream of the feed and its parts |

*** Edit, add and delete

- The edit dialog does not offer the engine. The engine changes on the process
  tab, where the process is converted with it.
- The add dialog offers the engine, so a new feed starts with one. A new feed
  opens on the process tab.
- Each field of the form can carry help, shown under it. A choice is explained
  by its option.
- Delete stops the feed, deletes its parts, then deletes the feed.

** The process tab

*** Layout

#+begin_src text
  [ 2 unsaved changes ]        Engine [Geometric ▾]  [Add] [Discard] [Save]
  ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
  │ # Description            Profile   μ        σ        w      Share   │
  │ 0 Primary · calibrated…  Normal    0        0.001    0.95   95.0%  ↑↓ Delete │
  │ 1 Tail · 4x primary…     Custom    0        0.004    0.05    5.0%  ↑↓ Delete │
  └─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
  ┌ Return distribution ─────────┐ ┌ Sample paths ────────────────┐
  │ chart                        │ │ chart                        │
  │                              │ │ price · ticks · paths · seed │
  └──────────────────────────────┘ └──────────────────────────────┘
  ⓘ The engine's note
#+end_src

- The toolbar holds the unsaved count, or "No unsaved changes.", the engine,
  and *Add*, *Discard* and *Save*. Buttons are one word.
- The list spans the full width. The previews sit side by side below it, and
  stack on a narrow screen. The engine's note sits under the previews.

*** The list

- The list is read only. A row opens the part in a dialog.
- The description shows its name highlighted and its note subdued: the text
  before the first colon is the name. A long description ends in an ellipsis,
  with the whole text in its tooltip.
- Numbers are right aligned in the mono face.
- A computed column states what the engine does with the numbers. For FX, the
  /Share/ column gives each weight as a percentage of the sum, and its header
  tooltip gives the sum. An engine that reads no shares has no such column.
- Every column header has a tooltip in the engine's terms, and every row a
  tooltip that says what one part means to the engine.
- Each row has *↑*, *↓* and *Delete*. The first row cannot move up and the
  last cannot move down.

*** Unsaved changes

Nothing is written until Save, and what is not saved is visible:

| Change | Mark |
|--------+------|
| A new part | The row tinted, and tagged /New/ |
| A changed value | The cell tinted, and the row tagged /Changed/ |
| A moved part | The row tagged /Moved/ |
| A deleted part | The row kept, struck through, tagged /Deleted/, with /Undo/ |
| A changed engine | The engine choice outlined |

*Discard* puts the saved process and engine back.

*** The part dialog

- The description is a text area of four lines, with a hint for the
  "Name: note" form.
- Each number has the engine's help under it, and the dialog refuses a value
  that is not a number or is negative before *Done*.
- *Done* puts the part back into the draft. It writes nothing.

*** Saving

*Save* is enabled while there are changes and no problems. It opens a dialog:

1. One line per kind of change: the engine change, such as "Engine:
   Geometric → Arithmetic.", and the counts added, changed and deleted.
2. The change reason and commentary, or the new record note when every change
   is new.
3. When the feed applies the change. A running feed keeps the process it
   started with, so the dialog says "The feed is running on the old mixture."
   and offers *Restart it to apply*, ticked. A stopped feed is told "It applies
   on start."

The save writes the feed's engine first, then deletes the removed parts, then
writes the changed parts, all under one reason. A restart, when chosen, stops
and starts the feed after the writes.

*** The engine

The engine decides what the numbers of a part mean. Changing it changes the
meaning of the saved process, so the screen converts the draft with it:

- The screen asks the server to convert the draft. The conversion is never
  computed in the browser.
- The converted values show as unsaved changes. A notice says what was
  converted and at which price, and says when a preview price stood in for a
  feed with no fixed price.
- For FX, the server operation is =convert_fx_mixture=: μ and σ scale by the
  initial price between log-returns and price units; into the mean reverting
  engine only the first part stays, with κ from a 15 minute half-life and no
  μ; out of it the one part takes the whole weight.

*** Previews

- The previews read the draft, not the saved process, so a person judges a
  change before saving it.
- The distribution is drawn per part, dashed, and as the whole.
- The sample paths come from the server's simulation, with their settings
  under the chart: the initial price, the ticks, the paths and the seed with
  *Reseed*. The screen waits 300 ms after typing before it asks.
- Neither preview saves anything.

** The process tab of a curve feed

A curve feed's process is the engine and a fixed set of parameters, so the tab
is the process tab above with the parts that follow from that.

- The toolbar is the same: the unsaved count or "No unsaved changes.", the engine,
  *Discard* and *Save*. There is no *Add*, and a row has no *↑*, *↓* or *Delete*,
  because the engine states which parameters it reads.
- The table lists the engine's parameters, read only: the parameter with its
  symbol and the value. Each row's tooltip is the parameter's description.
- A click on a row opens the parameter in a dialog with the description and the
  value. What the parameter accepts, such as "At least 0." or "Between 0 and 1.",
  is the tooltip of the field, and the dialog refuses a value outside it before
  *Done*. *Done* puts the value back into the table and writes nothing.
- A parameter with no saved value is tagged /New/ and shows the engine's default. A
  value that differs from the saved one is tinted and tagged /Changed/. A value the
  engine refuses is shown in red, and *Save* waits.
- A change of engine replaces the parameters, and a notice says that the old
  engine's parameters are removed when the process is saved.
- The previews below the table price the curve at the template's tenor entries and
  draw sample short-rate paths, both from the values on the screen.
- Under each number the table says what it means in plain words: the half-life of
  κ, the rate behind θ and the initial rate, and the yearly move behind σ. The
  server works these out, so the screen does no model arithmetic.

*** Notices and toasts

News of an action does not take a place in the page. A conversion that changed the
person's numbers, such as a change of engine or a switch to Simple, shows as a toast. So does the sentence that says what the Simple answers make of the model (the curve slopes up, or the price has no trend), which a new answer replaces in
the bottom right corner (=ui/Toasts.tsx=). It has a close button. Information leaves
by itself after ten seconds, and a warning stays until the person closes it. A fact
that stays true, such as an engine that can take a price below zero, is also marked
where it applies: a /Can go below zero/ tag beside the engine, with the full
explanation as its tooltip. A closed toast is not lost, because the tag remains.

*** Simple and Advanced

A process tab offers two ways to edit the same numbers. *Advanced* is the table
above, the engine's own parameters. *Simple* is for a person who does not know the
model, and it asks plain questions in place of the table.

- The choice is a switch on the toolbar. It is one choice for the whole web
  application, kept in the browser. A new person starts in Simple, and a person who
  picks Advanced stays in Advanced on every screen that offers both (=EditingMode.tsx=).
- A curve feed asks four questions: where rates are today, where they settle in the
  long run, how fast they come back (a half-life in years, with the words Slow,
  Medium and Fast), and how far they move in a year (basis points, with the words
  Calm, Normal and Stressed). A sentence under the answers says what they make of the
  curve: it slopes up, down or is flat. A line under the toolbar says in plain words
  what the engine does.
- The server converts both ways (=synthetic.v1.ops.convert_ir_parameters=; the FX
  mixture conversion, =convert_fx_mixture=, is its counterpart). Each engine reads σ
  in its own unit, and Black-Karasinski reads θ as a logarithm, so the screen never
  converts. An answer goes through the server and into the same draft the table
  edits. Save, Discard and the previews work the same from either mode.
- Switching from Simple to Advanced loses nothing. A feed whose parameters have no
  simple form, such as a reversion speed of zero or a Two-Factor Gaussian feed with a
  second factor set by hand, shows the table in either mode, with a note that says why.
- A warning appears when the parameters allow a risk, such as a
  Cox-Ingersoll-Ross rate that can touch zero.
- A field whose value differs from the saved one is tinted, as a changed cell of the
  table is.
- The FX price behaviour tab offers the same switch (=FxSimplePanel.tsx=). Its
  questions are per hour, so they do not depend on the feed's rate: which way the
  price drifts, how far it moves, and how often it jumps; the mean-reverting engine
  asks how long the price takes to come half way back, and how far it moves. The
  server converts both ways (=synthetic.v1.ops.convert_fx_simple=). The simple form of
  a mixture is one primary component and, if the feed jumps, one jump regime that has
  no drift and is four times as wide.
- Switching to Simple never leaves a dead end. Values that the simple form states
  exactly stay as they are. Values that it only approximates, such as three
  hand-set components, or a second factor and a correlation set by hand, are
  converted to the closest simple form, and a notice says what changed: how many
  components became how many, the jump share, or the factors that were set. The
  change is only in the draft. *Save* writes it, and *Discard* brings the old values
  back. Values with no simple view at all, such as a reversion speed of zero, show the
  table with a note that says why.

** The Ticks tab

#+begin_src text
  ┌ Ticks shown [200▾]  LAST PRICE  TIME  MOVE (200 TICKS)   ☐ EMA 20  [Export CSV] ┐   ┌ PROCESS  [Geometric] ┐
  ├ EUR/USD synthetic price path              High 1.17652  Low 1.17601 ┤   │ 1800 ticks per hour  │
  │ price chart, axis on the right, grid, fill                          │   │ #0 Primary   w 0.95  │
  │                                                                     │   │ note, subdued        │
  └─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘   └──────────────────────┘
#+end_src

- A strip of labelled figures over the plot: the ticks shown, the last price,
  its time and the move over the ticks shown, green or red.
- The chart card heads with what it shows and the high and the low.
- The price chart has its values on the right, a grid of labelled levels, a
  light fill under the line, and labels in the page's 12 px mono at any width.
- An optional moving average, an EMA over 20 ticks, drawn dashed.
- *Export CSV* hands the ticks shown to the browser.
- A column beside the plot shows the process that made the ticks: the engine,
  the rate, and each part as a card with its name highlighted, its note
  subdued, its weight in the part's colour, and its other numbers.
- A running feed's ticks are read again every two seconds.
- The tab says what it can and cannot show. The web server keeps the last 500
  ticks of each running feed from when it began to listen, and forgets a run
  when the feed stops, so the lead says so.

** The Ticks tab of a curve feed

A tick of a curve feed is one pillar of a curve. The feed publishes a curve as
one tick for each tenor entry of the template, in template order, all with the
time of the batch, so the ticks that share a time are one curve. The web server
keeps the oresmd URI of each tick, and the screen groups by time (=irTicks.ts=). A
batch with a pillar missing, which the server heard in part, is not a curve and
is not drawn.

- The strip shows the history, the time of the newest curve, its short end and long
  end, and the slope between them in basis points, green or red.
- One chart, the one the Qt market simulator draws: the tenors of the curve along
  the bottom, short end to long end, each named under its point, and the rate up the
  side, with a margin of a tenth of the range. The newest curve is bold and green.
  The curves before it are overlaid, thinner, grey-blue and fading with age: the
  oldest is the faintest, and none fades below 40 of 255. Every point has a dot.
  The legend under the chart says so: "Newest curve bold; older snapshots
  overlaid, fading with age."
- The history is a number from 1 to 20, five by default, as in the Qt window. It
  is the number of recent curves to overlay.
- *Export CSV* hands the curves shown to the browser, one row for each curve and one
  column for each pillar.
- A column beside the plot shows the process that made the curves: the engine, the
  rate and each parameter with its saved value.

** The Synthetic screen

Synthetic (=marketdata/synthetic/SyntheticScreen.tsx=) is one screen with two tabs, the
dashboard and the feed tree. It lists no feed list of its own per asset class: the feed
table lists every kind. Everything on it is synthetic, so no label says so. The
[[id:17E6CB2C-E1C9-4911-81AC-591D88AC5B89][Browse the feed tree prototype]] shows it.

*** The dashboard

The dashboard (=DashboardTab.tsx=) has one row for each provider. A provider is a row of
the provider catalogue, with a description of what it simulates, and every collection
names one, so every feed has a provider. The ORE examples are the provider /ORE/.

- A row shows what the provider simulates, its FX and IR counts, one of Running, Stopped,
  "n of m running" or Empty, and how many of its feeds should run and do not. Start and
  Stop on a row act on every feed of the provider.
- A banner counts the feeds that are enabled, start on their own, and are not running.
  A restart of the synthetic service stops every feed and nothing starts them again, so
  this is the first thing the screen says. /Start them/ starts exactly those feeds; /Show
  them in the feed tree/ opens the tree on them.
- The chosen provider has a panel with its asset classes and a link into the tree.
- The server counts. =synthetic.v1.ops.list_feed_folders= returns the children of one
  folder, or the roots, each with the feeds under it at any depth, how many run and how
  many are idle. The screen asks again every five seconds.

*** The feed tree

The feed tree (=FeedTreeTab.tsx=) has the providers and their folders on the left and a
table of feeds on the right. A provider with one collection stands for it, so its
children are the asset class folders; a provider with several lists its collections.

- The table lists the feeds under the chosen node, with the folders below it, a page at a
  time, with the standard pager. Its columns are state, enabled, name, asset class,
  underlying (a currency pair or a currency, with flags), engine and frequency. A row
  opens the feed's page.
- The filters are the state (All, Started, Stopped), the asset class, and the search
  words. The search reaches every provider. All of them run on the server
  (=synthetic.v1.ops.list_folder_feeds=), so the counts and the pages are right.
- A row has no Start or Stop. A person ticks rows, or the whole page, and a link then
  offers every feed that matches the filter. A bar shows how many are picked and has
  Start and Stop for the pick. The request carries the filter and the ids picked or left
  out (=synthetic.v1.ops.start_selected_feeds= and =stop_selected_feeds=); the server
  resolves the feeds with the same filter that pages the table, so a set of thousands
  costs one small request and the screen never holds the ids. A toast says what started,
  what was already running and what was skipped.
- /Add feed/ asks for the kind, FX or IR, and opens its form with the collection the person
  is looking at. The database places the feed in the folder of its kind under that
  collection, and makes the folders that are missing, so the feed shows in the tree at once.
  The feed's page opens when it is saved.
- Starting and stopping a feed needs the permission =synthetic::feeds:run=, beside the read
  of the feed configs the verb touches. The screen draws Start and Stop only for a person
  who holds it, on the dashboard, in the feed table and on a feed's page. Every verb that
  starts or stops, one feed, a folder or a selection, checks it in the service.
- A node has an address, =?tab=tree&node=provider:bloomberg= or
  =?tab=tree&node=folder:<id>=, and the filters are in the address too, so a link opens
  the tree as it was.
- A feed's page has the trail Home, Market data, Synthetic, Feed tree, the folders down to
  the feed, then the feed. Each folder opens the tree on it.

** History

The History tab is one stream of the feed and every part it holds, drawn as
the timeline of the
[[id:BD492939-118E-4B55-AE5B-300F7A1C3B41][Entity screen specification]]:
the event chart above a rail of one-line entries, each naming its record. Each
version older than its record's newest offers *Revert* to a person who may
write it. A part offers no history of its own in the list.

A part deleted from the process is not in the stream, and the tab says so in
one line under the timeline.

** Quant logic lives on the server

What the numbers mean is the engine's, and the engine is on the server. A
feed screen asks the server for every quant answer that decides a value
written to the feed: the sample paths, the conversion between engines, and any
calibration. The screen draws what the server answers.

** Words on a feed screen

- Buttons are one word: *Add*, *Discard*, *Save*, *Start*, *Stop*, *Undo*,
  *Done*.
- Notes are one short sentence. "The feed is running on the old mixture." says
  what the person needs; a paragraph that explains the engine does not belong
  in a dialog.
- The long explanations live in tooltips and field hints, where a person who
  wants them looks.

** How today's feed screens conform

| Screen | Conformance |
|--------+-------------|
| FX price feeds | Conforms. It is the screen this standard was taken from; its feeds are listed in the feed tree of Synthetic |
| IR curve feeds | Conforms: the page with Details, Process, Curve template, Ticks and History, the previews and the unsaved marks. The Ticks tab draws curves, see below. The task [[id:1E325838-917A-4F74-B880-15DEF844CD90][Build the IR curve feed screen]] states the aim |

** Building a feed screen for a new asset class

1. Give the server the kind's resources, with the filter that reads the rows of
   one parent, and the conversion between the kind's engines and its previews as
   operations. Test them there.
2. Write the kind: a =FeedKind= with its fields, columns, parts and tabs, and
   two pages that hand it to =FeedsPage= and =FeedPage=. Add the routes and the
   card on the Market data area, and the permission.
3. Write the kind's own tabs. Take the parts above where they fit.
4. Write the words of the kind in every language: the titles, the field hints,
   the engine names and the tab notes.
5. Check the screen against each section of this page, and add its row to the
   conformance table.

** Gaps

- The Ticks tab shows only what the web server heard. The stored observations are
  written under the feed's series identity, which a feed now names by its oresmd URI;
  the tab does not read its history from the store yet.
- The browser still computes some figures it only displays: the shares, the
  mean reverting half-life and band, the profiles and the distribution curve.
  Each moves to the server when a screen first needs it from there.
