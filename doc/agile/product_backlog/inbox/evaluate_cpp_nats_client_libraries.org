* What

Evaluate three community C++ NATS clients against the =ores.nats= wrapper we
maintain over =cnats=: =antlad/nats_asio=, =dancasimiro/nats.cpp= and
=ishbir/cppnats=. For each, record whether it supports JetStream (durable
consumers, explicit ack, nak and term, stream and consumer administration),
TLS with client certificates, headers, request and reply, and drain. Also
record its licence, its maintenance activity, whether it builds with our
C++23 and Boost.Asio toolchain, and what packaging it would need. None of
the three is in the vcpkg registry (checked 2026-10-26 at vcpkg
=0c2a9ace19=), so adopting one means writing and keeping an overlay port.
The outcome is one of three choices. Replace the wrapper, borrow its
ownership model for =ores.nats=, or keep =cnats= as it is.

* Why

=ores.nats= wraps the C client by hand, and every cnats object must be
freed by a matching destroy call. A missing =natsMsg_Destroy= on the normal
delivery path made every service keep every message it received. Under the
synthetic feed it grew =ores.marketdata.service= by about 195 MiB a minute
and took the machine down on 2026-10-26. The fix added =nats_handle=, a
unique_ptr owner for each cnats object type, and =ensure_result= for status
checks. A native C++ client could make that whole class of defect
impossible, if one is mature enough and covers the JetStream features the
fleet depends on. The official NATS project ships no C++ client.
