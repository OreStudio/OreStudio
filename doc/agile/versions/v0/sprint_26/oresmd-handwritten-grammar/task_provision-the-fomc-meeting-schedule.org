* Notes

** The cause

The feed =synthetic.2026realistic.usdsofrfomc= has published nothing in a
provisioned tenant since at least 2026-08-12. Its template starts with the
pillar =SPOT= to =1F=, and =1F= to =8F= are SPECIAL tenors of the convention
=RATES_SPOT_FOMC=. A resolution row of that convention names the schedule
=FOMC_MEETING= and a step count, so =1F= is the first meeting on or after spot.

On 2026-10-04, a database query found that the system tenant has these rows,
but the ACME tenant has the rows without =schedule_code= and
=schedule_step_count=. The provisioner's copy in
=projects/ores.sql/create/iam/iam_tenant_provisioner_create.sql= does not list
the two columns. A row with neither column falls back to the calendar axis,
which is spot plus the row's offset. The offset is =DAY 0=, so in the tenant
every FOMC tenor resolves to spot. The first pillar then has zero length.
Before the market tick change, the curve pricer refused it with "year_fraction
must be positive". After that change, =make_pillar_quote_key= refuses it first.
The cause is the same.

The provisioner also copies no calendar events, and row security on
=ores_refdata_calendar_events_tbl= shows a tenant only its own rows. So after
the columns are fixed, the resolver finds no meeting dates and stops with "no
event-lookup dates were supplied". Both gaps must close.

The events cannot simply be copied. The insert trigger of
=ores_refdata_calendar_events_tbl= checks that the tenant has the event's
calendar and its diary entry type. On 2026-10-04 the ACME tenant had 60 of the
system tenant's 61 calendars, all but =US.FOMC=, because its calendars come
from a dataset publish rather than the provisioner. It had no diary entry
types, because nothing copies them. So the tenant also needs =US.FOMC= and
=central_bank_meeting= before its meeting dates.

No pillar check stops a curve from publishing past data. A curve's dates are
calculated from its own as-of date, and only a pillar of zero length from spot
is refused.
