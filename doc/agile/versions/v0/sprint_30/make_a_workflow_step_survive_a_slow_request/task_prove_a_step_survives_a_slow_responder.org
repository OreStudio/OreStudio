* Goal

A test drives a provisioning step against a responder that answers after the first deadline and against one that refuses, and proves that the step retries the first, fails at once on the second, reports progress, and names the work in its failure.

* Acceptance

- A responder that misses the first deadline and answers the second time lets the step complete.
- A responder that refuses fails the step at once, with the server's reason.
- The test sees the progress lines the step published.
- The test fails if the retry repeats work that already happened.

* Plan

- Prove the request path at the helper, where the step's four behaviours live:
  a responder that misses the first deadline and answers the second, a
  refusal, the attempt notes, and a repeat that takes what an earlier attempt
  did.
- Prove the step path with a live run: =compass smoke ticks --leave-running=
  provisions =acme_corporation= with the =acme_demo= profile, follows the
  child run, and prints each step and its progress.
- Read the live output for the three lines this story promised: the progress
  line, the attempt line, and the failure line.

* Result

Proven, and by the live run rather than only by the helper's cases.

The request path is covered by nine cases: a responder that misses the first
deadline and answers the second, a refusal thrown at once, the attempt note that
reaches the run's record, and a repeat that takes what an earlier attempt
produced and does the work once.

The step path is covered by the live run. On a freshly recreated database,
`compass smoke ticks --leave-running` provisioned Acme and printed the
heartbeat's progress while it worked:

#+begin_src text
  [13/15] publish_party_surfaces: in_progress
      progress: Acme Corporation Plc: curves with their quotes 3 of 20
      progress: Acme Corporation Plc: surface blueprints with their quotes 9 of 10
#+end_src

The first attempt failed in `generate_trades` with `std::bad_alloc`; the retry
of that step completed the run:

#+begin_src text
  [13/15] publish_party_surfaces: completed
  [15/17] generate_trades: completed
  [17/17] complete_provisioning: completed
  ✓ All 17 step(s) completed.
#+end_src

The retry wrote 6,541 trades, 37 bond issues and 80 surface publishes, and
`publish_party_surfaces` published 40 surfaces. The `bad_alloc` is not memory
exhaustion: the trading service's cgroup peaked at 276 MiB of an 8 GiB limit
across both runs. It is data-dependent, and it is captured for triage as
[[id:9232CDC9-8444-4856-8D3D-CA7CD4EFA5EC][Generating a bond issue fails with std::bad_alloc]].

The recovery shows the design working as intended: the helper treats
`std::bad_alloc` as permanent, the step fails with the work named, and the
step-level retry — which is safe because the step repeats nothing — completes.

The review round added four cases to the request path: an attempt waits no
longer than the budget left to it, an attempt knows which time round it is and
what is left, the first attempt does not read back, and a repeat whose read
back answers does not do the work again. They close the two gaps the review
found: an attempt could wait a full patience past the deadline, and the read
back ran on the first attempt where nothing of its own was there to find.

What is not covered, and the review says so plainly: the cases drive the retry
helper and not a whole step, and the failure message is checked by the case
that names a party, an item and the attempts, not by a step that gives up. A
step-level harness is the check that would close both.
