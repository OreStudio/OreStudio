* Result

The story is scaffolded and its analysis is substantially further than
scaffolding required, because measuring the population path turned out to be
cheap once the lever existed.

Three scripts were written and all three are reusable.
=scripts/bench_trade_insert.py= times a single table, isolating the load method
and the notify trigger from the shape. =scripts/bench_trade_shapes.py= times a
population by instrument shape across all its tables.
=scripts/load_trade_population.py= fills every column of all 41 deployed
=ores_trading= tables against the real schema, and reports what it cannot
populate as findings rather than failing silently.

The measurements corrected the design twice. A synthetic table put the notify
trigger at 4.2x; on the deployed schema it is 1.5%, and the per-row insert
trigger costs about 25x. A million vanilla swaps is 7.6 minutes rather than the
15 seconds the synthetic path suggested. Both corrections are recorded in the
design with the numbers behind them.

One defect was fixed rather than recorded, because it blocked the story
outright. Acme's books and portfolios were staged in the DQ artefact tables and
never published into refdata, so a freshly provisioned database could not hold a
single trade. =populate/acme/acme_publish_populate.sql= now runs during
provisioning, =fpml/fpml_populate.sql= is wired in ahead of it, and a rebuild
from scratch was verified end to end: zero errors, 31 books, 36 portfolios, and
trades loading.

What was found and not fixed is recorded in
[[id:9F706D25-7C2F-4988-83F0-A7B5E92BD5CD][Investigation: Trade model defects]], which carries sixteen defects in the
trading model with a proposed repair for each. That is the reason the story
pauses here rather than continuing: two of those defects bound what any
population can contain, and repairing them first is cheaper than generating
against a model that is about to change.

Two things this task got wrong and corrected. A benchmark reported nonsense
numbers for a full session because its table copied a literal string into a
timestamp column, and the numbers were retracted rather than explained. The
loader left 588,000 rows in the development database because its cleanup used
=DELETE=, which an =ON DELETE= rule rewrites into a validity close on these
bitemporal tables; the rows were removed and the cleanup now suspends the rules
and verifies both the rows and the rules afterwards.
