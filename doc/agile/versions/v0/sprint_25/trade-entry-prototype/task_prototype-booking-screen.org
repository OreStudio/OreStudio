* Goal

Ticket type: prototype (human in the loop).

Build the throwaway clickable prototype of the booking screen. The screen is
not interactive in the pricing sense. It concentrates on the correctness and
validity of the booking.

The prototype covers:

- the structure panel, two levels deep, with named groups allowed;
- strategy mode and package mode;
- the component picker over every product type, grouped by asset class;
- four specialised widgets, plus the generic form that catches the rest;
- the version and authorisation header;
- the initial NPV, read-only, with its provenance beside it;
- a structure composed from nothing, and one formed from existing trades.

Fixtures only. No backend and no valuation.

* Acceptance

- The prototype opens on a structure and shows its components in the panel.
- A group holds components, and components move between groups.
- The picker lists every product type from the catalogue.
- Picking a product type opens its widget, or the generic form when it has
  none.
- The version header shows the structure version and the authorisation state.
- The initial NPV is read-only, with its market data cut shown beside it.
- A component can be added, edited and removed, and the version header
  responds.
- A structure can be composed from nothing, and formed from existing trades.
- The four specialised widgets show barrier, schedule, leg and notional
  management.
- Every field the booking captures is either filled by hand or carried from
  the pricing handoff.
- The prototype is reachable from the published site.

* Plan

Build both screens as a static web app under =doc/prototypes/trade-entry/=:
plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript, with no framework and no build step. The
site build already publishes =.js=, =.css= and =doc/...org=, and already
deploys a static app directory through =ores-deploy-web-app=, so the only
build change is one call to that existing function. Nothing new is
invented for delivery.

Six files: two pages, one stylesheet, and three scripts split by concern
— =catalogue.js= holds the product types, the strategies and the
fixtures; =booking.js= and =pricing.js= hold one screen each.

The booking screen is a three-part layout:

- a header carrying the structure identity, the mode, the version and the
  authorisation state;
- a structure panel on the left, a tree two levels deep, with the
  component widget on the right;
- an initial NPV strip below, read-only, with its provenance beside it.

The version rule is the load-bearing behaviour, so it is modelled
literally: every mutating action goes through one =mutate()= function
that appends to an audit log and increments the version, and the
authorise action deliberately does not. That makes the rule visible
rather than asserted.

State lives in memory and mirrors to =localStorage=, so the Book handoff
survives the page change from the pricing screen to the booking screen.

Fixtures are transcribed from the ORE example trades, not invented:
=FX_Option_European.xml=, =FX_Barrier_Option.xml=,
=Hybrid_CompositeTrade.xml= and a leg from =Cash_BondRepo_and_Bond.xml=.

Deliberately absent: a backend, a real valuation, live market data, and
any persistence beyond the browser.

* Result

Both screens are built and the site build deploys them.

The prototype is six files under =doc/prototypes/trade-entry/=:
=index.html= and =booking.js= for the booking screen, =pricing.html= and
=pricing.js= for the pricing screen, =catalogue.js= for the product types,
the strategies and the fixtures, and =prototype.css= for both screens.
There is no framework and no build step.

Every acceptance bullet is met. The picker offers the eight product types
grouped by asset class, and inside FX the six =trade_type_code= values
that have a dedicated form. Four widgets open: the FX vanilla option, the
FX barrier option, the swap, and the composite. Every other choice opens
the generic form.

The version rule is modelled rather than asserted. Every mutating action
routes through one =mutate()= function that increments the version and
writes the audit line. The authorise action deliberately does not. The
version chip animates on a change, so the rule is visible.

The topology is editable after creation. The component editor carries a
Group select, so a component moves between groups. A move counts as a
change, and the version follows it. Moving a component into a hedge group
also takes it out of the strategy invariant, which is the point of the
group being marked as a hedge.
