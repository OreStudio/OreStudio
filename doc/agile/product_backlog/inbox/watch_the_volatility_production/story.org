* Goal

A platform developer can watch the generated volatility of one subject and see why it
looks the way it does. The page reads the chain in dependency order: the series the
generator steps, the latent value it holds, and each vendor's view of it, ending at the
quotes the generator delivers. Each node states its own state, so a subject with no quotes
says which input it lacks.

The page ends at the quotes. What a party does with them -- fit a model, read the error,
publish a surface -- is the party's work, at domain level, with a helper of its own. This
page is the platform developer's view of the market data the platform generates, and it
holds no calibration and no publish.

The chain is the debugger. It is not a quotes board and not a feed page: the internal
values have no party to read them, and no existing screen joins the producer side to the
delivered quotes.

The page names no asset class. A quote set states its own axes -- an expiry and a delta
label for an FX set, an expiry, a term and a strike for a swaption one -- and the page
draws the grid the quotes declare. A subject whose axes the page cannot read is refused
with the axis it lacks, and is never drawn with a guess.

* Acceptance

- A subject and an as-of answer the whole chain in one read, from the inputs to the quotes
  the generator delivered. The subject is whatever the quote set's coordinates name: a pair
  for FX, a currency for a swaption one.
- The page renders the latent value, each vendor's view and the quotes the generator
  delivers, with the difference between them and the quote ladders beside them, on the axes
  the quotes declare.
- A broken chain names the node that failed and the input it lacked, and the node's own
  message log is reachable from it.
- The internal series of the latent value and of the derived quotes are readable under the
  system tenant, and no non-system party can read them.
- A person can step the latent value through a day and watch the views follow.
- The page holds no calibration, no publish and no party: an operator sees the market data
  the platform generates, and the party's fit is a page of its own.
- No part of the page names an asset class. A quote set of a class the page has never seen
  renders when it declares its axes, and a class that declares no axes is refused with the
  axis it lacks.

* Decisions

- *Two views, two audiences, and the quotes are the seam.* A platform developer watches the
  market data the platform generates, and that view ends at the quotes. A user of the domain
  learns to calibrate a surface by taking quotes as an input, whichever source they came
  from, and neither the screen nor the fit can tell a generated quote from a vendor's. The
  system view is this page; the domain helper is the party's calibration screens, which
  already have their journeys and prototypes. A calibration debugger inside the generator
  would serve neither reader and would duplicate the party's own work.
- *The page names no asset class.* The axes and their labels, the quote ladder's shape and
  the model a fit would use come from the description the quotes carry. When a second class
  needs something the page cannot show, the description gains a field; the page does not gain
  a branch. This is the rule the second class is the test of.
- *The internal values are stored and unentitled, not absent.* A latent surface and the
  derived quotes keep a series so their evolution can be watched; no non-system party may
  read them, and they are never metered.
- *The derived quotes serve one role.* They drive the generation of the simulated surface.
  They are not a quote source for a party.
- *The platform does not manufacture a surface for a party.* A party with no system-level
  quotes for its subject authors its own surface, calibrates it and publishes it.
- *A separate card is not warranted yet.* The page sits under the simulator area until it
  covers more than volatility.
- *The chain is the spine, and the heatmaps are the payload.* The dependencies are drawn as
  a graph whose nodes are market objects with states and whose edges state what they carry,
  because the two joins -- one latent value to many vendor views, many views to what leaves
  the platform -- are where the faults are. The columns are the pipeline's stages and the
  rows are alternatives, so the layout is computed and stable; a force layout would re-shuffle
  the picture a reader is learning. Selecting a node opens its heatmaps and ladders on the
  axes it declares. The graph is drawn from a typed node-and-edge payload, so a new class
  draws with the same component.
- *The producer of the platform's own arithmetic is =orestudio=.* It is a producer, not a
  publisher, and it replaces the mechanism-named =derived=.

* Out of scope

- An axis a surface cannot express. A surface with three axes is drawn as the slices it
  declares, not as a new page.
