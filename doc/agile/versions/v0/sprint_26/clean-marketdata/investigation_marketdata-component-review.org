* Paths taken

** 1. How much of ORE market data oresmd models

*** Instrument types

ORE defines 45 instrument types (=MarketDatum::InstrumentType=, without =NONE=).
oresmd names 41 of them. These four have no quote type:

| ORE type | What it is |
|----------+------------|
| =ASSUMED_RECOVERY_RATE= | Same shapes as =RECOVERY_RATE= |
| =BOND_FUTURE= | Price and conversion factor of a bond future |
| =BOND_FUTURE_OPTION= | Vol or price of an option on a bond future |
| =COMMODITY_CALENDAR_SPREAD_OPTION= | Vol of a commodity calendar spread option |

None of the four is in the corpus, so the coverage gate, which reads only the
corpus and the shape table, cannot see that they are missing.

ORE also accepts three aliases: =FX_SPOT= for =FX=, =FX_FWD= for =FXFWD=, and
the deprecated =RATE_GVOL= for =RATE_LNVOL=. oresmd rejects all three. An
import drops such a line with a warning. It does not normalise it.

*** Quote types (metrics)

ORE defines 16 quote types. The oresmd =metric= enum holds 8 of them.
=CREDIT_SPREAD=, =CONV_CREDIT_SPREAD=, =HAZARD_RATE= and =BASE_CORRELATION=
are not members, so =metric= is not set for credit quotes. The vol models
(=RATE_LNVOL=, =RATE_NVOL=, =RATE_SLNVOL=, =SHIFT=, =PRICE=) are in the
separate =volatility_model_subtype= enum. This is the gap the previous close-out
summary recorded for =BASE_CORRELATION=. It is wider than that one member.

*** Key forms inside a type

This is where the real distance is. ORE accepts many forms for most types. For
example, CDS has nine documented forms, and =CAPFLOOR=, =SWAPTION=,
=EQUITY_OPTION=, =COMMODITY_OPTION= and =INDEX_CDS_OPTION= each have between
four and eight. oresmd was shaped by the example corpus, so it reads the forms
the corpus uses and few others.

Of the 40 documented forms that are not in the corpus, oresmd names 17. At
least four of those 17 are named *wrongly*: the reader puts a token in the
wrong field.
The text round-trips, so no current test sees the fault.

| ORE key | What ORE means | What oresmd stores |
|---------+----------------+--------------------|
| =CDS/CREDIT_SPREAD/ACME/SNRFOR/USD/5Y/0.01= | term 5Y, running spread 0.01 | =restructuring=5y=, =tenor=0.01= |
| =SWAPTION/RATE_NVOL/EUR/5Y/2Y/ATM/R= | ATM receiver | =smile=ATM=, =strike=R= |
| =COMMODITY_OPTION/RATE_LNVOL/GOLD/USD/1Y/DEL/Spot/Call/0.25= | 25 delta spot call | =delta=DEL=, =premium=Spot=, =call_put=Call=, =strike=0.25= |
| =INDEX_CDS_OPTION/RATE_LNVOL/CDXIG/1Y= | expiry 1Y, no index term | =tenor=1y= |

The corpus has the same kind of fault. =EQUITY_OPTION/.../ATM/AtmFwd= and
=COMMODITY_OPTION/.../ATM/AtmFwd= store =AtmFwd= as =call_put=. The 9-segment
=ATM/AtmDeltaNeutral/DEL/Spot= form stores =DEL= as =call_put= and =Spot= as
=strike=. The readers choose a field by segment count alone. ORE chooses it by
the token values.

Forms that oresmd rejects outright include: the CDS =PRICE=,
=CONV_CREDIT_SPREAD=, 8-segment and 4-segment (MDX) forms; =CAPFLOOR= with an
index token or a =C=/=F= flag; the =CAPFLOOR=, =SWAPTION= and =BOND_OPTION=
shift forms with no surface; the equity =MNY/Spot/1.2= moneyness strike; and
=COMMODITY_OPTION/SHIFT/<name>=.

*** Other ORE market inputs

- *Fixings* are in good shape. All 158 corpus index names except the two that
  the corpus misfiles as fixings round-trip through the stored URI (probe 3).
- *Dividends* (ORE's =dividendDataFile=) have no reader and no entity. The
  corpus has no input dividends file, so nothing has asked for one yet.
- *Curve and market configuration* (=curveconfig.xml=, =todaysmarket.xml=) are
  out of scope by decision, and [[id:E44D54EF-14D9-4010-A626-12AF1EDC8F26][Add proper curve support to ores.marketdata]]
  holds the curve question.

** 2. What the round-trip tests prove

There are four round trips in the suite. Each one checks less than its name
suggests.

*** The coverage gate checks the in-memory path, not the stored path

=oresmd_ore_coverage_tests.cpp= checks =to_quote_key(from_ore_key(k)) == k=.
That never builds a URI string. Production stores the URI string:
=import_service= writes =to_series_uri= to =market_series.oresmd_uri= and
=to_uri= to =market_observations.oresmd_uri=. Probe 1 runs the stored path:

| Measure | Keys | Share |
|---------+------+-------|
| Distinct corpus keys | 108,057 | 100% |
| Named by oresmd | 108,057 | 100% |
| Round trip in memory (what the gate checks) | 108,057 | 100% |
| Round trip through the URI | 100,803 | 93.3% |
| URI the parser rejects | 4,269 | 4.0% |
| URI that parses but changes when written again | 2,944 | 2.7% |

The 4,269 rejected URIs come from five types. The writer makes a URI that its
own parser refuses:

| Type | Keys | Parser error |
|------+------+--------------|
| =INDEX_CDS_OPTION= | 2,993 | =oresmd://credit/... requires a ccy query key= |
| =ZC_INFLATIONCAPFLOOR= | 964 | =oresmd://inflation/... does not support the 'maturity' query key= |
| =YY_INFLATIONCAPFLOOR= | 298 | same as above |
| =INDEX_CDS_TRANCHE= | 8 | =requires a ccy query key= |
| =CDS_INDEX= | 6 | =requires a ccy query key= |

Probe 2 shows that the *series* identity fails too for the three credit index
families: 3,007 corpus keys import under a series URI that does not parse. The
rows are in the database. Nothing reads them back through the parser yet, so
nothing fails.

The other losses are case changes. The writer lower-cases the entity path
segment, and the parser upper-cases it and most coordinates. So
=EQUITY/PRICE/Lufthansa/EUR= comes back as =EQUITY/PRICE/LUFTHANSA/EUR=, and
=.../ATM/AtmFwd= comes back as =.../ATM/ATMFWD=. ORE names are case
sensitive. 1,845 of 1,866 =COMMODITY_OPTION= keys and 1,118 =EQUITY_OPTION=
keys lose their case this way. The capture
[[id:67A49A94-2588-4D17-BFCA-54813F3193B3][The forward projection uppercases free-text identifiers]]
recorded this fault in the in-memory path. That path is fixed. The URI path
still has it, and the capture still sits in the inbox as if nothing changed.

*** The ORE export echoes the input text

=import_service.cpp:358= stores the file's raw key in =market_observations.key=.
=ore_export_service.cpp:54= writes that column back out. So
=export_reproduces_the_market_data_file_it_imported= proves that a text column
survives a database round trip. It does not prove that the grammar can produce
the key.

The same column makes one row hold two identities that can disagree. When the
FX convention checker reverses a pair, the series and the datum URI take the
corrected pair, and =key= keeps the reversed one. Other writers
(=curve_republish_service.cpp:344=, =feed_ingest_loop.cpp:491=) store the
projected key instead. So the meaning of the column depends on which writer
wrote the row.

*** The corpus import compares values without their keys

=service_corpus_import_tests.cpp= compares the sorted multiset of values in the
file with the sorted multiset of values in the rows. Two values that change
places between keys pass. The test does not check that a value arrives under
its own key.

*** The model round-trip cases are text-only

The =* Round-trip= tables in the quote-type models check URI → identifier →
URI. That proves that the parser and the writer agree. It cannot prove that a
token is in the correct field, which is the fault in section 1.

*** Smaller defects in the tests

- =oresmd_ore_coverage_tests.cpp= has its own =key_of= tokeniser. It needs two
  commas on a CSV line, which is the fault that PR 2 fixed in the production
  parser. Lines with one comma drop out of the coverage count without notice.
- The comment above =expected_fully_round_tripped= says that four types still
  lose keys. The gap list is empty, and the four types (=COMMODITY_FWD=,
  =EQUITY=, =EQUITY_FWD=, =CORRELATION=) are missing from the expected set.
- =corpus_files.hpp= excludes =MD_*= files, but the rule is dead: those files do
  not contain "market" in their name, so the earlier rule already excludes
  them. Its comment also names =todaysmarketcalibration.csv=, which the code
  does not test for. The three =MD_*.csv= files are real market data in
  =DD.MM.YY= comma form, and the three =FD_*.csv= files are fixings in
  semicolon form. Neither reaches any corpus walk.
- I count 96 market payload files with the selection rule. The clean task's V08
  row says 118.

** 3. How clean the implementation and the code generation are

*** The grammar is generated in name only

The oresmd quote-type models declare the enums, the fields, the coordinates,
the reject keys and the test tables. Those parts are generated. The key
grammar itself is not:

- =oresmd_projections.cpp.mustache= has 2,585 lines and 187 template tags.
  =oresmd_parser.cpp.mustache= has 1,074 lines and 200 tags. Almost every
  =from_*= reader and =to_quote_key= branch is hand-written C++ inside the
  template.
- The model's =qualifier_shape= column only selects one of those hand-written
  functions. The segment layout is in the =notes= column as prose
  (="6-segment: ZERO/RATE/CCY/CURVE_ID/DAY_COUNT/POINT"=), and no code reads it.
- =model.org= carries =variant_order= and =parse_order= columns, and the IR
  model has a =URI order= table. Their only job is to reproduce the order of
  the hand-crafted files that the generator replaced.

So to add an ORE key form, you edit a 2,585-line template, not a model row.
That is the main obstacle to the code-generation goal.

*** Three descriptions of one key grammar

The same ORE key shapes are written down three times:

1. =ores_series_key_shapes_populate.sql= (203 lines), read by
   =ores.ore='s =series_key_registry= and used by =parse_market_data= and by
   =import_service= to split a key into =(series_type, metric, qualifier)=.
2. The oresmd models' =qualifier_shape= and =notes= columns.
3. The hand-written C++ in the two templates.

The import uses (1) to parse and classify, and (3) to name. A change to one is
not checked against the others except through the corpus.

*** The grammar story's acceptance, read against the code

| Acceptance | State |
|------------+-------|
| No key's absence carries meaning | *Not met.* The parser materialises =quote= and =model= defaults, and the writer emits them. But =from_ir_swap= drops a =2D= settlement (=oresmd_projections.cpp:1536=), and =to_quote_key= restores =2D= when =settle= is absent (=:531=). The design record still says that an absent =quote=, =metric= or =model= takes a default. |
| No coordinate is a comma-joined bag; each dimension is a named, typed key | *Partly met.* Each dimension is a named key. Every coordinate is =std::optional<std::string>=; =value_kind enum= is an annotation that nothing enforces, and =strike= is a string the parser upper-cases. Case handling differs by key: =maturity= and =tenor= are lower-cased, =expiry=, =delta= and =strike= upper-cased, =contract_month= kept. |
| Every key is declared identity or coordinate | Met in the models. |
| One canonical coordinate; =point_id= derived | Met. |
| The full corpus import runs by default | Met. The =[corpus-full]= tag is not hidden. |
| =market_data_urn.org= describes the shipped grammar | *Not met.* Its summary still calls oresmd "proposed", says "without per-asset-class variants", and describes the defaults above. |
| =todaysmarket.xml= ownership recorded | Met. |

The story's own =* Status= table still says "No implementation unit has
started".

*** Dead and duplicated code

- =oresmd_resolver= (306 generated lines) and =market_data_requirement= have no
  production caller. Under B06 that is dead code. The clean task's B06 row says
  the oresmd test-only symbols were closed.
- =to_curve_key= and =instrument_type::curve= have no production caller. The
  curves story already records this.
- Each of =market_observations= and =market_fixings= has two repositories: a
  generated singular one and a hand-written plural one. The record keeps the
  plural ones for =read_as_of=. H02 asks for one survivor.
- The =market_series= table comment (generated from the model prose) says that
  "those three columns carry the registry's decomposition". The columns are
  gone.

*** Comments

The hand-written code and the oresmd templates have a very high comment
density. Some functions have more comment than code. 14 comments narrate edit
history ("The silence the old grammar read as a default", "used to arrive
inside the point", "no longer"). Some of them come from the templates, so every
regeneration writes them again. Two hand-written end-of-line comments remain
(=crm_ingest_bridge.hpp:176=, =curve_republish_resolver.hpp:47=). The project
comment rules forbid all three patterns.

*** Test coverage

A name match finds no test for 73 of the 102 source files outside =main.cpp=.
Some of them are covered through other tests. But no repository test exists for
=feed_binding=, =observation_lineage= or =series_classification_rule=. No test
exists for any generated message handler, for =publish_from_dq_handler=, for
=curve_republish_service=, or for =market_data_client=. Only
=series_classification_rule= has an eventing integration test.

* Conclusions

1. *Modelling.* oresmd names 41 of ORE's 45 instrument types and every key the
   example corpus carries. It does not yet model ORE's market data in general.
   It models the forms the corpus uses. Outside the corpus, oresmd names 17 of
   the 40 documented forms I sampled, and at least four of those 17 wrongly. The readers choose fields by segment count. ORE chooses them by
   token value. That difference is the root of the wrong labels.

2. *Round tripping.* The suite proves that the in-memory projection is
   self-consistent on the corpus. It does not prove the stored path: 6.7% of
   corpus keys fail through the URI, and 4,269 keys produce URIs that the
   parser rejects. The suite cannot detect a wrong label, because every round trip
   compares text with text. The ORE export echoes a stored column, so it does
   not test the grammar.

3. *Implementation.* The component builds clean, its gates are green, its
   regeneration is byte-identical, and the fixing path is sound. But the key
   grammar is hand-written C++ inside templates, the same grammar lives in
   three places, the resolver is dead, and the comment volume breaks the
   project's rules.

4. *Checklist.* The story closed with its acceptance not met, and said so.
   That honesty is right. But the record is not reliable: two Fails are now
   Passes, 16 items are missing, V08 measured a different thing, and the
   registry lists the component with no accepted exceptions. Six of the 16
   missing items fail or are not done when checked.

* Recommendations

In order of value:

1. *Test the stored path.* Change the coverage gate to run
   =from_ore_key= → =to_uri= → =parse= → =to_quote_key= and to assert
   =to_uri(parse(u)) == u=. The same for =to_series_uri=. Fix the 4,269
   rejected URIs and the case loss. Keep the case of free-text names (entity,
   ticker, curve id, ATM type) exactly as ORE wrote it.

2. *Add a semantic oracle.* A text round trip cannot see a wrong field. Check
   the field assignment against ORE's own =parseMarketDatum= output, either by
   linking =OREData= in a test-only target or by a checked-in fixture that ORE
   produces. Feed it a catalogue of every form in =marketdatumparser.cpp=, not
   only the corpus.

3. *Make the segment grammar declarative.* Replace the =notes= prose and
   =qualifier_shape= with a machine-readable segment list per form (for
   example =IR_SWAP/RATE/{ccy}/{index?}/{settle}/{tenor}/{maturity}=, with the
   discriminating token values). Generate =from_ore_key=, =to_quote_key= and
   the SQL shape table from it, and retire the hand-written template code and
   the ordering columns. This is the step that makes "add an ORE form" a
   model edit.

4. *Make the export use the grammar.* Project the key from the datum URI and
   drop =market_observations.key=, or keep it only as an audit copy of the
   input, never as the export source. Then the export round trip tests the
   grammar, and a row cannot hold two identities.

5. *Close the type gaps.* Add the four missing types, accept the three aliases,
   and complete the =metric= enum.

6. *Correct the record.* Mark M06 and P04 Pass and close capture =386360B3=.
   Add =ACCEPTED_EXCEPTIONS= entries for W02, P01 and H01, or take
   =marketdata= out of the registry until they pass. Record the 16 missing
   items. Redo V08 as a per-file survey. Update =market_data_urn.org= and the
   grammar story's =* Status=. Move capture =67A49A94= to the URI path.

7. *Clean the code.* Put =marketdata= in the permission gate's =REGISTRY= and
   seed the codes. Add the three namespace headers. Remove or wire the
   resolver. Collapse the plural repositories. Cut the history narration from
   the templates and the hand-written code.

Recommendations 1, 2 and 3 are one story: they make the round trip prove
meaning, and they make the grammar a model. Recommendations 6 and 7 are a
clean-up task under the existing story.
