* Goal

Today anyone who holds the update permission changes a reporting line directly, with no second person. Make the change a proposal that the proposed new manager decides, as a role request is decided, so a line is never written without the person it makes responsible for someone agreeing to it.

* Acceptance

- A reporting line change is a proposal that names the person, the proposed manager and a reason; nothing changes while it waits.
- The proposed manager is the only decider. The person who proposed and the person it concerns are not the decider, and the decision is refused with a clear message when a stranger tries.
- The proposed manager sees the proposal in Requests, is told by a notification, and approves or declines it with a reason; a decline needs a comment.
- Only an approval writes the line, through the existing reporting line write with its loop check, and the history shows who proposed, who decided and when.
- The proposer sees the state of their proposal in their own requests and can withdraw it while it waits.
- The direct edit of the reporting line in the person page and the hierarchy is replaced by proposing a change.

* Context

This story comes from item P18 of the product owner's review of the people
screens (task =Address the review of the people screens=). The comment, in the
owner's words:

#+begin_quote
at the moment anyone can change hierarchy, its not 4 eyes. it should be
approved by the superior you propose to be your new boss. it should be like a
role request.
#+end_quote

** How it works today

- A person's reporting line is the =reports_to_account_id= field of their
  account. =iam.v1.ops.set_reporting_line= writes it on its own, with the
  account version as a precondition and a loop check, and records a change reason.
- The web offers it as a direct edit: *Change the reporting line* on the person
  page and the hierarchy. Anyone who holds =iam::accounts:update= changes any
  line at once. Nobody else sees it, and the manager it gives someone is never
  asked.
- The person page shows a disabled *Propose a change* button that says no
  approval exists yet; the =Draw the reporting line= journey owns the queue, the
  notification and the decision.

** What a role request does, which this follows

The inbox (=ores.inbox=) holds one record and one queue for anything that waits
on a person: an =approval_request= header (kind, who asked, why, state), a
decision, and a kind-owned detail table keyed by the request. A role request is
=iam.role_grant=: IAM raises the request as the person, records the roles it asks
for, and compensates by withdrawing the request if the second half fails. When
the request is approved the inbox publishes a change event, and IAM applies it
by reconciliation, so a missed event or a restart converges to the same result.
The record enforces four-eyes (the decider is not the asker), required comments
on a refusal, a deadline, and one open request per subject. Design:
=doc/knowledge/architecture/approval_requests.org=.

** What does not fit yet

The engine decides by permission only. A kind names =decide_permission_code=, and
anyone who holds it may decide any request of the kind. A reporting line
proposal is decided by one person: the one it would make the new manager. That
person holds no special permission, and no one else should be able to decide it,
even an administrator.

* Design

** The shape

| Part | Change |
|------+--------|
| Inbox, kind | =approval_kind= gains =decider_rule=: =permission= (today) or =named=. A =named= kind has no decide permission to check. |
| Inbox, request | =approval_request= gains an optional =assigned_decider_id=, set when the request is raised and fixed for its life. |
| Inbox, decide | For a =named= kind the decide operation accepts only the assigned decider, and still refuses the asker. The queue read offers a request to its assigned decider and to nobody else. |
| IAM, kind | The =iam.reporting_line= kind is seeded: =named=, refusal needs a comment, a deadline, no =held= state, one approval. |
| IAM, detail | =reporting_line_request=, keyed by the request: the account, the proposed manager, and the manager it replaces. Readable by the proposer, the person it concerns and the proposed manager. |
| IAM, propose | =propose_reporting_line= raises the request as the proposer and records the detail, and withdraws the request if the second half fails. It refuses a loop, a self-report and a second open proposal for the person. |
| IAM, apply | An approved, unapplied request is applied by reconciliation through =set_reporting_line=, as the decider, with the request as the change reason. The loop check runs again at apply time. |
| Web | *Change the reporting line* becomes *Propose a change*. Requests shows the proposal to its proposed manager with Approve and Decline; the proposer sees it among their own requests and can withdraw it. |

** Who may propose, who decides, who may not

- *Propose:* the person whose line it is, their current manager, or a holder of
  =iam::accounts:update=. The permission is kept for administrators; the person
  and their manager can propose without it. (To confirm: see Decisions.)
- *Decide:* only the proposed manager. Not the proposer, not the person, not an
  administrator.
- *No manager yet:* proposing a first manager for someone with none follows the
  same rule. Clearing a line (no manager) has no one to ask, so it stays an
  administrator's direct write, and says so.

** Edge cases the build must answer

- The proposed manager is out of the proposer's view (the hierarchy is party
  scoped). The proposal names them anyway, as a person, since the proposer chose
  them; the request detail is readable to all three parties.
- The tree changes while a proposal waits (the manager now reports to the
  person): the apply-time loop check refuses it and the request records why.
- The person's account changes while a proposal waits: the apply uses the
  current version, not the proposer's, and the loop check decides; only the
  manager field is written.
- The proposed manager is locked, removed or never signs in: the deadline
  expires the request, as for any kind.
- A second proposal for the same person while one waits is refused with a
  pointer to the first.

* Decisions

- The change is a proposal decided by the proposed manager, modelled on the
  role request. Source: the product owner, 2026-10-10.
- The engine gains a =named= decider rule and does not get a special case for
  this kind, because the next four-eyes kind will want it too.
- Open: who may propose (proposed above: the person, their manager, or an
  administrator). To settle with the product owner before task 3.
- Open: clearing a line stays a direct administrator write (proposed above).
  To settle with the product owner before task 5.

* Out of scope

- Approval by someone other than the proposed manager, such as the current
  manager or a second approver. The =named= rule leaves room for it.
- Changing the roles or the parties of a person. Only the reporting line.
- Bulk reorganisations. One proposal is one person.
- Pushed updates to the proposed manager's screen. The notification and the
  polled lists carry it; the story =Hear a change on the server and offer a
  refresh= owns the push.
