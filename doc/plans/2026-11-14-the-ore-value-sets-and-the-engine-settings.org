* Why this note exists

Nineteen routes of the reporting area's fifty were unassigned when this note was
written, and they are not one subject. The engine settings are a tree of named
parameters a person edits; the pricing engines are configurations with an ORE
document behind them; the ORE value sets are reference data the engine reads; and
one route tallies the health of a party's report definitions. Together they are
the family the task calls "the ORE value sets and the engine settings".

Two of the four translate between a screen's fields and an ORE XML document.
That translation is the question: it is the only place in this area where the
estate writes a format an outside program parses, and a copy of it in the
gateway would be a second version of the engine's own contract.

* What the BFF does today

** The engine settings

A page of settings, one setting with its parameters grouped, the write that
saves a group, the removal, the history of one setting, and a documentation
page built from the parameters themselves. The parameters are a tree: a group
is a field of the document, and a person edits the fields a group holds.

** The pricing engines

The same shape beside an ORE document: a page, one engine, a write, a removal,
its history, the document it renders, and an import with a preview that parses
an uploaded document and states what it would change. The import is the only
write in the area that reads a document a person did not build through a screen.

** The ORE value sets and the two moved reads**

Five routes: the value sets the engine reads, and the reference data reads the
task's Acceptance moved here from the reference data and pick-list tasks. One of
them answers a schema's contents as a zip, because a schema is complete only
with the files it includes.

** The health tally**

One read: the definitions of a party counted by the health word each carries.

* Where the cost lands

- **The gateway.** Nothing here composes across components the way a run's
  files did. The heavy part is the document translation, and it is the engine's.
- **The services.** The reporting service already holds the ORE documents the
  pricing engines render and the import parses, so the translation has a home
  that is not the gateway.
- **The screen.** A person editing a document through a parameter tree is the
  only reader who needs to know what the engine will object to, and the answer
  is the parse's.

* The decisions

** The document stays the service's

The gateway states a document as the service rendered it and hands an uploaded
one to the service to parse. It does not read XML, build XML or validate it.
The preview is the service's answer about what an import would change, which is
the same answer the import acts on -- one rule, one place.

** A setting's parameters are a tree the service names**

The groups and fields a screen draws are the service's, read with the setting.
The gateway groups nothing: a group that the gateway assembled would be a second
statement of the document's shape.

** The health tally is one read**

No composition, no derivation: the service counts and the route states the
counts and their total. It belongs to this family only because it was the last
route unassigned.

** The schema zip keeps its own route**

=GET /api/reporting/ore-files/:code/schema= answers a zip of a schema and every
schema it includes, at the engine version asked for. It is the one route in the
area that answers a binary made of several stored objects, and a missing include
is a 404 that names what is missing rather than a partial zip.

* The work, and who does it

The family is four units, and each is a verifiable step on its own:

| Unit | Routes | What it needs |
|------+--------+---------------|
| The health tally | 1 | one read, no composition |
| The engine settings | 8 | the settings model, its groups, its history and its documentation |
| The pricing engines | 10 | the engines model, its history, its document and the import with its preview |
| The ORE value sets and the schema's zip | 5 | the value sets and the schema's includes |

Twenty-four routes. The engine settings count is eight and not seven: the page,
one setting, its history, the documentation, the create, the update, the group
write and the removal.

Nothing here needs a service change that the first three families did not already
make: every read exists, and the document translation is where it belongs.

** Where the work landed

Two facts learned while splitting the units into tasks, recorded here so the next
reader does not have to rediscover them.

The value set reader belongs to neither the reporting part nor the reference data
part alone. The five routes the reporting task's Acceptance moves here are
reference data area routes, and the reference data part answers other convention
routes already. The reporting part links =ores.refdata.api=, so a reader in it
would close a cycle between the two part libraries. The reader lives in
=ores.http.core=, beside the history and change route helpers, and both parts
link it.

The pricing engines' vocabulary is a route the screen calls, at
=packages/web/src/api/pricingEngines.ts=, though the inventory states it as not
an API and in no area. It is served for the reason =choices= was served: the
screen breaks without it when the BFF goes.

* What this does not change

The engine keeps owning the document's shape, and the reporting service keeps
holding it. Nothing is translated twice and no screen learns the XML.
