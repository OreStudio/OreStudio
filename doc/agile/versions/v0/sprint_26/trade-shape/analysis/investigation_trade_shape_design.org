A point-in-time record, written while the story that commissioned it was open, of an [[id:A7F8D9E2-3C4B-4A5D-B67C-8E9F0A1B2C3D][investigation]] into the target shape of the trade on data-oriented principles.

It is a snapshot, not a description of the system. Whatever it concludes that the code must keep doing belongs on the page that owns the subject; cite that page from code, recipes and models, not this one.

* Context

The question came out of the review of PR 2496, which closed the
[[id:0DC1BAC7-DDAE-4F64-B800-49FBF8D723C1][Clean ores.trading]] story. =trade.hpp= defines =trade= as a struct of
five sub-structs (identity, parties, classification, lifecycle, audit),
which reads as the aggregate entity that data-oriented design argues
against ("there is no entity",
https://www.dataorienteddesign.com/dodmain/node5.html). The owner asked
why the type exists, what =trade_envelope= and =trade_identifier= add,
and whether the trade and its join to the instruments hold together.

** What the current model is

Read on main at =1786aa23cd= (2026-10-02).

- =trade= maps one-to-one to =ores_trading_trades_tbl=, 19 columns plus
  the temporal and audit columns. It holds no instrument, envelope,
  identifier or party role; those are separate tables keyed by the
  trade. At the top level the model already follows "there is no
  entity".
- The five sub-structs are not tables, are never loaded separately, and
  are used by no other model. The trade model states their reason: to
  keep each reflected struct under MSVC's C1202 limit in reflect-cpp's
  reflection (a reason section 16's correction shows does not hold for
  plain structs). They also fix the wire as nested JSON. =trade_audit= is a
  field-for-field copy of =ores.dq.audit_record=, which every
  instrument uses. The split is driven by a serialiser limit, not by
  any access pattern.
- The real aggregates are the export carriers: =trade_instrument= (a
  variant over eight families), the =*_data= carriers,
  =trade_export_item= and =instrument_payload=, built for the ORE
  import and export journey.

** What does not hold together

1. The family routing lives in three places: =trade_types.product_type=,
   a string =switch= in the hand-written export handler that maps
   trade-type codes to 30 instrument tables, and a check constraint on
   each instrument table's =trade_type_code=. Nothing checks that they
   agree.
2. =trades.product_type= is a nullable copy of
   =trade_types.product_type=, kept in step by nothing, and the
   dispatch reads the copy.
3. All 30 instrument tables copy =trade_type_code= with no check
   against the trade, and their trigger takes =party_id= from the
   session (=app.current_party_id=) while the trade derives its own
   from the book. The two can disagree, and row-level security filters
   on =party_id=.
4. Nothing stops one trade having rows in two instrument tables.
5. The counterparty is stated three times: =trades.counterparty_id=,
   =trade_party_role= and =trade_envelope.counter_party= (document
   text). The netting set twice.
6. Identifiers have two homes: =external_id= is the trade's key, and
   =trade_identifier= holds UTI, USI and the rest.
7. =trade_envelope=, its two children and =trade_identifier= never
   check that their trade exists; =trade_party_role= does.
8. Smaller: =trade_party_role= writes to =ores_trading_party_roles_tbl=;
   =trade_types= keeps two columns that only drove Qt forms; the
   instrument rebuild is a third hand-written operation besides the two
   exports P04 names.

* Paths taken

The design moved in sections. Each records the decision and what it
set aside.

** 1. Anchor and components

/Decided/: the trade becomes an immutable anchor row plus component
tables keyed by the trade, one per access pattern, each versioning on
its own timeline.

- The anchor holds only facts fixed for the trade's life. Because it
  never changes, other tables reference it with real foreign keys
  instead of trigger-checked soft keys against temporal rows.
- A copy is allowed only of an immutable fact, and is pinned by a
  composite foreign key, so it cannot drift. Instruments may carry
  =trade_type= and =party_id= (row-level security needs the party on
  every row) only through a key to the anchor. Each instrument table
  checks that =trade_type= is one of its own types, and the lists are
  disjoint, so a trade's instrument can sit in exactly one table. That
  closes findings 1 to 4.
- The five field groups go: each table is narrow (see section 16's
  correction on what the C1202 limit counts). =trade_audit= becomes =ores.dq.audit_record=;
  =instrument_identity= loses its unpinned copies and becomes the one
  shared key group.

/Set aside/: keeping =trade= as one wide temporal row, which versions
booking, lifecycle and classification together on every change.

** 2. Identity and the trade id

- =external_id= leaves the anchor. Every identifier scheme — UTI, USI,
  a legacy system's id, ORE's =Trade/@id= — is a row of
  =trade_identifier= (scheme, value, issuer), with a real foreign key
  to the anchor. That closes finding 6.
- The firm's own id is the anchor's UUID, called the *trade id*
  throughout; "deal id" is not used.
- /Set aside/: a human-readable deal number on the anchor, and the
  firm's number as one more scheme.

** 3. Party, counterparty and cancel-and-rebook

- The firm's legal entity (party) and the counterparty are fixed for a
  trade's life and sit on the anchor. A change of either is a cancel
  and rebook: the old trade is cancelled, a new trade is booked, and a
  typed link joins them (novation for the counterparty).
- =successor_trade_id= goes; the link carries the relation. The
  knowledge notes described the field two contradictory ways, and that
  is now resolved in Trade Versioning.

** 4. Links, sets and groups

The trade-links documentation merged on main during the design. Its
catalogue filed structures, merges, compressions, allocations and
process runs as links. The owner's principle: where a collection of
trades is named and an operation attaches to it, it is an entity with
an identity, not a by-product of links.

- The vocabulary of the analysis notes was adopted: a /set/ is trades
  sharing an attribute (book, counterparty, netting set), with the
  identity on the attribute's entity; a /group/ is trades tied to an
  operation, with an identity of its own (a structure, a compression);
  a /link/ joins particular trades for a reason and holds nothing.
- With the groups taken out, a link joins two trades, so the pairwise
  =trade_link= (from trade, to trade, type) is enough. Each link type
  names its two roles and states whether it has an economic effect.
- Exercise joined the catalogue: exercised option to resulting trade.
- Restructure is an amendment of the same trade; a roll that books a
  replacement is a link.
- /Set aside/: one link record with several ends, and links plus a
  separate run table.

Recorded in [[id:2AF58405-4F3D-4B74-AAD0-6A97E7DA215F][Trade sets, groups and links]],
[[id:5F12A37F-9F26-4B49-BAAF-1BAA7B2BB94F][Netting sets]], [[id:9BC498E0-91ED-4CA4-A7D7-7EF15684E799][Trade compression]],
[[id:BBD96309-7C59-4D94-BDA7-27EAB7A8612E][Trade merge]] and [[id:73766E2D-D01B-4628-A1A0-78C9BBF709F6][Trade allocation]], with the link
catalogue, Trade Links, Trade Versioning and Trade Activity brought
into line.

** 5. Book moves

Researched before deciding. No rule forbids moving a trade between two
books that share a legal entity, a branch, a regulatory book and an
accounting classification; the restrictions apply when a move crosses
one of them (RBC25.14-16 for the trading and banking book, IFRS 9 4.4.1
for classification). The accounting constraint people remember governs
the ledger postings, not the trade.

/Decided/: a move within those four lines amends the trade's booking,
cuts an internal version with /book move/ as the reason, and carries its
fair value and whether historic cash flows move. Any other change of
book is a close-out and rebooking, linked, or internal risk transfer
trades. The temporal booking keeps the book history, which removes the
P&L break the analysis notes describe.

Recorded in [[id:6CB4879F-3F4B-48CE-A01E-E1B9AEE866B0][Book moves]].

** 6. Dates

/Decided/: the dates are economic terms and live on the instrument;
each family states them in its own words. The blotter maps them to
common names (start, end, expiry, settlement) through one generated
view.

/Set aside/: dates copied onto the booking, and a separate dates table.

** 7. The operations queue

Authorisation is stored as decisions, not as a status column.

- =authorisation_decision=: the unit, the internal version decided on,
  the decision (authorise, deny, hold over, return to unseen,
  un-authorise), hold reason, a required comment, amendment reason,
  department, operational-error flag, escalation, who or which rule
  decided, and when. =auto_authorisation_rule= holds Operations'
  rules.
- The status is derived: a unit is authorised when its latest decision
  is authorise and covers the current version, so an amendment
  withdraws the release without anything resetting it.
- Denied is final, enforced in the schema. Decisions are append-only
  metadata and cut no version.
- /Decided/: the unit Operations authorises is the unit the customer
  confirms — a structure that confirms as a whole, or each trade.

** 8. One activity per version

/Decided/: =trade_activity= records each business event — type, actor,
time, comment, operational-error flag — and every component version
carries a foreign key to the activity that caused it. The activity
type is reference data stating the economic, confirmable and real axes
and the priority order. The external version moves only for a
confirmable activity; a null amend is recorded and versions nothing.

/Set aside/: a reason code per row grouped by transaction time, and an
activity table for confirmable events only.

** 9. Counterparty and netting set

- The anchor gains =counterparty_id=, immutable like =party_id=.
- =trade_booking= keeps =netting_set_id=, versioned, because
  re-papering can move a trade to another set.
- A composite key from the booking to =netting_set= (set, counterparty,
  party), pinned to the anchor, stops a trade being filed in another
  counterparty's or another entity's set.
- =trade_party_role= keeps only the other roles: broker, sales person,
  agent, calculation agent.

** 10. The ORE envelope

/Decided/: import resolves the envelope's counterparty name and
portfolio ids to entities and rejects a document that does not
resolve; export regenerates them. =AdditionalFields= becomes
=trade_additional_field= (name, value, order). That closes finding 5.

/Set aside/: a provenance copy of the document's text, and keeping
today's envelope tables.

** 11. Structures

- =structure= is an immutable anchor: tenant, party, counterparty,
  kind, template.
- =structure_kind= states whether the kind confirms as a whole
  (strategy, typed, dynamic) or trade by trade (package).
- =structure_template= states the roles and leg counts a strategy or
  typed product allows, checked when a leg is linked.
- =structure_member=, keyed by =trade_id=, is temporal: a trade sits in
  at most one structure at a time and can be linked and unlinked.
- A structure's legs share its party and counterparty.
- /Decided/: one level of nesting, two levels in total, through a
  nullable =parent_structure_id= and a check that a structure with a
  parent has no children.

** 12. Acts

/Decided/: an act is an activity. =trade_activity_participant=
(activity, trade, role) replaces the activity's single unit, and each
kind of act adds a detail table keyed by the activity: compression
(provider, cycle reference), allocation (block, source, template),
merge (policy, expiry-netting style), runs (process, run date), bulk
book move (books, cash flows moved). Links keep the activity that
created them.

/Set aside/: groups as separate entities pointing at an activity, and a
table pair per kind.

** 13. Workspaces, and what replaced them

The design first kept a copy of a trade in another workspace under the
same trade id, with the workspace in every key. It paused there, because
the layered workspace design overrides a parent's rows copy-on-write and
was never designed for a trade split into components.

The owner then reframed the question from the use cases rather than the
data model. Users need two things without disturbing official data: to
import trades and market data, and to experiment with trades and market
data and run reports against the changes. Prior art — Calypso's pricing
environment, Murex's what-if simulation and hypothetical trades, ORE's
run-time stress scenarios, and the analysis notes on personal
portfolios, test books and perturbed market data — isolates by explicit
inputs and by classification, not by a copy of everything. A layered
workspace is an application-level database branch, and it pushes a
workspace into every key, query and foreign key.

/Decided/: workspaces are removed from the trade model. The
[[id:4AB0BC63-D73A-4FC3-B9AF-16C1BB90653F][Sandbox]] replaces them:

- A sandbox is an entity anchored to a node of the official portfolio
  tree (a business, a desk), opened only by users holding that right at
  the node, private or shared through the anchor's permissions so desks
  keep their Chinese walls. A single tenant-wide sandbox portfolio was
  considered and rejected for exactly that reason.
- Its books are /virtual books/: created by the trading system, never
  mapped to the ledger, holding no position of the firm.
- A trade in a virtual book is a draft recorded before any agreement, a
  test or a hypothetical, and is never live. A draft becomes real by
  Execute into a real book; a hypothetical by a new actual trade linked
  to it. Both go through the ordinary lifecycle and authorisation.
- Real concluded deals are never parked in the sandbox: they are booked
  into real books at once and held as unauthorised until Operations
  releases them.
- Imported and perturbed market data sit in the universe under their
  own source; the official configuration does not resolve to them. A
  report definition selects its inputs explicitly.
- The regulatory boundary was checked: Basel RBC25.1-2 (every
  instrument held is in the trading or banking book), MAR12, EMIR
  Article 9 (reporting of concluded contracts), MiFIR Article 25 and
  MiFID II Article 16(6) (record keeping). Each is met by the rule that
  a sandbox trade is never live.

The anchor's key returns to (tenant, id), and the real foreign keys need
no workspace.

** 14. The instrument tables

The instrument refactor began with the bonds, and the clean-trading
story's decisions record its outcome (D10, D18, D19, D21, D28, D29). It
is not one table per trade type, nor one wide table per family, but a
factoring of the shape:

| Level | Table | Holds |
|-------+-------+-------|
| Family header | One per family | The fields every product in the family shares; a =trade_type= check listing exactly the family's codes; keyed by the trade and pinned to the anchor |
| Product facts | One per product with fields of its own, zero or one per trade | Only those fields; keyed by the trade, with a foreign key to the header and a check on its own codes |
| Shared children | Legs, schedules, option block, strikes, premiums | Keyed by the trade, an ordinal and a role code; the role is data, not a table |
| Security | Issue or contract | The terms the issuer fixes, keyed by the reference datum id and shared by every trade on it |

/Decided/: every instrument family is reworked to this rule, as the
bonds were. How far a family can be cleaned is a function of how clean
its data model becomes under the data-oriented review; the rule is the
target, and the review decides each family's tables. The rates, FX,
equity, credit and commodity families are still to do: ten of their
tables share one list of all 121 codes, five equity tables hold two
products each (defect 9), and the risk participation table has no
constraint.

The routing from trade type to instrument table becomes data: each
trade type names the table that holds it, and the generator produces
the dispatch the export handler hand-codes today.

/Set aside/: one table per distinct full shape, which repeats the
header's columns in every product table, and one table per ORE trade
type.

** 15. Where each entity lives

/Decided/: trading owns what describes trades; reference data owns
what exists independently of any trade.

| Entity | Component |
|--------+-----------|
| Trade anchor, components, instruments, links, activities, structures, agreement | ores.trading |
| =authorisation_decision=, =auto_authorisation_rule= | ores.trading |
| =trade_link_type=, =trade_activity_type=, =structure_kind=, =structure_template= | ores.trading |
| =netting_agreement=, =netting_set=, =csa= | ores.refdata |
| =sandbox=, the virtual flag on a book | ores.refdata |

Every foreign key between the two runs from trading into reference data:
a trade names a book, a counterparty, a netting set; reference data
never names a trade. A netting set exists before its first trade and
outlives its last.

/Set aside/: a credit component created now for agreements, netting sets
and CSAs, before anything in it computes; and placing them in trading.

** 16. The C1202 check

/Superseded on 2026-10-03/: the nine-field limit below does not hold.
220 of the 287 reflected structs the models generate have more than nine
members and compile on MSVC. The failure the CI log shows comes from a
tagged variant write over a large carrier; see the decomposition section of Data-Oriented

below still show that each table is narrow.

Every reflected struct keeps nine effective fields or fewer, a nested
group counting as one.

| Table | Count | Outcome |

* Conclusions

** The target model so far

| Table | Key | Content | Changes |
|-------+-----+---------+---------|
| =trade= (anchor) | tenant, id | party, counterparty, trade type, counterparty scope, booking nature, origin, audit | Never |
| =trade_booking= | trade | book, netting set, trade date, execution time | Temporal: moves, re-papering |
| =trade_state= | trade | trade status | Temporal: lifecycle events |
| =trade_identifier= | trade, scheme | value, issuer | When a scheme is added |
| =trade_party_role= | trade, role | the other parties | When a role is added |
| =trade_additional_field= | trade, order | name, value | With the trade |
| Instrument family tables | trade | the economics, including the dates | Temporal: economic amendments |
| =trade_link=, =trade_link_type= | from, to, type | roles, economic effect | When a link is made or removed |
| =trade_activity=, =trade_activity_type= | id | type, actor, time, comment; per-type axes and priority | Append only |
| =trade_activity_participant= | activity, trade | role | With the activity |
| Act detail tables | activity | per kind | With the activity |
| =authorisation_decision=, =auto_authorisation_rule= | id | the decision and its context | Append only |
| =agreement= | structure or trade | external version, confirmation status | On a confirmable activity |
| =structure=, =structure_kind=, =structure_template= | id | kind, template, parent | Never; reference data |
| =structure_member= | trade | structure, role, sequence | Temporal: link and unlink |
| =netting_set= | id | agreement, counterparty, legal entity, limit, call type, initial margin type | Reference data |

** The integrity the schema carries

- Real foreign keys to the immutable anchors, from every component,
  link end, participant, member, identifier, decision and agreement.
- Composite keys pin every copied fact: an instrument's trade type and
  party, a booking's netting set to the trade's counterparty and party,
  a structure's legs to its party and counterparty.
- Disjoint trade-type checks put each trade's instrument in exactly one
  table.
- A book move within the four-attribute boundary is the only change of
  book; anything else is rejected as a move.
- Denied authorisation is final; the authorised status is derived.
- Structures nest one level at most.
- A trade's booking names a virtual book only while the trade is a
  pre-agreement draft, a test or a hypothetical; going live requires a
  real book in the same write.

* Recommendations

** Workspaces are settled

The sandbox replaces workspaces in the trade model; the five questions
the layered design raised no longer apply. Removing workspaces from the
other entities that carry them, and from report definitions, is a
separate piece of work this design depends on only in that trades no
longer carry one.

** Build it

The implementation is scaffolded as stories, listed on the
[[id:7C7714A5-A590-4AEC-BDF9-9BF5A76E03F2][trade shape story]]: the code generator support first, then
netting agreements and sandboxes in reference data, then the split of
the trade, activities and links, authorisation and the agreement, and
structures. Each instrument family is a story of its own in the backlog,
reworked to the rule of section 14. The knowledge notes still say "deal
id" in places, and a task on the story renames it to trade id.
