* Plan

The part is stood up by the adapter recipe, copied from =ores.http.marketdata=:
its model, its CMake files, the composite's parts list, the umbrella header,
the aggregate, the file lists, the diagram, and the server's link and
registration. It links =ores.reporting.api=, =ores.analytics.api= and
=ores.refdata.api=.

The area's routes are ported in groups:

| Group | Routes | BFF source |
|-------+--------+------------|
| The part and the form's codes | 1 | =reporting.ts= |
| The report definitions and their runs | 15 | =reporting.ts= |
| The cuts, the cut kinds and the configurations | 10 | =reporting.ts= |
| The pricing engines | 10 | =pricing-engines.ts= |
| The engine settings and the ORE files | 9 | =engine-settings.ts= |
| The ORE value sets and the moved reference data reads | 5 | =ore-value-sets.ts=, =curve-configuration.ts= |

Fifty routes in all, of which ten are served. The conventions documents and
the curve configurations are the five routes the task's Acceptance moves here
from the reference data and pick list tasks.

The composed views belong to their own tasks: the report health tally, the
publishing queue and the run board among them, which is why the health tally
is not in the count above.

The four families that remain are designed before they are served, one at a
time, because each asks a question the code cannot answer on its own:

| Family | Design |
|--------+--------|
| A run's files and results | [[id:6C1F0B2E-58A4-41D9-9E52-3B2F7A1D4C88][A run's files and results: where the work belongs]] -- composed across three services and fetched whole over HTTP today |
| The cuts and the cut kinds | Not written |
| The schedule writes | Not written |
| The ORE value sets and the engine settings | Not written |

The five routes this task's Acceptance moves here -- =GET
/api/conventions/:family/documents/:id=, =PUT .../documents/:id/terms=,
=GET /api/refdata/curve-configurations= and =GET= and =PUT .../:id= -- stay
unpaired until the value sets family is designed, because the reader that
answers them is the one that family owns. They are named here so the inventory
gap is a decision and not an oversight.
