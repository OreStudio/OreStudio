* Summary

The practice of fixings implies a small set of entities: a /source/, with its
administrator, method, calendar and status; a /definition/, which is one
source's series for one asset, such as the WM/Reuters 4pm fix of AUD/CAD; an
/observation/ of a definition on a fixing date; an /override/ of an
observation; a trade's /schedule/ of fixing dates; and the /event/ that
authorises or undoes an observation. ORE Studio already holds definitions, as
ORE indices, and observations, as versioned fixing series. It does not yet hold
the source as an entity, the producer of each definition, the status of a
source, or the authorisation of an observation.

The three invariants of [[id:C6A619F3-B2B6-42D5-9C41-5269A84BC96C][observation storage]] apply: an
observation is never stamped on a trade, its history is append-only, and the
official observations of a definition form an unbroken prefix in time.

** The producer is separate from the source

A source says who is responsible for a value and how it is made. A producer
says how values reach the store. The two coincide for a live public source,
whose producer is an adapter that observes the publisher. They come apart in
three cases that matter here:

- *No free publication.* EURIBOR is published under licence, so a
  demonstration has no observed values for it. A simulated administrator
  produces them instead.
- *A ceased source.* LIBOR is no longer published. ORE Studio keeps it as a
  legacy source for teaching, with a simulated producer and a status that says
  it has ceased.
- *A derived source.* A cross fixing is produced from its parent definitions,
  not from a publisher of its own.

A simulated administrator fixes its definition once per fixing date from the
simulated market, as a real one fixes from the real market: an interest rate
index from the curve of its tenor, an FX index from the pair's spot at the
source's fixing time. It is not a quote feed, because a quote and a fixing are
different kinds of datum; see
[[id:86DA6985-C92E-40F6-AE43-71C4BBF126CB][A fixing is an agreed observation that settles a contract]].

** What ORE Studio holds today

| Entity | Held | Where |
|--------+------+-------|
| Definition | Yes | The oresmd fixing URI and its =market_index= model, one per ORE index. See [[id:C3E053CA-0D4B-480B-9119-E11530160EC1][oresmd: ORE Studio Market Data URI]]. |
| Observation | Yes, without origin or status | A versioned row of the fixing series of the definition, under the system party. See [[id:F4BBEF34-D84C-4AE6-A3AA-DC47C3EE06EE][Public publishers publish daily fixings through a supplier session]]. |
| Source | In part | The FX publisher is a field of the FX index (=source=ECB=); the public publishers are a catalogue with terms. No source holds a method, a calendar, a status or a version, and an interest rate index names no source at all. |
| Producer | Implicitly | The public adapters observe the public publishers. Each synthetic interest rate curve writes a simulated history of its own index. Nothing writes FX fixings for a source without a publisher. |
| Override, event, schedule | No | |

The gap that matters most is the missing source and producer. Without them the
system cannot answer which definitions the market fixes, who fixes each, and
whether the values are observed or simulated. Trade generation and fixing
checks both need that answer: a generated trade should name only a definition
that has a producer.

** Open questions

- How a derived observation is timed when its parents fix at different times:
  whether it waits for the later parent, and what it shows until then.
- How a definition records the version of its source in force on a trade date,
  so that a trade reads the terms it was agreed under.
- Whether a keyed future observation is an observation with a status, or a
  separate record that becomes an observation when it takes effect.
