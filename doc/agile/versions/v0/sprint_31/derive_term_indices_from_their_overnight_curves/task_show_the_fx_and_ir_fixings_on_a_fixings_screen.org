* Goal

The web UI has a Quotes screen, a Fixing series page that shows one item opened by its URI, and a Public rates screen that shows only the official publishers. Nothing lists the fixings. Add a system-level Fixings screen beside Quotes, for the super administrator, that lists the FX and IR fixing observations across every source: the index or pair, the source (TR20H, ECB, a curve's index), the publisher or generator, the latest value and its date, and how many days the series holds. A row opens the Fixing series page. It shows the fixings the platform
states and no way to change them: a series that ends because its curve
feed stopped is shown ending, not filled, because the system states what
it observed. A party that needs the missing day enters it by hand, with
an explanation, in the fixings manager that captures it: see
[[file:doc/agile/product_backlog/inbox/the_party_fixings_manager_enters_a_fixing_the_plat.org][The party fixings manager enters a fixing the platform never stated]]. The screen shows observations, which are system-level facts, and holds no authorisation; a party's fixings manager, which adopts observations for the party's trades, is a separate piece of work. See [[id:FA5C96A3-1F8E-4F9B-8973-40D92055D7DD][System-level fixing observations and party-level fixings]].

* Plan

*The screen is called Fixing observations, and it is the system view, not the fixings manager.* It states what the
sources published and offers no act; the party's fixings manager is another
screen, where a party requires, adopts, overrides and authorises the fixings its
trades use. The four fixing surfaces and the question each answers are set out in
[[id:5D2A8C41-6E3B-47F0-9A28-1C4B7E9D3F62][The fixing screens and what each one is for]].

Two consequences for this task:

- The list takes =/market-data/fixing-observations=, and the fixing series page a
  row opens keeps =/market-data/fixings?uri=, so the two names and the two
  addresses do not collide.
- Nothing on the screen offers to fill a missing day, to authorise a value, or
  to override one. A series that ended is shown ending.

The screen lists every FX and IR fixing series the store holds, one row each:
the index or pair, the source, the publisher, the latest value and its date,
and how many days the series holds. No existing operation answers that.
=GET /api/marketdata/fixing-series?uri=...= answers one series and builds its
answer from =list_market_series=, =list_market_fixings= and the catalogue
lists, which over every series would read hundreds of thousands of fixing rows.
So the summary is computed in SQL by the market data service, and the screen
reads it.

1. *The summary operation.* =ores.marketdata.fixing_read_operations= already
   holds the reads of the durable fixings, so the summary joins it rather than
   opening a model of its own: =fixing_series_summary= (the series id, its
   address, its asset class and display label, the source and provider, the
   latest value and date, and the day count),
   =list_fixing_series_summaries_request= with the asset-class, currency and
   source filters, and its response. =compass generate= writes the protocol,
   the codec, the TypeScript wire type and the round-trip test.
2. *The query and its handler.* One statement over the series and its fixings,
   the series left-joined so a series that holds no fixing keeps its row with a
   count of zero. The handler sits beside =party_demand_handler.hpp=, runs in
   the system tenant because the screen is system-level, asks the
   =market_fixings:read= permission the fixing reads use, and is subscribed
   beside the other market data operations.
3. *The route on both servers.* A route on the C++ =ores.http= server and the
   matching one on the TypeScript BFF, because the web app is served by both:
   =GET /api/marketdata/fixings= with the three filters, each side with its own
   test.
4. *The screen.* A =FixingsPage= beside the Fixing series page it opens, a
   table of the summaries with the three filters, and a row that opens
   =FixingSeriesPage= by the series' address. A series with no day reads
   *empty* rather than disappearing. No edit, approve or override control: the
   system states what it observed, and a party's fixings manager is another
   task's work.
5. *Prove it.* A test of the handler's query, the route tests on both servers,
   the screen's test, then a fresh provision with the screen showing the
   simulated, ECB, BOC, IMF and TCMB series and the counts the fixing series
   hold.

* Result

The platform has a screen that answers what it states. *Market data › Fixing
observations* lists one row per FX and IR fixing series the store holds: what it
fixes, its class, the underlying, the source its values came from, the publisher,
the value of its latest day, that day and the count of days. A row opens the
fixing series page by the series' address, and the page's trail runs back through
the list. A series that holds no day keeps its row and reads *no day* with a
count of zero, because a series that ended is what an administrator asks about.
The three filters are in the address, so a view can be sent to a colleague. No
control on the screen edits, approves or overrides a fixing: the screen states
observations, and the party's fixings manager is another screen.

The read runs in the database, not over the wire. =fixing_series_summary= joins
the series to its fixings once, left-joined so an empty series survives, and
answers the latest value, the latest day and the count. The handler sits in
=ores.marketdata= behind =market_fixings:read=, in the system tenant, and a
route each on =ores.http= and on the gateway serve it at
=/api/marketdata/fixing-observations=.

It was built after its journey and its prototype, not before: the journey
[[id:2B8F5C71-4A6D-4E39-9B12-6C7D0E3A5F84][See the fixing observations the platform states]]
and the prototype [[id:7E4C2F58-3B91-42D0-8C6A-9F15E2B7D348][Fixing observations]]
were signed off on 2026-11-26, and the screen's columns are the prototype's
columns.

Two pieces of work are left, and neither belongs to this task's acceptance.
The list prints the publisher's *code* where the series page beside it prints the
publisher's name, and it states the benchmark only inside the address; that is
captured as
[[file:doc/agile/product_backlog/inbox/the-fixings-list-states-the-publisher-by-code.org][The fixings list states the publisher by code, not by name]].
The gate's own instrument for a screen, the browser harness and the sign-off
sheet of [[id:BB919630-E0C1-42A6-965D-109EBE52227A][How do I sign off a screen?]],
was not run: the screen was walked by hand, which produced the trail and the
title change, and the sheet is owed to the story that finishes the fixing
screens.
