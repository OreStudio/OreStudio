#+title: Give the synthetic FX vol surface a dynamic SSVI gamma
#+description: The generator fixes gamma at one half and ignores the start's gamma; let gamma move, as a bounded driver, a function of the state or a regime, and hold each published slice to the no-arbitrage conditions that then depend on theta.
#+type: capture
#+level: cross
#+filetags: :synthetic:volatility:ssvi:quantitative_finance:
#+created: 2026-11-20
#+updated: 2026-11-20

This page is a [[id:671F18E4-E09C-4B3B-BD24-D33DF8AE38A6][capture]] in the *inbox* bucket of the product backlog — a pre-sprint idea, not yet pulled into a sprint as a story.

* What

=fx_vol_surface_generator= in =ores.analytics.quant= draws the synthetic FX
volatility surface as SSVI with gamma fixed at 1/2, so the curvature is
phi(theta) = eta / sqrt(theta). =make= ignores the start's gamma. Make gamma
move. An analysis by Gemini, pasted on 2026-11-20, gives three ways to do it:

1. /A bounded mean-reverting driver/, beside =skew_driver_= and
   =curvature_driver_=. It reverts to the start's gamma and maps onto an open
   interval such as (0.1, 0.9) or (0.25, 0.75) through a logistic function.
   =fx_vol_surface_dynamics= gains =gamma_reversion= and =gamma_volatility=, the
   correlation matrix and =factor_count()= grow by one (the gamma factor after
   the curvature factor), =make= validates the start's gamma instead of
   ignoring it, and =publish()= writes the driver into the surface's gamma.
2. /A function of the existing state/, for example of eta or of the term
   structure's slope. No new factor and no change to the correlation layout,
   but gamma is then tied to the other parameters.
3. /A gamma per regime or per path/: constant within a regime, switched
   between discrete regimes such as calm and stressed, not stepped every dt.

* Why

The calibration engine already fits gamma, as QuantExt does (story decision of
2026-11-09 in [[id:95541791-E25C-44A5-9625-C8A1E44976D7][Support volatility surfaces — the remaining work]]), and a fitted gamma on a real-shaped
smile is far from 1/2 (0.28 on a Heston surface). A generator that only ever
produces gamma = 1/2 does not test that fit across the range it will see.

The cost is the no-arbitrage conditions. With gamma = 1/2 the butterfly
condition theta * phi(theta) * (1 + |rho|) < 4 holds for every eta in (0, sqrt 2)
while theta < 2. For a general gamma it becomes
eta * theta^(1 - gamma) * (1 + |rho|) < 4, which depends on theta. So either it
is checked on every expiry slice at every step, or gamma is held at or above
1/2 and eta is bounded for the largest theta. A gamma and an eta that move
while the forward variance increments are small can also make the total
variance at a fixed log-moneyness fall with maturity. That is calendar
arbitrage, so the published slices must be checked or constrained for it,
because the consumers assume arbitrage-free quotes.

* What QuantExt does

Read from the ORE source (=QuantExt/qle/termstructures/sviparametricvolatility.cpp=
and =svimodeltraits.cpp=), not run.

- QuantExt calibrates. It does not simulate, so it has no dynamic gamma. Gamma
  is fitted once for each calibration, on one as-of date.
- =Gatheral2012SsviPowerLaw= has the parameters theta, rho, eta and gamma, with
  phi = eta * theta^(-gamma). The defaults start eta and gamma at 0.5, and
  theta is implied from the ATM quote. Gamma is bounded to (1e-6, 1 - 1e-6).
- =SsviParametricVolatility::calibrate()= solves one global problem: one theta
  for each expiry and one rho, eta and gamma shared by every expiry. That is the
  surface our calibration engine fits.
- With =enforceNoArbitrage= (on by default) a =PowerLawConstraint= tests both
  Gatheral–Jacquier butterfly conditions on every theta: theta below
  (4 / (eta (1 + |rho|)))^(1 / (1 - gamma)), and the second condition's bound,
  which is an upper bound when gamma < 1/2, a lower bound when gamma > 1/2 and
  eta^2 (1 + |rho|) <= 4 at gamma = 1/2. It has no calendar check, which agrees
  with the assessment below: for this family there is nothing to check beyond
  theta increasing.
- Other variants: =Gatheral2012SsviHeston= (one lambda in place of eta and
  gamma), the Hendriks–Martini eSSVI variants, where rho moves with theta and
  the second one uses phi = eta * theta^(-lambda) * (1 + theta)^(lambda - 1),
  and the Corbetta and Mingone global eSSVI fits.
- /A probable defect./ The second condition's bound reads its eta, rho and gamma
  at the fixed positions 2, 1 and 3, as if there were one theta. In the global
  fit the array holds every theta first, so with two or more expiries those
  positions hold thetas, and the second condition is tested against the wrong
  values. The first condition reads them from the end of the array and is
  right. Not reproduced; worth a test before relying on QuantExt's second
  condition, and worth reporting upstream if it holds.
  - Where: =svimodeltraits.cpp= lines 549–552 (ORE 1.8.17.0 tree at
    =~/Vault/Development/ORE/Engine=). =cond3Bound= reads
    =q[isFree_.size() - 3]= to =q[isFree_.size() - 1]=; =cond4Bound= reads
    =q[2]=, =q[1]= and =q[3]=.
  - Why the layout differs: =SsviParametricVolatility::calibrateModelParametersGlobal()=
    (=sviparametricvolatility.cpp= line 1535) passes the flat array to the same
    constraint, and =extractSsviGlobalSliceParameters= shows its layout: the
    thetas of every slice, then rho, eta and gamma.
  - Test that would show it: a global power-law fit on three expiries with
    gamma below 1/2 and a long-end theta past the second bound. The constraint
    should refuse that point and, if the defect holds, accepts it.

* Assessment of the analysis

Claude's opinion, 2026-11-20. The analysis is right about where the code changes
go and about the butterfly bound. It is wrong about calendar arbitrage, it
leaves out one butterfly condition, and its safe-side advice points the wrong
way.

- /The butterfly bound is right but incomplete./ Gatheral and Jacquier give two
  sufficient conditions: theta * phi(theta) * (1 + |rho|) < 4 and
  theta * phi(theta)^2 * (1 + |rho|) <= 4. The analysis states only the first.
  With phi = eta * theta^(-gamma) the second is
  eta^2 * theta^(1 - 2 gamma) * (1 + |rho|) <= 4, which is independent of theta
  at gamma = 1/2 and depends on it otherwise. A per-slice check has to test both.
- /The safe-side advice points the wrong way./ It suggests holding gamma at or
  above 1/2. The calibration fits gamma in (0, 1), and the value seen on a
  Heston surface is 0.28. A generator that never goes below 1/2 does not test
  the region the fit lands in. Below 1/2 the first bound tightens at the long
  end, because theta^(1 - gamma) grows faster. So the long expiry is the one to
  bound eta against.
- /The calendar concern is misplaced./ Calendar arbitrage is a property of one
  surface across its maturities, not of how the surface moves from one step to
  the next. For the power law with gamma in (0, 1), theta * phi(theta) =
  eta * theta^(1 - gamma) increases with theta, and
  d(theta * phi)/d(theta) = (1 - gamma) * phi stays within the SSVI calendar
  condition's upper bound. So each published surface is free of calendar
  arbitrage whenever theta increases with maturity, whatever gamma and eta did
  on the step before. What can break it is a theta that does not increase. That
  depends on the forward variance drivers, which exist now and do not depend on
  gamma.
- /An alternative it does not mention./ Gatheral and Jacquier's power-law form
  phi(theta) = eta / (theta^gamma * (1 + theta)^(1 - gamma)) is free of butterfly
  arbitrage for every theta when eta * (1 + |rho|) <= 2 and gamma is in
  (0, 1/2]. That would remove the per-slice check. But the calibration fits the
  pure power law, as QuantExt does, so the generator and the fit would then use
  different families. Keep the pure power law and check each slice.
- /On the three approaches./ The bounded mean-reverting driver fits the
  existing design best, but nothing yet says gamma moves day to day in real FX
  markets. A gamma coupled to the state ties it to eta with no evidence for the
  link. A constant gamma per path or per regime is the useful first step: it
  costs no factor and no change to the correlation layout, it uses the start's
  gamma instead of ignoring it, and it gives the calibration a known gamma to
  recover. That is the test this work exists for. A moving gamma makes that
  test harder to read, because there is no single true value to compare with.

Recommendation: take the start's gamma, held constant on the path, in (0, 1).
Check both butterfly conditions on each published slice, at the longest expiry
first. Add a stochastic gamma driver only when there is evidence that gamma
moves.
