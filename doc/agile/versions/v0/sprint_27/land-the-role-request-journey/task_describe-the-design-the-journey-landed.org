* Plan

The document is rewritten in place rather than replaced, because a user journey
is a design document that outlives its sprint, and a reader arriving from the
older text should be able to see what changed and why. Six sections move; the
rest — the goal, the actors, the steps' ordering, the screens — stand, because
the journey was right about what a person is trying to do and wrong only about
what holds the waiting.

The design change is stated where it matters rather than assumed. The old text
put the request in =ores.workflow= as an instance that suspends and resumes; the
new text says why that turned out to be unnecessary, in the section that made
the claim, so the two readings sit side by side. The same applies to the seven
gaps: they are kept as a table with a state per gap, because two of them were
closed by changing the design rather than by building what they asked for, and
that is the part a future reader would otherwise have to reconstruct.

The subjects are replaced with the ones that exist, taken from the models rather
than from memory: =compass codegen= generates every subject from a model, so the
models are the source and the document cites them. Nothing is spelled in a form
the protocol forbids, which is checked rather than asserted.

* Result

The journey document now describes the design that was built, and every one of
its seven gaps is marked.

Six sections moved. The request is an approval request in =ores.inbox= with a
kind, a state and a deadline, and not a workflow instance; the paragraph that
claimed otherwise now says where that claim came from and why it was
unnecessary, so a reader from the older text can follow the change rather than
notice a gap. The entities table names the request, its kind, its decision, the
IAM detail behind it and the notification a person is given. The operations
table names the subjects that exist, taken from the models that generate them.

The seven gaps are a table with a state each, because two of them were closed by
changing the design rather than by building what they asked for. Gap 2 — the
workflow engine cannot wait on a person — was answered by not needing the engine;
gap 6 — no query returns a request as a row — was answered by making the request
a table. Gap 7 is half closed: the deadline is built, and the reminder before
it is not. That remainder is captured, and the document says why it is a
different behaviour rather than a loose end.

Two things were decided while writing it. The document names the screens that
were built, because a journey that names only wireframes leaves a reader unable
to find the code. And the credential sentence was reworded to state the
guarantee without listing the fields: the acceptance said no credential field is
named, and the old sentence named four in order to exclude them.

Two subjects and two pages this document wants to link to arrive with the
deadline task — =inbox.v1.ops.expire_overdue_approvals=, the Approval request
expiry page and the reminder capture. None of them is on main yet, so the
subject is described in prose and the two pages are named rather than linked:
a dangling id link fails =compass lint=, and this document must stand on its
own. The links are worth adding once that work lands.
