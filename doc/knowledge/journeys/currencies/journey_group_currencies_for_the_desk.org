This page documents a [[id:F44766FA-1E9C-416F-849A-E131101B2ECA][user journey]]: A reference data administrator keeps a desk group of currencies, such as G11 or Scandies, and its members correct.

A user journey describes what one person is trying to do, before anything is
built. It is a design document that outlives any sprint, so it names the
screens, the entity fields each screen shows, and the operations and messages
each step needs.

* Goal

"I want each desk group to hold exactly the currencies the desk means by it,
so that every report and screen that says G11 or Scandies means the same set."
The journey is done when the groups read in the right order and each group
lists the right members.

* Actor and trigger

The actor is the tenant administrator, acting inside their own tenant. The
=Operations= role may add and remove members, but it cannot read or write the
groups themselves. The trigger is a new desk grouping, a currency that joins or
leaves one, or a report that grouped currencies wrongly. The journey starts at
*Desk groups*, or at a group link on a currency, and ends when the person
leaves the groups.

* Steps

1. *See the groups.* The person opens *Desk groups*. The system lists the
   groups in display order: code, name, description and the number of members.
2. *Add or correct a group.* The person adds a group, or changes a group's
   name, description or order, states a change reason, and saves. The system
   writes it with =must_not_exist= for a new group and =must_match_version= for
   a change.
3. *Open a group.* The person selects a group. The system shows its members:
   each currency's code and name.
4. *Add a member.* The person picks a currency that is not yet a member. The
   system writes one =currency_currency_group= row.
5. *Remove a member.* The person removes a currency from the group. The system
   deletes that row; the currency itself is untouched.
6. *Remove a group.* The person removes a group the desk no longer uses. The
   system first removes its members, then closes the group. See
   /What is missing/.
7. *Check the history.* The person opens a group's history. The system shows
   every version of the group with the field-level difference from the one
   before, and the person can revert to an older version.
8. *Leave.* The person goes back. Every screen that groups currencies reads the
   new sets.

No step waits on the server after its reply, and the journey keeps no state.

* What is missing

1. *Members cannot be read by group.* The membership filter takes currency
   codes only, and the only narrowed read is =list_by_currency_iso_code=.
   Listing one group's members, or counting them, means paging through every
   membership row in the tenant. The candidate is =list_by_currency_group_code=
   and a group filter. Until then the screen pages through the memberships
   once and groups them itself.
2. *A membership is not checked against its ends.* The membership insert
   checks the tenant only. It does not check that the currency or the group
   exists, so a row can name a currency or a group that is not there. The
   candidate is the same existence checks the other refdata writes make. Until
   then the screen offers only codes it has read from the two lists.
3. *Removing a group leaves its members behind.* Closing a group does not close
   its membership rows. The candidate is to refuse the delete while members
   remain, or to close them with the group. Until then the screen removes the
   members first, as step 6 says.
4. *=Operations= can change members but not see the groups.* It holds the
   membership permissions and reads currencies, but holds no permission on
   =currency_groups=.
