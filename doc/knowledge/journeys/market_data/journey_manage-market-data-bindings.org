This page documents a [[id:F44766FA-1E9C-416F-849A-E131101B2ECA][user journey]]: A person decides which producers of market data a party consumes: sees the bindings, what each feeds and when it last ticked, binds and rebinds a source, enables or disables a binding, and starts or stops the feed behind it, for synthetic data and, shown and not supported, for real data.

A user journey describes what one person is trying to do, before anything is
built. It is a design document that outlives any sprint, so it names the
screens, the entity fields each screen shows, and the operations and messages
each step needs.

* Goal

"I want to say which producers of market data this party consumes, to see what
each one feeds and whether it is still ticking, and to change that without
asking someone to run a script." The journey is done when each binding is
listed with its source, the series it feeds, whether it is enabled and running
and when it last ticked, and the person has bound, rebound, enabled, disabled,
started or stopped what needed it.

* Actor and trigger

The actor is the market data operator: a person who holds
=marketdata::feed_bindings:read= to look and =marketdata::feed_bindings:write=
or =marketdata::feed_bindings:delete= to change a binding. The system
administrator has the same screen for the whole installation, and the
[[id:986CED57-341F-49DE-8F5D-F186771F10D8][Keep the market data feeds running]]
journey covers the system administrator's view of what is running. Which actor
classes own the screen is an open question; see /What is missing/.

The trigger is one of four events. A party is new and has no market data. A
new synthetic feed exists and the party should consume it. A series has gone
quiet and the person looks for the binding behind it. A report asks why a value
is generated, and the person checks that the series comes from a synthetic
binding and not from a vendor. The journey starts at *Market data*, then
*Bindings*, and ends when the person leaves the screen.

Three facts shape the screen.

- A binding says that a party consumes one producer source. It does not name
  the series. A tick names its own datum by its oresmd URI, and the ingest loop
  stores it, once for each enabled binding of that source, in the party's own
  series. The series a binding feeds is therefore read from what has been
  written, and the screen says that it is inferred.
- A binding says what kind of producer it names: =SYNTHETIC= for a generated
  feed or =VENDOR= for a real one. The ingest loop stamps the series with it.
  The default is =VENDOR=.
- A synthetic feed has a binding mode. A bound feed publishes on the ingested
  subjects and gets a binding when it runs. A sandboxed feed publishes on
  subjects the ingest loop does not read and never gets one. At most one running
  feed may write a series, so two feeds on one pair are never both bound.

Two scenarios run through the journey.

- *Synthetic data* is the supported scenario. A person can follow every step to
  the end.
- *Real data* from a vendor is not supported. The journey and the prototype draw
  a vendor binding so that the design holds it, and they say in words that this
  product does not connect real feeds. No control creates, rebinds or starts a
  vendor binding. A vendor binding that already exists can be read, enabled or
  disabled, and deleted, because those acts do not connect anything.

* Steps

The steps are the synthetic scenario. The last block says where the real data
scenario differs.

1. *List the bindings.* The person opens *Market data* and then *Bindings*. The
   system lists the party's bindings: the source name, the kind (synthetic or
   vendor), the series the source feeds, written as its title and linked to the
   series page, whether the binding is enabled, whether the feed behind it is
   running, when it last ticked, and the row's version and who last changed it.
   A summary above the table counts the bindings, the ones enabled, the ones
   running and the ones quiet.
2. *Narrow the list.* The person filters by kind and by state: all, running,
   quiet, disabled. A binding with no tick for longer than its tick rate
   implies is quiet, and a quiet binding stays in the list. The threshold is
   provisional: nothing states how long a feed may be quiet yet.
3. *Read one binding.* The person selects a row. The system shows the binding,
   its versions, and the producer behind the source: the generation config
   with its folder, tick rate, enabled and auto-start flags, and its binding
   mode. It shows the series the binding feeds with a link to its page.
4. *Bind a source.* The person chooses *Bind a source*. The system lists the
   synthetic sources the party does not consume yet, each with the series it
   would write. The person picks one. The system says whether another running
   feed already writes that series, and refuses the pick if one does, naming
   it. Until a subject answers that before the write, this check is a target,
   and the system reports the refusal after the write; see /What is missing/. The person states a change reason and confirms. The system writes the
   binding with kind =SYNTHETIC= and enabled, and the ingest loop starts to
   store ticks for the party.
5. *Rebind a source.* The person chooses *Rebind* on a binding and picks another
   synthetic source. The system shows the series the old and the new source
   write, and warns when they differ, because the party's series then changes.
   The person states a reason and confirms. The system writes the new source
   name against the version it read. Ingestion switches without restarting any
   producer.
6. *Enable or disable a binding.* The person changes the enabled flag and states
   a reason. Disabling suspends ingestion and deletes nothing. It does not stop
   the producer, so the running state and the binding can disagree, and the row
   says so.
7. *Start or stop the feed behind a binding.* The person chooses *Start* or
   *Stop* on one binding, or on a folder or a collection. The system reports how
   many feeds started, were already running or were skipped. The folder and
   collection acts are those of
   [[id:A829B804-23A5-493C-BDC6-A3B513EBDFEF][Run a synthetic collection]], and
   the person can follow a link there.
8. *Remove a binding.* The person chooses *Remove* and states a reason. The
   system asks for confirmation and says that the series and its observations
   stay. It deletes the binding and ingestion for the party stops.
9. *See a binding that has gone quiet.* The person sorts by last tick and reads
   the bindings that have not ticked within the interval their tick rate
   implies. The system names the likely cause where it knows it: the feed is not
   running, the binding is disabled, or the feed runs and nothing arrives.
10. *Leave.* The person goes back to the *Market data* area.

*Real data differs at four steps.* In the list a vendor binding shows the kind
*Vendor* and a note that real feeds are not supported. In the detail there is no
producer behind the source, because no generation config exists, and the page
says so. *Bind a source* offers no vendor source and says why, in one sentence.
*Rebind* and *Start* are not offered for a vendor binding. The person can still
enable, disable and remove it.

* Screens and wireframes

One screen. The summary and the filters sit at the top, the binding table under
them, and the selected binding with the producer behind it and the series it
feeds in a panel beside or below the table, with its versions at the bottom. The
*Bind a source* and *Rebind* acts are a dialog over the screen, so the person
keeps the list in view while choosing.

* Prototype

A clickable prototype is built by the task Prototype the market data binding
journey, under =doc/prototypes/market_data/manage-market-data-bindings/=. It has
a synthetic scenario that runs from the list to a bound, running feed, and a
real data scenario that shows a vendor binding and says that real feeds are not
supported. Marco signs it off before the screen is built. This section is
filled in with its variants and states when it exists.

* What is missing

1. *No read for the series a source feeds.* A binding names a source, not a
   series. The candidate is a read that resolves a source to the series it has
   written, or a series reference on the binding. Until then the screen shows
   the series of the source's newest observations and says it is inferred.
2. *No read for the last tick.* The ingest loop keeps the count and the last
   tick of each binding in memory, and nothing reads them. The candidate is a
   read over those statistics, or a stored last-observation time. Until then
   the screen uses the newest observation of the series as the nearest answer.
3. *No list of the synthetic sources a party does not consume.* Bind needs the
   producers that have no binding for the party. The candidate is a read that
   joins the generation configs to the bindings. Until then the dialog lists
   every source and marks the bound ones.
4. *No answer to which feed holds a series.* The feed controller knows which
   running feed writes a series and refuses a second one, but no subject says
   so before the person picks. Until then the system reports the refusal after
   the write.
5. *No way to tell the kind of a producer from the source name.* The binding
   carries the kind, but a source with no binding has none. The dialog must
   not offer a source it cannot classify.
6. *The synthetic feeds stopped after a service restart.* The newest synthetic
   tick of AUD/USD stopped advancing after the marketdata service was
   restarted. The task Find why the synthetic feed went quiet looks for the
   cause. The screen needs the answer to say why a feed is quiet.
7. *No threshold for quiet and no alert.* The configs carry a tick rate. Nothing
   compares it with a last tick, and nothing notifies a person. The screen sorts
   by last tick and the person judges, until a threshold exists.
8. *No permission that gates start and stop.* The binding permissions are named.
   The producer start and stop subjects have none of their own, so what gates
   them on this screen is not stated.
9. *Who owns the screen is not decided.* The binding is per party and
   provisioning creates it from the system party's config. Whether a market
   data operator or only a system administrator may bind and rebind is a
   decision for the prototype to put to Marco.
