#+title: Trade allocation
#+description: Splitting a block trade across the accounts it was dealt for: when it happens, what the block and its allocations are, the no-residual rule, and why each allocation has an identity.
#+type: knowledge
#+level: cross
#+filetags: :knowledge:domain:finance:trading:allocation:

* Summary

An *allocation* splits a /block trade/ into child trades, one per
client, fund or account the block was dealt for. An asset manager
deals one large amount on behalf of many funds; an electronic
communication network (ECN) executes one block for many clients; a
client books a trade as pending and names its funds later. In each
case the block carries the risk at once, and the allocations say whose
it is.

The children together must account for the whole block: an
allocation that leaves a residual is refused. The firm's net risk does
not change, so an allocation is an annotation rather than a trade event
in the sense of [[id:2AAC92EB-2320-4385-999E-947E9E8FE5C5][trade links]]. What changes is who the
firm faces, which is why the allocations, not the block, are
confirmed and settled.

Each allocation is one operation over a set of trades, so it is a
[[id:2AF58405-4F3D-4B74-AAD0-6A97E7DA215F][group]] with an identity: when it ran, who ran it, the
template it followed, the block it split and the children it created.

* Detail

** When allocation happens

| Situation | The block | The allocations |
|-----------+-----------+-----------------|
| Block execution | One trade dealt with an asset manager for many funds | One trade per fund |
| ECN execution | One trade executed on the venue for many clients | One trade per client, possibly submitted back to the ECN |
| Pending client trade | A trade the client booked as pending, ahead of naming its funds | The trades that replace it once the client sends its allocations |
| Break account | A client trade held against a break counterparty until the correct fund is identified | The trade booked against the fund once it is known |

The analysis notes state the pending case most precisely. A pending
trade sends no confirmation and does not settle, but it does flow to
the trader's book, so the risk is managed from the start. The client's
later allocations, amendments or netting requests replace it.

A break account is the controlled holding place for the same gap from
the other side: Operations monitors how long trades sit there, and
reports their age.

** How an allocation is made

The blotter's /Allocate (Split)/ action splits a block into child
trades across clients, notionals or value dates. The user can:

- enter each child by hand;
- apply a saved allocation template, which fills in the split ratios;
- auto-split from a predefined template;
- submit the allocations to an ECN.

The analysis notes ask for /auto-linking/ as well: given the ECN's
specification, find the block automatically.

When a user starts a link from a block, the blotter restricts the
eligible children by product:

| Block | Eligible allocations |
|-------+----------------------|
| Spot | Spot, outright, swap |
| Outright | Outright, spot |
| Swap | Swap only |

** What an allocation does to its trades

| Role | What happens to the trade |
|------+---------------------------|
| Block | Stays as the record of the execution. It stops settling and confirming in its own right once allocated. |
| Allocation | A new trade with the block's economics, its own amount and its own counterparty or account. It is the one that confirms and settles. |
| Bridge | An inter-desk transfer generated where allocations land in books other than the block's, so that no book's risk moves. |

The inter-desk transfer mechanics are those of deal netting, run in the
other direction: [[id:BBD96309-7C59-4D94-BDA7-27EAB7A8612E][Trade merge]] describes them.

** Effects

- /Risk./ Unchanged for the firm: the allocations sum to the block.
- /Counterparty./ Changes. The block faces the dealing agent or the
  venue; each allocation faces its own fund or client. Credit, netting
  set and confirmation follow the allocation.
- /Confirmation and settlement./ The allocations confirm and settle;
  the block does not, from the moment it is allocated.
- /Regulatory reporting./ Each allocation is reported as a trade with
  an identifier of its own. How the block is reported depends on the
  regime and on whether it is allocated on the day.

** The no-residual rule

The allocations must add up to the block exactly, for each currency
amount. An allocation that would leave an unexplained remainder is
rejected rather than reconciled approximately. The analysis notes on
internal trades state the rule for netting and allocation together.

** Why an allocation has an identity

An allocation is an operation that attaches to a set of trades as a
whole, so under [[id:2AF58405-4F3D-4B74-AAD0-6A97E7DA215F][trade sets, groups and links]] it is a
group. The business needs it in its own right:

- /When and who./ The time of the allocation, the user or process that
  made it, and any notes.
- /How./ The template applied, or the ECN message the allocation came
  from.
- /What it did./ The block, its children and any bridges, so the
  block's history lists its allocations and each child names its
  block.
- /Correction./ A wrong allocation is replaced as a whole: the old
  children are cancelled and a new allocation of the same block is
  made, which keeps the history of both.

** What follows for the model

This section is analysis rather than reported practice.

- /The allocation./ One row per allocation: the block, its source
  (manual, template, ECN or client request), the template where one
  was used, its time, who made it, and notes.
- /Participation./ One row per child and per bridge, with its role and
  its share of the block.
- /One live allocation per block./ A block has at most one current
  allocation. A re-allocation supersedes the previous one and records
  that it did.
- /The no-residual rule./ The children's amounts equal the block's
  amounts, checked before anything is written.
- /Created together./ The allocation, its participation rows, the
  children and the bridges are written in one transaction.
- /The block stays./ The block trade is not deleted or cancelled by the
  allocation. Its contribution to settlement and confirmation stops,
  which is a property of its allocated state rather than of its terms.

** Open questions

- /Economic effect./ The [[id:04F5EFF1-D4B5-4BD0-8CBC-4EF5DDF97921][link catalogue]] files allocation
  as an annotation, because the firm's risk does not move. But the
  counterparty does move, and the allocations need confirming. Whether
  that makes allocation a trade event for the children is not settled.
- /The block's end state./ Whether an allocated block keeps its state
  and gains an "allocated" flag, or moves to a state of its own, decides
  how reports exclude it.
- /Partial allocation./ Whether a block may be allocated in stages,
  with the remainder held, conflicts with the no-residual rule unless
  the remainder is an explicit holding allocation.
