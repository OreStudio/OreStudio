* Goal

Answer one question with a throwaway prototype: what should the *Reporting
lines* screen look like in =ores.web=?

[[id:36FC0D92-F532-4B6C-99FE-B669DB87C0FE][Draw the reporting line]] carries
one screen with three panels: the tree, the selected account, and the save
controls with their change reason. The prototype decides how the tree is drawn
from a flat read, how the selected person and their manager are shown, what a
change states about who approves it, and how the screen presents the operations
the server does not have.

* Notes

The journey's gaps, from the =* What is missing= section of
[[id:36FC0D92-F532-4B6C-99FE-B669DB87C0FE][Draw the reporting line]]. The
screen states each one, and the prototype decides how.

1. *No tree read.* The server answers one flat page of accounts with
   =reports_to_account_id= repeated per row. There is no children-by-manager
   read, no depth, no root list and no subtree, so the whole roster has to
   arrive first and the shape is computed in the browser.
2. *No cycle check.* Nothing refuses =A reports to B= when =B= already reports
   to =A=, so the server accepts a loop that has no root.
3. *No reporting-line-only write.* Setting one field means sending the whole
   =update_account_request=, so a concurrent edit to another field is silently
   overwritten by the stale copy the screen holds.
4. *Nothing in the web client ships the screen.* No web screen draws a tree or
   offers a manager picker. The Qt client did: =OrgChartWidget= read every
   tenant account and drew one card per person, and it was removed with
   =ores.qt=.
5. *Party names are still unreachable.* The card shows the office, which needs
   the party's name, and the account-party read carries =party_id= alone. This
   is the same gap as on
   [[id:0B3A2F4E-F4F0-4F19-8D07-EED38EF65B61][Choose where I work]].
6. *No history of the line.* The account is versioned, so a change is
   recoverable through =iam.v1.accounts_versions.list=, but the tree screen
   states none of it.
7. *No approval.* A reporting-line change is a proposal that a hierarchical
   superior or a tenant administrator approves, and nothing in the platform
   holds such an approval: no request, no queue, no notification and no
   decision. Until there is, the screen states who approves and the line does
   not move.

* Result

Built and accepted 2026-10-09. Variant A.

*Accepted by the product owner on 2026-10-09: variant A, tree and inspector.*

The recommendation was A. What it settles: depth and branches are plain, the
whole tenant stays on one screen, and the controls have a fixed place beside
the tree. The chart's fidelity to an org chart and the table's search-friendliness
are not carried; the table remains the easier read for a large tenant, and the
tree read the journey proposes would let both scale.

*The three variants.*

- *A · Tree.* An indented tree with expand and collapse, and the selected
  person in a panel beside it. Accepted.
- *B · Chart.* Boxes joined by connector lines.
- *C · Table.* One row per person, indented by depth, with a manager column.

*Decisions the prototype settles.*

- *The shape is drawn from the flat roster, and the page says so.* The journey
  says the tree is assembled by the client, and the prototype shows the cost:
  every account arrives before one branch is drawn. The tree read is argued for
  rather than hidden.
- *The manager picker refuses a loop the server would accept.* Everyone who
  reports to the selected person, directly or through others, is left out of
  the picker, and the panel states that the screen is doing the check the
  server does not.
- *The waiting proposal is drawn and cannot move the line.* The page names who
  decides — a hierarchical superior or a tenant administrator — and states that
  nothing in the platform holds the approval.
- *An unnamed office is marked, not invented.* Office values carry an amber
  underline with the reason, because the association read cannot name a party.
