* Goal

Every live consumer watches the vendor subjects its party subscribes to, and nothing else, so the subscription is the only door to live market data, as it is to its history.

* Acceptance

- Each consumer the analysis listed reads the vendor subjects, chosen from its party's subscriptions.
- A party cannot watch a vendor it does not subscribe to, by the enforcement the analysis chose.
- The system tenant watches every vendor.
- Two parties with the same subscriptions receive the same ticks.
- When a party's route or subscription changes during a watch, the watcher asks again and drops the feeds the party may no longer watch.

* Plan

1. *The rule.* =watchable_feeds(ctx, for_display)= in =tick_entitlement=:
   - for the system tenant, every feed of the catalogue;
   - for a party, each route whose provider the party subscribes to with the
     right the use needs: display for a screen, display or non-display for a
     service.
   The answer is the provider code and the feed id of each feed.
2. *The operation.* =marketdata.v1.ops.list_watchable_feeds=, modelled in
   =ores.marketdata.operations.org=, answered by a handler beside the history
   call, with the read permission of the subscriptions.
3. *The subjects.* =feed_tick_watch_subject()= in =tick_subjects.hpp=: a point
   watches its own subject on each feed, and a series watches its series token
   with a wildcard coordinate. No filter is needed in the client.
4. *The shell.* =marketdata stream= asks for the feeds for display, and watches
   the subjects for each one. It also watches the route and subscription events,
   and on each one it asks again, adds the feeds the answer now holds and drops
   the feeds it no longer holds.
5. *Tests.* The rule for a party with each right, for a route without a
   subscription, and for the system tenant; the subjects for a point and a
   series; the shell's watch for two feeds and its change after an event.

* Notes

- From [[id:B33AEA9B-907A-4B91-A94F-DC99A17ADDCF][the analysis]]: the only reader of the party subjects is the shell's stream watch (=marketdata_commands.cpp=). The new work is a market data operation that answers which feeds a party may watch, by the rule =may_read_ticks= uses, with every feed for the system tenant. The question names the use: a watch for a screen needs the display right, and a service with no screen needs the display or the non-display right. The broker checks only certificates, so the web servers and the shell check before they subscribe.
- From [[id:D0F85D26-99A9-42E3-9FB3-DCDEFA7E225B][publishing on the feed subject]]: the ingest still drops a tick from a source that no route names, before it is stored or published. The system tenant watches every feed, but a feed that no party routes to has no ticks to show. Decided: the ingest publishes them live and does not store them; [[id:6EEF6F65-3CCF-435F-8C28-1973F2FCBC9D][a task of its own]] does it.

* Result

- The market data service answers =marketdata.v1.ops.list_watchable_feeds=. The
  rule, =watchable_feeds()= in =tick_entitlement.cpp=, gives the system tenant
  every feed, and a party each feed its route picks where its subscription
  holds the right the use needs: display for a screen, display or non-display
  for a service.
- The shell's =marketdata stream= is the only reader the analysis found. It
  asks for the feeds it may show and watches the URI's subject on each one. A
  series watches its series token with a wildcard coordinate, so the shell
  filters nothing. It listens to the route and subscription events, asks again
  on each, and adds or drops feeds to match.
- The per-party subjects are still published, and no consumer reads them.
- Tests: five database tests of the rule (display, non-display only, derived
  only, two parties with different routes, the system tenant); the watch
  subject of a point and of a series; the shell's subjects and its change of
  feeds. Suites: market data API 1136 cases, market data core 458, shell
  application 65, HTTP market data 34; all pass.
- Not tested: a live watch that asks again after a real change event. The
  decision is unit tested; the event path needs a running fleet.
- The decision on the ticks of an unrouted feed went to its own task.
