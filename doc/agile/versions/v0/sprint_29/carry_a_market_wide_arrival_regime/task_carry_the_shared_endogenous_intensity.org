* Acceptance

- Every event adds to a shared intensity that decays at its own rate.
- Each row's rate gains the shared intensity scaled by its sensitivity.
- Counts of two series are positively correlated in the active state and near zero in the calm one.
- An unstable parameter set is refused before anything is built.

* Plan

Three slices, in this order, because each one is what the next needs.

1. *The heat.* =domain::shared_intensity=: every event adds a jump, the heat fades
   at its own rate, and the stationary mean is the event rate times the jump over
   the decay. Readers load one relaxed atomic; an event is one atomic add; the
   owner fades with a compare-and-swap loop, so an event that lands during a fade
   is never lost. It is tested for keeping every event under concurrency.
2. *The baseline as a rate.* A series' intensity is
   =sensitivity * (mean_rate * S + C)=, which is a rate, not a multiple of the
   configured one. =i_arrival_process= therefore moves from
   =set_baseline_multiplier= to =set_baseline_rate=, and the process keeps its own
   Hawkes term on top. This is the slice that makes C(t) reach the feeds.
3. *The one clock.* Aggregate thinning over every registered feed: a bound on the
   total intensity, a candidate time, a stream chosen in proportion, and an
   acceptance test. Every component of a stream's intensity is non-increasing
   between events — the Hawkes excess decays, C decays, and S is constant between
   transitions — so the value at the start of an interval bounds it, which is what
   makes the thinning exact rather than approximate. Each process gains
   =intensity_at(t)= and =accept_event(t)=, and a feed stops being a thread and
   becomes a body the clock calls.

The test that proves the whole thing is cross-series correlation of counts: it
must be clearly positive under a live intensity and near zero with the intensity
off, and every per-series statistic must still hold.
