* Goal

A party demands, is entitled to, and reads exactly the market data its trades
name. Every fixing convention a generated trade can reference has at least one
supplier in the catalogue. The demand a party raises is the set of items its own
trades resolve to, not its provider's whole catalogue. The party's subscription
covers that set, and a booking that names a convention no entitled supplier
covers is refused at the boundary, naming the supplier that is missing.

This task is the connection. It does not supply market data for an asset class
and it does not change how a report binds to a published set.

The predecessor, [[id:9EFEAA52-D458-4CCF-A6C6-7C5A6A2EF6DC][Give every generated trade type its market data and pricing
engines]], measured what the generated population names and what the market
answers: 133 addresses, 124 unanswered. This task reads that record rather than
repeating it. Both live in this story's =evidence/= directory:

  =party_market_data_rights.sql= and =market_data_storage.sql= — who can supply
  an item, who may read what, and where the data sits.
- =fx_and_ir_publishing_setup.txt= — the same facts read against the
  architecture, with the findings numbered.
- =generated_population_gap.txt= with its two queries — what each generated trade
  type needs and what no series answers.

* The model

** Two axes, not one

The word =source= is doing two jobs in this subject, and the confusion in the
diagnosis came from that.

- *The fixing convention* is the instrument. It says who calculates the rate, at
  what time, by what method: the ECB's 14:15 euro reference rate, WM/Refinitiv's
  4pm London fix, which ORE spells =TR20H=, ICE's LIBOR, the New York Fed's
  SOFR. A trade references a convention, because a convention is what its
  cashflows settle on.
- *The fixing supplier* is who sells us that convention's values: LSEG,
  Bloomberg, a central bank's own portal, or this platform's derived feed. A firm
  may hold one convention from several suppliers, and the choice between them is
  commercial and operational rather than economic.
- *The fixing series* is the stored data for one convention from one supplier for
  one party.

The model already carries both axes. The convention is inside the oresmd URI:
=source= for FX, as in
=oresmd://fx/EUR?type=fixing&index=fx&source=TR20H&ccy=USD=, and =name= with
=tenor= for interest rates, as in
=oresmd://ir/GBP?type=fixing&index=ibor&name=LIBOR&tenor=3M=. The supplier is
=market_series.provider_code=. The word =source= names the convention inside the
URI and the supplier in every other column.

Two consequences follow, and both are in the measured data.

1. A convention that one publisher calculates has that publisher as its only
   supplier. The ECB's reference rate is published by the ECB. So the nine FX
   pairs this tenant holds under =source=ECB=, =source=IMF= and =source=BOC= are
   *different conventions* from the 4pm London fix the trades name, not alternate
   spellings of it, and they cannot answer it. This corrects an earlier reading of
   the same numbers.
2. A vendor supplies a convention it does not calculate. LSEG supplies the
   WM/Refinitiv 4pm London fix; that is the normal shape of a fixing contract, and
   it is the shape this catalogue has no row for.

** The chain

The connection is a closure, and every link has one rule.

1. *A trade names conventions, never suppliers.* Its market references are the
   conventions its cashflows settle on.
2. *A convention resolves to catalogue items.* The catalogue states the item's
   identity, which includes the convention.
3. *An item has suppliers.* A provider's vendor instrument maps to the item, so
   the provider can serve it. Several providers may serve one item; then a rank
   decides.
4. *A party is entitled to suppliers, not items.* A subscription grants display,
   non-display or derived rights on a provider, and a route names the source the
   party uses for it. Derived data needs derived rights on the suppliers of its
   inputs, which is what the =derived= provider exists for.
5. *Demand is the party's items.* It is the union, over the parties routed to an
   adapter, of the items their trades require, intersected with the feed's scope.
6. *Storage is one copy per item and supplier.* The installation owns it.
7. *The read resolves the convention to a supplier the party is entitled to,* by
   the stated rank, and records the choice so two reads of one report agree.

** How a bank does this**

The chain above is the ordinary shape of market data management, and it is worth
saying so, because it tells us which parts are conventions of the industry and
which are ours:

- A firm holds a *security and instrument master* — what exists — apart from its
  *vendor inventory* — who can supply each item, in the vendor's own notation.
  One instrument maps to several vendors' ids, and a *rank* gives a primary and
  its fallbacks.
- *Entitlements* are commercial grants per provider, per legal entity or desk,
  with display, non-display and derived rights. They are audited, and metering
  prices them.
- *Derived data* carries its own rights regime: a curve bootstrapped from a
  vendor's quotes is redistributable only if the licence says so.
- *Consumers ask for instruments.* A risk run asks for the GBP 3M LIBOR fixing of
  a date. It never names a vendor. The market data service answers from an
  entitled supplier, and it is the service's job to know which.

Our tree has the same parts and stops short of the consumer asking for
instruments: it stores a supplier's identity in the trade, and then asks the
market for that supplier by name.

* What exists, and what does not

| Link | State |
|------+-------|
| A trade names a convention | Yes, an oresmd market reference on the trade. The FX generator also freezes the convention it names. |
| Convention to catalogue item | Yes, =ores_marketdata_market_data_mappings_tbl= maps a vendor instrument to the item. |
| Item to supplier | Partial. Public publishers supply their own conventions, one vendor instrument each. *No data vendor supplies any FX fixing*: the 200-odd fixing mappings belong to ecb, imf, boc, tcmb and a dozen central banks, and the vendors supply FX spot only. |
| Supplier to entitlement | The machinery works. The data does not: the demo's =subscribe_market_data= step is 18 hand-written rows, four vendors plus =derived=, with no public publisher and nothing for interest rates. |
| Demand is the party's items | The hook exists and is never supplied. =demand_policy::requirements(party, provider)= can narrow a party's demand; =adapter_supervisor.cpp:229= calls =compute_demand(snap->catalogue, now)= with no policy, so every entitled route demands its provider's whole scoped catalogue. |
| Entitlement follows the trades | No. Nothing computes a party's required items from its trades, and nothing checks that its subscriptions cover them. |
| One copy per item and supplier | Partly. Fixings are stored once, in the system tenant. The read still goes through a party's own series. |

| Supplier chosen by rank | No. There is no market data configuration in the schema, and no rank anywhere. |

* Acceptance

- Every fixing convention a generated trade can name has at least one supplier in
  the catalogue, and the simulator serves it. A query over the population's
  conventions returns no convention without a supplier.
- =compute_demand= is called with a requirements policy for every route, so a
  party's demand is the set of items its own trades resolve to. A test pins the
  demand of a party with two trades as those trades' items.
- A party that is routed and subscribed still demands nothing for a provider
  whose items its trades do not name.
- A booking, or a run, that names a convention no entitled supplier covers is
  refused, and the refusal names the convention and the supplier it would have
  needed. A test asserts the refusal for one convention of each kind.
- A freshly provisioned Acme's parties hold the subscriptions their trades
  require, with no hand step. The closure of the population over the catalogue is
  a subset of the parties' entitlements, and a check fails when it is not.
- A report's fixings are read by convention and supplier under the party's
  entitlement, and two runs of the same report over the same as-of read the same
  values.
- The supplier of a convention is chosen by a stated rank held in the tenant's
  market data configuration, and the choice is recorded with the run.
- A party that consumes a derived curve, surface or composite holds derived
  rights on the suppliers of its inputs, and a run records which.
- The number of market data errors a provisioned Acme's reports record for the
  generated trades falls to zero, or to a list this task asserts by name with a
  reason for each.

* Plan

The chain is a closure, so build it from the supply end inwards: each step is
verifiable on its own and leaves the next one possible.

1. *Give the vendor conventions a supplier.* Add vendor instruments and mappings
   so that LSEG supplies the 4pm London FX fixings and ICE the LIBOR-style ones,
   in the vendors' own notation, and add them to the feeds' scopes. The
   simulator stands in for the vendor: no code path may know whether a source is
   simulated, so the catalogue is the only change. Verify: a demand for a 4pm
   London item yields the vendor's ids, and a provisioned tenant's series appear
   under that provider.

2. *Wire the requirements policy.* Point =demand_policy::requirements= at the
   closure over the party's trades — =ores_trading_market_references_fn= already
   lists a party's required addresses from its trades — and add
   =composite_inputs= so a composite's inputs join its demand. Verify: the
   demand of a party equals the items of its trades, and a party whose trades
   name nothing demands nothing.

3. *Make the entitlement follow the trades.* Compute the closure over a
   population at booking and at run start, and refuse a booking whose conventions
   no entitled supplier covers, naming the convention and the missing supplier.
   This is the difference =closure(rho(...)) \ U= that
   [[id:FD427B5B-BDC5-4803-AFCD-97A59E31FE25][Market Data Requirements and Resolution]] says must be raised and never
   silently discarded. Verify: a unit test refuses one convention of each kind
   with its reason, and a provisioned tenant raises nothing.

4. *Read a fixing by convention and supplier.* Change the export and the fixings
   read to resolve the convention to an entitled supplier, rather than to a
   party's series, using the rule the target already states in
   [[id:F4BBEF34-D84C-4AE6-A3AA-DC47C3EE06EE][Public sources publish daily fixings through an adapter]]: the reader reads
   by item. Verify: a party with no public route reads the installation's
   fixings of a convention it is entitled to, and reads nothing for one it is
   not.

5. *Choose the supplier by rank.* Add the tenant's market data configuration: a
   rank per (item or convention family, provider), with a primary, fallbacks and
   the derived source, and record the choice with each run. Verify: with two
   suppliers of one item, the primary answers; remove its entitlement and the
   fallback answers; the run records which one did.

6. *Seed the demo from the closure.* Replace the hand-written
   =subscribe_market_data= list with the subscriptions the closure over the
   demo's trades requires, per party, so a fresh provision needs no hand step.
   Verify: the closure check of step 3 passes on a fresh provision with no
   manual subscription.

7. *Derive the crosses.* The fifteen FX pairs no supplier quotes as a fixing are
   crosses of two published bases. Build them as composites with lineage, and
   give the consuming parties derived rights. Verify: a trade on one of the
   crosses prices, and its fixing traces to the two driver fixings.

Steps 1 and 2 are independent. Step 3 needs 1 and 2. Steps 4 and 5 need 3. Step
6 needs 3, and step 7 needs 2 and 4.

- =demand_calculator.cpp= already refuses =not_entitled= and =terms_not_accepted=,
  and =demand_policy= already documents the hook. Step 2 supplies an input to code
  that exists, which is why it is early in the plan.
- The vocabulary is the likely cause of the confusion this task resolves. A
  column called =source= holds a convention inside an oresmd URI and a supplier
  outside one. If the model is touched anyway, renaming the URI's =source= to
  =convention= is worth a separate task, and it is not this one: ORE's own index
  names carry the same token, and the boundary format is not ours to change.
- The earlier reading of the same numbers, that nine of the twenty-four FX pairs
  "exist under another source" and so needed only a rename, was wrong in a way
  worth keeping: the ECB reference rate and the 4pm London fix are two
  instruments. A trade on one cannot be valued from the other.

  live source; connecting a real vendor is its own task.
