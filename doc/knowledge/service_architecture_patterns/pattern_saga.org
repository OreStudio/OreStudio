This page defines the [[id:68FAC606-569C-4B9B-932B-3B3A0181AC8B][service architecture pattern]]
Saga in the [[id:EA249899-6611-407F-A43A-B37019EBD1A7][pattern language]].
A long piece of work is a sequence of local steps, each with a compensating step,
so work that spans services stays consistent without a distributed transaction.
In ORE Studio the workflow engine runs sagas by orchestration.

* Also known as

- /Orchestration-based saga/, the form ORE Studio uses: one coordinator drives the steps.
- /Choreography-based saga/, the alternative not used here: services react to each other's events.

* Context

One business action writes in several services: provisioning a party, running a
report. No transaction spans two services, and holding locks across them would
stall the platform.

* Problem

How does work that writes in several services stay consistent when one of its
steps fails?

* Forces

- No transaction spans services.
- Other readers see the intermediate state; there is no isolation.
- Undoing a completed step may itself fail.
- Some completed work is worth keeping, and undoing it helps nobody.

* Solution

The work is a list of steps, each a local transaction in one service, and each
with a compensating step that undoes its effect. A coordinator runs the steps in
order. When a step fails, it runs the compensations of the completed steps in
reverse. A definition may choose instead to stop and wait for a retry, when its
completed work is worth more than a rollback.

* Failure handling

#+ATTR_HTML: :class hug-leading
| Concern | What the pattern does |
|---------+-----------------------|
| Errors | A step that reports failure triggers the definition's policy: compensate, or stop for a retry. |
| Timeouts | A step that goes silent is failed by its deadline, and the run stops; a silent step's work is unknown, so it is never compensated. |
| Retries | A stopped run resumes at its failed step; completed steps keep their results. |
| Idempotency | Every step and every compensation must converge on repeat. |
| Observability | The run, its steps and their outcomes are the record a person reads. |

* Pitfalls

- *Compensation read as rollback.* Other services saw the intermediate state; a compensation is a new action, not an erasure.
- *Compensating a step that timed out.* It may have finished. Stop and let a person decide.
- *A compensation that cannot run.* A step whose effect cannot be undone belongs last, or in a stop-and-retry definition.
- *Steps that are not idempotent.* Recovery re-dispatches steps, and a repeat that accumulates corrupts the run.

* Security

Each step runs with the identity its service needs, as for any call; the saga
adds no authority.

* Resulting context

- Benefits :: Consistency across services without distributed locks, and a record of every step.
- Liabilities :: No isolation, and every step needs a compensation or a reason it has none.
