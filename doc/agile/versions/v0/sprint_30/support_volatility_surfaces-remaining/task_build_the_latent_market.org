* Goal

Every synthetic feed draws its noise alone, so every pair of synthetic prices is
uncorrelated. This task builds the latent market that ends that: a set of
shared Brownian factors in the run's simulated time, from which each series
reads the increment of its own driver at its own ticks. The first user is the
synthetic FX volatility surface, whose skew must lean with spot by a stated
correlation
([[id:67D0F734-5703-43DF-BF16-F5513C85A01F][Build the synthetic FX volatility surface generator]]).
Only block factors are built: a pair's spot and its surface. Global factors and
their calibration stay in Phase 5 of the approach note.

The design is settled; build it as the notes state it:

- [[id:25C467BE-07A3-432C-8CB8-6B194E748570][Shared Brownian factors give every synthetic series a signed correlation]]
  -- the driver, the requirements R1 to R8, the block rule, determinism and its
  two bounds, the zero-loading identity, the cost, and the measures.
- [[id:14C79EA2-4E36-4EF2-A0DB-0EB932655029][A keyed Brownian path is a pure function of seed and time]]
  -- the cell grid, the dyadic bridge, the node addressing and the resolution
  bound.
- [[id:9B577C48-A237-445B-A331-AD0CB42FAAE1][Counter-based random number generators]]
  -- the Philox stream the nodes are drawn from.
- [[id:92644AAC-BE5F-43B6-96E4-084226CDA094][Tick-to-tick returns understate a correlation that holds exactly]]
  -- how the tests measure the correlation.

* Acceptance

- A =latent_market= in =ores.analytics.quant=, owned by the scheduler and
  touched only on its thread, holds named block factors, per-series loadings and
  a keyed path per factor, and returns a series' standardised increment
  between its last tick and now.
- Two drivers sampled at independent irregular times show the stated
  correlation within a stated tolerance, measured by the Hayashi-Yoshida
  estimator, not by tick-to-tick returns.
- A series' increments are identical to the last bit when another series is
  added, removed or bursts, for a run without the heat in the loading.
- A factor queried at the same time in two different orders returns the same
  value; two ticks inside one finest bridge step behave as the keyed path note
  states.
- A set of loadings that is not a correlation is refused, never projected.
- =i_stochastic_process= takes one standard normal in for a driven step. A
  series with every loading zero that anchors no block reproduces today's path
  bit for bit for the same seed. A process that cannot take an external normal refuses loadings.
- The FX spot feed and the FX volatility surface read their drivers from the
  tick context; the surface's three correlations become its block's loadings,
  and its strikes are solved against the forward from the published spot and
  curves.
- The factors and loadings are typed configuration tables, shown on the Market
  regime tab and set from the shell.
- No arrival law reads a price: the arrival interface takes no price, and a
  test pins it.

* Plan

One PR a step, in this order; each ends in its test. Steps 1 and 2 change no
published price.

1. *The keyed path.* A factor's value at a time from keyed cells and a dyadic
   bridge, through direct Philox block access at a draw index. Tests: the
   bridge's law at its nodes, order independence, the resolution bound, a jump
   over many cells, and Random123's known-answer vectors for the block function
   if =rng_stream= does not already carry them. Record the cost of a read at
   depths 16 and 30 in the Measurements table, and the licence of every source
   copied.
2. *The latent market.* Block factors, loadings, the anchor rule (a member loads
   on its anchor's whole driver), the per-series last driver value, and the
   increment. Tests: Hayashi-Yoshida correlation under irregular sampling;
   isolation when a series is added or bursts; reproducible by seed.
3. *The driven step.* =i_stochastic_process= takes the normal it would draw; the
   latent market draws a series' own noise from its existing price stream, and
   hands a series with every loading zero its drawn normal unscaled. Test: zero
   loadings reproduce today's path bit for bit, for a series that anchors no
   block. An anchor's own noise is a keyed path, so its draws change once it
   anchors a block, and the test says so.
4. *The tick context carries the market.* FX spot moves onto its driver.
5. *Configuration.* Factor and loading tables through codegen, the Market regime
   tab and the shell.
6. *The FX volatility surface.* Its feed declares its block with its spot; this
   resumes the generator task's runtime PR.

Static loadings only. Per-regime loadings, global factors, calibration and
derived =CORRELATION/RATE= quotes are later work, as the design note's
"Later work" section lists them.

Step 2 keeps the many case in view: a factor owns a path and a fifty-six byte
walk, a series owns two doubles, and the loadings are one flat array written in
a single pass while the market is built. Its tests recover the stated
correlation with the Hayashi-Yoshida estimator under independent irregular
sampling, and pin that a series is untouched by another series' ticks.

What step 2 leaves to step 3 is the series' own motion: the market returns only
the shared part of a driver's increment, and the process adds its own draw
weighted by the square root of one less the squared loadings. A series with no
loadings is therefore still to be handed its drawn normal unscaled, which is the
migration test.

** Which processes can be driven

A process is drivable only when its step is a function of one standard normal.
Eight are: Ornstein-Uhlenbeck, Hull-White, Black-Karasinski, single-factor
Heath-Jarrow-Morton, Vasicek through its inner process, both Gaussian mixtures,
and the GJR-GARCH variance wrapper over any of them. Six are not, and refuse by
the interface's default: the affine and quadratic Gaussian processes and Libor
market model draw one normal a factor, the two-factor Gaussian draws two,
Cox-Ingersoll-Ross draws a Poisson and a normal, and Black-Derman-Toy walks a
lattice node. Widening any of them means widening the driven step to a vector of
normals, which is a decision for the step that needs it, not a mechanical change.

A mixture is driven conditionally on its component. The component stays the
process's own draw and the market's normal is the step's direction, so the price's
correlation with the factor is exactly the loading while the mixture's shape is
untouched. One normal is therefore one tick, because the component is drawn per
tick, and a driven step over several ticks is that many driven ticks.

** Steps 4 and 6 are one increment

A spot feed on its own has nothing to correlate with. Driving it changes every
synthetic FX price for a correlation no screen can show, so its wiring lands with
the surface that loads on it rather than before it. What lands first is what makes
the wiring possible: the driven step reaching the mixture and its variance
wrapper.

** Where the loadings are configured

Nothing in codegen has to be built for a block's loadings. The
=fx_vol_surface_feed= entity already carries the three numbers its matrix is
built from, =spot_variance_correlation=, =spot_skew_correlation= and
=segment_correlation=, with their SQL checks, the domain type, the codec, the
wire protocol and the shell's =fx_vol_surface_feeds add|set= verbs. A spot
anchors a block by being the series its surface names, so its own row states no
loading. The block's factors are derived from the block, not stored.

What does not exist is the read. No code outside =ores.analytics.quant= reads
those columns, and the FX feeds screen does not show them. The remaining
configuration work is therefore a read over the run's market and somewhere to
show it, not a table.

The same holds for the surface's own laws. =fx_vol_surface_feed_expiry= carries
=variance_reversion= and =variance_volatility= for each segment, and the feed's
own row carries the skew's and the curvature's, so between the two tables the
generator has every number it needs. Nothing is missing from the configuration.

** How the surface and the market divide the correlation

The generator applies the three numbers through its own Cholesky root, and the
latent market applies them through a block's loadings. Two modules cannot both
own the correlation, so the market does and the generator is given the identity:
it then keeps the normals it is handed, and a test pins that, so the two sides
cannot drift apart. =fx_vol_surface_block= builds the block from the three
numbers and names the drivers in the order the generator reads them, and
=fx_vol_surface_correlation= is the one place the matrix is built. Both refuse
three numbers that are not a correlation.

Two surfaces on one spot share its factor and its series, because a run has
thousands of surfaces on a handful of spots and two copies of one spot would let
two surfaces on one pair drift apart.

** What the feed does now

The feed is complete enough to start and run. A person states a surface as a feed
row, one expiry row for each segment and one contributor row for each observer;
the service reads them, builds the surface from the levels and laws they state,
and publishes every point each contributor observes under the market data
notation. An integration test starts one over NATS through the same handler a
person's request reaches.

Three things it does not do yet, each stated rather than hidden:

- *The pair's curves.* The market the surface is quoted against has no rates and a
  spot of one. That is exact while the two discounts are equal, because a smile is
  read at a moneyness and a common scale cancels, so the quoted volatilities are
  the surface's own. It is not exact once the pair's curves differ, and reading
  them is the next wiring.
- *The pair's convention.* A refdata row holds it, and the mapping from that row
  lives in the market data service. The feed states the convention the derived
  source quotes until it is handed that mapping.
- *The finest gap.* A second, until the arrival laws are read at assembly. Every
  configured rate is far slower, so nothing is interpolated.

A surface started after the run built its market has no block in it, because a
block's loadings are written in one pass while the market is made. It publishes
nothing and says what it is missing, rather than stepping on zeros.

** What step 6 still owes

The start level is configured (see below), the emitter steps a surface on the
market's normals, and the =fx_vol_surface= kind is built and registered in the feed
factory. What is still owed is the wiring that makes it startable and correlated:

- *The kind in the service's registry.* =feed_kind_registry= needs a
  =make_fx_vol_surface_kind()=: a =candidates= read of the feed, its expiry rows and
  its contributor rows, and a =build_input= that turns one into the factory input.
- *The market the feed quotes against, which the synthetic service cannot build.*
  The build input carries an =fx_market=: the spot and the two discount factors.
  Today nothing in =ores.synthetic= resolves one. The market data service does it
  from a published forward set, in
  =ores.marketdata.service/src/app/fx_vol_surface_calibration_plan.cpp=, which is a
  calibration path and not a synthetic one. This is the one thing standing between
  the kind and a row a person can start, and it is a design decision rather than
  typing: the feed either reads a published forward set or the configuration names
  the spot and the curves directly.
- *The service's market.* =run_market= holds it and answers the scheduler, and its
  tests drive a spot's one normal and a surface's seven through a tick. What is
  left is the assembly: the service must record a block for each surface it knows
  from the loaded configuration, build the market before any feed starts, and keep
  it alive for the scheduler's lifetime. Two things make that more than typing:
  a block's anchor is the *spot's series id*, so the assembly has to decide which
  spot feed a surface hangs off -- by currency pair, and a pair with no spot feed
  has no anchor; and the market cannot grow after it is built, so a feed started
  after the build is refused rather than driven by nothing.

** What the surface's configuration now holds

The generator needs a level and a law for every driver, and it has both.

| What | Where | Column |
|------+-------+--------|
| The skew the surface starts at and reverts to | the feed's row | =start_skew= |
| The curvature the surface starts at and reverts to | the feed's row | =start_curvature= |
| The ATM volatility of each expiry | each expiry row | =atm_vol= |
| Each segment's forward-variance law | each expiry row | =variance_reversion=, =variance_volatility= |
| The skew's and curvature's laws | the feed's row | =skew_reversion=, =skew_volatility=, =curvature_reversion=, =curvature_volatility= |
| The correlation of the shocks | the feed's row | =spot_variance_correlation=, =spot_skew_correlation=, =segment_correlation= |

The ATM total variance is the volatility squared times the expiry. It must rise
strictly from one expiry to the next for the segment between them to have a
positive forward variance; no check on one row can decide that, so the maker
refuses a set that does not and names the expiry it failed at. A tenor is a count
and a unit, and =fx_vol_tenor_years= is the whole of the mapping until a resolved
calendar and day count replace it.

** What the tests found

The bridge's heap index counted intervals where it had to count nodes, so a
draw was reused across levels. The midpoint's variance looked right while an
increment's variance was double, which the covariance and residual tests caught
at once; =quant/keyed_brownian_path_tests.cpp= now pins the addressing against
the draw the stream returns at the documented index.

** What step 1 does not do

The walk is per factor and forward only. Per-series driver values, the loadings
and the block anchor are step 2, and nothing here allocates or holds a series.
