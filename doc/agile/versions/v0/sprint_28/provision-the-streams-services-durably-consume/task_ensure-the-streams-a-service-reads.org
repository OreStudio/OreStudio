#+title: Task: Ensure the streams a service reads
#+description: Apply the documented stream provisioning pattern to the services that durably consume a subject. Each such service ensures its stream in its startup path, before it registers handlers, over a filter covering the subjects it reads, and fails to start when it cannot. This is what makes a step command survive being published before its consumer is running, and it removes the five second publish timeout that makes provisioning look flaky.

* Result

* Result

No change was needed. Every durable step consumer already ensures its stream at startup through
=serve_step_commands=, before it subscribes, and the pattern was implemented on main after this story
was captured.

Verified from an empty JetStream: all twenty streams were deleted, the database recreated, and the
services alone recreated nineteen streams before provisioning ran. Provisioning then completed end to
end, tenant setup nine steps of nine plus nineteen bundle publishes, with every step delivery acked,
nothing pending and nothing redelivered. The step streams drained to zero messages, which is the proof
each command was consumed rather than merely published.

The overlap that stopped the IAM service starting was a legacy store from before main split the step
streams, not a defect in the current code.
