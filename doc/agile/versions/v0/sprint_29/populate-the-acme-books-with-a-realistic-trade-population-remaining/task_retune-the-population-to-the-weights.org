* Goal

Trade Population Weights states a trade-count mix: interest rates 42%, fx 25%, equity 12%, credit 6%, commodity 5%, bonds 4%, inflation and scripted 3% each. The seeded configs were never sized to it. On a fresh tenant the mix is rates 27 of 162 (17%), fx 64 (40%), equity 17 (10%), credit 5 (3%), commodity 25 (15%), bonds 24 (15%); inflation and scripted book nothing at all. Each class books one trade per routed code, which is what coverage needs, and a weighted population needs a different rule: the weights set the counts, and the codes are drawn within those counts. Doing that changes the seeded configs for every class at once and needs the weights page's tolerance stated, so it is its own task.

* Plan

The weights set the counts; the codes are drawn within them.

Today every family states one trade per routed code, which is what coverage
needs: a config shorter than its code list stops at a prefix, and that was the
defect the family tasks fixed. A weighted population needs the other rule. The
config states a count per class, and the planner cycles its codes inside that
count, so coverage survives and the mix matches the page.

Two things this task must settle before it changes anything:

- The tolerance. A page that states 42 per cent and a population of 190 cannot
  hit it exactly: 42 per cent of 190 is 79.8 trades, and the family routes nine
  codes, so the count is a multiple of nine near 80. The page states a tolerance
  or this task states one and records it.
- Which number moves. The seeded configs size every class at once, so a retune
  changes the population of every family together. A class that gains trades
  must not lose a code, and the smallest class cannot go below one trade per
  code without dropping coverage.

The measure is a fresh provisioning run: the family counts over the total, and
each one against its weight within the tolerance.

* Result

Every class is its weight of a thousand trades, and every routed code still
appears.

Measured on a fresh tenant: 1000 trades — interest rates 420 (42%), FX 250
(25%), equity 120 (12%), credit 60 (6%), commodity 50 (5%), bonds 40 (4%),
inflation 30 (3%) and scripted 30 (3%). Each family's codes carry 2 to 16 trades
apiece, so the mix moved without any code losing its first trade.

The two rules decide the total, and that is the decision worth keeping:

- A class's count cannot fall below the number of codes its planner cycles. The
  planner draws codes by the slot's position, so a short config states a prefix
  and drops the codes past it.
- A class's share of the total cannot fall below that floor either. Commodity
  routes twenty-five codes and weighs five per cent, so a thousand trades is the
  smallest round population where its floor is also its weight; and bond trades
  are ten per book across four entities, which is exactly four per cent of a
  thousand.

Nothing smaller satisfies both. At 190 the commodity floor was ten per cent of
the population, and at 500 the four bond books were eight per cent. So the
tolerance this task settles is exactness at a thousand: every class states its
weight because the total was chosen to let it.

The cost is the run: five and a half minutes for the provisioning script, against
two for the population of 190. That is the price of a mix that matches the page.
