This is the structure note for the cuts cluster. It states the general notion,
the kinds and the composition, then the order to read the component pages in.
Return to [[id:7474935C-95D1-3034-B833-83DCD9FB3A09][Knowledge]].

* Summary

A *cut* is a *label* for a *timepoint* and an *object* it fixes. The label names
the cut. The timepoint is the moment held still. The object is what is held still
there, and it may be a deal population, a market data set, both, or anything else
a valuation reads.

The timepoint is the defining property, and it is not the same thing as a
business day or a centre. A cut is not daily because a cut is not defined by
being daily, and it does not belong to a centre because belonging to a centre is
not what makes it a cut. The end of day cut is the daily kind, taken at a
centre's close. Other kinds are taken at an opening, mid-day, or at an instant a
process names, and each is still a cut.

An end of day cut is the widest case, and it is wider than most people assume.
It fixes the whole world one valuation runs against, including the reference
data that gives the deals and the quotes their meaning, such as conventions,
calendars and the valuation settings. A valuation rebuilt from the same quotes
under a different day count or a different calendar is a different number.

The kinds of cut say why a cut was taken and what it is built on. The components
say what its object is. The cluster has one page for market data, one for deals,
and one for the expiry sense of the same word.

* The shape of a cut

Three parts, and a claim about a cut is incomplete until all three are named.

- The *label*. The name a person or a system uses for the cut: a code, a
  reference date, a version. It is what a report, a deal or a book points at.
- The *timepoint*. The moment fixed, with its timezone. A kind that belongs to a
  centre names the centre as well, and a kind that does not belongs to no centre.
- The *object*. What the cut holds still.

Two cuts with the same label and the same timepoint can fix different objects.
Two that fix the same object can fix it at different timepoints. Saying "the EOD
cut" names a kind and leaves the other two open.

A cut that has been authorised also carries who signed it and when. That
signature is what makes it official rather than a candidate, and it belongs to
the object rather than sitting beside it.

* Kinds of cut

A kind states why the cut exists and what it is built on. Every kind can carry
the same object, so a kind on its own does not tell a reader what the cut holds.

** End of day cut

The primary, authoritative cut of a centre's business day. It carries the widest
object and serves the widest purpose: the day's official revaluation, the
movement from yesterday's cut to today's, and the reports built on both. It is
sometimes called the *independent cut* because approved rates replace the rates
the front office used during the day. The name should be used with care, because
the verification cut below is the one that is independent by construction.

** Independent price verification cut

Built on the EOD cut. It keeps that cut's object and replaces selected market
data items with values sourced independently of the front office, so the firm
tests its own marks against data it did not produce.

A prudential standard fixes what may count as an independent source. It accepts
exchange prices in a liquid market, trades in the same or a very similar
instrument, tradable quotes from brokers and other market participants,
consensus service data, indicative broker quotes, and counterparty collateral
valuations
([[https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/101/chapter/I][EBA RTS (EU) 2016/101 on prudent valuation]], Article 3). The same article
names the independent price verification process as the process whose market
data range those sources serve. Which items a firm replaces is its own policy.
The standard fixes the sources, not the items.

** Flash cut

A tentative cut that a valuation runs against before the day's cut is
authorised. Its purpose is to test the object while the object can still change,
so the output is indicative rather than official. A flash cut usually holds the
narrowest object of the day, enough to give the reader confidence and not
everything a full run reads.

** Start of day cut

A cut taken at the opening of a centre. It carries the previous EOD cut's object
forward, so the new day begins from a known reference and the day's movement is
measured from it.

** Where a kind belongs

A kind qualifies the cut as a whole, and the process that takes the cut chooses
it. A component qualifies the object, and what the valuation reads chooses it.
The two are separate answers to separate questions. A flash cut of the market
data alone and an EOD cut of deals and market data are both coherent cuts, and a
reader has to be told which of the two questions was answered.

* What an EOD cut must freeze

An EOD cut is *all* the data a valuation reads, and not only the deals and the
market data.

- The *deals*. The population the valuation values.
- The *market data*. Spots, curves, forward points, volatility surfaces,
  fixings, and the model parameters that are driven by the market.
- The *reference data*. The conventions, the calendars and holiday sets, the
  valuation and pricing settings, the curve configurations, and anything else
  the run reads to turn a deal and a quote into a number.
- The *identity and the authorisation*. The label, the timepoint, who signed it,
  and the version.

The reason is reproducibility. The same deals and the same quotes under a
different day count, a different calendar, or a different set of valuation
settings produce different numbers. A cut that fixed the quotes and not the
conventions could not reproduce the valuation it claims to have produced. The
object of the cut is the fixed world, and the quotes are only the part people
notice.

So the question to ask of any cut is not "which deals and which rates" but
"everything this run read". A cut that answers the narrower question is a
snapshot of a component and not a cut.

* Components and their order

The two components are frozen by separate acts, and our working convention is
that the deals come first and the market data second. The deal cut is retaken
until the population is clean, because a person may still correct a deal in
production. The market data cut follows, because a provisional market data set
is needed to value the deals and confirm they are right before anybody commits
the day's rates.

The argument for keeping the acts separate is ours. Each has its own owner, its
own review and its own failure. A corrected deal does not move a rate, and an
approved rate does not move a deal. One act that froze both would make every
trade correction a re-approval of the day's rates.

* Reading order

** The market data component

[[id:DD59B04C-DAD2-458C-B35E-3F6B0B3D6DC6][Market data cut]] — the values a
valuation reads at the timepoint: the quotes, curves, surfaces and fixings, the
approved rates applied on top, the snapshot that fixes them, the published sets
and their provenance, and what our system holds today.

** The deal component

[[id:19630B5D-A163-42B5-B9C4-61A87BAE8F5F][Trade cut]] — the population a
valuation values at the timepoint: which deals, the state each holds, the book
each belongs to, the corrections applied, and what our system holds today.

** The expiry sense of the word

[[id:3B39B2E2-6E05-4DF8-B68B-6ACA12F2FCE9][The option-expiry cut code]] — the
same idea applied to expiry: a label for the time of day at which an option
expires on its expiry date. It fixes an instant rather than a population.

* What the sources establish

Three claims in this cluster have a public source. That an independent price
verification exists, and which market data sources count as independent, rests
on a prudential standard
([[https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/101/chapter/I][EBA RTS (EU) 2016/101]], Article 3). That an official rate is published once
per working day at a stated time rests on the central bank reference rates
([[https://www.ecb.europa.eu/stats/policy_and_exchange_rates/euro_reference_exchange_rates/html/index.en.html][ECB euro foreign exchange reference rates]]). That a centre's business day has a
stated open and close, published by the settlement system for its currency,
rests on one settlement system's published hours
([[https://www.federalreserve.gov/paymentsystems/fedfunds_about.htm][Fedwire Funds Service]]).

Everything else is a definition of a term or our working convention. The order
of the two component acts, the flash runs, the start of day cut, the numbered
end of day sequence, one cut per book, the after-hours rule, and the times that
cut codes name have no public source we could verify. They are written down as
the convention we work to, not as established fact. A reader who needs the
practice of a particular firm should ask that firm.

* What this cluster does not cover

The pricing settings themselves, the engine's configuration documents, and the
reads served over stored market data belong to other clusters. This cluster says
what a cut must hold and why, and leaves the meaning of each setting to the page
that owns it.
