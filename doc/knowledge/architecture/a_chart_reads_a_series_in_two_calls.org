#+title: A chart reads a series with one history call and one live subscription
#+description: How a client plots a series: it subscribes to the live subject, makes one history call that returns every tick of the range in one reply, and merges the two by coordinate and time itself.
#+type: knowledge
#+level: cross
#+filetags: :marketdata:ticks:reads:architecture:

* Summary

A client plots a series with two separate calls. The first is a live subscription that
delivers each new tick. The second is a history call that returns every tick of a
time range in one reply. The client subscribes first, calls for the history second, and
merges the two itself by coordinate and time. The server never merges history with the
live stream for a client.

The history call is the one read of ticks. The list of observations does not return
ticks. A tick is an immutable fact in the tick table. An observation is a curated,
bitemporal row in the observation table. The two have different keys, different
corrections and different costs, so they have different reads.

* Detail

** The rule

1. *One call for the past.* The history call returns all ticks of the requested range
   in one reply. It has no pages and no cursor.
2. *One subscription for the future.* The live subject delivers each new tick. It
   carries no history.
3. *The client merges.* The client joins the two by coordinate and time. The server
   does not know which ticks the client holds from the stream.

** The live subscription

The live subject names the series in its tokens, so the broker sends the client the
ticks of that series and no others. See [[id:6F3F1A80-5B4C-4F33-ADF0-F42CDE8FF8C2][The tick ingest pipeline: freshness first]].
The lane conflates the stream: it publishes the newest tick of a datum once in each
class interval. The stream can therefore skip ticks that the history holds. A chart
buckets its points, so the skipped ticks change nothing that it shows.

The client subscribes before it asks for the history. It buffers each live tick until
the history reply arrives. A tick that arrives between the two calls is then in the
buffer, and not lost.

** The history call

The call is =marketdata.v1.ops.get_series_ticks=. It is a request and a reply.

*** The request

- *The series,* named as the live subject names it.
- *From,* the first instant of the range, inclusive.
- *To,* the end of the range, exclusive. A request without it means now.
- *The coordinates,* optional. A client that plots one pillar of a curve names it, and
  the reply holds that coordinate only.

The request has no count. A client asks for a span of time, never for "the newest N".
A span of time has the same meaning on both tiers, and it is what a chart shows.

*** The reply

The reply holds all the ticks of the range, in one message.

- The coordinate URIs of the series come first, once each.
- The ticks follow as three arrays of equal length: the time, the index of the
  coordinate, and the value. The order is by time, then by coordinate.
- The =as_of= time of the read ends the reply. Every tick that the pipeline took before
  that time is in the reply, except what a lane shed under overload.

A tick has no id and no source in the reply. A chart needs neither, and each field adds
to the size of a reply that the client reads whole. The tick table keeps the =source_id=
for audit.

A series that does not exist yet, or a range with no tick, gives an empty reply and
success. An empty series is a normal state, as it is for the snapshot.

*** The ceiling

The reply is one message, and the broker caps a message at 1 MB. The service declares a
ceiling of 50,000 ticks for each reply, about 18 bytes for each tick. This is an
estimate, and the measurement of the first build confirms or changes it. A range that
holds more ticks is refused, and the refusal names the ceiling. The service never
truncates a range, because a client that asked for a day and received an hour would read
the answer as complete. The same rule holds for the instant ceiling of the slice and the
evolution. See [[id:4EE26B23-003B-4DF1-AA83-1196CC6810C3][Snapshots and cuts]].

*** The refusals

- A context with no party is refused. The party policy of the tick table shows such a
  context no tick, and an empty answer would look complete.
- A range with =from= after =to=, or a bound that is not a time, is refused.
- A range over the ceiling is refused, as above.

The read uses the tenant-aware pool. The policies of the tick table decide what the
session sees. No role skips them.

*** Two tiers behind one reply

The lane keeps a window of recent ticks in memory. The store holds the rest. The
service answers a range from both and sends one reply:

- The span from the start of the window to now comes from the window.
- The span before the window comes from the tick table, in one probe of the index on
  series and time.

The client cannot know where the window starts, so the service joins the two tiers
inside the one reply. The join is not a merge of two sources of the same span. The
window and the store cover different spans, and the service prefers the window for the
span that it covers. A range that the window does not reach is answered from the store
alone. A restart empties the window, and the store then answers the whole range.

** The merge on the client

The key of a tick is its coordinate and its time. A tick is immutable, so two ticks
with one key are the same tick. The client keeps one of a pair.

1. Put the ticks of the history reply in the series.
2. Add each buffered live tick that the series does not hold.
3. Add each later live tick as it arrives.

The overlap between the reply and the buffer is harmless. It is the safe side of the
race between the two calls.

** What a chart does

- *On open.* Subscribe, then call for the span the chart shows, then merge.
- *On reconnect.* Subscribe again, then call with =from= set to the newest tick the
  client holds. The reply holds the missed span.
- *On scroll to an older span.* Call with =to= set to the oldest tick the client holds.
  The reply holds the older span only.

No call repeats a span that the client holds. Each call returns everything its span
holds, so a chart never loops on pages.

** What this read does not do

- *It does not list observations.* The observation list returns curated, imported and
  manual points. It never returns a tick, and a screen of a live series calls the
  history read. See [[id:E4736D88-03B2-4A40-B7F4-01B06B9B9FD7][A quote series is keyed by item, provider and party]].
- *It does not value a series at a date.* The as-of reads, the snapshot, the slice, the
  evolution and the ORE export read the tick table and the observation table, and take
  the latest value of each coordinate. That is a question of valuation. This read is a
  question of history. See [[id:4EE26B23-003B-4DF1-AA83-1196CC6810C3][Snapshots and cuts]].
- *It does not stream.* The live subscription is the stream.

** Why one reply and not pages

A client that opens a chart wants the whole span at once. Pages add round trips, a
cursor that the server must keep correct while ticks arrive, and a partial chart that
the user sees filling. A time cursor over a hypertable is cheap, but the reply still
costs a trip for each page. One reply costs one trip and one index probe. The ceiling
protects the broker, and it states its limit in the refusal.

** Open questions

- Does the first build keep the ceiling of 50,000 ticks? The measurement of reply size
  and of the read time decides.
- Does a result larger than the message cap need a path? The package design hands a
  large body over as an object in storage and sends only its reference. A history call
  could do the same above the ceiling. This page does not state it, because a client
  that needs it can narrow the range today.
- Should the reply carry the =source_id=? The page says no. A client that audits a tick
  reads the store.
