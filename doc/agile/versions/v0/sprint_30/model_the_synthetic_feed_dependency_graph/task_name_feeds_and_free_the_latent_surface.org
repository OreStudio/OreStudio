It captures the goal, current status, acceptance, and any notes or results.

* Goal

Every synthetic feed is named by the signed-off scheme, wherever a name is made: the publish, the screens' Add dialogs and the adapters' holds. The latent vol surfaces leave the Bloomberg collection for a latent collection of their own, seeded by their own dataset, and each names its spot and its two discounting curves as stored edges, so no edge is found by a rule.

* Acceptance

- Every seeded feed carries its scheme name, and a test refuses two feeds that derive one name.
- A vendor's quotes of a surface run under the vendor's own fx_option name.
- The latent surfaces are in a latent collection with no provider, seeded by synthetic.feeds.latent, and the Provider tab still shows them.
- A surface stores its base and quote discounting curves by name, a curve publishes to the board under its own name, and the surface reads the curves it names.
- The vol ticks still publish live for every pair, and the Calibration tab links the stored curves.

* Plan

One PR, by the owner's choice, in slices that each end in a check.

1. *A system provider.* The provider's kind gains =system=: a provider no
   tenant or party sees. The provider table's shared read admits a system
   tenant row only when its kind is not =system=, through a new optional model
   flag, =rls_shared_read_condition=, that the RLS template appends to the
   system-tenant clause (an entity without the flag generates byte-identical
   SQL). The provider validation runs as the table's owner, so a subscription
   to a =system= provider is refused explicitly. The =latent= provider is
   seeded with kind =system=. Tests: a tenant reads no =latent= provider; the
   system tenant reads it; a tenant's subscription to it is refused.
2. *The latent collection.* A dataset =synthetic.feeds.latent= seeds the vol
   surfaces, their expiries, contributors and links, which leave the Bloomberg
   dataset. Its feeds name the =latent= provider, so the publish makes a
   =latent= collection as it makes any other. The artefact carries the spot
   pair and the two curve names, since the collection no longer gives them.
3. *Curve edges as columns.* The surface gains =base_curve_name= and
   =quote_curve_name=, the scheme names of its discounting curves. A curve
   publishes to the board under its own name, as a spot does, and the surface
   reads the curves it names; the rule that searched the collection goes. The
   wide type ceilings of the surface's three types rise by two, as with the
   spot pair.
4. *Names.* The publish derives every feed's name by the scheme; the vendor
   views run under =synthetic.<provider>.fx.fx_option.<pair>=; the screens' Add
   dialogs default to the scheme. A test refuses two seeded feeds that derive
   one name.
5. *Screens.* The vol feed's Calibration tab links the stored curves; the
   Provider tab lists the =latent= provider for a system session only.
6. *Proof.* Rebuild, recreate, provision; check live that every pair's vol
   ticks publish, that the names are the scheme's, that a tenant sees no
   =latent= provider, and that the Calibration tab links resolve, on the BFF
   and on ores.http.
