* Goal

A pair's market at a value date is one rule in one place. The calibration reads a forward set and the surface feed reads whatever it is given, and both resolve a market -- the spot and the two discount factors -- by the same code, so the surface cannot be struck against one forward and read back against another.

Landed on this task's branch: =math::pair_market_at= in =ores.analytics.quant=, the calibration's adoption of it, one market per expiry in the surface feed's build input, and the proof that a surface's quoted volatilities are invariant to the level of its spot at =1e-12= even when its two discounts differ.

* Acceptance

- A pair's market at a value date is resolved by one block in =ores.analytics.quant=, which an exact date takes from its row's discounts and a date between rows interpolates log-linearly in days from the spot date.
- The base currency's discount is the foreign side and the quote currency's the domestic side, in the block and not in each caller.
- The calibration adopts the block and its 251 tests pass unchanged, so the move is a move.
- The surface feed carries one market for each of its expiries instead of one shared by every expiry, and refuses an expiry with no market and a market that names no expiry.
- A surface's quoted volatilities move by no more than =1e-12= when its spot moves from 1.0 to 1.30 with its discounts held, and by about =7.8e-05= when the discount ratio moves from one to =0.99/0.97=.

* Result

The block is =ores.analytics.quant/math/fx_pair_market.hpp=, with =pair_market_at= and the neutral =fx_discount_point= the two callers each build. The calibration's private =discounts_at= is deleted and =terms_at= calls the block; its suite passes unchanged, which is the proof that the move changed no number. The surface feed's build input carries =std::vector<fx_vol_expiry_market>= keyed by expiry tenor, and the emitter refuses an expiry with no market and a market that names no expiry.

Why the invariance proof mattered: it lets a surface be quoted against the discount ratio alone, without first resolving the pair's spot, which is what makes a per-expiry market a small piece of data rather than a pair of curves. It is measured, not asserted: the test holds the two discounts and moves the spot.
