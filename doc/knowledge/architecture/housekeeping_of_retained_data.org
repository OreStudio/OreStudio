#+title: Housekeeping of retained data
#+description: Every retained store is bounded by a declared policy, executed by one sweep and observed as telemetry.
#+type: knowledge
#+level: cross
#+filetags: :housekeeping:retention:storage:scheduler:architecture:
#+created: 2026-11-24
#+updated: 2026-11-24

* Summary

Housekeeping is the means by which a store the platform keeps is held to a
bound. A bound exists only where three parts are present together: a policy
that declares it, an executor that applies it, and a measurement that records
what it did. A declaration without an executor is an intention, and an executor
without a measurement cannot be shown to work; the platform has held both kinds
of half-measure, which is why the three are stated here as one mechanism. This
page gives the shape of the policy record, the single executor that applies it,
the path taken when the storage engine will not apply it itself, and the
telemetry each policy reports.

* Detail

** The three parts

| Part | Question it answers | What its absence produces |
|------+---------------------+---------------------------|
| Declaration | what is kept, and for how long | a constant in code, invisible to the operator and changed only by a rebuild |
| Execution | who applies the bound | rows, objects and directories that accumulate while the declaration reads as satisfied |
| Observation | whether it ran, and what it freed | no way to tell a working policy from an inert one |

The platform's failure has been the middle one. Every hypertable that declares a
retention interval and receives none is a declaration, and the schema guards
that decide whether to install such a policy are the reason: the guard tests the
storage engine's licence tier and takes a notice branch when the entry is
missing, so the declaration reports itself as applied while nothing drops.

** The policy is a record

A policy names the target, the action to take on it, the threshold past which
the action applies, the cadence at which it is considered, whether it is
enabled, and the fallback to take when the engine cannot act. It is stored as
data rather than held in frontmatter or in code, for two reasons: a threshold
changes without a schema rebuild, and one screen can show every policy at once
beside the size of what it governs.

** The actions

Four actions bound a store by removing what it no longer needs:

- drop the chunks of a time-partitioned table that lie past a threshold;
- delete the rows of an ordinary table that lie past a threshold;
- delete the objects under a key prefix that lie past a threshold;
- release a working directory as soon as what it produced is stored elsewhere.

Two further transforms bound a store without removing anything, and belong to
housekeeping for that reason:

- carry a counter forward instead of appending a new version of it;
- recompute derived data instead of keeping a copy per owner.

The action is a property of the class of data, not of the table. Which class
takes which action is the subject of
[[id:21D539CD-AF3B-4479-81D5-F195756C064F][Classification of retained data]].

** One executor

A single scheduled job reads the policies and performs their actions, taking its
cadence from the configured setting that the platform's other sweeps already
use ([[id:DFC57D4F-9AB2-4428-BDEC-BF44D106A75D][Scheduler]]). One executor
serves every class because the classes differ in what they hold and in when
their contents stop being useful, not in how a sweep runs. The established
example is [[id:F06175E8-CA73-4A21-BEC5-898D0A388E17][Approval request
expiry]], whose sweep a service registers at runtime from a system setting; it
demonstrates the shape that housekeeping generalises from one subject to every
store.

** The fallback path

A time-series engine applies a retention policy as a background job, and the
job exists only where the engine's licensed edition includes it. Where that
edition is absent the policy is never created and the declaration reads as
satisfied. The executor therefore performs the action itself whenever the engine
will not, and records which path ran, so that the same declaration is honoured
on either edition and the difference is visible rather than inferred. This is
the gap recorded for the tick store in
[[id:A79C25FA-9F70-453B-A52E-780B4402ABAA][Bounded retention in the tick store]],
generalised to every class.

** The telemetry

Each policy reports, per sweep, the size of its target before and after, the
rows, chunks or objects removed, the time taken and the outcome. The measured
size over time yields a growth rate, which is the quantity a capacity decision
needs and the one an operator cannot obtain from a single reading.

The telemetry belongs beside the declaration rather than in a separate console:
a policy's row shows what it governs and what the last sweep freed, together, so
that a policy which is enabled and freeing nothing is visible as such. The
sources of the size are the database's own catalogues for tables and chunks, the
object store's listing for key prefixes, and a directory walk for the caches and
working directories a node holds.
