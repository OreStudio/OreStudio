* Summary

The prototype argues that a wrong publish is undone as an act with a reason, and
that the withdrawal can need its own approval. Three variants draw the undo, and
three states the kind that governs it.

It is throwaway: it reads no service, every value is mock data, and no operation
stands behind the controls. The
[[id:B05A31CC-FA22-4534-848E-601A30822992][journey]] settles what undoing a
publish *does*; this page settles what the trader *sees* when the version that
was published cannot be named.

Open the [[file:withdraw-or-replace-a-published-curve/][screen]].

* Detail

** The question

The journey gives the trader one screen: the published curve at the top, its
versions and the proposed change in the middle, and the reason, the action and
the effect on consumers at the bottom. The open question is how that undo is
drawn when nothing marks which version was published, and how the same screen
reads a count that is zero or more: at zero the trader withdraws alone and the
act applies at once, at one or more a person stands between the ask and the undo.

** The variants

Switch with the bottom bar, or with =?variant=a=, =?variant=b= or =?variant=c=;
the default is =a=.

| Variant | Name | The structural argument |
|---------+------+-------------------------|
| =a= | The withdraw form | The curve and its versions, the reason and the withdraw action, then the consumers and the decisions. The undo as one act. |
| =b= | The replace form | The same, with the replacement's as-of and source beside the reason and the act named replace. |
| =c= | The version being retired beside the replacement | The retired version against the replacement proposed, with the field-level difference under them, so the change is read before it is made. |

The variants disagree about which act the screen is, not about the curve: all
three render the same header, versions, consumers and decisions.

** The states

Switch with the bottom bar, or with =?state=no-approval=, =?state=approval= or
=?state=withdrawn=; the default is =no-approval=. The state sets the kind's
=approvals_required= and the record already in the history.

| State | What it shows |
|-------+---------------|
| =no-approval= | =approvals_required= is 0, so the withdrawal applies at once. The field reads *0 - the trader withdraws alone*, and nothing waits. |
| =approval= | One approver holding =marketdata::curve_withdrawal:decide= must decide. The decisions panel carries one waiting row raised by =a.reyes= under =marketdata.curve_withdrawal=. |
| =withdrawn= | Withdrawn at 2026-10-06 17:05 by =m.oduya=. The act is a new version, not a deletion: =v4.1= withdrawn beside =v4= superseded, and the decision approved with *Stale pillar confirmed.* |

The state is the argument in one control: =no-approval= makes the click the act,
=approval= adds a waiting row, =withdrawn= shows the reason attached.

** Driving it

The bottom bar switches variant and state and writes every change to the query
string through =URLSearchParams=, so any pair can be linked:
=?variant=c&state=withdrawn=. The banner states the variant, the state and the
kind's count.

The reason field and the replacement as-of are live inputs but inert: nothing
reads them. *Raise withdraw request*, *Raise replace request*, *Withdraw now*
and *Replace now* are disabled because no operation answers them. The one live
control is the curve identity, which opens the oresmd URL editor in an overlay.

** What is fake

Everything: the curve, its identity, derivation, recipe and observations; the
six points and their values; the two versions with their writers, clocks and
sources; the reason; the three consumers; the kind and its count; and the
decision, its approver and its comment are constants in the page.

There is no server, no session and no build step. The controls call nothing: no
curve is withdrawn, no version is superseded, no consumer is told and nothing is
stored.

** Where the code lives

=doc/prototypes/market_data/withdraw-or-replace-a-published-curve/=. The
directory holds one file, =index.html=, with its CSS and JavaScript inline: no
framework, no =prototype.css=, no =prototype.js= and no build step. The site
copies the directory as it stands and adds nothing to it. The identity button
opens =../oresmd-url-editor/index.html= in an overlay, borrowing the editor
prototype beside it.

** The verdict

The group accepted the argument recorded as the row this note answers to: a wrong
publish is undone as an act with a reason, and the withdrawal can need its own
approval. What the group took is the act with its tail: the reason is labelled
*kept against the act*, so the undo leaves a why as well as a what, and the
retired version stays beside its replacement rather than being deleted. The
count is the kind's rule, read at both ends, so a tenant may let a trader
withdraw alone or require an approver.

The three variants, the three states, the disabled controls and the consumers
and decisions panels stayed on the page as the record.

** What it does not settle

The withdrawal and replacement operations. No operation undoes a publish, so the
controls are disabled; the candidates are =curve_bootstrap.withdraw= and
=curve_bootstrap.replace=.

The published state. Nothing marks a version published: no =published_at=, no
=published_by=, no superseded link, so the screen cannot say which version
pricing is using.

The withdrawal kind and its count. No market-data withdrawal kind is seeded, and
=iam.role_grant= does not withdraw a curve; the candidate is
=marketdata.curve_withdrawal= with a configurable =approvals_required=, and the
four-eyes rule is drawn but nothing here enforces it.

The consumers and the baseline. No read maps this curve to its consumers, no
notification kind tells them it changed, and the compare uses the version read
back.
