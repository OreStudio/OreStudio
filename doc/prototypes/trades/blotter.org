* Summary

The arc built two screens: a blotter of trades and a list of structures with a
page of its own. Neither can answer a question that spans them. "What does this
book hold?" needs the trades *and* the structures, and "what have we done with
this counterparty?" needs both again under a different order.

This prototype argues for one screen, as the owner shaped it on 2026-10-29: a
portfolio tree on the left, the trade list beside it, and rows that are the
top-level trades and the structures. A structure is one row and its legs are
inside it, so a click opens the structure view; a trade that belongs to no
structure opens the trade view. A *flat* option reads the same list as leaves.

The screens it replaces are [[file:structure-page.org][the structure page]] and the
structures list inside it. The trade view it opens is
[[file:trade-page.org][the trade list and page]].

* Detail

** The tree, and where a structure sits

The tree is the portfolio: the party, its desks and their books, from the mock
book table. A row's book is the book it was booked in. A structure has no book of
its own, so it takes the book its legs share; when its legs span books it sits at
the node that holds them all — the desk when they share one, the party when they
do not — and the row says *spans books*. The investigation
[[id:1E035C4E-119B-472E-96FA-310D8B7C7CD8][The book of a structure]] records why
the rule is not enforced.

The mock data has both cases. =PKG-0004=, =STR-0009= and =STR-0007= sit in one
book each. =STR-0010= spans =EMEA-FX= and =EMEA-CREDIT= through its inner package,
so it sits at the party and carries the chip.

** The list

The rows are the top-level trades and the whole structures. A structure's legs are
never rows of it: they are inside the structure. The row states the structure's
kind, and the one chip it carries is *spans books*, when its legs are booked in
more than one book. The columns are the identifier, what it is, the book, the
counterparty, the trade date, the state and the external version, and a count of
legs for a structure. A structure has no state and no external version, and the
cells say so rather than borrowing one leg's.

** Flat

=Flat= expands every structure into its legs, so every row is a trade and the
structure a leg belongs to becomes a column of its own. The flat list is what a
reader uses to see risk trade by trade; it loses the commercial unit, which is why
it is an option and not the default.

** Perspectives

The toolbar holds three: *Portfolio*, *Counterparty* and *Asset class*. The first
is the default and the one that ships first. A perspective is a topology over the
same rows and not a different read: the rail changes, the rows narrow, and the
columns stay the ones a trade has. The task
[[id:5057B9A6-66F0-4E4E-967F-DF915809DF3E][Merge the trades and structures screens]]
carries the exploration of the word itself.

* What is fake

Every number and row is the shared mock data of the prototypes; nothing is read
from a server. A row's link opens the trade page or the structure page prototype
rather than a record page of this screen. Paging is stated and not drawn: the
list is one page of 32 top-level rows, which is small enough to show whole. The
"spans books" chip is real for the mock data and would be computed from the legs
in the product.
