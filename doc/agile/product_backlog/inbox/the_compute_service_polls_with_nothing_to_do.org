This page is a [[id:671F18E4-E09C-4B3B-BD24-D33DF8AE38A6][capture]] in the *inbox* bucket of the product backlog — a pre-sprint idea, not yet pulled into a sprint as a story.

* What

Find out what ores.compute.service does with an idle grid, and stop it paying
for it. Either the loop is doing work nobody asked for, or its interval is far
shorter than the work needs.

* Why

Seen on brave_hopper on 2026-10-13: the service sits at roughly 5% CPU
permanently, with no batches in flight and no workunits to claim. Its log is a
continuous stream of NATS request/reply round trips, about one every half a
second, never stopping, in three repeating shapes:

#+begin_example
NATS publish: _INBOX.AJ0Q0GTSB8IB.0_LR000000000
NATS publish: _INBOX.S76BH2UBYPTY.0_lR000000000
NATS publish: _INBOX.6I20BC0M9WLH.0_iR000000000
#+end_example

A call every half-second that changes nothing is the same shape as the market
data ingest's cost: a per-iteration round trip rather than payload. On its own
5% is survivable, but this is one of nineteen services on one machine, and the
whole fleet pays an idle tax for each loop like it.

Three timer-driven loops and the dispatcher are the candidates, all in
=projects/ores.compute/service/src/app/=:

- =compute_grid_poller= — "Samples server-side compute grid metrics on a fixed
  interval", once per interval.
- =lease_reaper= — reclaims the claims a host abandoned; it publishes on the
  work-available subject.
- =batch_workflow_bridge= — "runs every @p interval_seconds seconds. For each
  pending…"; it publishes and then removes, with a comment about a restart
  between the two.
- =workunit_dispatcher= — publishes one assignment per result.

Each takes its interval as a constructor argument from
=cfg.telemetry_interval_seconds= (=application.cpp:145=).

What is not yet established, and is the first thing to find: which of these owns
the half-second traffic, and whether the rate is intended. The guess worth
testing first is the grid poller, because sampling a grid that is doing nothing
is the one loop whose work does not depend on there being anything to do — but
that is a guess and not a measurement. The three shapes repeating may be a clue:
if each shape is one loop, then more than one of them is running at that rate.

The next probe is cheap and decisive: name each loop's interval as it starts,
or watch which subject each shape is addressed to, since an =_INBOX= reply
subject does not say what was asked. Querying the service's own logs at debug
level for a few seconds should separate them without a rebuild.
