* Goal

Every durable NATS handler takes an =ores::nats::delivery= and settles it
once. The transport asks cnats for manual acks, so the delivery is the only
owner of its cnats message. A subscription is destroyed only after its open
deliveries end. =ack_guard=, =ack_handle= and the =ack= field of =message= are
gone.

* Acceptance

- =delivery= is move-only. =commit=, =rollback=, =rollback_after= and =reject=
  are rvalue-qualified, and the destructor rolls back an unsettled delivery.
- =keep_alive= reports progress on a thread the delivery owns, and the thread
  ends before the delivery is settled or freed.
- Both durable subscribe calls build their options in one function, which sets
  =ManualAck=.
- =drain_and_release= waits for the subscription's open deliveries before it
  destroys the subscription.
- The 37 durable registrations and their handlers take a delivery. The two
  core claim handlers lose their no-op guards.
- Unit tests pin the settlement rules with a fake settler. An integration test
  keeps a delivery past its callback and settles it on another thread.

* Plan

1. Add =delivery= and =delivery_settler= to =ores.nats/domain=. The keep-alive
   thread lives behind a private implementation, so the header carries no
   thread type.
2. In the client, replace =ack_sink= with =jetstream_settler=, which owns the
   cnats message and one count of its subscription's open deliveries. The
   callback builds a delivery for a durable subscription and a plain message
   for a core one, from a =std::variant= of the two handler types.
3. Move =serve_step_commands= to deliveries. The claim renewer becomes the
   delivery's keep-alive.
4. Migrate the registrations and the handlers with two scripts, one for the
   registration lambdas and one for the handler signatures, guards and
   answers. Remove each comment that introduced a removed guard.
5. Update the pattern pages /Acknowledge What Was Handled/ and /At Least Once
   Prerequisites/.

* Result

=ores::nats::delivery= is the only owner of a durable cnats message. Both
durable subscribe calls set =ManualAck=, so cnats no longer acknowledges and
frees the message after the callback. That removes the double free that
crashed IAM when a step ran on a worker thread. Every durable handler takes a
delivery and settles it once; a dropped delivery rolls back. A subscription
waits for its open deliveries before it is destroyed. =ack_guard=,
=ack_handle= and the =ack= field of =message= are gone.

All acceptance points are met:

- =delivery= is move-only, its settlements are rvalue-qualified, and the
  destructor rolls back. Eight unit tests pin this with a fake settler.
- =keep_alive= runs on a thread the delivery owns and stops before the answer.
- One function builds the durable options and sets =ManualAck=.
- =drain_and_release= waits for open deliveries.
- The 37 registrations and their handlers take a delivery.
- An integration test keeps a delivery past its callback and settles it on
  another thread.

The build also fixed three IAM guard tests that named accounts the shared
test database refuses, and the workflow engine tests now pass deliveries.

Provisioning Acme on a fresh database completed steps 1 to 12 with no service
crash. Step 13 (=generate_trades=) fails on the GBP swap convention defect,
which the hotfix /Draw only swap indices with one convention/ fixes.
