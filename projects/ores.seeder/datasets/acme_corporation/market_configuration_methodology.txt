ACME Market Configuration Methodology
=====================================

Each ACME legal entity holds two documents that say which market it marks its
trades on: a curve configuration, which defines its curves and surfaces, and a
today's market, which names the ones a run reads. Both are generated by
generate_configuration_data.py in this directory and staged through DQ, one
dataset per entity and document (acme.<entity>.curve_configuration and
acme.<entity>.todays_market). The publish writes them to the party a publish
names. The documents hold no market data. The levels come from the quotes the
party's subscriptions deliver.

1. CURRENCIES

   An entity builds curves for its own currencies (generate_party_data.py,
   CURRENCIES) that have an overnight swap convention. The holding company
   builds them for the currencies of its subsidiaries' intercompany loans and
   hedges: GBP, USD, HKD and EUR.

2. DISCOUNT CURVES

   One overnight discount curve for each currency, <CCY>1D, bootstrapped
   from that currency's overnight index swaps. The index is the currency's
   risk-free overnight rate (SOFR, ESTER, SONIA, TONA and so on), as the
   FSB's 2014 report "Reforming Major Interest Rate Benchmarks" and the
   transitions that followed it make the market discounting standard.

   The pillars are the ladder the synthetic feeds publish, 1M to 30Y, each
   starting on the trade date. A curve can only name quotes a feed publishes.

   The curve interpolates log-linearly in the discount factor, on Actual/365.
   This keeps the instantaneous forwards piecewise flat and positive between
   pillars (P. Hagan and G. West, Interpolation Methods for Curve
   Construction, Applied Mathematical Finance 13(2), 2006).

3. INFLATION CURVES

   A zero coupon inflation curve for each currency the refdata catalogue
   carries an index for: UKRPIX for GBP, USCPIA for USD and BECPIHL for EUR.
   Each is named after its index, discounts on the currency's overnight
   curve, observes with a three month lag and interpolates linearly in the
   zero rate.

4. FX VOLATILITY SURFACES

   One FX volatility curve for each pair the system calibrates a surface for
   (see the FX Volatility Surface Blueprint Methodology) whose two currencies
   the entity builds a curve for. A surface needs both curves to turn a delta
   into a strike. On the current currencies this gives the holding company
   EUR/USD, GBP/USD and EUR/GBP; London all ten pairs; New York EUR/USD,
   GBP/USD, USD/JPY, USD/CAD, EUR/GBP and EUR/JPY; and Hong Kong AUD/USD,
   USD/JPY and NZD/USD.

   Each curve reads the published surface as ORE quotes, so ORE prices from
   the published grid and from nothing else:

   - Dimension Smile and smile type Delta. The export writes each grid point
     as FX_OPTION/RATE_LNVOL/<base>/<quote>/<expiry>/<delta>, which is the
     quote this smile type reads (ORE FXVolatilityCurveConfig).
   - Expiries 1W, 1M, 2M, 3M, 6M, 9M and 1Y, and deltas 10P, 25P, ATM, 25C
     and 10C: the grid of the pair's blueprint.
   - Conventions <base>-<quote>-FXOPTION, the pair's FX option convention,
     which states the delta and ATM types the quotes are read in. The
     blueprint's grid uses the same delta type, so a quote and the strike it
     was computed at agree.
   - The spot FX/<base>/<quote>, the foreign curve Yield/<base>/<base>1D and
     the domestic curve Yield/<quote>/<quote>1D: the entity's own overnight
     curves, so the strikes are those of its own market. ORE builds a cross's
     spot by triangulation from the dollar spots the entity holds.
   - Cubic smile interpolation in delta. The grid is already a smooth,
     arbitrage-free SSVI slice, and a cubic spline keeps its curvature between
     the five deltas, where a linear one would cut it into chords. Flat
     extrapolation beyond 10 delta, ORE's default, so a far strike takes the
     wing's last volatility and never an extrapolated slope.
   - Time interpolation V, in variance, the blueprint's own rule between
     expiries, and a butterfly error tolerance of 0.01. Both follow the
     EUR/USD entry of ORE's Products example
     (external/ore/examples/Products/Input/curveconfig.xml).
   - No calendar and no day counter. ORE then takes the pair's joint
     calendar and Actual/365, which is what the example states explicitly.

   The curve id is the pair, such as EURUSD, as in ORE's examples.

5. TODAY'S MARKET

   One configuration, default. It discounts every trade on its currency's
   overnight curve, forwards each overnight index on the same curve, names
   the spot of each dollar pair, and names the volatility curve of each pair
   of section 4 under the pair, as FXVolatility/<base>/<quote>/<pair>.
