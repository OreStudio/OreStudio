* Summary

The registration door prototype argues what the [[id:33B0C287-8262-4C2A-9F7B-3F74C06586AE][Sign up]] screen should look
like before any of it is built. It carries three structural variants of the same
door, and the five states the door can be in, so the layout can be judged with
something concrete in front of you rather than in the abstract.

It is throwaway. It is not production code, it reads no service, and every value
on it is mock data. It exists because the [[id:84706C3E-D48D-4A4B-967A-4AADB3CF44B2][target state]] settles what a
registration *does*; nothing yet settles what the person *sees* while it does it.

Open the [[file:registration-door/][door]].

* Detail

** The question

The target state gives the door one answer to ask for and five states to render:
closed, the form, a refusal, a usable account, and a waiting one. The open
question is how those belong on one screen, and especially how a person learns
that their new account may not be usable the moment it exists. Getting that wrong
produces the journey's original defect: a person registers, then fails at sign-in
with a message about a party they have never heard of.

** The three variants

Switch with the bottom bar, the left and right arrow keys, or =?variant=.

| Variant | Name | The structural argument |
|---------+------+-------------------------|
| =A= | One panel | The form and the destination in a single column. The outcome replaces the form. Closest to the journey document, which says the whole door is one screen with panels. |
| =B= | Form and status | The form beside a live column that states the tenant, the party, the role, and whether the account will work at once. The consequence is visible before the person submits, not after. |
| =C= | Step rail | The journey shape the bootstrap screens use: a named rail, one step at a time, review before the write. Consistent with [[id:84C231B8-C861-44CF-89FB-D7A840D3EBDE][First run]], and the most work for the least certainty. |

The variants disagree about structure, not colour. A is one column and hides the
consequence below the fold until the outcome. B gives the consequence equal
standing and spends horizontal space on it. C defers the write to a review step.

** The five states

Switch with the buttons at the top right, or =?state=.

| State | What it shows |
|-------+---------------|
| =closed= | Self-registration is off. The screen states the reason and offers the way back to the door. |
| =register= | The form: username, email, password and confirmation, with the password rules beside them. |
| =refused= | The form with a refusal, carrying its code: =username_taken=. |
| =created= | The confirmation for an account that can sign in now, naming the party and the role it received. |
| =waiting= | The confirmation for an account that cannot sign in yet, naming what it waits for and carrying =account_pending=. |

The nomination toggle changes the deployment's answer to "does this tenant
nominate a default party?" while the form is up. With it on, the account is
usable; with it off, the same screen produces a waiting account. That toggle is
the whole point of the prototype: it makes the two confirmations distinguishable
by a person who is not reading the code.

** Driving it

The form is live. Fill it in and press *Create account* on any variant, and the
outcome follows the nomination toggle. Every control is prototype chrome, drawn
to look unlike the design being judged.

** What is fake

Everything. The tenant, the party, the role and the rules are hard-coded. There
is no policy read, no server, no account, and nothing is stored.

** Where the code lives

=doc/prototypes/registration-door/=. Three files, plain HTML, CSS and
JavaScript, no framework and no build step. The site build copies the directory
through =ores-deploy-web-app=, beside the trade entry prototype.

** What it does not settle

Which variant wins, and what is stolen from the others. The target state fixes
the policy the door reads; this page only argues how that policy is shown. The
implementation is scheduled from the target state's change table, not from here.
