This page documents a [[id:699A8D45-B67E-4D3E-9206-24FA17C51ADA][story]] in [[id:9F7C5484-E2D1-40E0-B24D-AD1B69A057F5][Sprint 30]]. It captures the goal, current status, acceptance criteria, and the tasks that compose it.

* Goal

A workflow step calls a service over the bus, and every one of those requests
takes the same 30-second deadline, whatever work it asks for. A provisioning run
failed at step 13 of 15 because one calibration took longer than that on a
loaded host. The step had already published 9 of its 10 surfaces. The run failed
on the first timeout, with no retry, and the only thing it reported was
=NATS request timed out=.

After this story a step's requests are bounded to the work they ask for. A
transient failure is retried with backoff and jitter inside the step's own
budget, and only when repeating the request changes nothing. A refusal fails at
once. A step that gives up names the party and the item it was working on, with
its reason, instead of the transport's words. Both clients show that failure,
and the browser shows the progress a step already reports.

The engine now fails a step only when its progress stands still, and a step
reports progress as its heartbeat. That arrived in
[[id:A4D0E305-CEF6-4684-ABBA-0D09657479F1][Keep workflow steps alive while they work, and off iam]], whose
own task bounds the work one request may do. This story depends on that work and
does not repeat it.

The focus is the requests a workflow step makes and what a person sees when one
fails. This is not a general retry framework for every call site, and not the
whole audit of the NATS patterns.

* Acceptance

- Every request a provisioning step makes either takes the shared default
  deliberately or states a deadline sized to its work, and the reason is in the
  code beside it.
- A transient failure is retried with backoff and jitter inside the step's
  remaining budget, and every attempt is logged with its number and its reason.
- A refusal is never retried, and no request is retried unless repeating it
  leaves the store unchanged.
- A step that retries and then succeeds completes. A step that still cannot
  finish fails with a message that names the party and the item it was working
  on, the attempts it took and the reason.
- ores.shell and ores.web both show the failure, and the browser shows the
  progress a running step reports, which the wire already carries.
- A test drives a step against a responder that answers late and one that
  refuses, and proves the retry, the refusal and the failure message.

* Decisions

- Retry only a transient transport failure, and only where repeating the
  request changes nothing. A refusal is never retried, and neither is a request
  whose second attempt would write a second row.
- A deadline is stated against the work, in one place per class of request, so
  no call site reinvents the number. The shared 30-second default stays the
  deadline for a read or a handshake.
- The step's remaining budget is the outer bound of its retries, so a retry
  cannot outlive the step the engine judges.
- The progress channel is not built here. A step reports progress as its
  heartbeat, the engine fails on a stall rather than on elapsed time, and the
  shell prints the line. That arrived with
  [[id:A4D0E305-CEF6-4684-ABBA-0D09657479F1][Keep workflow steps alive while they work, and off iam]].
  This story consumes the channel and fills the gap it left: the browser
  rendering and a failure that names the work.
- The responder bounds its own work, and that belongs to
  [[id:9CA04D58-E330-4F27-AF8A-B3BDBB2E63EC][Bound the work of a request]] in the same story. The calibrate
  handler's whole-table pillar read is recorded there as evidence.
- A failure names the work: the party, the surface or pair, the attempt and the
  reason. The transport string alone is not a report.
- A fault that is not a transport failure stays permanent for the retry helper,
  and the step-level retry is what recovers it. The live run proved this: a
  =std::bad_alloc= failed =generate_trades=, and the engine's retry of that step
  completed all 17 steps. The fault itself is data-dependent and is captured as
  [[id:9232CDC9-8444-4856-8D3D-CA7CD4EFA5EC][Generating a bond issue fails with std::bad_alloc]].
- The live run completes provisioning on this host: 6,541 trades, 37 bond
  issues, 80 surface publishes and 40 surfaces, with the heartbeat's progress
  printed for each step.

* Out of scope

- An asynchronous calibrate that accepts the work and answers with an id to
  poll. It is the fallback if the bound on a request's work cannot meet a sane
  deadline, and it belongs with that bound.
- Retrying every request at the NATS client for every caller. Only the helper
  the workflow steps use changes.
- The engine's dispatch, delivery and claim retries, and the heartbeat deadline,
  which are already built.
- Rewriting the provisioning steps that are not requests: the trade generator,
  the calendars and the bundles keep their current shape.
