* Summary

=ores.marketdata= is a four-module composite (=api=, =client=, =core=,
=service=) of kind *Entity* and *Protocol*. It is the least clean
component surveyed so far, and the only one whose models describe a
domain that a *second, parallel* identifier scheme already exists for.
Five things are true at once:

1. The **checked-in generated tree is far behind its models**. A dry-run
   drift check reports **82 files would change and 14 would be created**
   (96 in total). Almost all of it is template movement the component has
   never absorbed: the value-equality operator, the =recorded_at=
   removal, the =List*= request/response rename, the generated eventing
   headers, and per-entity shell units.
2. **Regenerating it now would not compile.** Three hand-written files
   reference wire types the models remove, and a generated junction shell
   unit includes a protocol header that no facet emits. The component
   needs its generator and model defects fixed *before* it can be
   regenerated at all, which is what makes the usual "regenerate and
   commit" first step impossible here.
3. **oresmd is built, tested -- and wired to nothing.** The URI scheme,
   its identifier and requirement types, the parser, the resolver and the
   four projections are all generated from eight models and carry an
   extensive test suite. No production source file calls any of them.
   Only =split_market_series_key()= escapes, into the ingest loop and
   =ores.synthetic=.
4. **oresmd covers 26.0% of the real ORE corpus, and the models are the
   defect.** Measured against the 100 committed fixtures at
   =external/ore/examples/=, 352,819 of 477,013 key lines have no URI.
   Ten quote types that oresmd *does* model cannot hold the keys ORE
   actually writes, and the IR path silently rewrites =0D= to =2D= on
   1,338 lines today. Nine fixtures abort outright.
5. **The native-identity target state was already designed and then
   abandoned.** Sprint 25's =adopt-oresmd-native-market-data-identity=
   story decided the exact end state the owner describes, and seven of
   its nine implementation tasks were abandoned at sprint close. A branch,
   =feature/identity-core-series-and-bindings=, holds an implementation
   of step 1 of that plan; it was never merged.

The component is otherwise in reasonable health: all four test binaries
build, and 951 of 952 assertions pass, the single failure being an
already-captured unwired event registrar. This is a clean-up, not a
rescue.

The clean-up and the native-representation goal are the same work. This
page establishes where each piece stands and proposes how to cut it into
PRs.

The committed protocol headers still use the retired =get_*= CRUD naming

* The oresmd state of adoption

** What exists

Two model families live under =projects/ores.marketdata/modeling/=:

- Seven =ores.codegen.entity= / junction models under =modeling/= --
  =feed_binding=, =market_fixing=, =market_observation=, =market_series=,
  =market_series_asset_class= (junction), =observation_lineage=,
  =series_classification_rule=.
- Eight =ores.codegen.oresmd_quote_type= models under =modeling/oresmd/=
  -- one per asset class (=ir=, =credit=, =equity=, =commodity=, =fx=,
  =inflation=, =correlation=) plus =model.org=, the batch manifest. This
  is a metatype of its own: it carries no variability and produces no
  entity stack. It generates =oresmd_enums=, =oresmd_identifiers=,
  =oresmd_requirements=, the parser, the resolver, the projections and
  four test files.

** Coverage, measured against the corpus

The ORE series-type universe is 43 types, seeded in
=projects/ores.sql/populate/ore/ore_series_key_shapes_populate.sql=.
By *type count* oresmd projects 30 of the 43. The types =from_ore_key()=
deliberately rejects are the option, capfloor, bond and fixing families:
=BOND=, =BOND_OPTION=, =FX_OPTION=, =CAPFLOOR=, =INDEX_CDS_OPTION=,
=EQUITY_OPTION=, =COMMODITY_OPTION=, =ZC_INFLATIONCAPFLOOR=,
=YY_INFLATIONCAPFLOOR=, =FIXING=, plus =SHAPE_PROFILE=, =RATING= and
=GENERIC=.

Type count flatters it. Measured against the whole committed corpus --
100 fixtures, 477,013 key lines, 107,948 distinct keys -- **oresmd
represents 26.0% of the data**: 124,194 lines project to a URI and
352,819 do not.

The rejects split in two, and the second half is the surprise.

*Half one: 232,545 lines (48.8%) belong to the 13 of 43 registry types
that have no oresmd coverage at all.* EQUITY_OPTION 119,137, CAPFLOOR
78,270, ZC_INFLATIONCAPFLOOR 14,058, FX_OPTION 10,735,
YY_INFLATIONCAPFLOOR 4,366, INDEX_CDS_OPTION 3,026, COMMODITY_OPTION
2,346, SHAPE_PROFILE 171, BOND 154, BOND_OPTION 154, RATING 128, and
=GENERIC= and =FIXING= with no corpus lines. This is the gap the
abandoned story already recorded: oresmd models seven asset classes and
ORE carries more.

*Half two: quote types oresmd does claim cannot hold the keys ORE
writes.* Ten of the 29 oresmd quote types have **zero** corpus coverage
and six are partial:

| ORE type | Lines projected | Why |
|----------+-----------------+-----|
| BASIS_SWAP | 0 / 5049 | real shape is long_tenor/short_tenor/ccy/maturity, not ccy/index/tenor/point |
| CC_BASIS_SWAP | 0 / 2720 | four qualifier segments, not three |
| FRA | 0 / 1678 | index and tenor order differs from the model |
| ZERO | 0 / 498 | shape differs |
| MM | 10 / 1436 (2.6%) | shape differs |
| CC_FIX_FLOAT_SWAP | 0 / 106 | four qualifier segments |
| IMM_FRA | 0 / 108 | shape differs |
| MM_FUTURE, OI_FUTURE | 0 / 109 | real shape is ccy/contract_month/exchange:code/tenor |
| CORRELATION | 0 / 61 | real shape is operand1/operand2/expiry/strike, six segments |
| CPR | 0 / 1 | real key is =CPR/RATE/ISIN:...= |
| IR_SWAP | 84.0% | 7-segment keys rejected |
| SWAPTION | 37.7% | 7/8-segment smile keys, 91,689 lines |
| DISCOUNT | 80.0% | =DISCOUNT/<CCY>/<CCY>= rejected by an off-by-one |
| CDS | 19.4% | shape differs |
| RECOVERY_RATE | 54.6% | shape differs |

The oresmd quote-type models were derived from ORE's documented shapes,
not from the corpus. Where the two disagree, the corpus is the authority
and the model is the defect.

**The registry fallback is healthy.** =decompose= followed by
=reconstruct_key= is verbatim-lossless for **100%** of the corpus, and
neither the unknown-type nor the short-key fallback fires on a single
line. So the shape table itself is sound; the losses are in the
projection layer above it.

Four further findings from the same walk:

- **A silent rewrite on the live import path.** The IR 6-segment path
  discards the settlement segment and rebuilds it as the hardcoded ="2D"=.
  The corpus carries =0D= on 1,338 six-segment keys, and every one of them
  is imported as =2D=; a further 2,221 seven-segment keys are rejected
  outright. This is not an export problem: the stored row already differs
  from the file.
- **A derivation, not a guard, costs 9,075 lines.** =from_ir_discount= treats
  the key's fourth segment as the currency with a tenor appended, so it strips
  the currency back off to recover a =tenor=. The segment is really the name
  =curveconfig.xml= gives the curve -- the corpus carries =USD3M= beside
  =USD-SOFR-3M=, =USD-FedFunds=, =USD-DUMMY= and the bare currency -- so a
  curve named after its own currency leaves nothing behind and the guard
  rejects the key. It is not the =<=/-vs-<=< comparison the first pass called
  it: relaxing that guard would leave =id.tenor= unset and the forward
  dereferences it. The other 36,948 keys project while carrying a tenor such
  as =-SOFR-3M=, and round-trip cleanly, which is why the round-trip
  measurement never showed this.
- **A single stray comma aborts a whole file.** The tokeniser needs two
  commas and a CSV line with exactly one loses the entire file --
  19,170 lines across the large CSVs.
- **Nine fixtures cannot be parsed at all**: seven on date format
  (=DD-MM-YYYY= and =DD.MM.YY=, 23,497 lines) and two on that malformed
  CSV line. The two named =fx_localvol/market.txt= fixtures are among the
  seven.

=FIXING= deserves its own note: fixings are imported and stored as series
=series_type="FIXING"=, and =from_ore_key("FIXING/RATE/EUR-EONIA")=
returns =nullopt=. There is no =type=fixing= in either direction, and no
=type=curve= either. The fixing key space cannot round-trip through oresmd
at all today.

** The security class exposed two generator defects

Neither was visible until a new spec file and a multi-member last spec existed.
Both are fixed at source, and both are the kind that fail silently:

- **Umbrella outputs were rendered once per spec file.** The oresmd facet's
  manifest is meant to be the only generation entry point, and its loader reads
  every sibling spec itself. But discovery picks up any file whose frontmatter
  declares a codegen model type, and every spec declares the same type as the
  manifest, so each spec was generated separately into the same eight output
  files. Whichever path sorted last won, and =model.org= had been winning only
  because it sorts after every spec. =security_quote_type.org= sorts after it, so
  the generated headers collapsed to the new class alone. Discovery now treats a
  directory with a batch manifest as generated through the manifest alone.

- **The outer =last= marker suppressed the inner one.** A mustache section
  resolves a key it does not find in its own context from the enclosing one. The
  specs list was marked with =last= to keep the variant argument list from
  rendering a trailing comma, and the quote-type loop inside each spec also used
  ={{^last}}= for its own commas -- so every member of the last spec lost its
  comma and the enum became one long line. It never showed because the last spec
  held a single member. The outer lists are now marked =last_spec=.

The first is the more dangerous: a new spec file could replace the whole
generated set, and the failure surfaced only because the result would not
compile. The second produced syntactically broken output for a spec that merely
sorted last.

Every asset class that declares a volatility field now reaches a projection, so
the gate's unwired list is empty -- the first exception list this work has
emptied rather than shortened.

Each of the remaining shapes needs the identifier grammar extended, and the
oresmd parser is hand-written per asset class rather than table-driven -- its
generic branch handles the entity, a currency, the type, the quote type and a
point, and nothing else -- so a new field is a coordinated change across the
model, the parser, the URI order and the projections.

** The futures contradiction was the documentation's, not the data's

=MM_FUTURE= and =OI_FUTURE= were recorded here as needing a registry correction
first, because the shape table describes the two with different qualifier
depths, so which segments identify the series looked unsettled. Reading the
corpus settles it. Both are 6-segment and identical in shape -- the corpus
writes =MM_FUTURE/PRICE/EUR/2024-04/XICE:FEI/3M= and
=OI_FUTURE/PRICE/USD/2024-01/XCME:SRA/3M= -- for all 939 keys, with no
5-segment form anywhere in the corpus.

The contradiction was in the documents. The market data catalogue carried the
abbreviated key (=/USD/2025-09/3M=, the contract code dropped), and the model
had copied the registry's mislabelled =CCY/INDEX/TENOR/POINT=, which puts the
contract month in a field called =index= and the contract code in a field
called =tenor=. The catalogue examples are corrected, and the model now
declares =CCY/CONTRACT_MONTH/CONTRACT_CODE/TENOR=. The
=contract_code= is kept verbatim: ORE writes =XICE:FEI= and =XCME:SRA=, and
the token names the contract rather than decomposing into fields we can check.

** Grammar holes

The URI layer is not only unwired; it has its own defects. =model= is an
accepted query key on all seven asset classes and is discarded for six.
=parse_credit= does not type-gate =quote=. The =ir= entity is never
validated as a currency. And =split_market_series_key= keeps no point,
contradicting the four-field key the registry defines -- which is the
function the ingest loop relies on.

** Why it is unwired

Sprint 25's =adopt-oresmd-native-market-data-identity= story
(=E62808C1-D692-4877-835D-3A512A9F4160=) set the target state and was
abandoned at sprint close on 2026-09-23:

| Task | State |
|------+-------|
| Analysis: identity inventory, consumers, fields-vs-URI | DONE |
| Inverse projection: ORE key to oresmd identifier | DONE |
| Identity core: =market_series= and =feed_binding= store URIs | ABANDONED |

** Data-loss enumeration for a round trip

A round trip =ORE file -> stored rows -> ORE file= cannot be lossless
today. In rough order of severity:

1. **No export exists.** 100% of stored rows. The serializer has no
   production caller, there is no =rows -> market_datum= mapper and no
   ORE-text protocol struct. Everything below is what a new exporter must
   also solve.
2. **74.0% of the corpus cannot become an oresmd URI at all**, and the
   ingest path never calls the projection library. This is a coverage
   problem, not a storage one -- see the two halves above.
3. **A single-comma CSV line aborts its whole file.** The tokeniser
   requires two commas, so one malformed line costs 19,170 lines across
   the large CSVs.
4. **The verbatim key is not stored.** =market_datum= keeps =key=
   verbatim; =import_service= decomposes it into
   =series_type=/=metric=/=qualifier=/=point_id= and discards the string.
   This is less dangerous than it looks, because
   =decompose= then =reconstruct_key= is verbatim-lossless for 100% of the
   corpus -- but it is still a reconstruction where a copy would do, and
   it is the reason the point must be re-derived (item 6).
5. **The point is defaulted on ingest with no inverse.** A key with no
   point of its own is stored with =default_point_for(series_type)=. An
   exporter that does not reproduce the registry's =has_point_dimension=
   rule emits =FX/RATE/EUR/USD/SPOT= for =FX/RATE/EUR/USD=. The corpus
   already carries 1,365 FX, 401 RECOVERY_RATE, 154 BOND, 105 EQUITY, 43
   COMMODITY and 2 CPR lines in this shape.
6. **A one-character projection bug rejects 9,075 lines.** =from_ir_discount=
   refuses a =curve_id= equal to the currency that the *forward* projection
   can emit, because =oresmd_projections.cpp:644= compares with ="<="=
   where it means ="<"=.
7. **The IR par-rate settlement segment is destroyed.** The 6-segment path
   rebuilds it as the hardcoded ="2D"=, so the 1,338 corpus keys carrying
   =0D= are imported as =2D=. A further 2,221 seven-segment keys are
   rejected. This is the most serious of the fidelity findings because the
   **stored row already differs from the file** -- it is not something an
   exporter can repair.
8. **The reversal is a rewrite, not a round trip.** The FX convention
   correction mutates the qualifier when the pair is reversed relative to
   refdata. The value is untouched, but the key is not.
9. **No provenance at all.** =market_observation.source= is the caller's
   =--source= string. There is no source file, line number, import id,
   ingest time or raw line, so no row can be traced to its origin.
10. **=source= is not in the unique key.** Re-importing under a new tag
    supersedes the old tag rather than adding a second observation.
11. **Duplicates are resolved destructively at parse time.** The last
    occurrence of a =(date, key)= pair wins and the earlier one is gone;
    =duplicate_policy= only chooses whether the loss is a warning or an
    error, and the strict mode is unreachable from the CLI.
12. **Comments, blank lines, delimiter, spacing, date format and file
    order are dropped.** The parser discards them by design and the tables
    have no column for them, so a byte-identical round trip is impossible
    without new columns. Both =YYYYMMDD= and =YYYY-MM-DD= parse; the
    market serializer writes =YYYYMMDD= and the fixings serializer writes
    =YYYY-MM-DD=.
13. **Shape-table row descriptions disagree with the metrics oresmd
    emits.** Latent today, because nothing validates a metric.

Items 1, 5, 6 and 7 decide whether "no data loss" is achievable at all.
Item 7 is the only one where the damage is already done on the ingest
path; 5 and 6 are properties of the projection layer, and 1 is the
missing code.

** How far we are from reading every ORE sample file into the database

Three different things are usually run together when this question is asked,
and after PR 3 they sit at very different stages. Stated separately, because
a single percentage hides which of the three it measures.

*Naming and representing every type: done.* The gate reports 43 ORE series
types, 41 represented in oresmd and 2 recorded with a reason (=FIXING= and
=GENERIC-MD=), zero shape mismatches, 42 quote types over 38 distinct ORE
types, and 6 of 6 volatility-declaring asset classes wired. The two
allowlists in =oresmd_ore_coverage_tests.cpp= -- =types_oresmd_cannot_name()=
and =types_with_round_trip_gaps()= -- are empty and asserted equal to empty.
So no ORE type is unnamed, and no key projects without reading back.

*The database is not yet on that path.* =import_service.cpp= parses through
=ores::ore::market::parse_market_data= in =ores.ore.core=. The import path
names no oresmd symbol at all. The representation at 100% coverage is
therefore not load-bearing at the write boundary, and it is reached by tests
alone. This is B06's recorded exception, and wiring it is PR 4.

*And there is no end-to-end evidence either way.* =service_import_service_tests.cpp=,
the only test that drives an import into the database, feeds it two
hand-written lines. Nothing imports a real ORE sample file and asserts the
rows. So "every sample file reads into the database correctly" is unmeasured
rather than unfinished. That test is the first thing PR 4 builds.

*Update: it is built, and the answer is yes.* =service_corpus_import_tests.cpp=
imports every market payload under =external/ore/examples= -- 118 files -- and
reads the rows back, and all of them pass. It found three defects on the way
that this analysis had recorded as fixed in PR 2, which is the argument for
having built it before touching the import boundary:

- =parse_date= read two of the five date formats the corpus uses.
  =DD-MM-YYYY= (9,302 lines), =DD.MM.YY= (96) and =DD.MM.YYYY= (25) all threw
  =unrecognised date format=, so two 4,652-line files could not be imported at
  all. Ten characters is not enough to tell =DD-MM-YYYY= from =YYYY-MM-DD=;
  the separator positions decide.
- The tokeniser took its comma path only when a line held two commas or more,
  so a line like =2025-02-10,RECOVERY_RATE/RATE/BANK/SR/EUR 0.4= -- one comma,
  a space before the value -- fell through to the whitespace path and aborted
  its whole file.
- ORE's own examples repeat (date, key) pairs, and the import de-duplicates
  them by last-line-wins and reports each as a warning. The first draft of the
  test read that as data loss, which is a reminder that the expectation has to
  be the contract rather than the line count.

So the distance to "reads every sample file into the database" was three
defects, not a programme of work. What remains for PR 4 is the boundary

The line counts, for the record and for the two bases they are quoted on:
the corpus is 511,002 lines, 486,436 of them (95.2%) on modelled types. The
remainder are lines whose first token is not a series type at all

** What PR 3 actually landed, and the two items it moved out

Landed: the regenerate (97 files across four modules plus the web twin), the

** PR 4 -- Wire the import boundary through oresmd, and add ORE export

*Scope.* Make the oresmd layer load-bearing at the ORE-format edge, and
close the fidelity gaps that stop an export being lossless. Re-read on
=main= after PRs 1 to 3 landed: several items this section used to list are
now closed, and they are recorded as closed below rather than repeated as
work.

*First, the measurement.* No test in the tree imports a real ORE sample file
and asserts the rows it produced. =oresmd_ore_coverage_tests.cpp= and
=classification_series_classifier_tests.cpp= walk =external/ore/examples=
and assert projection and classification in memory;
=service_import_service_tests.cpp=, the only test that drives the import into
the database, feeds it two hand-written lines. So "every sample file reads
into the database correctly" is **unmeasured rather than unfinished**, and
the first thing this PR builds is that test: import every file under
=external/ore/examples/= into a scratch tenant and assert the resulting
series, observation and fixing rows. It is what turns the coverage figure
into a statement about stored rows, and it will separate a real parsing gap
from non-series noise in the 4.8% of corpus lines that are not on a modelled
type.

Then, in order:

- The import boundary gains the inverse projection:
  =parse_market_data()= -> =from_ore_key()= -> =market_data_identifier= ->
  the series and observation rows it already writes. This is the consumer
  the B06 exception for the oresmd layer names, and the boundary is not
  half-wired: =import_service.cpp= names no oresmd symbol at all today.
- The point-elision rule in the export mapper: drop the point for series
  types that carry no point dimension. =has_point_dimension= no longer
  exists anywhere in the component, so this is a rule to write, not a
  predicate to call.
- The IR settlement segment: replace the =std::string{"2D"}= fallback in the
  =ir_swap= projection with the per-currency spot lag from refdata. The
  segment survives the parse now -- PR 2 added =settle= to
  =ir_market_data_identifier= and stopped rewriting it -- so what remains is
  the case where the input carried no settlement segment and the projection
  currently invents one. **Re-checked and withdrawn; the source named is the
  wrong convention.** See "The settlement segment is not a currency's spot
  days" below.
- The verbatim key, so the export is lossless for every case the
  reconstructed key cannot reproduce. A =key text not null= column on the
  observations and fixings tables, populated from =market_datum::key= in
  =import_service.cpp=. Absent today.
- A new export path. A =rows -> market_datum / fixing= mapper plus one
  serializer caller (a new =core/src/service/ore_export_service.cpp=),
  exposed as a shell verb and a subject, asserting a file round trip
  through the database.
- =--duplicates-are-errors= on the shell import verb, so the strict policy
  is reachable (=marketdata_commands.cpp=).
- The grammar holes, each re-checked against the current projections before
  it is claimed: =model= accepted on seven quote classes and discarded for
  six, =parse_credit= not type-gating =quote=, the =ir= entity never
  validated as a currency, and =split_market_series_key= dropping the point
  against the registry's four-field key.

*What PR 4 delivered.*

The boundary, the grammar and the export are in. Two items were re-checked
and withdrawn, below. Two were built differently from the plan, and the
reasons belong here rather than in a commit nobody re-reads.

- **The boundary.** =import_service= runs every parsed datum through
  =from_ore_key()= -> =market_data_identifier= -> =to_quote_key()= and builds
  the series and observation rows from the registry's decomposition of that
  canonical spelling. A key oresmd cannot name keeps the parser's own
  decomposition and is imported anyway. The FX reversed-pair correction moved
  onto the identifier with it. Proved on the corpus: all 118 market payload
  files still import and read back under =[.corpus-full]=.
- **The verbatim key**, which the wiring made necessary: the import now
  deliberately rewrites a key it can name, so the series' columns hold the
  importer's text and an export reading them back cannot return the file's.
  Built as a *nullable* column on =market_observation= only. Nullable because
  the rows no file produced -- feed ticks, curve bootstraps -- have no producer
  key to preserve, and forcing every writer to invent one would give the column
  two meanings. Observations only because a fixing's index name is already
  stored verbatim as its series qualifier: there is no rewrite to undo, so a
  column there would carry nothing new.
- **The export.** =ore_export_service= reads the tenant's series, observations
  and fixings and hands them to =ores.ore.core='s serializer, so both ends
  agree on the format by construction rather than by two implementations kept
  in step. A key line is the producer's own text where the row has one, and
  otherwise rebuilt from the series with the point dropped for a type that has
  no point dimension -- every point-free row stores one anyway, =SPOT= for an
  FX rate, and emitting it would turn =FX/RATE/EUR/USD= into
  =FX/RATE/EUR/USD/SPOT=, a key no producer writes.
- **The subject and the verb.** =marketdata.v1.export= and =marketdata export
  [--market-data path] [--fixings path]=, mirroring the import pair so the two
  read as inverses. The handler asks for =marketdata::observations:read=
  rather than a code of its own, because the export only reads and W02 already
  records this component's permission seeding as failing -- seeded codes no
  handler checks, and a handler checking a code that is not seeded. A seventh
  hand-written protocol header joins the six M06 tracks.

*And the test that was missing.* The first two export cases imported a real
ORE file and asserted the exported keys were the file's own. **Both passed
with the point-elision rule disabled**, because an imported row always carries
a producer key and the rebuild path never ran -- so the rule the export exists
for was untested by the test written to test it. The third case writes
observations directly, as a feed or a bootstrap does; disabling the rule makes
it report =FX/RATE/EUR/USD/SPOT= against =FX/RATE/EUR/USD=. That is the lesson
the corpus walk taught earlier in this task, in a new place: a test that
exercises the path you had in mind is not the same as a test that exercises
the rule.

*What the re-checks found.*

The grammar bullet asked for four holes to be re-checked against the current
projections before any of them was claimed, and that was the right
instruction: one of the four is not a defect, one of the four is a defect
with no affected caller, and one of the seven items above rests on a
convention that does not exist.

- =model= was accepted on every asset class and kept on none. It names a
  volatility surface's own subtype, so it means something only when the URI
  also asked for =type=vol=; a quote URI naming a model parsed successfully
  and dropped the value. The parser now emits one check per class --
  =reject_model_unless_vol= for the six that carry a surface,
  =reject_if_present= for the four that do not -- because no class gives
  =model= a second meaning. Ten rejection cases, generated from the models.
- =credit='s =quote= key was the one class not gated on =type=, so
  =type=curve&quote=cds= parsed and the quote type was kept. Its model no
  longer sets =quote_type_checked: false=.
- An =ir= URI's entity segment is its currency and was never checked. It is
  now a currency for every IR type except the bond option, whose third key
  segment is the underlying bond curve's own name -- =EUR_GENERIC= in the
  corpus, and the one IR identifier the round-trip walk produces that is not
  three letters. The claim as written needed that exemption to be true; it
  was not.
- =split_market_series_key= does fold a point-bearing key's trailing point
  into the qualifier, so =DISCOUNT/RATE/EUR/CURVE/2Y= yields the qualifier
  =EUR/CURVE/2Y= where the import stores =EUR/CURVE= and the point =2Y=. But
  all four of its callers pass an FX key, and FX has no point dimension, so
  no stored series is looked up under a qualifier that never existed. The
  fix needs the shape table, and three of the four callers -- two in
  =ores.synthetic=, one free function -- have only a NATS client and no
  context to read it from. The docstring now states the precondition and
  points at =series_key_registry::decompose()= for the general case. Left as
  a recorded limitation rather than a signature change three callers cannot
  satisfy.

** The settlement segment is not a currency's spot days

The bullet above proposed =ores.refdata='s per-currency spot lag as the
source for the =IR_SWAP= settlement segment. It is the wrong convention, and
the corpus says so twice over.

=ores_refdata_currencies_tbl= holds exactly two values: USD is 1 and every
other currency is 2. =ores_dq_currencies_artefact_tbl= shows the same split,
1 currency at 1 and 12,510 at 2. That is FX spot settlement, which really is
T+1 for USD.

The segment the corpus writes for an =IR_SWAP= key is not that. Across
=external/ore/examples= the settle segment is =2D= for 2,158 USD keys and
=0D= for 821, and =1D= never appears for USD at all. It also appears as an
explicit date -- =IR_SWAP/RATE/GBP/20220922/...= -- which no per-currency
integer can produce. So the segment is an ORE key component that is
sometimes a lag and sometimes a date, and the field refdata exposes is the
FX spot convention.

Substituting it would also break the round trip the coverage gate measures.
=from_ir_swap= records =settle= only when the segment is not =2D=, so a USD
=2D= key carries no settle in the identifier and the forward's fallback is
what reproduces it. A fallback of =1D= for USD turns
=IR_SWAP/RATE/USD/2D/3M/PAR_RATE= into =IR_SWAP/RATE/USD/1D/3M/PAR_RATE=,
and =oresmd_ore_coverage_tests.cpp= asserts all 485,606 corpus keys project
back to the spelling they arrived in. The item is withdrawn, not deferred:
there is nothing here to wire.

*Closed by PRs 2 and 3, and struck from this list.*
- The modelled-but-unrepresentable quote types -- BASIS_SWAP,
  CC_BASIS_SWAP, ZERO, FRA, IMM_FRA, MM, MM_FUTURE, OI_FUTURE, CORRELATION,
  CPR and the 7-segment IR and SWAPTION keys. All of them project and
  round-trip; =types_oresmd_cannot_name()= and
  =types_with_round_trip_gaps()= are both empty in
  =oresmd_ore_coverage_tests.cpp= and asserted to be.
- The one-character projection bug. The DISCOUNT projection emits exactly
  =DISCOUNT/METRIC/CCY/CURVE/TENOR= and its inverse requires exactly five
  parts, so the two agree; and the forward requires both =curve_id= and
  =tenor=, so the four-part form the inverse used to reject cannot be
  emitted. The bug is gone rather than merely unreachable from the corpus.
- CPR's shape mismatch, which was a recorded exemption. PR 2 moved CPR to
  the security class, where it asserts the ISIN-keyed shape it writes.

*Why here.* It delivers the owner's first two goals without the schema
cutover, and it is the smallest change that makes the oresmd layer
load-bearing. It also surfaces where the URI and the stored columns
disagree, which is PR 5's input.

*The honest limit, restated.* The figure this section used to carry --
"lossless only for the 26.0% of the corpus oresmd can name" -- is spent:
every ORE series type is now represented or recorded, and the corpus names
486,436 of its 511,002 lines. What limits a lossless export is no longer
naming coverage but the two things above that are still unwired: the
boundary itself, and the verbatim key. Until both land the export can be
lossless in principle and still not lossless in fact, and until the
end-to-end import test lands we cannot say which.

*Note.* =split_market_series_key()= currently serves as the identity split
in the ingest loop. Under this PR it stays -- the cutover that retires it
is PR 5.

** PR 5 -- The identity cutover

*Scope.* Revive =feature/identity-core-series-and-bindings=: the
=oresmd_uri= column on =market_series= and =feed_binding=, the repos and
registrars and ingest loops reading URIs, the =point_id= repurposing, then
the deletion of =series_type=/=metric=/=qualifier=/=ore_key= and the
import classification table. Hard cutover, no backfill, per the decided
target state.

*Why last of the substantive PRs.* It is the only irreversible one -- it
drops columns -- and everything before it makes it verifiable. It should
not be attempted before the corpus exists and the projections are
lossless.

*Not startable as scoped, and the corpus says why.* The cutover's premise is
that every identity-bearing row can be given an =oresmd_uri=: the column is
=not null=, the natural key moves to it, and the decided policy rejects a type
oresmd cannot name. For a series that arrives in a =market.txt= that premise
holds -- =from_ore_key()= names all 485,606 corpus keys and the coverage gate
asserts it. For **fixings** it does not, and the fixing half is the half the
target state never measured.

A fixing's key space is the index name, not the =TYPE/METRIC/QUALIFIER/POINT=
grammar, so it needs an inverse of its own. The abandoned branch wrote exactly
one, =from_index_name()=, whose contract is =CCY-FAMILY[-TENOR]= against the
=index_family= enum. Measured against the corpus with that contract:

| files                        | distinct index names | nameable | not nameable |
|------------------------------+----------------------+----------+--------------|
| =fixings*.txt=               | 96                   | 24       | 72           |
| the same plus =fixings*.csv= | 158                  | 34       | 124          |

The 72 in the first row are not a residue. They are 48 =POWER-ICE:PDQ-...=
contracts, 6 inflation indices (=UKRPI=, =USCPI=, =EUHICP=, =EUHICPXT=,
=FRHICP=, =ZACPI=), 3 equity tickers (=EQ-SP5=, =EQ-RIC:...=), 2 FX fixings
(=FX-ECB-EUR-USD=), one credit name (=GENERIC-JuniorNote=), and twelve
interest-rate names whose family the enum does not declare -- =EUR-EONIA=, the
=CIBOR=/=NIBOR=/=STIBOR=/=SIOR= families, and =USD-FedFunds=, =USD-CMS-5Y=,
=USD-SIFMA=.

The abandoned branch handled this by skipping: on a failed =from_index_name= it
pushed =="Skipped fixing '...'"= into =warnings= and wrote no row. On the corpus
that drops three quarters of the distinct fixing series, and it drops them
*quietly* -- which is why it went unnoticed. The branch predates
=service_corpus_import_tests.cpp= by five weeks, and the test this task built in
PR 4's first half is what makes the loss visible, because it asserts the warning
count is exactly the de-duplication count. The branch's own verification line,
"ctest 69/71 passed", could not have caught it.

So PR 5 needs a predecessor the six-PR plan does not have: **map the fixing key
space to oresmd, asset class by asset class.** That is a grammar question before
it is a code question, and adding enum members does not answer it:

- an inflation fixing names an index the inflation asset class already models
  as =index_code=, so =UKRPI= is close to nameable already;
- an equity fixing (=EQ-SP5=) names a ticker and no currency, and the equity
  identifier requires one, so the name does not carry enough to build the URI;
- an FX fixing (=FX-ECB-EUR-USD=) carries its *source* as well as the pair, and
  the FX identifier has no field for a source;
- a power fixing (=POWER-ICE:PDQ-2021-01-04-14400-18000=) is an hourly
  contract rather than a point on a curve, and needs a shape the grammar lacks;
- =GENERIC-JuniorNote= is a curve name, not an index;
- and the twelve interest-rate names are producer spellings the =index_family=
  enum does not hold -- the same spelling-versus-family gap the ESTER alias
  already papers over for one of them.

Until that lands the cutover cannot be attempted: adding =oresmd_uri= and
dropping the triple would leave the fixing boundary unable to write the fixings
ORE's own examples carry.

*What landed from it meanwhile.* =from_index_name()= and =is_scalar()= are in,
generated from the projections templates, with corpus-driven cases that pin the
round trip and the limit -- =EUR-EONIA=, =UKRPI= and a power contract are
asserted unnameable rather than left undocumented. =is_scalar()= is the
primitive the point repurposing needs, and it is where the abandoned branch
disagreed with its own plan: it kept the ="SPOT"= sentinel, while both the story
and the deletion task say the sentinel dies and a scalar series carries a null
point. That repurposing is still to do.

*Update, 2026-09-28 (task AB2922AA, commit 495dc8d2e3).* The interest-rate third
of the gap is closed. The =index_family= enum gained the fifteen families the
corpus uses and the two spelling aliases, and the tenor rule moved out of a
hand-written header into the model: =from_index_name()= now names **all 57**
distinct interest-rate index names and projects every one back to the name it
arrived as, from 34 named and 28 round-tripped. The table above stands as the
measurement at the revision that made PR 5 unstartable; the total named is now 57
of 158.

A corpus-driven test now walks the fixing payloads class by class and fails when a
class that named names stops naming them, so the remaining classes are measured
rather than described: inflation 7 names, equity 8, FX 7, commodity 25, power 48,
security 2, =GENERIC= 4. It also asks whether the reader reads each payload at
all, which found =CurveBuilding/Input/fixings_bondyieldshifted.csv=: the
=fixingDataFile= of an example ORE runs, semicolon-separated, and unreadable here.
That is fixed too (commit 6fa10a87f4), so the census covers all 66 payloads rather
than 65.

*Update, 2026-09-28 (task AB2922AA, commit fb3c5a4f31).* The FX class followed,
and it needed a field rather than data. An FX fixing's identity now carries
=source= (lower-cased) and =source_spelling= (the token as ORE wrote it), both
meaningful only when =type=fixing=, and keeps the pair as it was published rather
than a canonical order: the corpus writes =FX-TR20H-EUR-GBP= and
=FX-TR20H-GBP-EUR= as one rate both ways on 122 dates, and folding them together
would put two values under one date. All seven FX names name and round-trip. The
corpus total is 64 of 158, and six classes remain.

*Update, 2026-09-28 (task AB2922AA, commit 995efd81e2).* The power and commodity
halves are closed. ORE states the intraday power index grammar in its own parser,
=POWER-COMMNAME[-YYYY-MM-DD[-START-END[-DST]]]=, and its commodity parser reads a
contract month the same way, so a fixing's delivery period is a =delivery= field of
its own rather than a =point=: =point= is what a quote or a vol names, and a fixing
whose coordinate is a field stays scalar, as the interest-rate tenor already does.
Power is a class of its own because ORE accepts a bare =POWER-ICE:PDQ= beside a bare

name is a key space of its own rather than a quote key, and the boundary that
consumes it is the cutover this section describes.

Two of the four =GENERIC= tokens are market-data keys that sit inside a fixings
file rather than index names, and the reader reads them as fixings because that is
the file they are in. Whether it should refuse a key that is not an index name is
part of what remains.

*Update, 2026-09-28 (task AB2922AA, commit 7b3918b6fe).* The generic class is
closed, and the two market-data keys are settled. ORE resolves =GENERIC-= in a
parser of its own, and the name is all the identity there is: the identifier
carries it upper-cased and the case ORE wrote it in beside it in =name_spelling=,
which is the FX =source_spelling= rule. Both names the corpus carries name and
=GENERIC-JuniorNote= and =GENERIC-PortfolioBasket#1=, the second through the =%23=
its =#= becomes in a URI -- so the corpus total is **139 of 158**. The two
=GENERIC-MD/EQUITY_OPTION/PRICE/...= rows are SPX call prices in a fixing column
rather than index names; the index-name projection refuses any name that carries a
slash, which keeps them out of the fixing space, so the target is **156 of 158**
rather than 158. Three classes remain: inflation (7), equity (8) and security (2).
The =FIXING= exemption in the oresmd coverage gate still stands, unchanged.

** PR 6 -- Join =COMPONENTS_UNDER_TEST=

*Scope.* Add =marketdata= to =component_registry.py= and run every gate:
component drift, model drift, protocol twin coverage, handler permissions,
populate references, recipe inventory, CMake source lists, plus
=compass build=, the suites and =compass db recreate=. This is item V06
and the standard's own closing condition.

*Why separate.* Registration is a claim about the whole component. It
should be its own commit so the claim can be reverted without reverting
the work.

* Decisions the owner must make

1. **Is the oresmd cutover in scope for this story?** The clean standard
   does not require it. PRs 1 to 3 and 6 stand alone as the clean-up; PRs
   4 and 5 are the native-representation goal. Recommendation: take 1 to
   3 plus 6 as this story, and 4 to 5 as a resumed story, because 5 drops
   columns and deserves its own acceptance.
2. **=FIXING= coverage.** Give fixings an oresmd representation, or
   record them as an accepted exception at the import boundary. They are
   imported and stored today, and =from_ore_key= rejects them, so the
   export path cannot even attempt them.
3. **The uncovered and mis-modelled series types.** The corpus, not the
   documentation, is the authority. 13 types have no oresmd identifier
   and account for 232,545 corpus lines (48.8%); 10 more have an
   identifier that cannot hold the keys ORE writes for them. Growing
   oresmd to cover the option and capfloor families is a modelling
   programme in its own right -- decide whether it is in scope, or
   record rejection at import and accept that 74.0% of the corpus cannot
   round-trip.
4. **The "no data loss" bar.** A byte-identical file round trip, or
   value-and-key equivalence? Comments, blank lines, file order, the
   losing duplicate in a =(date, key)= repeat, and the =YYYYMMDD= versus
   =YYYY-MM-DD= asymmetry cannot survive storage without new columns.
5. **Revive the abandoned branch or re-derive it?** It holds ~41
   applicable files of PR 5's work. Reviewing it is cheaper than
   rewriting it if its assumptions still hold.
6. **The =client= module.** Its only live class is =market_data_client=.
   Deleting the CRM stack leaves a one-class module -- keep it, or fold
   the class into =core= and retire the module.
