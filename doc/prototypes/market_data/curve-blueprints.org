* Summary

The curve blueprint screens, as one plain page: the list of the party's
blueprints, the record page a row opens with five tabs, and the four dialogs the
record opens. A curve blueprint is the recipe one curve is built from.

The page argues four things. A blueprint is a record a person maintains, so it
is a list and a record page rather than a workbench with three modes. Its pillars
are parts of it, ordered by maturity and not by the order a person typed. Its
check is a tab, because what a recipe must satisfy is something a person reads
beside the recipe and not a third of the act of authoring it. And the published
sets that stand on it belong on the same page, so a person sees what a change
reaches before making it.

It is throwaway. It reads no service, it holds no session, and every value on it
is mock data. It exists because the journeys settle what a blueprint *is* and
nothing yet settles what a person *sees* while reading one, testing one or
ending one.

* Detail

** The question

The Qt app has the bootstrapping screens and the web has none. The workflows
exist: the =CurveBuilderWorkbench= defines a recipe, builds it and publishes it
from three tabs of one window, and the =IrCurveBootstrapConfigController= opens
it. What the web has no answer for is the shape. The
[[id:2BF7F03F-6BF4-45FF-8E26-4D3B4E2D943D][Record screen standard]] gives a
record a list, a record page, tabs, dialogs and states; the Qt window is none of
those, and copying it would put a desktop apparatus in front of a web session.

So the question this prototype answers is: does the bootstrapping job read as a
record screen, with the same parts as every other record kind, and which of the
Qt window's parts does it need?

The answer it draws is five tabs: the recipe, its pillars, its check, the sets it
fed, and its history. The Qt window's three tabs map onto the first three; the
last two are the record standard's.

=?tab=check=, =?tab=sets=, =?tab=history=.

** The list

The list follows the record standard: the breadcrumb Home › Market data ›
Curves, the plural as the title, one sentence, a search, two filters, the table
and the pager.

| Column | What it holds |
|--------+---------------|
| Curve | The curve code, monospace, and the first column. Under it, one line of state: *No pillars yet*, *Never published*, or *No set in use since <date>* |
| Currency | The currency the curve is quoted in |
| Role | =FUNDING= or =PROJECTION=, as a badge |
| Index tenor | The tenor of the index the curve stands for |
| Interpolation | The method that joins the pillars |
| Day count | The day count applied |
| Pillars | How many parts the blueprint holds, right-aligned |
| Version | The record's version |
| Last changed | Relative, with the exact value on hover in the product |

Two filters stand beside the search: a currency select and a role select. The
search covers the curve code and the currency. A row opens the record page.

*RS-LIST-21* gives the list its states, and the prototype draws each one:

- No records. "There are no curve blueprints yet." It says what a blueprint is,
  and offers Add.
- Nothing matches. "No curve blueprints match the search." and a way to clear it.
- First load. "Loading…" in the body of the table.
- Error. The words of the server and Retry, and never a modal box.
- After a delete. One success notice, and the list without the row.

Two facts of the model shape the table. A blueprint has no name, so the code
stands in the display field's place; the journeys record the gap. And the key is
a UUID that RS-LIST-13 keeps out of a table, so the code is the only readable
handle the list has.

** The record page

The header carries the curve code as its title, the lead line =<currency> ·
index tenor <tenor> · <role> · version <n>= and the access mark, and three
actions: *Copy blueprint*, then *Edit*, then *Delete*. Copy is the kind's own
action, in the place RS-PAGE-03 gives one, and it is the whole of the Qt
window's /New from Existing.../ button.

The tabs are Details, Pillars, Check, Published sets and History, in the
standard's order: the record's fields, then a part, then the kind's own view,
then a related plural, then the timeline.

- *Details.* The fields in the field table's order: currency, index tenor, role,
  discount curve, interpolation method, day count, tenor convention, split
  tenor, output series and *Used by*. A value that names another record is a
  link: the discount curve opens that blueprint, the output series names the
  market series the curve is published into, and *Used by* lists the projection
  blueprints that discount off this one. The tab ends with the last-changed line.
  A blueprint with no pillars says so in a notice that opens the Pillars tab.
  A blueprint whose sets have all ended says so in a notice that names the
  withdrawal and the account that took it.
- *Pillars.* The parts panel of RS-RELATED-04 to RS-RELATED-06: one row per
  pillar in maturity order, with the sequence, the start and end tenor, the curve
  role, the version, who changed it and when, and History, Edit and Delete on the
  row. Add stands beside the panel's title. The panel's one paragraph says what a
  pillar is, because the Qt window's paragraph is the only teaching either screen
  has, and that the order follows the end tenor rather than the order typed.
- *Check.* The recipe's own checks, what the market supplies, the points, two
  charts and the findings. It has a section of its own below.
- *Published sets.* The sets published from this blueprint, newest first: the
  published as-of, the party, the publisher, the published instant, the points
  and the state. A row opens the published record. A blueprint nobody published
  from says "None yet." and names the publish journey. The sets are read and
  never changed here: a withdrawal is its own act with a reason.
- *History.* The shared timeline, as the record standard states it: the
  blueprint's versions and every pillar's versions, newest first, each entry
  named by its part, with the field-level difference behind *Details*, the
  current version marked, and Revert on the older ones.

** The Check tab

The tab carries the as-of, then the checks, then what the market supplied, then
the points and the two lines, then the findings.

| Part | What it draws |
|------+---------------|
| As-of | The instant the test reads, now by default, in the address so a link reopens it |
| The recipe's checks | Six checks with Pass or Fail: at least one pillar; each pillar matures after the one before it; every =DEPOSIT= pillar starts at =SPOT=; the split tenor is the last pillar's end tenor, or a tenor inside the ladder for the two-segment method; a =PROJECTION= names a =FUNDING= blueprint of its currency and not itself; the blueprint it names is not a projection |
| A failed check | The finding in words, and the act that fixes it: *Open the pillars*, *Edit the blueprint*, *Add a pillar* |
| What the market supplies | One row for each pillar, *Observed* or *No rate*, where no rate names the instant the market had nothing |
| The points | The pillar, its date, the discount factor and the instantaneous forward, in maturity order |
| The two lines | The discount factors and the forwards, one chart each, plain SVG, the pillar tenors along the bottom |
| Findings | What the screen derived from the points, and one line that says the screen derived it |

A recipe that fails its own check stops before the market is read: the tab says
so, names every failed check, and draws no points. That is the *validation
failure* state. A recipe that passes but whose market has no rate for one pillar
still builds, and the points table draws a muted dash for the gap, the chart
breaks its line, and one finding names the gap.

The tab never shows an unrun check as passed. Before the test it says *Not run*
on every check and *Not checked yet* above them.

** The dialogs

The record dialog is the standard's: one dialog, no tabs, two columns, the fields
in the field table's order, the label above the control, one line of small faint
text under a value that needs it, Cancel and the primary button in the footer.

| Field | Control | Asked when |
|-------+---------+-----------|
| Currency | A record picker over the currencies | Always |
| Index tenor | A select over the tenors =1D=, =3M= and =6M= | Always |
| Role | A select over =FUNDING= and =PROJECTION= | Always |
| Discount curve | A record picker over the currency's =FUNDING= blueprints | The role is =PROJECTION= |
| Interpolation method | A select over the three methods | Always |
| Day count | A select over the day counts | Always |
| Tenor convention | A select over the conventions | Always |
| Split tenor | A select over the tenors | The method is =FLAT_FORWARD_THEN_LOG_LINEAR= |

Two fields the Qt window draws are absent, on purpose. *Source series* is not
asked at all: the config names no input series, and each pillar reads the series
its own tenor, role and index resolve to. *Output series* is not asked either:
the server mints it from the currency, the index tenor and the role, so a value
that follows from other fields is not a question (RS-WRITE-07). The two pickers
the Qt window opens for them are therefore not needed; the picker the web screen
does need is the one over a record kind, and it is drawn inside the dialog for
the currency and for the discount curve.

The pillar dialog asks for three fields: the start tenor, the end tenor and the
curve role, each a select over the codes the system keeps, and says that the
sequence follows the end tenor. The copy dialog is the record dialog with the
conventions and the pillar count carried over and the currency, the index tenor
and the role blank, because those are what make it another curve. The delete
dialog says what is lost, and refuses in the server's own words when a projection
names the blueprint as its discount curve.

** Covering the journeys

The prototype does not mirror the journeys step by step. It carries their
intent, and this table says how each one lands on a screen, and what it does not
draw.

*** Maintain the curve blueprints

| Step | Intent | How the prototype covers it | What it does not draw |
|------+--------+-----------------------------+-----------------------|
| 1 | List the party's blueprints | The list, with the code first and the state under it | The column menu that hides and orders columns, and the audit columns (RS-LIST-13) |
| 2 | Search and filter | The search over code and currency, the currency and role filters, and the no-match state | A server search: the prototype filters on the page, and RS-LIST-04 wants the filter on the contract |
| 3 | Open a blueprint and read its fields | The Details tab, the fields in the field table's order, the output series and *Used by* as links | The output series' own read, which is a different component's screen |
| 4 | Read the pillars | The Pillars tab in maturity order, with the empty state and its Add | Nothing |
| 5 | Add a blueprint | The Add dialog: six fields, plus the discount curve for a projection and the split tenor for the two-segment method; the output series is not asked | The write, and the new page it opens: nothing on the page writes |
| 6 | Define the pillars | The Add a pillar dialog's three fields, over the parts panel | Editing a pillar: the dialog is drawn in its Add state only, though it is the same shape |
| 7 | Read the order, and the refusal a DEPOSIT gets | The panel says the order follows the end tenor; the Check tab names a =DEPOSIT= that does not start at =SPOT= and the act that fixes it | The message under the start tenor field: the standard puts a failed field's message under the field (RS-WRITE-05), and the dialog is not drawn with one |
| 8 | Change a blueprint | The Edit dialog with the current values, the version it claims, and the discount curve asked when the role becomes =PROJECTION= | The notice for a role change the server refuses, because a projection still discounts off the blueprint; the delete dialog's refusal is the pattern it would use |
| 9 | Copy a blueprint | The Copy dialog: the conventions and the pillar count carried over, the currency, the index tenor and the role asked | The reason each copied pillar carries: the journey leaves it open |
| 10 | Check the recipe | The Check tab, and its own journey below | The check's result held against a version: nothing holds one |
| 11 | Read the published sets | The Published sets tab, with the sets and the empty state | The published record's page, which the published-curves prototype draws |
| 12 | Read what it feeds | The output series and *Used by* on Details | The link to the list filtered by this blueprint when more than three name it: no filter exists to carry it |
| 13 | Read the history | The History tab: the blueprint's versions and its pillars' in one rail, with the difference of each | The event chart over the entries; Revert is drawn as its words and no dialog |
| 14 | Delete a blueprint | The delete dialog in both states: what is lost, and the refusal while a projection names it; then the list's notice | The delete that succeeds: nothing on the page writes |
| 15 | Leave | The breadcrumb and the List control of the bar | Nothing |

The journey's *retire* has no home on the page, because the record has no status
and no end date. The nearest expression is drawn: a blueprint whose published
sets have all ended says so in the list's state line and in a notice on Details,
and names the withdrawal that ended it. The state reads "no set in use" and never
"retired", so the page claims no field the model lacks. The journey records the
gap, and this note repeats it: *retire* is a status column, or it is nothing.

*** Check that a curve blueprint builds

| Step | Intent | How the prototype covers it | What it does not draw |
|------+--------+-----------------------------+-----------------------|
| 1 | Arrive at the blueprint | The record page, opened from the list | The arrival from a failed bootstrap: nothing today links a failure to its blueprint |
| 2 | Open the Check tab | The tab with the as-of and *Run the test*, and *Not checked yet* before a run | The instant a person names: *Earlier instant* sets one mock value rather than opening a date field, which the standard has no field kind for |
| 3 | Read the recipe's own checks | Six checks, each Pass or Fail, each with the act that fixes it, and *Not run* before the test | A check the server answers: the screen answers all six from what it read, and says so |
| 4 | Read what the market must supply | The pillar table with *Observed* or *No rate* | The series each pillar resolved to: no read answers it for a whole recipe |
| 5 | Read the built curve | The points table and the two lines | Nothing |
| 6 | Read the finding | The findings panel, and the line that says the screen derived it | The server's own diagnostics, which no operation returns |
| 7 | Fix the recipe | The buttons a failed check carries: *Open the pillars*, *Edit the blueprint*, *Add a pillar* | The field-level correction, for the reason step 7 of the other journey gives |
| 8 | Run it again | *Run the test* again, and the result replacing the last one | The clearing of a result after a save: nothing writes |
| 9 | Leave | The tabs and the breadcrumb | The publish, which this journey never takes and the page never offers |

*** What the journeys ask for that the page does not draw

Four things are deliberately absent, and each is either a part the standard does
not have or a server answer that does not exist.

- *A retire action.* The record has no status, so the page draws the state and
  offers no action. The journey leaves it open.
- *A field-level message under a wrong field.* RS-WRITE-05 asks for one, and the
  only wrong value the page knows how to draw is a pillar that the Check tab
  judges. Adding a fourth state to the pillar dialog would invent a pattern for
  one case; the Check tab is the pattern the page has.
- *A result held against a version.* Nothing holds the test's result, so the tab
  shows the reading of the person who ran it. The journey records it.
- *A save that writes the recipe and its pillars together.* The page draws one
  write at a time, because no operation composes them; the journey records it.

** What is fake

Everything on the page is mock data: eight blueprints, their pillars, their
published sets, their history, the currencies, the tenors and the accounts are
hard-coded literals. There is no server, no session and no build step. The
mock clock is 7 October 2026, 17:00 UTC, and every relative time is measured from
it.

The curves are computed from a formula, not from a market. Each blueprint carries
a base rate, the pillars carry tenors, and the discount factor at each pillar is
the compound of the base rate over the pillar's years; the forward rate is the
ratio of two adjacent discount factors. That gives a smooth curve, which is what
the tab needs to look sane, and it is not a curve anyone could price from.

Three of the eight blueprints exist for one state each: =GBP-SONIA-1D= has no
pillars, =SEK-STIBOR-6M= fails two checks (a deposit that starts at 3M, and two
pillars that both end at 10Y), and =JPY-TONA-6M= has a bumped discount factor so
that its forwards jump and a finding has something to say. =USD-SOFR-1D= carries
three published sets and is named by another blueprint's discount curve, so the
Published sets tab and the *Used by* value both have something to hold.

The reviewer should know two assumptions. The role of a blueprint and the
currency it is quoted in are drawn as the fields the model holds, and the curve
code is a stand-in for the output series' URI, which the model does not store and
the screen cannot yet spell. And the party is always the session's, because the
list read scopes by party and the page has no party filter of its own.

** Where the code lives

The prototype is =doc/prototypes/market_data/curve-blueprints/=. The directory
holds one file, =index.html=, with its CSS and its JavaScript inline: no
framework, no =prototype.css=, no =prototype.js=, and no build step. The site
build copies the directory as it stands and adds nothing to it.

The product screens would live here:

| Part | Where |
|------+-------|
| The list and the record page | A new =CurveBlueprintsPage= and =CurveBlueprintPage= under =projects/ores.web/packages/web/src/marketdata/=, with a route at =/market-data/curve-blueprints= and a path in =shell/paths.ts= |
| The parts panel | The shared =PartsPanel= in =web/src/refdata/records.tsx= |
| The dialogs | =RecordDialog=, =RemoveRecordDialog= and =RecordPicker=, from the shared parts; the pillar dialog is the same =RecordDialog= over the pillar kind |
| The History tab | The shared =Timeline= in =web/src/timeline/= |
| The two charts | =web/src/marketdata/LineChart.tsx=, as the check tab draws them |
| The list, the record and the writes | The BFF's record routes, which need an entry in =REFDATA_RECORDS= in =wire-protocol/src/records.ts= and a write schema in =bff/src/records.ts=; the subjects exist in the generated protocol |
| The check | A read over =marketdata.v1.ops.compute_curve=, which has a handler and no web route, and the checks the screen derives |

Three reads are missing, and the note names them where the screen draws them: a
filter by =bootstrap_config_id= on the pillars and on the published curves, and
one operation that answers a blueprint's dependants.

** The verdict

Open. It is offered for sign-off as the shape of the two journeys' screens: the
list with its state line, the record page with five tabs and no button that
repeats one, the pillars as parts in maturity order, the Check tab with its six
checks and its two lines, the published sets on the record, and four dialogs with
eight fields between them.

What sign-off settles is that the Qt window is not the web's layout: a record
page is where a person acts on a blueprint, and the test is a tab a person
arrives at rather than the third panel of an editor.

** What it does not settle

The place in the menu. The
[[id:339F0405-43AE-4049-B6B3-EB12E5A38CA4][Menu structure]] gives Market data
five entries and none of them is Curves. This page uses the journeys' word and
stands at =/market-data/curve-blueprints=; whether the area gains a Curves entry,
and whether the published curves of the
[[id:654E4DFD-FD96-4A65-A350-AC8DD3F87BE0][published-curves prototype]] sit
beside it, is the menu's decision and not this prototype's.

The key in the address. The record's key is a UUID and the readable handle is the
output series' URI. The prototype uses the curve code the published-curves screen
already uses; the address needs one of the two, and the journeys leave it open.

The name. A blueprint is titled by the series it publishes into. Whether the
config gains a name or a description column, so that two currencies' curves are
not told apart by a URI, is open.

The check's home. The Check tab is the kind's own tab, and the standard's tab
table names field groups and related plurals. The published-curves prototype drew
a comparison tab and left the same question open; the two should be settled
together.

Retire. A status column, or nothing. The prototype draws the state a person can
infer and names it in the standard's words rather than inventing a badge.

The pillars' edit. The pillar dialog is drawn in its Add state. Whether the parts
panel edits a pillar in a dialog, as RS-RELATED-06 says, or in the table, is
settled by the standard and not drawn here.

The channel of a finding. The screen derives the forward rate, the jump and the
missing rate, because the server returns none of them. How much of a diagnostic a
screen may derive is open.
