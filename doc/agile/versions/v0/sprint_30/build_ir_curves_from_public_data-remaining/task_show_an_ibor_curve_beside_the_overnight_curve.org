* Goal

The system publishes an IBOR curve for a currency beside its overnight curve, states on the screen that the IBOR curve is a projection curve, and keeps it out of discounting and surface calibration, so a person can compare the two and learn from the comparison.

* Acceptance

- A learning bootstrap config publishes an IBOR curve for one currency beside its overnight curve, with the overnight curve as its discount curve.
- The IBOR curve publishes with role projection, and no consumer takes it for a discount curve or a surface input; a test states the rule.
- The curves screen and the coverage screen show both curves for the currency, with the index family and the role.
- One page states the difference the comparison teaches: an overnight average is compounded and discounts, a term IBOR is quoted and projects.

* Notes

- The IBOR feeds are already seeded and publish as projection curves: ICE's five LIBOR
  curves (USD 1M/6M, GBP 3M, CHF 3M, JPY 3M) and Tradeweb's three (GBP 1M, CHF 6M,
  JPY 1M), plus EURIBOR. Evidence: =evidence/ibor_feeds_2026-11-13.log= in
  [[id:4B5701D6-16C6-445B-BDD4-C60050FED95C][Replace the synthetic feeds of ceased or misnamed indices]].
- The curve inspector the comparison should reuse is captured in
  [[id:FB59FE7A-B0D1-4DF7-AFB1-07F426D0C9D9][Inspect a curve's pillars and conventions]].
- The coverage screen takes one feed config per currency today, so it shows AONIA for AUD
  and never the IBOR curve; the grouping is part of this task.
- The bootstrap config seeds live in =refdata_ir_curve_bootstrap_configs_populate.sql= and
  name a discount curve, so AUD-LIBOR-3M points at AUD-AONIA rather than at itself.
- The comparison prototype is =doc/prototypes/market_data/ibor-beside-overnight/index.html=: the
  inspector drawn for two curves at once, with the conventions strip for each and a panel
  that states the four differences (underlying, fixing, use, why the shape differs). It is
  static data for the design; the task builds the live version against it.
- Signed off on 2026-11-13. The inspector keeps all three read-outs of a curve — zero rates,
  forward rates and discount factors — because each carries part of the comparison: zero for
  the level, forward for where the term premium sits, and the discount factor for what a
  curve that discounts actually does. Forward is the view to open first when a term curve is
  present. The build must carry the switch; it is not a prototype-only convenience.
