* Goal

The observation table stops at about 8,300 rows per second for one connection because its insert trigger runs for every row. An append-only table with one index reaches about 187,000 at 162 bytes for each row. Move ticks to it, and give the readers of the observation table a view over both.

* Acceptance

- A hypertable for ticks with no trigger, no valid_to and one index on the series and the time, with a compression policy and a retention policy for each class.
- The ingest writes ticks to it, and a batch needs no dedupe against a trigger.
- A view joins it with the observation table, and the readers keep their results: snapshots, evolution, slices, ORE export, curve pillars, lineage and the feed binding activity.
- The ingest drains a burst at more than 100,000 rows per second on one connection, measured with the tick injector.

* Plan

(Implementation strategy. Written when work starts; key decisions
are distilled into the parent story's =* Decisions= at close, but the
plan itself stays — it is the historical record of what we did.)

Decision of the owner, 2026-10-21: this task keeps one row for each series. A series is one
observation with its provenance, owned by a tenant and a party, so ticks are not shared and one
row for each source does not apply.

The design is in the section on tenant and party isolation of the tick ingest pipeline note.
In short: one append-only hypertable for each retention class, with =tenant_id=, =party_id=,
=series_id=, =ts=, =coord= and =value=. The reads run under the tenant policy and the party
policy, in the scalar subquery form. A writer role with =BYPASSRLS= and =INSERT= only writes
with =COPY=. The tests of write rate, read cost and isolation are done and recorded in the
note. The order of work:

1. Provision the writer role in the role script, with =INSERT= on the tick table and no =SELECT=.
2. Write the table, its index, its two policies and its retention class, in the model of the
   marketdata component, with the notification trigger off.
3. Move the ingest to =COPY= with the owner copied from the cached series, and give the service
   a second pool for the writer role.
4. Point the readers at the table, one at a time, with a test for each. A reader of a party or a
   tenant lists its series first.
5. Measure a burst with the tick injector.
6. Open a separate task for the session functions of the policies, which cost seven times on an
   indexed read.
