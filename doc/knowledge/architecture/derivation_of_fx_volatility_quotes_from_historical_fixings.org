#+title: Derivation of FX volatility quotes from historical fixings
#+description: Where no free source publishes FX implied volatilities, a derived source of our own derives ATM, RR and BF quotes from the historical spot fixings: an adapter that reads them through the market data reader and publishes under its own identity, so a vendor of the same quotes replaces it by rank.
#+type: knowledge
#+level: cross
#+filetags: :marketdata:synthetic:architecture:adapters:
#+created: 2026-11-03
#+updated: 2026-11-03

* Summary

Some starting values have no free public source, the implied volatilities of an
FX surface first among them. A derived source supplies them. It is a publisher
in the catalogue like the ECB, and its adapter reads stored fixings through the
market data reader, computes values from them, and publishes those values as
quotes of the same items a vendor would quote. Because the items are the same, a
vendor of them ranks above the derived source in a calibration policy and
replaces it with no change to any consumer. Its quotes carry its own identity,
never that of the publishers whose fixings it read.

* Detail

** The shape

#+begin_example
 durable fixing ticks (ECB, Bank of Canada, ...)
        |  latest on or before, window
        v
 the market data reader
        |
 the derived source adapter  -- announce, demand, tick, heartbeat -->  ores.marketdata
        (once a day, after the day's fixings)                          | stored as series
                                                                       | keyed by item, provider, party
                                                                       v
                                                   synthetic surface generator at start
                                                   (calibration policy ranks the providers)
#+end_example

- The derived source fetches nothing from outside. It reads only what the store
  already holds.
- It is not part of the public sources adapter, which publishes only what it
  fetched, in the supplier's own notation, and knows nothing of statistics.
- It reads through the reader of
  [[id:F4BBEF34-D84C-4AE6-A3AA-DC47C3EE06EE][Public sources publish daily fixings through an adapter]],
  as any client of the market data service does, and it publishes through the
  four messages every adapter uses.

** Same items, another provider

The derived quotes are oresmd quotes of the same items a vendor's vol feed
carries, one per pair, expiry and strike label, in the pair's quoting
convention. A quote series is keyed by item, provider and party, so a vendor's
quotes of an item sit beside the derived ones, and the generator's calibration
policy chooses between them by rank. A vendor ranked first replaces the derived
source; nothing downstream changes, as
[[id:FA280E9B-7E00-4747-8D29-42B3D4258873][A source switch changes one route and nothing else]]
describes for a live source.

** Kept as observations, not as fixings

The derived quotes are kept for good, and kept as what they are: the observations
of a quote series, not fixings. Each pair has one =fx_option= series of the derived
provider, whose derivation kind says it is computed rather than observed, and each
day's quotes are its observations at the end of that day, one for each expiry and
strike label. An observation is stored with no retention and a correction is a new
version of it, so a backtest reads any past day as it was published, through the
market data service's window reader of a series' observations. They do not enter =market_fixing=, because a derived quote is not a fixing.

The derived source keeps its own history. On start it reads the days each series
holds and derives every other day the stored fixings cover, each from the fixings
on or before it, so a past day reads no later fixing; afterwards it writes each new
day as the day's fixings arrive. It writes those days through the market data
service rather than through the ingest, whose age gate admits the current day only.
The quotes it publishes as ticks are the current day's, so a subscriber sees the
same values the series keeps.

** Its own identity

A derived quote is the output of our computation, and the system presents it as
such. Its provenance names the derived publisher. It carries no credit of the
publishers whose fixings fed it and no claim to be their figures; those fixings
are stored exactly as published, with their own credit, and appear in the
derived source only as internal inputs. The rule is stated with the terms of
each source in
[[id:3F9D6EAE-9FB8-4A0D-9BAF-1EA3EA93AB4F][Synthetic prices are calibrated from free public sources]],
and how the values are computed is described there too.

** In the code

The derived source is the provider =derived=, served through the feed
=derived-fx-vol= of the session =derived-local=, whose adapter module =derived= the
synthetic service runs; =marketdata_derived_catalogue_populate.sql= holds its rows.
The synthetic service starts =derived_fx_vol_adapter= for each feed of that session
beside the simulator. Its universe is the vendor instruments the catalogue holds for
the provider, one for each pair, expiry and label in the source's own notation, such
as =EURUSD/1Y/25RR=, each mapped to the =fx_option= quote of the pair. =derived_fx_vol_feed=
in =ores.synthetic.api= reads each demanded pair's ECB fixings through the market data
reader as the system party, takes a cross's returns as the difference of its two euro
legs on the days both hold, reads the pair's =fx_option_convention= from refdata, and
publishes each demanded quote not yet sent for the latest day its legs hold; it keeps
every day of every pair of its universe in the pair's series through the market data
service; the computation itself is the one =ores.analytics.quant= holds. The source reads no curves,
so it takes the spot as the forward: a quote under a forward delta or a forward ATM is
read against a forward that ignores the rate differential, which grows with the expiry.
