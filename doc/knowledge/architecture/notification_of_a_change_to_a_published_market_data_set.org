#+title: Notification of a change to a published market data set
#+description: Two channels tell a reader that a published set moved: the entity change event and the notice the apply writes, retried until it is delivered.
#+type: knowledge
#+level: cross
#+filetags: :architecture:marketdata:publish:notification:events:
#+created: 2026-11-24
#+updated: 2026-11-24

* Summary

A change to a published set is told on two channels, and the two answer different
questions. The entity change event, raised by a database trigger on the publish
tables, says that a row changed. The notice, written as an outbox row in the
apply's own transaction, says that the set the party prices from has moved, and
it names the subject, the act and the instant without carrying a single value.
The first channel serves code that follows a table; the second serves a person who
must know that the market moved under them.

Only the second is a promise. It is written with the publish or not at all, it is
delivered after the commit, and a delivery that fails is retried rather than
allowed to undo the act.

* Detail

** The two channels

| | The change event | The notice |
|--+------------------+------------|
| What raises it | A database trigger on the publish table | The apply, in its own transaction |
| What it says | A row of a table changed | The live set of a subject moved, and by which act |
| What it carries | The row's identity and its new state | The subject, the party, the as-of, the instant, the publisher, the request and the set it replaced |
| Who consumes it | Services that mirror or follow the table | The people who read the set, through the bell and optionally by mail |
| If delivery fails | The consumer catches up at its next read | The notice is retried until it is delivered |
| What it never carries | Meaning | Values, secrets or trade economics |

The change event is not sufficient as the notice, and the reason is not the
failure path. One act of the apply supersedes every live set of the series, so a
trigger fires once for the set published and once for each set it ended, and no
reader could tell from those rows which of them was the act. The notice is
written once, by the act, and says what the act meant.

** What the notice names**

The same shape serves a publish and a withdrawal, and the difference is one
field.

- The subject, in the words of its kind: the series' oresmd identity and the
  party that holds it.
- The as-of the set was built at, and the instant it moved.
- The account that published it, and the request it stands on.
- The as-of it replaced, where it replaced one.
- For a withdrawal: the reason, in words, because a reader must act on the
  reason and not on the fact alone.

The notice names the subject and the act and never the values. A notice that
carried the points would be a second copy of the set, and the two copies would
drift. It carries a link to the record page instead, which is where the values
are read ([[id:A541CB69-7CB6-4FF6-9AF5-DA0179C59B5E][Reading a published market data set]]).

** The outbox**

The notice is a row written in the transaction that publishes or withdraws the
set, and it is delivered from there after the commit
([[id:86D8986F-5247-4138-85C7-F8899C029F24][The trader applies an approved publish, which supersedes the live set and tells its readers]]).
The order matters in both directions. Written outside the transaction, a publish
that rolled back would have told its readers of a move that never happened.
Delivered inside the transaction, a mail server that was down would hold a
database transaction open and could undo a publish that had nothing wrong with
it.

Telling is therefore never the act. A failure is logged, retried and left in the
outbox, and the set stays published.

** The recipients**

The recipients are the accounts of the party that holds the set and the accounts
of the tenant that may read market series, resolved when the notice is raised so
that a new reader is not told about yesterday's move.

The consuming services are not reached this way, and that is a deliberate limit.
The bell hangs on an account and the subscription names a party, so a service is
not an account. A service that must react to a published set binds it and reads
it, and it learns of a move from its own read and from the change event, not from
the notice.

** The kinds, per kind**

Every kind that publishes must seed a notice kind for its publish, and every kind
that withdraws must seed one for its withdrawal. The rule is the same for each,
and the seed is the only per-kind part.

| Kind | Publish notice | Withdrawal notice |
|------+----------------+-------------------|
| IR curve | =marketdata.curve_published= | =marketdata.curve_withdrawn= |
| FX forward set | none | none |
| FX volatility surface | =marketdata.surface_published= | none |

The gap in the table is the lifecycle's own gap seen from the reader's side. A
forward set is published and superseded by no notice, and a surface can be
published but not withdrawn, so it needs no withdrawal notice yet. A kind whose
row is empty here is a kind whose readers find the change only at their next read
([[id:186B44DD-51EA-45AA-971E-0C2D4D21A6DD][The publication lifecycle of derived market objects]]).
