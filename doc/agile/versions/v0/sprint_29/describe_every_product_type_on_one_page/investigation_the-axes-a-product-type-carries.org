#+title: Investigation: The axes a product type carries
#+description: The product type row carries a set of markets, a product family and a construction method, and the eight enum values map onto them with no loss.
#+type: investigation
#+level: s1
#+filetags: :describe_every_product_type_on_one_page:sprint_29:v0:
#+created: 2026-10-31
#+updated: 2026-10-31

A point-in-time record, written while the story that commissioned it was open, of an investigation into the axes a product type carries: which classifications the row holds, which it references, and what each one means.

It is a snapshot, not a description of the system. The conclusions that the code must keep doing belong on the page that owns the subject. This one feeds the model task.

* Context

The catalogue row states a code, a one-line description, a =product_type=, and two flags. The routing to the table that holds the instrument lives in the catalogue model rather than in the row.

The =product_type= column is the eight-value =product_type_t= enum. Its own declaration says what it is:

#+begin_quote
This is a structural/routing classification, not a risk taxonomy. It answers
"what kind of financial product is this?" (FpML: productType), not "what
economic risk does it carry?" (FpML: assetClass).
#+end_quote

The values do not answer that question either. They mix three axes:

- a market (=fx=, =bond=, =credit=, =equity=, =commodity=);
- a contract shape (=swap=);
- a construction method (=composite=, =scripted=).

[[id:936ACDC4-830B-434E-82E7-A92276869869][Product Family]] reached the same conclusion while classifying the catalogue: the
column "conflates them. Its values mix asset classes, one contract shape and two
construction methods, which is three axes in one attribute and the reason no
value fits every product type cleanly."

The same axis is spelled five ways across the tree: =rates= in the knowledge
notes, =interest_rates= in our taxonomy, =swap= in the enum, =InterestRate= in
the FpML scheme and =interest_rates= in the ORE scheme. The market for
foreign exchange is spelled =fx= and =ForeignExchange=.

The 88 product notes state an asset class and a product family for each product,
in prose tables. Nothing reads them, and nothing in the store agrees with them.

The model task cannot begin until someone says which columns the row has. This
investigation says.

* Methodology

- Read the enum, its DDL and every consumer in C++, SQL, TypeScript and the
  shell, with =grep -rn product_type= over the tree.
- Read the catalogue model and the generated seed, and counted the rows and the
  routing tables.
- Read [[id:4043DE4C-5CDB-455E-B208-5AFF69AA76AE][ores.refdata.asset_class_catalogue]] and queried the two external
  schemes the store holds.
- Extracted the =* Identifiers= table of every knowledge note that has one, and
  aggregated the class and the family each states by enum value.
- Queried the database for the instrument codes' asset class column and for the
  rows each scheme holds.

The tree is =78c91e47b4=. The database schema is behind the checkout
(=3d319f922611918b= against =e0d684c360b6c178=), so where the two could differ
the counts are read from the seeds, and the queries only confirm them.

** What the eight values cover

From the notes, which state the class and the family of each product:

| Value       | Codes | Markets it holds         | Families it holds                                                     |
|-------------+-------+--------------------------+-----------------------------------------------------------------------|
| =swap=      | 13    | rates 9, unstated 4      | Swap 5, Option 2, Forward 1, Swaption 1, unstated 4                   |
| =fx=        | 26    | fx 24, unstated 2        | Option 16, Swap 3, Forward 3, unstated 4                              |
| =bond=      | 11    | bond 10, unstated 1      | Security 3, Option 2, Forward 1, Future 1, Repo 1, Swap 1, Position 1 |
| =credit=    | 8     | credit 7, unstated 1     | Swap 3, Option 1, unstated 4                                          |
| =equity=    | 32    | equity 30, unstated 2    | Option 18, Swap 4, Forward 3, Position 3, unstated 4                  |
| =commodity= | 25    | commodity 19, unstated 6 | Option 9, Swap 3, Forward 3, Swaption 1, Position 1, unstated 8       |
| =composite= | 1     | inherited, by definition | none; a container                                                     |
| =scripted=  | 5     | determined per trade     | Option 3, unstated 2                                                  |

The "unstated" counts are the 18 codes no note describes and the notes that say
the class or the shape is decided per trade. They are evidence, not a census:
the notes are prose and their coverage is 103 of 121.

No value is a value of a single axis. =swap= holds two markets and four shapes.
=bond= holds seven shapes. =composite= and =scripted= are construction methods,
not markets at all. The enum is strictly less informative than the axes it is
made of, so replacing it loses nothing and gains the distinctions it cannot
state.

** The classes and their spellings

The store already holds two external schemes and one taxonomy of our own.

| Our taxonomy code | ORE scheme    | FpML scheme           | Notes spell it | The enum says |
|-------------------+---------------+-----------------------+----------------+---------------|
| =fx=              | =fx=          | =ForeignExchange=     | =fx=           | =fx=          |
| =interest_rates=  | =rates=       | =InterestRate=        | =rates=        | =swap=        |
| =credit=          | =credit=      | =Credit=              | =credit=       | =credit=      |
| =equity=          | =equity=      | =Equity=              | =equity=       | =equity=      |
| =commodity=       | =commodity=   | =Commodity=           | =commodity=    | =commodity=   |
| =inflation=       | =inflation=   | =Inflation=           | =inflation=    | =swap=        |
| =bond=            | =bond=        | =Bond=                | =bond=         | =bond=        |
| =scripted=        | =cross_asset= | =SecuritiesFinancing= | none           | =scripted=    |

=SecuritiesFinancing= is FpML's name for the repo-like class, and it does not
mean what our =scripted= value means; the two sit on the same row only because
either is the nearest value in its scheme to the other.

The resolution is that the product type stores *our taxonomy code*. The external
schemes stay registered as schemes and stay mapped to the taxonomy, which is what
[[id:7F5EBFBD-FB8C-4E79-B386-7098104D3EDB][Asset Classes]] argues must not be collapsed into one attribute.

The =scripted= value needs a word of its own. Its prose in the catalogue
describes products, not a market: "trades whose payoff is stated as a script
rather than as a named product, because the payoff spans asset classes". That is
a construction method. No instrument code uses the value. And it cannot classify
=CompositeTrade=, whose class is inherited from its components and which is a
container rather than a script.

Two consequences follow, and only one belongs to this story:

- This story gives a product with no single market no market rows, and states what
  that means. =CompositeTrade= proves the case: it is a container, so =scripted=
  would be wrong.
- The taxonomy's =scripted= value is left alone. Renaming it to =cross_asset=
  (the ORE scheme's word) or retiring it is the taxonomy's own change, and it
  has to migrate every row that uses it.

** The families

Eight shapes appear across the notes: Option 50, Swap 19, Forward 11, Position 5,
Security 3, Swaption 2, Future 1, Repo 1. That accounts for 92 codes, and the
other 29 carry no shape. The counts are the notes' own, before the authoring task
resolves the places where two notes claim one code.

Twenty-nine codes carry none, for five different reasons, and the page must not
draw them the same way:

- 16 codes no note mentions at all, so the shape is unknown rather than absent:
  =BondFutureOption=, =CommodityAsianOption=, =CommodityDigitalOption=,
  =CommodityDigitalAveragePriceOption=, =CommoditySpreadOption=,
  =CommodityForwardVolatilityAgreement=, =ContractForDifference=,
  =CreditDefaultSwapOption=, =CrossCurrencySwap=,
  =EquityForwardVolatilityAgreement=, =ForwardVolatilityAgreement=,
  =FxAverageForward=, =FxForwardVolatilityAgreement=, =InflationSwap=,
  =IntradayPowerForward= and =SwaptionStraddle=. =CommodityAveragePriceOption= is
  not in the list: its note states Option.
- 2 codes whose note names another code: =Autocallable_01= and
  =PerformanceOption_01=, whose identifier tables say =ScriptedTrade= while their
  bodies name the variant. The authoring task fixes the tables.
- 6 variance, pairwise-variance and basket-variance codes that only the umbrella
  note covers, and that it leaves without a shape.
- 4 codes whose shape the corpus states it has not settled: =CBO=,
  =SyntheticCDO= and =RiskParticipationAgreement=, which are tranched or
  guarantee-like, and =CompositeTrade=, a container.
  [[id:936ACDC4-830B-434E-82E7-A92276869869][Product Family]] records the first three as open questions.

=ScriptedTrade= is the one conflict the census cannot settle: two notes claim the
code, one stating Option and one stating none. It is counted as none here, and
the authoring task decides.

The family is therefore nullable for a stated reason, and the page draws the
gap rather than hiding the row.

** Routing

91 of the 121 codes route to one of 22 instrument tables; 30 route nowhere, so
nothing holds a trade of those types. The catalogue model declares the routing as
==instrument_table=, and every consumer of it wants the table name: the trade
type check per instrument model, the export batch arrays and the importer
routing.

Nothing wants an eight-value routing family. The Qt form that the enum's
documentation mentions is gone, and the web reads the instrument from the
trade's own row.

** The markets a product type references

A product type does not always reference one market, and the store already holds
the proof in its payloads.

| Product type                                          | The second market | What shows it                                                                                                       |
|-------------------------------------------------------+-------------------+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| =CrossCurrencySwap=                                   | fx                | =swap_leg.currency= is per leg, and the model's own prose says a cross-currency swap has legs in two currencies.    |
| =InflationSwap=                                       | interest_rates    | The inflation leg carries =inflation_index_code=; the nominal legs sit beside it.                                   |
| =EquitySwap=                                          | interest_rates    | The equity side carries =underlying_name=; the funding legs are rate legs.                                          |
| =BondTRS=                                             | interest_rates    | The bond is the return leg and the funding leg is an index.                                                         |
| =ConvertibleBond=                                     | equity            | =bond_issue_conversion_target=: the issue converts into equity shares.                                              |
| =Ascot=                                               | equity            | Its brief: a convertible bond, an option, and a reference swap.                                                     |
| =QuantoEquityOption=, =EquityEuropeanCompositeOption= | fx                | Two of the gap products: an equity payoff struck in another currency.                                               |
| =CompositeTrade=, =ScriptedTrade=                     | decided per trade | A container of legs, and a payoff stated as a script.                                                               |
| =TotalReturnSwap=                                     | open              | The notes say equity; the routing puts it in the composite table, whose legs decide. The authoring task settles it. |

Six product types state a second market today, two more would if the gap products
were added, two have none by construction, and one is open.

* Conclusions
** Decision: the columns

| Column                | Type    | Null | Meaning                                                                            |
|-----------------------+---------+------+------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| =code=                | text    | no   | The ORE code. The identity.                                                        |
| =name=                | text    | no   | The name a desk uses.                                                              |
| =description=         | text    | no   | What the product is, in prose.                                                     |
| =source=              | text    | no   | The body that states the description, such as the ORE User Guide.                  |
| =source_ref=          | text    | no   | Where in that body, as a link.                                                     |
| =product_family_code= | text    | yes  | FK to =ores_refdata_product_family_codes_tbl=. Null means the shape is not stated. |
| =construction=        | text    | no   | =native=, =scripted= or =container=.                                               |
| =has_options=         | boolean | no   | A trade of this type carries an option block.                                      |
| =has_extension=       | boolean | no   | A trade of this type carries an extension block.                                   |
| =instrument_table=    | text    | yes  | The table that holds a trade of this type. Null means nothing holds it yet.        |
| =display_order=       | integer | no   | The order the catalogue lists it in.                                               |
| =knowledge_note=      | text    | yes  | The note that describes it, by document id.                                        |

The alternatives, and why they were not taken:

- *Keep the enum as a routing family.* Rejected: nothing routes on it. The
  table name routes, and it is already on the row.
- *Use the taxonomy's =scripted= for a spanning product.* Rejected:
  =CompositeTrade= is a container, and calling it scripted is false. No market
  rows, with a stated meaning, say the true thing.
- *Make the family an enum.* Rejected: the page groups and labels by family, and
  a label and a display order are data. The lookup follows the
  =asset_class_codes= precedent, which the page already reads for the other axis.
- *Make the construction a lookup.* Rejected: three structural values, curated by
  nobody, read by nobody as a list.

The market is the one axis the row does not hold as a column. It is a junction,
for the reason the next section gives.

The two flags are kept, with the meaning restated. No code branches on them
today; they are facts about the trade a type produces, the catalogue already
declares them per row, and the page is the place that finally states them. A row
that says a type carries an option block is information about the product, not
dead weight.

** Decision: the asset class is a junction

The classification is a set, so it is a junction, not a column:

| Column              | Type    | Meaning                                               |
|---------------------+---------+-------------------------------------------------------|
| =product_type_code= | text    | The product type, half the key.                       |
| =asset_class_code=  | text    | A market it references, half the key.                 |
| =is_home=           | boolean | True on the one market the catalogue groups it under. |

The key is both codes. A partial unique index allows at most one =is_home= row
per product type, and a check refuses a product type that holds rows and no home,
so the only representable states are *one home plus its secondaries* and *no rows
at all*. Zero rows means the markets are decided per trade, which is what
=CompositeTrade= and =ScriptedTrade= carry.

The alternatives, and why they were not taken:

- *One column and no junction.* Rejected: it cannot state that a
  =CrossCurrencySwap= references foreign exchange, and that is exactly the
  special product type the question is about.
- *A column for the home market plus a junction for the rest.* Rejected: the
  home would be held twice, in two mechanisms that can disagree, and every
  consumer that wants the full set would have to union them.
- *A junction with no home marker.* Rejected: the page groups products by
  market, and a product that appears under two markets would appear twice in a
  list that is meant to place it once. The marker is what makes "the asset class
  it belongs to" answerable while the set stays honest.
- *Reusing the risk-class relation for this.* Rejected for now: discounting
  makes almost every product sensitive to rates, so a bond's risk classes are
  rates and credit while its own markets are bond and equity. The two relations
  share a vocabulary and answer different questions, and the risk work builds
  its own.

The junction carries the same tenant column as the catalogue and lives in the
system tenant with it. The rule it enforces, and the meaning of each column out
of the context of this investigation, is on [[id:ED964111-1F24-4A3B-B4CE-665E2CF527B0][Product Type]].

** What each value of the enum becomes

| Value       | Home market                                                      | Family                                                                   | Construction |
|-------------+------------------------------------------------------------------+--------------------------------------------------------------------------+--------------|
| =swap=      | =interest_rates= (rates products), =inflation= (=InflationSwap=) | from the note: Swap, Option, Forward or Swaption                         | native       |
| =fx=        | =fx=                                                             | from the note: Option, Swap or Forward                                   | native       |
| =bond=      | =bond=                                                           | from the note: Security, Option, Forward, Future, Repo, Swap or Position | native       |
| =credit=    | =credit=                                                         | from the note: Swap or Option, or null                                   | native       |
| =equity=    | =equity=                                                         | from the note: Option, Swap, Forward or Position                         | native       |
| =commodity= | =commodity=                                                      | from the note: Option, Swap, Forward, Swaption or Position               | native       |
| =composite= | none, inherited from the components                              | null, a container                                                        | =container=  |
| =scripted=  | none, decided by the underlyings                                 | from the note: Option, or null                                           | =scripted=   |

The secondary markets come from the table above; no value of the enum states
them, which is the point.

Every value becomes a smaller set of rows than the enum's single value covered,
and the distinctions the enum could not state come back: a swap and a swaption
are both =swap= today and differ in family; an inflation swap and a rates swap
are both =swap= today and differ in market; and a =CrossCurrencySwap= and a
=Swap= are both =swap= today and differ in the markets they reference.

* Recommendations

- *Model the product type* takes the column list above and the junction as the
  market axis, drops =product_type_t=, and keeps the two flags with their
  restated meaning.
- *Author the description of every product type* fills the gaps: the 18 codes
  with no note need a market and a shape as well as prose, the second markets in
  the table above need confirming, and the unsettled shapes need a decision or a
  stated non-decision.
- *Serve the product type catalogue* returns the markets as a set with the home
  one marked, groups by the home market and then by family, and draws a product
  with no home as "decided per trade" and one with no family as "shape not
  stated".
- *Make the knowledge corpus read from the catalogue* changes the notes' =rates=
  to =interest_rates= when it links them to the row, and stops stating a class
  and a family that the row now holds.

The rule that outlives this work is on [[id:ED964111-1F24-4A3B-B4CE-665E2CF527B0][Product Type]]: what the catalogue
holds, the market junction and its two invariants, and how the pricing engine
types join. This page is the snapshot of how that rule was reached.

Outside this story: renaming or retiring the taxonomy's =scripted= value, adding
=cross_asset= if the taxonomy wants a value for a spanning product, and the
risk-class relation, which is a different set.
