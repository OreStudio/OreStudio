* Acceptance

- A gated put or delete from any caller but the applier writes nothing and answers approval_required.
- A contact change is written directly.

* Plan

(Implementation strategy. Written when work starts; key decisions
are distilled into the parent story's =* Decisions= at close, but the
plan itself stays — it is the historical record of what we did.)

- Turn the gate on in the models: the counterparty, the identifier and the party link move from =:pending_change:= to =:gated:=, and the generated services ask the policy as the book's does.
- Give the junction service the same gate. The templates had none. A link has no columns, so the service asks the policy with none. The line the request holds names the link by its two halves.
- Name the link's policy rows after the model, =party_counterparty=, because the generated service uses the model's name.
- Let the proposal service open the gate: the preview is a rehearsal, and the apply names its request.
- Write the live rows of the test fixtures through the repositories, which sit below the gate.

* Notes

- The counterparty and the identifier have no delete policy row, so a removal of either is free. The link has a put and a delete row. The book has the same gap. Adding delete rows would gate the removal of a counterparty, and it needs a decision.
- The shell commands for counterparty identifiers, and any other client that writes the three over NATS, now get =approval_required=. The raise operations are the way in. I did not check the ORE import for counterparty writes.
- The contact information and the business centres of a counterparty have no policy row and are written directly, as the acceptance asks.
- The other junction services are not regenerated, so they have no gate until a model sets =:gated:=.
- Not done: a run against the running services with the web.

* Result

A put or a removal of a counterparty, an identifier or a party link is refused with =approval_required= unless it carries out a line of an approved request.

- The three models are gated. The generated services ask =ores_inbox_gate_fn=, and the counterparty proposal service opens the gate: the preview is a rehearsal and the apply names its request.
- The junction service templates have the gate for the put, the batch put, the delete and the batch delete.
- The link's policy rows use the model's name, =party_counterparty=.
- A contact change is written directly.

Evidence: the whole =ores.refdata.core.tests= binary passes (416 cases), with seven new cases in =service_counterparty_gate_tests.cpp=. They cover a refused counterparty put, identifier put, link put and link removal, a request that is not approved, an approved request that opens only the lines it holds, a link put under an approved request, and a direct contact change. The catalogue drift sweep, derived-model, handler permission, cmake source, pasted block symbol, populate reference and test reachability checks pass.

Not done here: the callers named in Notes, and a run against the live services.
