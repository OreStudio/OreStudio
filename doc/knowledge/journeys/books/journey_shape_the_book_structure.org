This page documents a [[id:F44766FA-1E9C-416F-849A-E131101B2ECA][user journey]]: A tenant administrator walks the portfolio tree, opens or creates a book, classifies it, sets the portfolio rights, and reviews the write.

A user journey describes what one person is trying to do, before anything is
built. It is a design document that outlives any sprint, so it names the
screens, the entity fields each screen shows, and the operations and messages
each step needs.

* Goal

"I want the book structure to say exactly what we mean by it: which portfolio
each book sits in, what that book is for, how it is classified, and who may see
it." The journey is done when the tree reads correctly, each changed book shows
its new version, and the rights at the portfolio node are the ones the
administrator intended.

* Actor and trigger

The actor is the tenant administrator: an account with the =TenantAdmin= role,
acting inside their own tenant. The trigger is a new desk, a book that must be
opened or reclassified, a book the desk no longer uses, or a colleague who needs
sight of a portfolio. The journey starts at *Book tree*, or at a book or
portfolio link on another screen, and ends at the outcome when the writes land,
or at the refused screen when the server rejects one. The journey keeps no
server-side state: every step completes when the server replies.

* Steps

1. *See the tree.* The person opens *Book tree*. The system shows the tenant's
   portfolios and the books under them, with a search over both. Every row is
   the tenant's own data. Each book row carries its status, its regulatory type
   and its purpose.
2. *Choose the portfolio.* The person chooses the portfolio that will hold the
   book, or creates a new one. The system shows what the book reads from that
   node: the aggregation currency, the owner business unit, the portfolio
   purpose, the sandbox, and who may see it. A book copies none of these. It
   reads them from its portfolio, and the portfolio's rights cover the book.
   The model says the book's owner unit must be one named in the portfolio
   ancestry chain, and the owner-unit picker marks a unit that is not.
3. *Create or open the book.* The person names the book and gives it the fields
   finance and the ledger use: the functional currency, the GL account
   reference, the cost center, the owner unit, and a description. The name is
   the natural key and does not change after the write. A book belongs to
   exactly one portfolio. The system writes =refdata.v1.books.put= with
   =must_not_exist= for a new book and =must_match_version= for a change.
4. *Set its classification.* The person sets the status and the three
   independent classification axes, plus the rates centre and the sweepable
   flag. The axes combine freely: regulatory type, ledger feed and book purpose
   are not correlated by any check. The book carries exactly one purpose. Each
   picker names one row of a shared system-tenant list, so the person can choose
   a value here and cannot edit the list here. The person can also close the
   book. The close the book cannot make is the one with activity still open: the
   system must refuse it and name the trades that remain. See /What is missing/.
5. *Set the rights.* The person grants or removes =read= and =open_sandbox= at
   the portfolio node. The system shows the effective rights for every account,
   with the ancestor a right was inherited from. A right at a node covers every
   node below it, and not its siblings. A right granted to a closed account
   stays on record, and the function stops honouring it.
6. *Review.* Nothing is written until the person confirms. The system lists each
   change with its field, its old value and its new value, and the subject that
   will carry it. The person picks a reason code.
7. *Read the outcome.* The system writes the book and the rights, and shows the
   book's new version. The person can open the history, go to the rights, or
   return to the tree.

Two states sit beside the steps. *Refused* shows the error the server returns
when a write is not allowed, with the subject, the intent, the error code and
the record version. *History* shows every version of one book with the
field-level difference from the version before it, and offers a revert, which
the system writes as a new version.

No step waits on the server after its reply, and the journey keeps no state.

* Screens and wireframes

One screen with a step rail: the seven steps in order, with the two side states
beneath them. The step in view is a card. The tree step splits into the tree on
the left and the chosen book or portfolio on the right. The classification step
splits into the book's own pickers on the left and the shared taxonomy on the
right. The rights step splits into the direct-rights editor on the left and the
effective-rights table on the right. The person moves between books and
portfolios without leaving the screen.

* Prototype

A static page at =doc/prototypes/books/shape-the-book-structure/index.html=: mock data
only, no backend, no build step and no session. It is the design record for this
journey. It settles three questions. First, what a book reads from its portfolio
rather than copies: the aggregation currency, the owner unit ancestry, the
portfolio purpose, the sandbox, and the rights. Second, which rows are the
tenant's own data and which are the shared taxonomy: portfolios, books and
rights are the tenant's, and the five classification lists are the system
tenant's. Third, the close the book cannot make: a close while the ledger still
shows open activity.

* What is missing

One item is the missing row of the coverage table above. One is a rule the
journey draws that cannot be enforced where it reads as if it would be, and it is
recorded rather than dropped. One is out of scope by the story's own decision.

1. *Nothing refuses closing a book with open activity, and the store cannot.*
   The journey wants a refusal on the close that names the open trades, unsettled
   cash flows and open positions the book still holds. Those rows belong to the
   =trading= component, and no component's SQL reads another's tables — refdata's
   =create/= directory contains no reference to a =trading_= table, and neither
   does any other component's. Refdata's service layer does not depend on
   =trading= either, so it cannot ask. A refusal here would have to be the first
   cross-component read in the schema, or a wire round trip that cannot be atomic
   with the book write. Until that decision is made, the screen checks before it
   writes and shows what the server would say; the refusal is not enforced, and
   this document does not claim it is.
2. *The rights panel is drawn here, although the catalogue assigned
   =portfolio_right= to the Access group.* The panel belongs to that group's
   journey, which owns the permissions and the change reason. This journey draws
   it because a book carries no rights of its own and inherits its visibility
   from the portfolio node. Until the rights journey exists, this screen carries
   the panel and links to the Access group. This is out of scope for the book
   work by the story's own decision.

The four items this section used to carry are built, and the coverage table says
so: the book carries and saves its purpose and its ledger feed, the status
transition rule refuses an edge the lifecycle does not draw, and the owning unit
is checked against the portfolio ancestry chain.
