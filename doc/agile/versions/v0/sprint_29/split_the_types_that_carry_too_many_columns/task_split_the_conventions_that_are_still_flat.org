* Plan

** The rules a split obeys

The data-oriented model decides the shape, not the column count. Its rules, and
the reasons behind them, are in
[[id:BBD43BF2-9FEE-42B0-A667-9468AF759DC2][Data-Oriented Design in ORE Studio]] and
[[id:56AD6F46-99E0-482E-B7B5-C9FDEC8A7641][The data classification checklist]]; this task applies them and
does not restate them. In short:

- a type holds one component: fields that share one invariant, hold at one
  instant, and are keyed by the same key;
- a part is joined by key and never nested, so the wire carries one typed array
  per component and no part's fields appear inside another's object;
- a field read on a different cadence, or by a different screen, job or service,
  goes with its readers and not with the row it happens to sit in;
- the classification comes first. A compile error never reclassifies data: if a
  shape has to be denormalised for a compiler, the classification stays and the
  denormalisation is recorded;
- the centre keeps the identity and whatever the parts must agree on. A part one
  reader needs does not stay on the centre.

** This grouping

Three convention kinds never moved onto the family's shape. The family's other
kinds are a central [[id:9477A085-0822-485F-BDF7-3C8F4A306E93][convention]] row
plus parts; these three are still one flat row each:

| Type | Members | Columns | The blocks that should be parts |
|------+---------+---------+---------------------------------|
| =commodity_future_convention= | 53 | 53 | expiry anchor, option expiry, power peak and off-peak, averaging, the ORESmd link |
| =cross_currency_basis_convention= | 27 | 27 | =flat_*= and =spread_*=, two legs |
| =cross_currency_fix_float_convention= | 20 | 20 | the fixed and the floating leg |

Each block has its own invariant: the option expiry fields only mean anything
when the convention states an option expiry, the averaging fields only when the
future is averaged, and each leg of a cross-currency swap carries its own index,
tenor and conventions. That is what makes them parts rather than columns.

The target shape is the family's, not a new one: see
[[id:B607E5B7-190F-4D38-B515-8A7B27FB6B47][The atomised entity: a central row and its parts]] and the
tenor basis swap kind, which already carries a pay/receive and a long/short
part.

** What proves it

- Only the central convention row carries the kind.
- A kind-specific block exists in exactly one part, and no other kind's row or
  part carries it.
- The ORE projection round trips: the document states the convention once and
  each part's fields once.

* Notes

** Cross currency fix float: where the swap terms go

The flat type had five fields that neither leg holds: =settlement_days=,
=settlement_calendar=, =settlement_convention=, =eom= and =is_resettable=. The
first draft put them on the central =convention= row. We rejected that,
because the central row is shared by every kind. Five columns that only this
kind states would then sit on every kind's row, and the data-oriented model
forbids a column that belongs to another kind's part.

The five fields are one part, =cross_currency_fix_float_swap_terms=. The
name is "swap terms" and not "settlement", because =eom= rolls the coupons
of both legs and =is_resettable= resets the notional. Neither is a
settlement term. The cross currency basis kind carries the same five fields,
so it can use the same part shape.

|-------+--------|
| Data and operation | The terms of one cross currency fix float convention that apply to both legs. The ORE conventions import writes them, and the export and the convention screens read them. |
| Classification | Family part: one part of the =cross_currency_fix_float_convention= family. |
| Invariant, or none | Each field applies to the whole swap at the instant the convention is written, so no leg can hold it alone. The settlement days, calendar and convention fix the settlement date together. |
| Owners | One: ores.refdata. |
| Bundle or package, and why | Neither. The part is related to its central row and its two legs by =convention_id=, and the family write puts all four rows in one unit of work. |

| Consistency point | None: the part is not a view of the world. |
| Forbidden here | Putting kind-specific columns on the shared central row, and nesting the part inside another part's object on the wire. |

** Cross currency basis: three parts, and why the leg columns keep their prefix

The basis kind takes the fix float kind's shape: the central =convention=
row, the swap terms both legs share, and one part per leg. The swap terms are
=SettlementDays=, =SettlementCalendar=, =RollConvention=, =EOM= and
=IsResettable=; the fix float kind states a settlement convention where this
kind states a roll convention, so the two kinds do not share one part type.
=FlatIndexIsResettable= goes on the flat leg, because it states whether that
leg's index resets. =oresmd_uri= goes to the central row, which every kind
carries; an atomised central row stages no URI, as the fix float kind's does.

The two legs carry the same fields, and the screen shows a composite as one
row with every part's columns laid over it. A leg column named =index= or
=tenor= would then be written twice, and the spread leg would hide the flat
leg. So each leg column keeps the leg prefix that ORE's element states:
=flat_index=, =flat_tenor=, =spread_index=, =spread_tenor= and so on. That is
what the fix float fixed leg (=fixed_currency=) and the tenor basis swap
parts (=pay_index=, =long_index=) already do, and the column names stay the
ones the flat table had.

** Commodity future: contract terms and four optional parts

The commodity future kind takes the family's shape, but its blocks are not all
present, as the cross currency legs are. ORE requires the contract frequency
and the calendar of every commodity future, and makes each other block
optional. So the kind has one part every convention fills and four it fills at
most once:

| Part | Columns | Filled in the seed |
|------+---------+--------------------|
| =commodity_future_contract_terms= | 18: the frequency, the calendars, the expiry rules, the contract months, the hours per day, the index name, the savings time, the delivery location, the balance of the month fields, the prohibited expiries, the future continuations | 790 of 790 |
| =commodity_future_expiry_anchor= | 7 =anchor_*= columns: one form of =AnchorDay= | 645 |
| =commodity_future_option_expiry= | 13 =option_*= columns, with the option underlying and the option continuations | 38 |
| =commodity_future_off_peak_index= | 4: =OffPeakPowerIndexData=, all required | 26 |
| =commodity_future_averaging_terms= | 8 =averaging_*= columns: =AveragingData=, four of them required | 60 |

The seed counts are the rows the DQ seed stages from the corpus. No seeded
anchor states more than one form.

A part makes required what ORE requires together. The flat row held the
off-peak and the averaging fields as nullable columns, and only the mapper
knew that the four off-peak fields come together. Each is now a part whose
required columns are not null, so the table holds that rule.

=hours_per_day= and =savings_time= stay on the contract terms. They are not
inside =OffPeakPowerIndexData=, and the seed states the hours per day on more
conventions (31) than it states the off-peak data on (26), so they are not
that part's invariant. The lists stay text columns, as on the flat row.

The column names already differ from part to part, so no column needs a new
prefix. =oresmd_uri= goes to the central row, as for the other atomised kinds,
and the central row stages no URI. The flat row took its URI from
=IndexName=, so the 34 seeded conventions that state an index name no longer
stage one. This is the same loss the fix float and basis kinds accepted.

The seed does not decode =OptionNthWeekday=, so it stages no
=option_nth_nth= or =option_nth_weekday=. The flat seed did not stage them
either. The ORE mapper reads and writes both.

* Result

No convention kind is a flat row any more. The three kinds this task found
flat now take the family's shape: a central =convention= row that carries the
kind and the ORE document id, and one part per block or leg, each joined by
=convention_id= and written with the central row in one family write.

| Kind | Parts | The invariant each part holds |
|------+-------+-------------------------------|
| Cross currency fix float (PR #4351) | swap terms, fixed leg, float leg | The swap terms apply to the whole swap at once. Each leg states how that one leg pays. ORE requires every block, so a convention fills all three. |
| Cross currency basis (PR #4359) | swap terms, flat leg, spread leg | As for the fix float kind. The legs keep their =flat_= and =spread_= prefixes, because the screen lays every part over one row by column name. |
| Commodity future | contract terms, expiry anchor, option expiry, off-peak index, averaging terms | The contract terms state the listed contract, and ORE requires them. Each other part means something only when the element states that block, so a convention fills it at most once. The off-peak and averaging parts make required the fields ORE requires together, a rule the flat row left to the mapper. |

The acceptance holds as follows:

- No kind's row or part carries another kind's column. Only the central row
  carries the kind, and the central row carries nothing a kind alone states.
- The commodity future's anchor, option expiry, power and averaging blocks are
  parts. The two cross currency kinds model each leg as a part.
- The wire carries one typed array per part, the ORE projection maps each part
  on its own and round trips the corpus with no difference, and the DQ seed
  stages one block per part.
- Each part's invariant is in the table above and in the notes of each kind.

The live data proves the shape: after a recreate and Acme provisioning, every
convention of the three kinds has its required parts, none has two rows of
one part, and a republish inserts nothing. The measurements table has the
counts.

Three things are left as they were, and each is recorded. The atomised
kinds' central rows stage no =oresmd_uri=, so the commodity future's 34
seeded index names, like the cross currency indices, carry no staged URI.
The seed does not decode the option's nth weekday, as the flat seed did not.
And the three composite publishers repeat one shape; generating them is
codegen work.
