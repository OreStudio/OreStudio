#+title: A leverage loses its digits at the ORE binding
#+description: A range bound's levels keep the document's text except the leverage, which is a plain float leaf: a document stating 1.05 exports as 1.04999995 while its sibling levels keep their digits. Fixing it needs a lexical double leaf in the ORE binding, which is work on the reapply script and the binding's double writer, not an entity unit.

This page is a [[id:671F18E4-E09C-4B3B-BD24-D33DF8AE38A6][capture]] in the *inbox* bucket of the product backlog — a pre-sprint idea, not yet pulled into a sprint as a story.

* What

Give the ORE binding a lexical double leaf, the double counterpart of
=moneyFloat=, and make =rangeBound.Leverage= use it. The leaf keeps the
document's text beside the parsed value, and the writer prints the text when it
has one, exactly as =_serialize_moneyFloat= does for the money leaves.

* Why

=RangeFrom=, =RangeTo=, =Strike= and =StrikeAdjustment= keep the digits the
document states, because they are =moneyFloat= leaves. =Leverage= is a plain
=xsd::optional<float>=: it is a multiplier stored in a =double precision=
column, and its text is lost at the binding. A document that states
=<Leverage>1.05</Leverage>= exports as =1.04999995=, so a round trip changes
the document it read.

The fix is the same shape the money leaves already have, and the lever is
[[file:../../../../../../scripts/reapply_ore_lexical_form.py][reapply_ore_lexical_form.py]]: a member table entry, a setter that stashes the
text, and a writer that prints it. It is filed separately because it touches
the binding for every family that states a double, not only this one.
