#+title: Directory journeys
#+description: The account roster, and bringing a person in.
#+type: knowledge
#+level: cross
#+filetags: :knowledge:user_journey:security:
#+created: 2026-09-23
#+updated: 2026-09-23

The roster is the administrator's starting point, and creating an account is the
journey that fills it. Both treat an account as a record in a directory rather
than as one person's own details.

The roster journey has no single entity behind it. Its usefulness comes from
joining the account, its sign-in state, its parties and its roles into one row,
so the administrator can see who is locked, who is online, and who must change
their password at next sign-in. That join is the journey's real work.

An account is not always a person. It has four types — =user=, =service=,
=algorithm= and =llm= — and only the first has a photo, a job title, a contact
record or a party in the ordinary sense. A non-human account is still an
identity that logs in, holds roles and appears in provenance, so it is created
and managed here, but the journey is not the same one.
