* Goal

Each of the five volatility entries holds one kind of entry, and the block that only exists for one kind is a type of its own. Cap floor and swaption share one proxy part, so the same split is made once and used twice.

* Acceptance

- Every one of the five types ends with at most one block per entry, and no column is meaningful only for a value another column selects.
- The proxy block is one part type that cap floor and swaption both carry, not two copies.
- The ORE export and import of a volatility entry writes one typed array per part, and the bytes the reader sees stay what the ORE file states.
- No compiler flag, unity exclusion or field-group reshuffle is added.
- The split is argued by the invariant each part holds, not by the entry's member count, and the argument is in the task's Result.

* Plan

** The rules a split obeys

The data-oriented model decides the shape, not the column count. Its rules, and
the reasons behind them, are in
[[id:BBD43BF2-9FEE-42B0-A667-9468AF759DC2][Data-Oriented Design in ORE Studio]] and
[[id:56AD6F46-99E0-482E-B7B5-C9FDEC8A7641][The data classification checklist]]; this task applies them and
does not restate them. In short:

- a type holds one component: fields that share one invariant, hold at one
  instant, and are keyed by the same key;
- a part is joined by key and never nested, so the wire carries one typed array
  per component and no part's fields appear inside another's object;
- a field read on a different cadence, or by a different screen, job or service,
  goes with its readers and not with the row it happens to sit in;
- the classification comes first. A compile error never reclassifies data: if a
  shape has to be denormalised for a compiler, the classification stays and the
  denormalisation is recorded;
- the centre keeps the identity and whatever the parts must agree on. A part one
  reader needs does not stay on the centre.

** This grouping

Five refdata volatility entries, and their shapes come from
[[id:E6C92BB1-8AB4-4580-A318-A5EB2A10BBB1][the inventory]]:

| Type | Members | Columns | The block that should not be a column |
|------+---------+---------+---------------------------------------|
| =cap_floor_volatility_config= | 39 | 39 | =has_proxy_config= and seven proxy columns |
| =curve_volatility_config= | 32 | 32 | =kind= and one curve column per surface |
| =swaption_volatility_config= | 27 | 27 | =has_proxy_config= and five proxy columns |
| =fx_volatility_config= | 22 | 22 | =dimension= and the smile columns it selects |
| =inflation_cap_floor_volatility_config= | 22 | 22 | =quote_type= and one column per quote structure |

The proxy block is the same component in the cap floor and swaption entries: it
names a source curve, an index, a rate computation period, a target and a
scaling factor, and it is present or absent as a unit. It is one part type, not
two copies, and its own key is the entry's key.

The kind columns are the entry's kind stated as a column. =kind=,
=dimension= and =quote_type= select a construction, and a column that exists for
one construction is that construction's part.

** What moves in one change

Per entry: the model, every generated address, the ORE projection and the
fixtures, and the screens that read the volatility section.

** What proves it

- No row carries a column its own kind cannot use.
- The proxy part is one type, and both entries use it.
- A round trip of the ORE fixture states the same values, written as arrays per
  part rather than one row of nulls.
