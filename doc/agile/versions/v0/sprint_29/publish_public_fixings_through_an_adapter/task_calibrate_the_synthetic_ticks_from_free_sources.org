* Goal

* Acceptance

- The task reads fixings through [[id:BB5DC80B-EC57-4029-8A8B-6A98E0EE7D6D][Read the latest fixing and a window]]. It fetches nothing itself.
- A generator config carries =price_source= of =fixed= or =fixing=. For =fixing= it names the FX fixing items it may start from, each with a rank, a policy (=latest_on_or_before_clock= or =exact:<date>=) and a window in days for the volatility. The task drops the vintage columns.
- A feed starts from the latest fixing of its best-ranked item that has one, on or before the date of the market clock, so a feed that stops and starts takes the newest value. Its volatility is the spread of the log returns over the window.
- A pair with no fixing keeps its fixed level, and the feed screen says so.
- The feed screen shows the provider, the item, the vendor time and the time received of the value a feed started from.
- The 2016 ORE vintage and the vintage datasets calibrate no generator, and the task removes the dependency rows that required them.

* Plan

Decisions taken with the user (2026-10-28):

- Volatility: the spread of the daily log returns over the window, in hourly terms,
  sets the size of the feed's mixture. Every component's deviation is scaled by one
  factor, so the configured shape, its jumps and tails, is kept.
- Items: a pair may start from an item that publishes it directly or the other way
  round, read as 1/x; no crosses, since the ECB counts a computed rate as a change
  to its data.
- Vintage goes altogether, from FX and from IR. Market data as of a past date for
  specific uses is a separate investigation:
  [[id:39EBABD1-CE5F-4D9C-A47C-CD0FCD492B46][Investigate backdated market data for specific use cases]].

Three PRs, each checked on its own:

1. Calibrate FX from fixings.
   - A child entity of the FX config, =fx_spot_fixing_item=, modelled as
     =gmm_component= is: the item, an optional provider, a rank, a policy
     (=latest_on_or_before_clock= or =exact:<date>=) and a window in days, staged
     through DQ like the components.
   - =price_source= takes =fixing=; =gmm_initial_price= stays the level a pair
     keeps when no item has a fixing.
   - At start, the feed reads its ranked items through =get_fixing= and
     =get_fixing_window=, takes the first with a value, inverts it when the item
     quotes the pair the other way, and scales the mixture to the measured spread.
     The provenance of the start is kept for the screen.
   - The seeds rank, for each FX config, every catalogue item of its pair, direct
     or inverted: ECB, IMF, BOC, TCMB. The thirteen vintage configs become
     =fixing=.
2. Remove vintage: the FX and IR columns and checks, the vintage lookup and the
   validity handler, the marketdata vintage read, the shell and web surfaces, the
   vintage dataset dependency rows.
3. The feed screen shows the provider, the item, the vendor date and the time
   received of the value a feed started from, or that it kept its fixed level.

The three items shipped in one PR. Item 3 was done last, against a live
environment, and that check found two gaps in item 1 that no test covered: the
service held no =marketdata::market_fixings:read= permission, and a fixing read
demands a party while a service holds none. Both are fixed here: the role gains
the permission and the fixing reads name the party whose data they want, as the
series reads already did. The feed names its own config's party.

* Notes

- No test drives a whole fixing feed end to end. The pure calibration helpers
  are tested, the marketdata fixing read is tested over NATS with, without and
  beyond a party, and the screen is tested, but nothing starts a feed whose
  item has a stored fixing and asserts the level it took. That gap is what let
  the missing permission and the absent party reach a live environment. It is
  worth a service-level test with a stored fixing.
- A feed that cannot read its fixing says why on the screen, in the start's
  note, so the failure is visible rather than silent.

* Result

An FX feed whose price source is =fixing= starts from the newest public fixing of
its best-ranked item, on or before the date of the market clock or the date its
policy names, and takes the size of its mixture from the spread of the item's
fixings over its window. An item that quotes the pair the other way round is read
as one over its value. A pair no source fixes keeps its configured level, and the
feed records why.

The feed page shows where the run started: the provider, the item as its oresmd
URI, the publisher's date, the time the value arrived, and the spread the window
measured; or that no fixing set the level and the run kept it. A curve reports no
start, since it starts from no published value.

The vintage goes from FX and IR, and with it the columns, the lookup, the
validity handler, the shell command, the web fields and the dataset dependency
rows.

Checked live: the two seeded FX feeds start from an ECB fixing of EUR/CAD and an
IMF fixing of USD/CHF, both published on 2026-10-28, and a curve feed reports no
start.
