* Plan

Serve the last four access routes at the BFF's addresses. The picture, the
reporting line and the reporting tree belong to the people screens and join
=account_screen_routes=; the ways to hold a permission reads the roles
catalogue and joins =role_write_routes=.

- The picture reads the account, the caller's own by the self read, then the
  image from the assets service, and answers its bytes with the BFF's headers:
  kept privately for five minutes and never run as a document. The account read
  is shared with the one-account route, so the self read is decided once. The
  part takes =ores.assets.api= as a public dependency, because its header states
  an image.
- The reporting line writes and answers the account as written; the reporting
  tree answers the camelCase shape, an empty reference read as null.
- The ways to hold answers the requestable roles, not a service's and not
  already held, that grant the permission by its code, its area's wildcard or
  everything; any failure answers no ways, as the BFF did. The roles read is
  shared with the roles catalogue route.

* Result

The C++ server answers the last four access routes at the addresses ores.web
calls. With them, every one of the access area's 30 BFF routes has a C++ route
at its own address and in its own shape, each paired in the inventory. The
tests prove the reporting tree's shape, the picture's headers, the ways-to-hold
rule over each kind of role, and the refusals before forwarding.
