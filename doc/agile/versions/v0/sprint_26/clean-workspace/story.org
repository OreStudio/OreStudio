* Decisions

- *Keep the two operations the component serves but nothing calls.* The
  ancestor resolution chain and the trade-scope whitelist have no
  consumer in the tree. They are modelled as an operation model and
  served by hand-written handlers rather than deleted under P03,
  because they are the workspace feature's intended surface and the
  design doc treats the trade-scope filter as unfinished, not
  abandoned. Deleting them would be a product decision, not a clean-up
  one.
- *Split the component's SQL in the model, not in the aggregator.* The
  table, its triggers and its hierarchy read generate; the
  resolution-order function, the workspace FK validation function, the
  trade-scope table and the row-level security stay hand-written.
  =workspace_create.sql= is the aggregator and states which includes
  the generator owns, so the split is readable in one file.
- *The Live workspace's lifecycle guard is recorded, not
  reimplemented.* The canonical protocol makes archiving a put and
  deleting a window close, and the generated service has no seam for a
  rule the hand-written one enforced. It belongs to the workspace
  feature story.
- *Rename the schema's ownership in the ignore list rather than in the
  schema.* The =validation_ignore.txt= comment claimed workspaces are
  tenant-independent; the schema has always carried =tenant_id=, a
  version and RLS. The inert entries are removed and the comment now
  describes the two tables.
- *List the component with one accepted exception rather than keep it
  out.* S01 applies -- the component has entity verbs -- and it does not
  pass, because the shell composite carries no workspace part for the
  generated units to land in. The two honest alternatives were to build
  the part now or to hold the component out of the registry until it
  exists; the exception names the gap with its reason and its acceptor,
  which is what the standard prefers over withholding every gate from
  the component in the meantime.
