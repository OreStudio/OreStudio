* Goal

Barclays Plc has been our stand-in test/demo legal entity because it
has a "realistic" corporate shape, but it is not fit for purpose: it
has no employees, so we cannot exercise features that depend on
staff/role identity — e.g. verifying that logging in as Middle
Office, or as a Trader, resolves the correct permissions and data
visibility.

Introduce *Acme Corporation* — a synthetic holding-group legal entity,
structured after Barclays' real corporate shape (holding company →
Retail Banking, Investment Bank, and other divisions, across
multiple global business centres) — and populate it fully across
every entity ORE Studio already supports: parties, business units,
portfolios, books, and staff accounts. Not every business
unit/portfolio/book combination needs to exist, but every division
that trades derivatives must have a complete chain down to book
level. Staff accounts should exist for the roles needed to exercise
RBAC (trader, middle office, etc.), with names generated to match
the locale of their business centre (e.g. Korean names for the Seoul
desk) and a profile picture per staff member.

Acme Corporation becomes the system's reference "fully populated" entity —
extended over time as new entities and features are added, so it
stays comprehensive rather than needing to be rebuilt from scratch
each time.

* Acceptance

- A holding-group party hierarchy exists for Acme Corporation, modelled on
  Barclays' real corporate structure: a top-level holding company
  with subsidiaries for at least Retail Banking and Investment
  Banking, spread across multiple business centres/locations.
- Every subsidiary/division that trades derivatives has a full chain
  of business units, portfolios, and books underneath it — divisions
  that don't trade derivatives may stop at the party/business-unit
  level.
- Staff accounts exist for the roles needed to exercise RBAC (at
  minimum: trader, middle office), attached to the business
  units/desks they belong to, with correct role/permission
  assignments.
- Staff names are generated to match the locale of their business
  centre (e.g. Korean names for a Seoul desk, not generic Western
  names everywhere).
- Every generated staff account has an associated profile picture.
- The generation is repeatable/scriptable (generator, not a one-off
  manual data entry exercise) so the entity can be regenerated and
  extended as new entities/features land.
- Data is seeded through the same provisioning path used for other
  tenants (see how Barclays Plc is currently provisioned), not via
  hand-written SQL.

* Decisions

- *Acme owns everything.* Existing orphaned business-unit/portfolio/
  book/report example fixtures get re-parented under Acme rather than
  staying dangling test data with no real owning party.
- *Integrate, don't invent a world.* Unlike =slovaris=, Acme is not a
  self-contained fictional universe: its own legal entities carry
  fake-but-checksum-valid LEIs (GLEIF's reserved =9695= pre-LOU test
  prefix), but its counterparties are the real GLEIF-imported set —
  the same one Barclays trades against.
- *Business-unit/portfolio/book population was deferred* until
  prime_origin's unified DQ dataset-publish refactor landed, to avoid
  building bespoke seeding that would be thrown away — [[https://github.com/OreStudio/OreStudio/pull/1662][PR #1662]] merged
  2026-07-22 (=risk_management= bundle: business units, portfolios,
  books, and report definitions published together). No longer
  blocked: the generator task should populate Acme's organisational/
  portfolio/book structure through that bundle, not a bespoke path.
- *Three branches* (London, New York, Hong Kong) — enough for
  follow-the-sun trading without needing "the biggest bank."
- *Soft-FK codegen generalised, not counterparty-only.* Attaching a
  logo to a counterparty needed a soft FK to =ores_assets_images_tbl=,
  whose PK column is =image_id= (not =id=, the shape every existing
  soft-FK target used). Extended the =domain_entity= soft-FK codegen
  facet with an optional =:target_column:= FK property (default
  =id=, byte-identical output for every existing entity) rather than
  hand-editing counterparty's generated SQL — the next entity needing
  a differently-keyed soft FK gets it for free.
- *Account pictures*: generalise the existing =ores_assets_images_tbl=
  (currently SVG-only) to a binary-agnostic asset table, rather than
  add a parallel face-images table. Implemented as =mime_type= +
  base64-encoded =data= (text, not =bytea= — no existing pqxx/mapper
  precedent for binary columns in this codebase); the DQ
  artefact-staging table stays untouched SVG-only text, since it's
  inherently a flag/GLEIF-style import pipeline, not a generic image
  store.
- *Account =image_id= is a plain UUID with a nil sentinel, not
  =std::optional=.* A second =std::optional<boost::uuids::uuid>= field
  on =ores::iam::domain::account= triggers a real reflect-cpp
  field-count-detection bug (segfault/corrupted JSON on multi-element
  vectors) specific to that struct; =boost::uuids::nil_uuid()= as
  "unset" is an existing codebase convention and sidesteps it.
- *Four legal entities, each with its own full structure.* Acme
  Corporation Plc (holding), ACME Corporation UK plc, ACME Corporation
  US Inc, ACME Corporation HK Ltd — each needs its own business units, portfolios, books, and
  staff, not just the holding company. Existing publish-from-dq
  functions are single-party-scoped per call (=params.party_id=), so
  giving four entities their own structure means four calls against
  four small per-entity datasets — this is a real, accepted platform
  characteristic, not a bug to route around by modifying the generic
  publish functions.
- *Provisioning architecture, four tasks, clean separation*:
  1. *Source data* — a Python script generates a versioned,
     extensible JSON dataset (=ores.seeder='s manifest/methodology
     convention, internal not =external/=) describing all four
     entities: parties/LEIs, contacts, business units, portfolios,
     books, staff. Config-driven over an office list (adding e.g.
     Singapore later is a config change); the org-chart/book shape
     per office is fixed, employee names/emails/addresses are
     generated (Faker for GB/US, a curated pool for Hong Kong since
     Faker's non-Latin locales don't fit ASCII usernames). All
     generated accounts share one fixed password ("Secure-Password-123")
     — a real scrypt hash computed via =ores::security::crypto::password_hasher=,
     not a placeholder.
  2. *Codegen* — =ores.codegen= (extended if needed) turns that JSON
     into populate SQL matching hand-written =ores.sql= conventions,
     landing DQ artefact rows exactly like any other dataset. Data
     and SQL generation are deliberately separate steps/tasks.
  3. *Server-side orchestration* — the actual missing piece is
     accounts (parties/business-units/portfolios/books already have
     artefact tables and publish functions via the GLEIF-import reuse
     described above): a new accounts DQ artefact table,
     publish-from-dq function, and NATS handler, plus a new
     tenant-provisioning source (e.g. =--source acme=) that
     server-side creates the tenant, imports the LEI hierarchy (all
     four parties in one shot), and loops the per-party
     business-unit/portfolio/book publishes plus the group-wide
     accounts publish — no repeated per-party logins, no
     orchestration logic duplicated client-side.
  4. *Client UX* — =ores.shell=/=ores.qt= just invoke the new
     server-side command; the tenant-creation wizard offers "ACME
     (full sample bank)" vs "Manual" (existing GLEIF/synthetic flow,
     kept — Acme's own party creation depends on it; hiding it behind
     "Manual" is a UI simplification, not a removal).
  Plus a fifth, smaller, independent task: a nullable =image_id= FK
  on counterparties (same pattern as accounts) so a counterparty can
  carry a logo.
- *No account profile pictures in the JSON-generation task.* Deferred
  to whichever task actually populates =image_id= — the schema
  supports it (=generalise-account-images=), wiring FaceStudio
  selection is separate follow-up work.
- *Account contact information (real names) moved earlier*, merged
  into the codegen task rather than scheduled after the whole
  provisioning framework — doing the junction table, Acme contact
  data, and the =ores.qt= notebook page in the same pass as the SQL
  generation avoids a second, separate round of end-to-end retesting.
- *Business-unit/portfolio/book artefact parent/owner columns are
  uuid, self/cross-referential within the dataset* — not natural-key
  codes. =parent_business_unit_id=/=parent_portfolio_id=/
  =owner_unit_id= hold the artefact-space =id= of another row in the
  same (or a matching) dataset; the existing publish functions
  resolve these to real ids via a breadth-first temp-table walk
  matched by name, not by code. =generate_sql.py= builds code→id maps
  from the JSON's own deterministic uuid5 ids to populate these
  columns correctly.
- *Publish-from-dq functions must guard their temp tables and scope
  ref-map lookups by party.* Looping the orchestrator over three
  companies in one transaction surfaced two general bugs: =on commit
  drop= temp tables collide when the same publish function runs more
  than once per transaction (needs a =drop table if exists= guard
  before each =create temp table=), and sibling-dataset ref-map
  lookups (=bu_ref_map=, =portfolio_ref_map=) matched by name only,
  unscoped by party, so identical unit/portfolio names across
  different companies collided. Both are now fixed in
  =refdata_publish_from_dq_create.sql= and apply to any future
  publish-from-dq function, not just Acme's.
- *Existing publish-from-dq functions are hardcoded, not generic,
  and none of them work for Acme's dataset codes yet.*
  =ores_refdata_publish_lei_parties_from_dq_fn= is hardcoded to
  =gleif.lei_entities.<size>=/=gleif.lei_relationships.<size>=
  dataset-code patterns regardless of what dataset triggered it; the
  business-unit/portfolio/book publish functions are single-party-
  scoped and keyed to the =testdata.*= dataset codes. Generalising
  these (or adding Acme-specific equivalents) is squarely the
  server-side-orchestration task's job — no =ores.shell= script for
  Acme can work before that lands, so it isn't attempted here.

* Out of scope

- Retiring or removing Barclays Plc — Acme Corporation supplements it as the
  richer/fully-populated entity; Barclays Plc may still be useful for
  simpler/lighter-weight test scenarios.
- Modelling every possible business unit/portfolio/book combination
  across non-derivative-trading divisions — only derivative-trading
  divisions need the full chain (see Acceptance).
- Actual trade/position data — this story is about the corporate,
  organisational, and staff structure; trade population is a
  follow-on concern.
