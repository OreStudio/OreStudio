#+title: Menu structure
#+description: The one menu of the web shell: the same entries in the same order for every person, an entry offered by the narrowest read its screens need, and the area each screen belongs to, which is the middle of its breadcrumb.
#+type: knowledge
#+level: cross
#+filetags: :web:ux:standards:navigation:
#+created: 2026-10-10
#+updated: 2026-10-10

* Summary

There is one menu and one set of cards. They are the same for every person.
An area is drawn for a person who holds the read that one of its screens needs.
A card is drawn for a person who holds the read of its screen. Nothing in the
menu or in an area is written for a kind of user, because permissions change
over time and a role is only a bundle of them. Every screen belongs to one
area, and that area is the middle of its breadcrumb: Home, the area, the
screen. An area is a topic that a person looks in, such as Reference data, and
never one kind of record. A party and everything about a party is in Reference
data. A person's own pages are in the account menu and not in the menu.

* Detail

** Where this page applies

This page governs the menu in the header of the shell, the cards on each area
page, and the breadcrumb of every screen. A card on Home is a shortcut to a
screen and is not a second home for it.

The [[id:BD492939-118E-4B55-AE5B-300F7A1C3B41][Entity screen specification]] and the [[id:2BF7F03F-6BF4-45FF-8E26-4D3B4E2D943D][Record screen standard]] already say that a
breadcrumb is Home, the area, the list, then the subject, and that the menu
offers an entry by the narrowest read the screen needs. This page names the
areas and says which screen belongs to which.

** Words

| Word | Meaning |
|------+---------|
| /Menu/ | The list of areas in the header. It is one list |
| /Area/ | One entry of the menu, and the page of cards it opens |
| /Card/ | A tile. It is a folder card, a file card or a workflow card |
| /Folder card/ | A card that opens a page of more cards, such as an area. It is named for the area |
| /File card/ | A card that opens one screen. It has the screen's name, word for word, in every language |
| /Workflow card/ | A card that starts a workflow. Its title is a verb and its object. It has its own hue and a tag |
| /CRUD command/ | Add, Edit or Delete: one record, one write, in a dialog. It is a button on a record list or page, never a card |
| /Workflow/ | A guided sequence of steps with several writes, with a step rail. It is not a CRUD command. A user journey is the design document of one, and is not a name for a screen |
| /Read/ | The permission a screen needs. It is the only thing that decides what is drawn |
| /Account menu/ | The person's own pages and signing out. It is not part of the menu |

** Folder cards and file cards

A card is like an entry in a file manager.

- A /folder card/ opens a page of cards. The areas are folders. A folder card
  has the name of its area and a line about what is in it.
- A /file card/ opens one screen. Its title is the title of the screen, word for
  word, in English, French and Portuguese. Its line is a short form of the
  screen's own lead. It has the glyph of the screen.

A screen has one definition of its card (=shell/cards.ts=). The area page and
Home both draw from it, so a screen has the same name, line and glyph wherever it
is offered. A screen that has no header of its own, such as a journey, takes its
title, its line and its trail from its file card (=CardScreen=). A test holds
the card title and the screen title equal in every language.

Home's Active modules tab shows one folder card for each area the person is
offered, in the order of the menu, and then the cards of their own account. It
does not repeat the file cards of the areas. A person opens a folder to reach its
screens.

*** Workflows

A /workflow/ is a guided run of steps. Onboard a party, Onboard a counterparty,
Shape the book structure and Define instrument conventions are workflows. They
are not CRUD commands, and the two are not given each other's names.

A workflow is kept only where the guidance has a value that an ordinary edit does
not: Onboard a party provisions a hierarchy of parties from a legal entity, links
the accounts that work in them and publishes their data. Correcting a party is
not such a task. A party is changed on its own page, with an Edit button on each
card, as a currency is.

- A workflow is listed on the screen of the thing it acts on. The Parties list
  has a Workflows menu in its header with the three party workflows. A person
  who looks at the parties finds how to onboard one there.
- A workflow that has no such screen has a workflow card on its area page.
- A workflow is never listed in both places.
- A workflow card is teal and carries the tag Workflow, so it is not mistaken
  for a screen card. The menu on a screen lists the workflows the person may
  start, and is not drawn when there are none.
- The trail of a workflow is Home, the area, the workflow: Home › Reference data
  › Onboard a party.

*** The area page

An area page is the cards of its area and nothing else: no headings and no
groups. The cards are dense, with the glyph beside the name and one line, so the
page fits on a screen. The screens come first, then the workflow cards.

** The rule

- The menu is one list, in one order. It has no version for a mode, a role or
  a kind of user.
- An area is drawn when the person holds the read that any one of its screens
  needs. If the person holds none, the area is not drawn. It is not disabled.
- An area page shows the same cards to everyone. A card is drawn when the person
  holds the read of its screen. If they do not, the card is not drawn.
- No area, card or screen is named for a role. A role is a set of permissions
  that changes. The screen names the read it needs, and the read decides.
- A new screen is added to the area it belongs to. It then appears for every
  person who holds its read, with no change to the menu.
- The breadcrumb of a screen is Home, then its area, then the screen. A record
  page adds the list and the subject, as the entity specification says.
- A screen has one area. A card on Home or in another area leads to it, and the
  screen still shows the trail of its own area.

** Why the menu was confusing

The menu had seven entries, and they were of three different kinds.

- Some were a kind of record: Parties.
- Some were a body of data: Reference data.
- Some were a question the person asks: Requests, Operations.

A person cannot guess where a screen is when the entries answer different
questions. Parties was drawn beside Reference data, but the party journeys are
reference data: a party is made of identifiers, contacts, countries, currencies
and business centres, all of which are reference data. So a person who looked in
Reference data for a party did not find it.

Four other faults made it worse.

- The same screen had two addresses, such as =/classifications= and
  =/refdata/classifications=, and =/hierarchy= and =/reporting-lines=.
- Home repeated the screens as cards with other names, so one screen had two
  labels.
- A person's own pages (=/access=, =/where-i-work=, =/profile=, =/security=) were
  mixed with the pages about other people.
- Some areas were named for a record kind and others for a topic, so the menu
  had no one rule for what an entry is.

** The rule for an area

An area is a /topic/ that a person goes to, and its name is the journey group
that [[id:D602E114-F896-42CA-893A-841AD6FC3221][User Journeys]] uses for it. It is never one kind of record. A kind of
record is a card inside the area whose topic it belongs to.

The test for a screen is one question: which topic would a person look in?

| Topic | The question the person asks |
|-------+------------------------------|
| Reference data | What are the things the work is made of? |
| Organisation | Who is here, and what may they do? |
| Trades | What does the tenant hold, and what happens to it? |
| Requests | What is waiting for an answer? |
| Operations | How is the installation running, and who has signed in? |
| Development | What is the team doing to the product, and what can a tester run? |
| Tenants | Which tenants does this installation hold? |

** The areas

#+begin_src text
  Home   Reference data   Organisation   Market data   Trades   Requests   Operations   Development   Tenants
#+end_src

| Area | Screens | Drawn when the person holds the read of |
|------+---------+------------------------------------------|
| Home | The dashboard, Active modules, Upcoming modules | Always |
| Reference data | The cards below | Always |
| Organisation | Staff, Hierarchy, Roles and Rescue access | Any one of those screens |
| Market data | The prices and curves the platform receives or generates: the FX price feeds and the curve evolution | Any one of those screens |
| Trades | Trades, and Structures and packages, with the workflows Import a portfolio from ORE, Export a portfolio to ORE and Group trades | Any one of those screens |
| Requests | Every request, a person's own, and the queue of those who decide | Always, because every person has their own |
| Operations | Running services, Compute grid, Message bus, Telemetry logs, Versions and the database, and Sign-ins | Any one of those screens |
| Development | The tests a tester can run, the board and the backlog, and the documents they open | Always |
| Tenants | The tenants, their parties and people, and onboarding | Any tenant screen |

Requests is the one entry that is not a topic. It is an inbox: the work that
waits for a person, with the notification bell beside it. It is not in
Operations, which says how the system is running, and not in Organisation,
because four-eyes approval will cover reference data as well as access. Every
person has their own requests, so the entry is drawn for everyone.

Tenants is last because it serves only the installation, and it is drawn only
for a session that acts on the deployment (see [[*How today's menu differs][How today's menu differs]]).

*** Reference data

| Cards | Journeys |
|-------+----------|
| Parties, with the workflows Onboard a party and Onboard a counterparty | [[id:86CB724A-66E3-4FB3-A943-3CE22D41213A][Parties]], and new party in [[id:F1C4E8A2-9D3B-4F6E-8A17-2C5B9E4D7A30][Setup]] |
| Currencies, Currency pairs, Desk groups | [[id:9D60A92B-A65F-4474-896C-1C1E751FDEA3][Currencies and FX]] |
| Holiday calendars | [[id:A38B168A-8F37-4D8D-A99D-C485E32B9DCE][Calendars]], [[id:296089DE-83BF-4493-9DD1-E265A168773D][Tenors]] |
| Classifications | [[id:85930439-D6B2-4811-81FB-C28DD3F45F75][Classifications]] |
| The workflows Shape the book structure and Define instrument conventions | [[id:A08424B7-E6D7-4993-955C-53AEC241ABA5][Books and portfolios]], [[id:72375ADC-BD12-4821-AE1C-C895EB084872][Conventions]] |

A party and everything about a party is reference data. None of it has an entry
in the menu.

Operations is not an area of one kind of user. It holds every screen that says
how the installation or the tenant is running. Each screen in it needs its own
read, so a person sees the cards they may open. Versions needs no read,
because every person must be able to report a problem to IT and says which
build they use. So the Operations area is drawn for every person. A later screen
that serves only some people is added to Operations and guarded by its read. It
does not get an area of its own.

*** Trades

| Cards | Journeys |
|-------+----------|
| Trades, with the Book a trade button, and the workflows Import a portfolio from ORE, Export a portfolio to ORE and Group trades | [[id:1B23A456-EC3C-4C5F-AE3A-34F21E7C7B33][Trade journeys]] |
| Structures and packages | [[id:1B23A456-EC3C-4C5F-AE3A-34F21E7C7B33][Trade journeys]], and [[id:FC31FC9C-7A6E-4EFD-8A62-6DE4137F7160][Trade Structures and Deal Composition]] |

A trade is an entity, so it has a list and a page of its own and is not a card
of Reference data. Trades is its own area because the question a person asks of
it differs from the question they ask of reference data: reference data is what
the work is made of, and a trade is what the tenant holds. A structure is the
tree of a deal, and a package is a structure of kind Package, so one screen
serves both. The Trade entry prototype named its subject a structure and sat
under Books and portfolios. It now sits with the other prototypes of this area:
see the [[id:741FE353-C420-41C1-913D-EA99A73754CA][Trades prototypes]].

*Book a trade* is the Add of the trade list: a button in the list header that
opens the new-trade page. It is not a workflow, because the page has no step
rail. It is one page in tabs, and the [[id:BD492939-118E-4B55-AE5B-300F7A1C3B41][Entity screen specification]] says how.

** The account menu

The account menu stands in the header with the notification bell. It holds My
profile, My access, Where I work, Security and signing out. These are not
areas. A person reaches each in one place, as the entity specification says.
Each has a breadcrumb of Home, then the page, because it belongs to no area.

Every screen has a breadcrumb. Home is the one page without one, because it is
the first step of every trail.

| Screen | Area | Why |
|--------+------+-----|
| Parties, and its workflows Onboard a party and Onboard a counterparty | Reference data | A party is reference data. The journeys group it with the countries, currencies and business centres it connects to |
| Shape the book structure, Define instrument conventions | Reference data | They are workflows on shared lists |
| Currencies, Currency pairs, Desk groups, Holiday calendars, Classifications | Reference data | They are the shared lists |
| Staff, Hierarchy | Organisation | They are about the people of the tenant |
| Roles | Organisation | A role is held by a person |
| Rescue access | Organisation | It acts on one person's account |
| Sign-ins | Operations | It reads sessions and failed sign-ins. It is the Audit of how the installation is used |
| Tests, Board, Backlog | Development | They are the work a team does on the product, not how an installation runs |
| Versions and the database | Operations | They state the build and the store |
| Requests | Requests | It is the one place for every kind of request |
| FX price feeds | Market data | A feed generates the prices that market data holds |
| Trades, Book a trade, Import a portfolio from ORE, Export a portfolio to ORE | Trades | A trade is held by the tenant and is not a shared list |
| Structures and packages, Group trades | Trades | A structure is the tree of a deal made of trades |
| Tenants | Tenants | The roster of the installation |

** How today's menu differs

The menu, the Reference data cards, the addresses and the cards follow this
page. One difference is left.

| Where | Today | The rule |
|-------+-------+----------|
| Tenants, and the Services, Grid, Message queue and Telemetry cards | Drawn only where the session acts on the deployment (the =installation= scope), because the server guards these reads by the session mode | Drawn by a read |

** Open points

- The installation screens read the whole installation, and the server guards
  them by the session mode. They need a read of their own, so that the read
  decides who sees them. A tenant's administrator holds every read, so a read
  that the wildcard grants would show them the installation. The new read must
  be one that the wildcard of a tenant does not grant.
- Whether Rescue access belongs in Organisation, because it acts on a person, or
  in Operations with Sign-ins, because it answers a problem with signing in.
  This page puts it in Organisation.
- The Trades area has journeys and prototypes and no web screens yet. The BFF has
  no trading route, and the trade list can filter only by trade id until the
  model declares the columns a person searches by.
- Market data (curves, surfaces and their feeds) has journeys and no web
  screens yet. When it has screens it may need an area of its own, because it is
  data that changes by the minute and not a list that changes by the month.

** Changing the menu

Work in this order. Each step ends in something to check.

1. Name the screen and the narrowest read it needs.
2. Choose its one area by asking which topic a person would look in. If none
   fits, add the topic here first. Never add an area for one kind of record.
3. Add the screen to the area page as a card, drawn only for a person who holds
   the read.
4. Give the screen a breadcrumb of Home, the area and the screen.
5. Check it with a person who holds the read and a person who does not. The
   first sees the card. The second sees neither the card nor, when it was the
   only one, the area.
