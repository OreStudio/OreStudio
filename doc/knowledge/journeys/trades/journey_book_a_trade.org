This page documents a [[id:F44766FA-1E9C-416F-849A-E131101B2ECA][user journey]]: A trader records a new deal: its trade type, its party and counterparty, its book, and its instrument terms.

A user journey describes what one person is trying to do, before anything is
built. It is a design document that outlives any sprint. These trade journeys are
use cases, so they name the operations and messages each step needs and leave the
screens to the UX standards.

* Goal

"I have agreed a deal and I want the firm to hold it exactly as agreed, in the right
book, against the right counterparty, so that it is valued, confirmed and reported from
the first day." The journey is done when the trade reads back with its instrument, its
booking, its first state and the activity that created it.

* Actor and trigger

The actor is a trader, or an operations user booking for a trader, inside one party of
the tenant. The trigger is a deal agreed by voice, by chat or on a venue, or a draft the
trader wants to capture before the deal is agreed. The journey starts with the choice of
a trade type and ends when the trade is booked, saved as a draft, or refused.

* Steps

1. *Choose the trade type.* The person names the kind of deal. The system offers the
   trade types from the catalogue. The trade type decides which instrument terms the deal
   has, because each trade type routes to one instrument table.
2. *Say who is on the deal.* The person names the party, the counterparty and the book.
   The system offers only the counterparties the party may trade with. A book must be a
   real book, unless the trade is a pre-agreement draft, a test or a hypothetical.
3. *Say how it was struck.* The person gives the trade date, the execution time, the
   booking nature, the entry channel and the counterparty scope. The system offers the
   values each lookup holds.
4. *Enter the instrument terms.* The person gives the terms the trade type needs:
   notional, dates, rates, legs and so on. The system shows the fields for that trade
   type and no others.
5. *Add what identifies and classifies the trade.* The person adds identifiers (the
   firm's own, a venue's, ORE's trade id), party roles, portfolios and additional fields.
   All are optional.
6. *Choose live or draft.* The person books a live trade (activity =new_booking=) or
   captures a draft (activity =draft_capture=).
7. *Confirm.* The system writes the anchor, the booking, the first state and the
   activity in one transaction. The system then writes the instrument. It reports the
   trade, its state and its version.
8. *Handle a refusal.* The system refuses a trade id that is already booked, a book that
   is not allowed, and a field that fails the trade type's checks. The system keeps what
   the person entered and says what was refused.

* Screens

This journey is a use case. It has no screen of its own. Booking starts from the trade
list and opens a new trade page, which is the page of one trade in the
[[id:BD492939-118E-4B55-AE5B-300F7A1C3B41][Entity screen specification]]. The page holds the trade's cards (parties and book, how it was struck,
instrument terms, identifiers) and the person fills them in. A guided sequence of
steps is the one allowed exception in that specification, and it is allowed only if it
makes booking easier or quicker. A prototype decides that. The prototype must also keep
the trade as the subject: the person must be able to leave the sequence and land on the
same trade page.

The [[id:741FE353-C420-41C1-913D-EA99A73754CA][Trades prototypes]] draw it in =book-a-trade=, as a page and as a sequence. No wireframe is drawn here. The prototype story draws the screens, from the
[[id:00BA1689-B060-4BD8-BC46-67034D663B7F][UX standards]] and from this use case.

| Step | Operation | Subject | Status |
|------+-----------+---------+--------|

| Say how | Offer booking natures, entry channels and counterparty scopes | =trading.v1.booking_nature_types.list=, =trading.v1.entry_channel_types.list=, =trading.v1.counterparty_scope_types.list= | exists |

| Identify | Write identifiers, party roles, portfolios and additional fields | =trading.v1.trade_identifiers.put_many=, =trade_party_roles.put_many=, =trade_portfolios.put_many=, =trade_additional_fields.put_many= | exists |
| Confirm | Book the anchor, the booking, the first state and the activity | =trading.v1.ops.book_trade= | partial |
| Confirm | Write the instrument and its child rows | =trading.v1.<instrument>s.put=, then each child table's =put_many= | partial |

Status is one of =exists=, =partial=, or =missing=. A subject that contains
=<instrument>= stands for the one instrument table the trade type routes to. The plural
is written with a plain =s=. A table whose plural is irregular, such as
=trade_activities=, has its own subject: check the protocol header, not the pattern.

* What is missing

- =book_trade= does not write the instrument. A trade is booked, and for a moment has no
  instrument, until a second write. One operation must write both, or the order and the
  repair of a half-booked trade must be defined.
- No operation tells a client which fields a trade type needs. The client would have to
  read the model. A catalogue read would remove that.
- The web app and the BFF have no trading route. Nothing here is reachable from a browser
  yet.
- The four-eyes rule for a trade, if any, is not stated for booking. The activity types
  carry a =requires_confirmation= flag, which is the counterparty's confirmation and not an
  internal approval. No journey covers the counterparty's confirmation or Operations'
  authorisation of a new live trade. The story [[id:DFA36716-D1F7-47F5-833C-A5EBC48198B3][Build the four-eyes framework]] owns any escalation.
