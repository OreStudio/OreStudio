This structure note gathers the cuts cluster. It states the definition, the body
a cut must hold, the components the body divides into, and the order in which to
read the pages. The classification of kinds is a page of its own. Return to
[[id:7474935C-95D1-3034-B833-83DCD9FB3A09][Knowledge]].

* The definition

A *cut* fixes a body of data at one timepoint. Three parts follow, and each
answers a separate question.

- The *label* names the cut. A code, a reference date or a version serves as the
  handle that a report, a deal or a book carries.
- The *timepoint* fixes the cut. It is the instant held still, with its timezone.
- The *object* is the body of data the cut fixes. It may be a deal population, a
  market data set, both, or anything else a valuation reads.

The label answers which cut is meant. The timepoint answers when it holds. The
object answers what it holds. A statement that omits one of the three leaves the
cut unidentified, and a claim about a cut carries the three together.

The sentence fixing the definition constrains two things. Something is fixed, and
one instant fixes it. Every further property of a cut is a fact about the kind:
how often the instant recurs, which location owns it, and which process takes it.
Those facts qualify a cut without entering the definition, and
[[id:33E879B7-496A-406D-B3F2-72637379AD02][Kinds of cut]] works out what
follows from that.

* The kinds

A kind states what a cut is for, and the purpose fixes the body and the instant.
The classification, and the argument for it, is on
[[id:33E879B7-496A-406D-B3F2-72637379AD02][Kinds of cut]], which places each
kind under its purpose and relates it to the components below.

* The body a cut must hold

The definition fixes a body of data without saying how large that body is. The
size follows from the purpose, and for an EOD cut the purpose is to reproduce an
official valuation.

The argument has three premises. A valuation is determined by its inputs. Two
runs over the same deals and the same quotes produce different numbers when the
day count, the calendar or the valuation settings differ. Those settings are
therefore inputs to the valuation in the same sense that the quotes are. It
follows that a cut holding the deals and the quotes while leaving the settings
free cannot reproduce the valuation it claims to have produced.

An EOD cut therefore holds four parts.

- The *deals*. The population the valuation values.
- The *market data*. Spots, curves, forward points, volatility surfaces,
  fixings, and the model parameters that are driven by the market.
- The *reference data*. The conventions, the calendars and holiday sets, the
  valuation and pricing settings, the curve configurations, and anything else
  the run reads to turn a deal and a quote into a number.
- The *identity and the authorisation*. The label, the timepoint, the signatory
  and the version.

A cut that holds the first two alone answers the question "which deals and which
rates", and reproducibility requires the wider question, "everything this run
read". The narrower object is the object of a component, and the component pages
below treat it as such.

* Components and their order

The body divides into two components, the deals and the market data, and the
division has consequences for the process that fixes them.

The components differ in owner, in review and in mode of failure. A deal is
reviewed by reading it and comparing it to what the desk booked, and it fails by
being absent or wrong. A rate is reviewed by comparing it to an external source,
and it fails by being stale or unapproved. A corrected deal moves no rate, and
an approved rate moves no deal. One act that fixed both would tie every trade
correction to a fresh approval of the day's rates, and would therefore slow the
correction the review exists to serve.

Our working convention follows from that. The two components are fixed by
separate acts, the deals first and the market data second. The deal cut is
retaken until the population is clean, because a person may still correct a deal
in production. The market data cut follows, since a provisional market data set
is needed to value the deals and confirm they are right before the day's rates are
committed.

* Reading order

The classification comes first, because purpose fixes the body and the instant a
kind contributes. The component pages then take the definition in the two
directions the body admits, and the last page takes the same definition into the
expiry sense of the word.

** The classification

[[id:33E879B7-496A-406D-B3F2-72637379AD02][Kinds of cut]] — what a cut is for,
placed under the four purposes in use, with what each kind fixes, the attributes
its purpose gives it, and how each relates to the components.

** The market data component

[[id:DD59B04C-DAD2-458C-B35E-3F6B0B3D6DC6][Market data cut]] — the values a
valuation reads at the timepoint: quotes, curves, surfaces and fixings, the
approved rates applied on top, the snapshot that fixes them, the published sets
and their provenance, and what our system holds today.

** The deal component

[[id:19630B5D-A163-42B5-B9C4-61A87BAE8F5F][Trade cut]] — the population a
valuation values at the timepoint: which deals, the state each holds, the book
each belongs to, the corrections applied, and what our system holds today.

** The expiry sense of the word

[[id:3B39B2E2-6E05-4DF8-B68B-6ACA12F2FCE9][The option-expiry cut code]] — the
definition applied to expiry: a label for the time of day at which an option
expires on its expiry date, fixing an instant rather than a population.

* What the sources establish

Three claims in this cluster have a public source, and each is recorded on the
page that makes it. The admissible sources of an independent price verification
rest on a prudential standard
([[https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/101/chapter/I][EBA RTS (EU) 2016/101]], Article 3), and the stated open and close of a
centre's business day rests on one settlement system's published hours
([[https://www.federalreserve.gov/paymentsystems/fedfunds_about.htm][Fedwire Funds Service]]); both appear in
[[id:33E879B7-496A-406D-B3F2-72637379AD02][Kinds of cut]]. That an official rate
is published once per working day at a stated time rests on the central bank
reference rates
([[https://www.ecb.europa.eu/stats/policy_and_exchange_rates/euro_reference_exchange_rates/html/index.en.html][ECB euro foreign exchange reference rates]]), which appears in
[[id:DD59B04C-DAD2-458C-B35E-3F6B0B3D6DC6][Market data cut]].

Everything else is a definition of a term or our working convention. The order
of the two component acts, the existence of the flash and start of day kinds, the
numbered end of day sequence, one cut per book, the after-hours rule, the
arbitrary instant a process may name, and the times that cut codes name have no
public source we could verify. They are written here as the convention we work
to. Practice at a particular firm is a matter for that firm's own record.

* What this cluster does not cover

The pricing settings themselves, the engine's configuration documents, and the
reads served over stored market data belong to other clusters. This cluster
states what a cut must hold and why, and leaves the meaning of each setting to
the page that owns it.
