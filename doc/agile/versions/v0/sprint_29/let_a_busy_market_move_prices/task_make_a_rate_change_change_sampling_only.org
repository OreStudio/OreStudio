* Goal

A rate change changes how often a series is observed and not how much time its
process covers, for every process the feeds use. An operator who doubles a rate
gets twice the samples of the same path, not twice the realized volatility.

* Acceptance

- Variance per wall-clock second is invariant under a rate multiplier of two,
  measured on a long run, for every process a feed can build.
- The three processes that answer =supports_variable_step() == true= keep their
  behaviour: the Gaussian mixture, the arithmetic mixture and the
  Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process.
- The processes that answer false gain a time-scaled law, so a feed can hand them
  the gap. They are converted, not refused: refusing would break every feed that
  runs today.
- A non-periodic arrival mode is still refused for any process that cannot take a
  gap, as it is today.
- The existing time-rescaling test stays green, and the new test names the process
  it measures.

* Plan

1. Enumerate the processes and their =supports_variable_step()= answer; the list
   is the test's parameter.
2. Give each process that answers false a law in elapsed time, keeping its
   existing parameters and its one-tick behaviour exact.
3. Add the invariance test: the same seed, the same window, one run at the
   configured rate and one at twice it, and the two realize the same variance per
   second within sampling error.

* Result

* Finding, before any code

The task was raised against the claim that a rate change moves time. Measuring the
class first shows the claim is half wrong, and the half that is wrong is the half
this task would have spent effort on.

- *Spot feeds are already time-scaled.* The three processes that take a gap are
  the Gaussian mixture, the arithmetic mixture and the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process,
  and those are exactly what the three FX engines build: "geometric" is the
  mixture, "arithmetic" the variant, "ornstein_uhlenbeck" the mean-reverting one.
  A rate change on a spot feed already changes sampling only.
- *Every other price process is a curve engine*, and all of them answer
  =supports_variable_step() == false=. A curve feed steps one **simulated calendar
  day per tick** by design, so its rate is a calendar speed — one a minute means
  one simulated day a minute — and not a sampling rate at all.

So the two feed classes use one word for two quantities. That is a real problem,
but it is a naming and a decision problem, not a bug in the spot path, and a
volatility loading — which belongs to the price law — is unaffected by it.

Measured at =4b49b4ef2e=: 13 price processes, 3 time-scaled (the FX engines), 10
one-tick (the curve engines).

* Proposed re-scope

Replace this task with one that decides and states the curve convention, in the
model, the config and the screen: either a curve keeps a calendar-speed rate and
both surfaces say "one simulated day per tick", or curves move to sampling like
spot feeds, which changes what a curve's configuration means and needs its own
migration story for existing rows. Until that is decided, no code changes here,
and the story's second task (the volatility loading) proceeds on its own because
it applies to spot feeds.
