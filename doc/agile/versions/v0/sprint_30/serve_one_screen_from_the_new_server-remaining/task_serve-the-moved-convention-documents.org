* Plan

The two routes extend =convention_screen_routes=, because the family table, the
404 that names a family and the flattener for a grouped wire row already live
there; a second unit would restate the table and the routes would disagree about
which families exist. The table gains two per-family functions: one that reads a
family's row by its identifier, and one that writes its terms.

The terms are data, not wiring: =convention_kind_term= states each kind's terms
by entity, column, schema and XPath, and the ORE parameter contract and the value
sets fill in the words and the values. A composite family's terms live on its
parts, so the parts the row actually holds choose the terms.

The write changes the columns the caller names on the row it reads here, in the
column's own type, and sends the whole row under =must_match_version=. A stale
version is answered as the service's conflict with 200, because the screen reads
the result's outcome and not the status, which is what the BFF did.

The new units are =convention_terms= (the join and the refusal naming a term),
plus the shared =wire_cells= and =catalogue_choices=, which the curve
configuration routes needed first.

* Result


Done. The reference data part answers both convention document routes at the
BFF's paths, member names and status codes, and the reference data area has no
unpaired route left.

** The two routes

- =GET /api/conventions/:family/documents/:id= answers one family's convention as
  the record page reads it: its family, the kind the address stands for, the party
  that holds it, its version, and its terms, each with the value the row holds, the
  words the ORE parameter contract gives it and the values its set accepts. A
  composite family's parts are read for the same key and laid over the central row,
  and only the terms of the form the row holds are drawn.
- =PUT /api/conventions/:family/documents/:id/terms= changes the terms the caller
  names, writes every term the row holds and sends the row under the version the
  caller read. A composite family is a 400, because its terms are changed on its
  parts.

** The decisions worth keeping

- The routes extend the convention screen's unit rather than a new one: the family
  table and the 404 that names a family are already there, and two tables would
  disagree the first time a family is added.
- A stale version is the service's conflict result with 200, not a 409, because the
  screen reads =result.outcome= and puts the refusal on the page it drew.
- A term the store refuses carries the store's sentence and is put on that term's
  column, by the wording the validation function raises.
- Two families, the tenors and the currency pairs, state no filter by identifier,
  so their document read filters the rows it reads. Their ids are identifiers and
  their lists are small.

** What is not proved

The routes are proved against a client that is not connected, so their refusals
are covered: an unknown family, a malformed identifier, a malformed party, an
unknown query parameter, a composite write, a family with no write, and a body the
route cannot read. Their happy paths, and the conflict and the term-level refusal,
need a live estate, which this task does not raise.
