This page documents a [[id:31C868B9-306E-4282-BA8C-07E647021B34][task]] in the [[id:F2F266AE-8B43-41DD-89AF-7A22D52D1C74][Derive term indices from their overnight curves]] story. It captures the goal, current status, acceptance, and any notes or results.

* Goal

A provider names the publisher that owns an item: Bloomberg for a ticker, the ECB for a reference rate, WM/Reuters for a fix. Being computed rather than observed is not a publisher, yet the store holds a provider called derived, which states both that we made the value and, by omission, that no one owns the item it belongs to.

The confusion is visible where it costs most. USD SOFR 1M is an index CME Group administers; we compute it from our own overnight curve, and the row names derived, so a reader cannot tell whose index it is and the real publisher cannot replace it by rank because the rank belongs to another provider. The same holds for the 2,280 derived FX volatility quotes and for the 194 series and 2,612 fixings already filed under derived.

The annotation already exists and is unused for this purpose. market_series.derivation_kind says whether a series is OBSERVED or produced by a named derivation (IR_CURVE_BOOTSTRAP, FX_VOL_DERIVATION, FX_VOL_SURFACE_CALIBRATION), and observation_lineage records the point's source kind and the configuration that produced it. A derived value should be a value under the provider that owns the item, marked by that annotation, so a real source of the same item replaces it by rank, as it does for every other provider.

This contradicts the page that blesses a derived source of our own (Derivation of FX volatility quotes from historical fixings: publishes under its own identity, so a vendor of the same quotes replaces it by rank). The task settles which of the two statements the store follows.

* Decisions

** The three term indices are the administrators', not the run's

USD SOFR 1M is CME Group's index, IBR 3M is Banco de la República's and WIBOR 6M
is GPW Benchmark's. The catalogue now installs CME and GPW as publishers (GPW
returns, having left when WIBOR had no source to state it), and each index is
stated under its administrator by that administrator's simulated source, in the
administrator's notation. The derivation is the series' =derivation_kind=, which
says =IR_CURVE_BOOTSTRAP= and names the generation config and version; it is
seeded before any fixing lands, so a reader never meets the series as observed.

A published collection stands for one provider, so the three indices left the one
derived dataset for three: =synthetic.feeds.cme=, =synthetic.feeds.banrep= and
=synthetic.feeds.gpw=.

** The FX volatility quotes stay under =derived=, for now

The 35 series and 2,275 instruments that remain under =derived= are the FX
volatility quotes inferred from the pairs' stored fixings. No party in the store
owns the item: it is our inference of a quote no vendor we hold states. Filing it
under =ore= would be false, because ORE's example files quote other pairs, and
under =composite= it would be false in another way, because that provider derives
from quotes rather than from fixings. The architecture is explicit that such a
value is internal and does not become a series at all, and the store cannot say
that yet: the producer and the standing are the subject of
[[id:142FDA27-36E6-4C0D-95F2-6FAE223FC5E3][Split the publisher of a market data value from its producer]],
which states that this task re-files the items whose owner exists. So the
provider survives this task for those quotes alone, its fixing source is gone,
and the re-file of the quotes is a task of that story.

* Plan

1. *Inventory and ownership.* List every series, vendor id and source filed under
   =derived=, and name the publisher that owns each item: CME Group for Term
   SOFR, Banco de la República for IBR, GPW Benchmark for WIBOR, and for the FX
   volatility quotes the vendor whose quote the item stands for. Where no
   publisher owns the item, decide between the provider =ore=, which the bundle
   already installs for the platform's own values, and deletion.
2. *The annotation.* =market_series.derivation_kind= already says how a series
   was made (=OBSERVED=, =IR_CURVE_BOOTSTRAP=, =FX_VOL_DERIVATION=,
   =FX_VOL_SURFACE_CALIBRATION=), and =observation_lineage= already records the
   point's source kind and the configuration behind it. Add what is missing: the
   field on the reads that answer a series, and the line on the screen that shows
   one.
3. *Re-file.* Move the series and the vendor instruments to the owning provider,
   and retire the sources =derived-a= and =derived-fx-vol=, or rename them as
   sources of that provider. The 19 fixing series and the 175 FX series move
   together.
4. *Rank.* Keep the source ranking that lets a real source replace a derived
   value, and prove it with a test that adds a live source of a derived item.
5. *The contradiction.* Settle it against
   [[id:099697A6-8526-48E0-A63D-DB3932C06468][Derivation of FX volatility quotes from historical fixings]],
   which says a derived source publishes under its own identity, and update
   whichever page states the losing rule.
6. *Proof.* A fresh provision with no =derived= provider, every series naming its
   owner, its derivation visible in the API and on a screen, and the rank
   replacement working.

* Result

The fixings no longer name a mechanism. Term SOFR 1M is stated by CME Group, IBR
3M by Banco de la República and WIBOR 6M by GPW Benchmark, each in the
administrator's own notation, each from that administrator's simulated source.
The run computed the values, and the series say so: =derivation_kind= is
=IR_CURVE_BOOTSTRAP= and the series names the generation config and version
behind it. A fresh provision shows three such series, 520 fixings each, and no
fixing series under a provider called =derived=.

The catalogue grew the two publishers the indices needed. CME Group is new, and
GPW Benchmark returns: it left this catalogue when WIBOR had no source that could
state it, and it has one now. Because a published collection stands for one
provider, the three indices left the single derived dataset for three, one for
each administrator.

The screen is where a reader meets the answer. The observations list prints
"computed: ir curve bootstrap" under the provider that owns the index, and the
series page names how the value was made beside the publisher. Both say
=observed= for a series a source stated, so the two kinds of value are told
apart without reading a provider name.

What this task could not finish is the other half of =derived=: the thirty-five
FX volatility series and 2,275 instruments the run infers from stored fixings.
No party in the store owns those items, and the architecture says such a value is
internal and never becomes a series, which the store cannot say until a value has
a producer beside its publisher and a standing. That is captured as
[[file:doc/agile/product_backlog/inbox/the-fx-volatility-quotes-have-no-owner-to-file-them-under.org][The FX volatility quotes have no owner to file them under]],
and the provider survives for them alone, with its fixing source gone.

The rank replacement the acceptance asks for was not built: a computed fixing is
stated by the administrator's simulated source, and a live source of the same
item would win by the route resolution the store already applies. No test proves
that for a computed item, which is captured as
[[file:doc/agile/product_backlog/inbox/no-test-proves-a-live-source-replaces-a-computed-fixing.org][No test proves a live source of a computed item replaces the simulated one]].
