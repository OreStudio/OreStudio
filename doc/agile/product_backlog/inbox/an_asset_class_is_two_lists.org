* What

An asset class is two lists, not one, and the second is much the looser of the
two.

The *product asset class taxonomy* is the authoritative one. It is
runtime-managed in =ores_refdata_asset_class_codes_tbl= -- =fx=,
=interest_rates=, =credit=, =equity=, =commodity=, =inflation=, =bond= -- and
every market series, feed binding, classification rule and instrument code
validates against it through =ores_refdata_validate_asset_class_code_fn=. Adding
a code there is what makes it usable by a series.

The *market data namespace* is a different axis: the key grammar an =oresmd_uri=
authority names, holding =ir=, =credit=, =equity=, =commodity=, =fx=,
=inflation=, =correlation=, =security=, =shape_profile=, =rating= and =power=. It
accreted from ORE's own key-prefix conventions rather than from any product
taxonomy, so it carries members no product list would have.

The two share five spellings and diverge on the rest, so they read as one list
with typos. They are not. =SHAPE_PROFILE= is classed =commodity= and =RATING= is
classed =credit= in the product taxonomy, yet both are authorities of their own
in the namespace. =ir= and =security= are the namespace's spellings of
=interest_rates= and =bond=. ORE writes power fixings as =POWER-= while a power
trade is a commodity, so a power market series is classed =commodity= and carries
=power= in its URI.

Neither list should be extended without saying which is meant. A new oresmd
authority does not by itself become a product asset class. When a market series
needs a class it takes a refdata code, and the namespace lives in the URI.

The correct modelling of the two is left open on purpose:
[[id:47B2C888-A936-4CF9-8629-CADE44C6310E][Decide the modelling of the two asset class lists]] holds the analysis, deferred
until the fixing identities, the classification rules and the consolidation of
the hand-written copies have solidified.

* Why

Nothing in the repository declares the mapping between the two lists, so reading
one as the other is the default mistake, and it produces wrong identities and
wrong classifications. The single =FIXING= classification rule already
mis-classifies every non-rates fixing because of it: it classifies all index
fixings as =interest_rates=, while the corpus carries FX, equity, inflation,
commodity, power and security fixings as well.

The distinction is easy to miss because the overlap is large and the divergent
members look like spelling errors. It was nearly missed while adding the power
identity, where the question "is power a commodity" has two different answers
depending on which list is meant.

Both lists are also hand-copied in more than forty places, and the copies have
already drifted, so a change to either list has no single place to land.
