* Plan

Seven routes in six views. The inventory states what each one weaves and how
long its answer is kept, and that statement is the design: it is not decided
again here.

| View | Route | Weaves | Kept |
|------+-------+--------+------|
| the run's progress | GET /api/workflow/:instanceId | one read of the run's steps, which is already the shape the rail renders | fresh for 5 s |
| the request story | GET /api/requests/:id/story | the request's versions, the decisions taken on it, the roles it asked for and the notices, or the change lines of its kind beside the live record | fresh for 15 s |
| the request changes | GET /api/requests/:id/changes | the change lines of the request's kind beside the live record | fresh for 15 s |
| the approval inbox | GET /api/requests | every kind of request this person may decide, gathered into one queue with each kind's subject | fresh for 15 s |
| the authorisation queue | GET /api/queue/authorisations | the queue for this viewer, each open and answered request joined to the deal it names | not kept |
| the request checks | GET /api/requests/:id/checks | each proposed line run through the real write against the record as it stands | fresh for 30 s |
| one subject's story | GET /api/timeline | every component's rows for one subject, each source read under its owner's permission, a refused source reported as a gap | fresh for 30 s |

They are served in that order, one commit each, smallest first: the run's
progress weaves nothing, and one subject's story reads every area the
deployment holds. A view is finished when its route answers at the BFF's
address, in its member names and with its status codes, its ETag and freshness
rule are tested, and its BFF route is paired in the inventory.

The gateway composes and does not compute. A view that needs a collection no
service exposes gets that read added to the service that owns the data, beside
the operations it already answers, and never a database connection in the
gateway: the reporting views were served that way, and the workflow views take
the same road.

A source that cannot be read is a gap and not a failure, where the view states
one: one subject's story names the sources it could not read and answers the
rest, and a source that refuses its permission is reported as a gap because
that is what the person's grant allows. A view whose only read fails is the
service not answering, which is a 502.

Every view answers through =ores::http::net::view_answer=, the one place the
ETag, the =If-None-Match= answer and the freshness rule are stated, so the four
obligations are met once rather than once per view.

* Notes

The views are served smallest first, and the second one needed something the
first did not: a view that draws a request reads the same joins the request list
reads, and those lived inside the request routes. They are shared before the
views that need them, so each join is stated once for all four request views.

What the approval inbox owes, for the next family: the queue read itself, the
joins this part now shares, and one subject join per kind, because a kind's
subject lives in the component that owns it — a curve publish carries the subject
it publishes or the reason there is none, a cut carries the cut, and an
operational authorisation carries the trades it is about. A reader who may not see
one kind's subject keeps the other rows.
