* Acceptance

- Every dimension that grows without bound is identified, with its current size, its growth rate and the artefact that produces it
- The concepts are named and decided: what a retention policy is, what it attaches to, who runs the sweep, and what happens when the licence forbids the obvious mechanism
- The configuration is data, modelled and stored like any other entity, not hard-coded per table
- A person can see and change the policy of a dimension from a screen
- Each dimension has a mechanism that bounds it, or a recorded decision that it is kept indefinitely and why

* Result

The story's scaffold is superseded by the tasks it was broken into, and the
work it named is delivered: task_break-the-story-into-tasks split it, and every
store the installation keeps now has a catalogue row, a policy where a period
can bound it, and a mechanism that applies it — the SQL sweep for the tables, the
object expiry for the prefixes, the node's own release for a job's folder, and
the keying for a package.

Two stores read unbounded, and both are decisions rather than gaps. The calendar
dates cannot be bounded by a period: they are held once and rebuilt on a horizon,
so the screen states that a period is not the mechanism that bounds them
(task_decide-whether-calendar-dates-are-stored-or-computed). A node's scratch
folder is released by the node when it uploads a job's output; the folders of
jobs that failed are kept as evidence and are the capture
a-node-scratch-store-reads-unbounded-for-a-reason-the-screen-states-wrong, whose
subject is the screen's wording and not the mechanism.

The screen states every verdict, the notices tell a person about the stores
nothing effective bounds, and the object prefixes stop reading as unbounded now
that the sweep expires them (#5215). This task is closed as delivered rather
than as abandoned: it was the scaffold, and its goal is the story's.
