#+title: Official rate adapters go stale together
#+description: Every official rate adapter misses its heartbeat at the same moments, about fifteen times an hour, so the dashboard reports demanded ids not served while the adapters still run.
#+type: capture
#+level: cross
#+filetags: :marketdata:official:adapters:heartbeat:
#+created: 2026-11-23
#+updated: 2026-11-23

This page is a [[id:671F18E4-E09C-4B3B-BD24-D33DF8AE38A6][capture]] in the *inbox* bucket of the product backlog — a pre-sprint idea, not yet pulled into a sprint as a story.

* What

The market data service marks every official rate adapter stale at the same
moment, about fifteen times an hour, and each recovers seconds later. In
environment =eager_maxwell= on 2026-11-23, over one hour of
=ores.marketdata.service=, =rba-aonia= went stale 16 times and =nyfed-sofr=,
=ecb-frankfurter=, =imf-frankfurter=, =mas-sora=, =norgesbank-nowa=,
=riksbank-swestr= and =tcmb-frankfurter= 15 times each, every adapter at the
same timestamps, such as 21:40:32. The adapters also announce
again often: =ecb-estr= announced 38 times between its start at 15:39 and
21:40. That is neither one announce per =reannounce_interval= (60 seconds) nor
one per stale, so what triggers those announces is still open. While they are stale, the dashboard reports the demanded ids as not
served (153 at one reading), although nothing is wrong with the rates.

* Why

An operator reads a warning that is false most of the time, and a real outage
of an official source looks the same as the flapping.

Hypothesis, not yet proved: the cause is the adapter's single loop
(=official_adapter::loop= in =projects/ores.marketdata/official/src/app/official_adapter.cpp=).
One thread serves every source. Each pass sends every source's heartbeat. Then
one source may read its publisher through a blocking =refresh=. The same
thread also runs two other calls that can be slow: =load_universe=, while a
source is not ready, and =announce=, once each =reannounce_interval=.

A source beats every 5 seconds (=heartbeat_interval=), and the supervisor marks
it stale after 15 seconds of silence (=adapter_registry::default_heartbeat_timeout=).
So a stale means the loop stalled for at least 10 seconds. One slow call holds
back every heartbeat at once, which fits all the adapters going stale together.

The service's NATS log fits this but does not prove it: it is silent from
21:40:04 to 21:42:34. A stall in the NATS client, such as back-pressure on a
publish or a reconnect, would give the same silence and the same symptom. The
log does not say which call was slow, because a refresh logs only what it sent.

To settle it:

1. Log the duration of each =refresh=, =load_universe= and =announce=, with its
   source, and of each heartbeat publish.
2. Line the slow calls up with the stale timestamps.
3. If a publisher call is slow, beat from a thread of its own, or read the
   publishers off the loop. If the publish is slow, look at the NATS client
   instead.
