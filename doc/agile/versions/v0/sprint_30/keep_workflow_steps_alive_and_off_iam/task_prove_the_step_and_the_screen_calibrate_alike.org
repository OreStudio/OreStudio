Hold the two claims the surface step rests on: that reading a party's catalogue and market once and fitting from it answers what reading per surface answered, and that one surface's failure leaves the party's other surfaces published.

* Acceptance

- A test fits one recipe through =calibrate()= and through =calibrate_from()=
  with the recipe and quotes read once, and the two answers are equal: the same
  points, the same as-of, the same freshness.
- A test drives a party's lanes with one unit that throws, and asserts that the
  outline reports that unit as not published while the party's other units are
  published.
- A test asserts that a calibration asked for a context with no party, and for
  a blueprint the catalogue does not hold, answers with =success=false= and a
  message rather than raising.

* Plan

The first test needs a fixture with one party, one blueprint with its pillars
and sources, and the quotes they read; =curve_publish_fixture.hpp= and
=published_surface_fixture.hpp= already build most of it. The equality is the
points and the as-of, because the freshness annotation is derived from them.

The second test drives the lane runner rather than a NATS message, so the lane
runner has to be reachable from a test: it is a member of the handler and takes
the step's context for its heartbeat, so either it moves to a free function
that takes a progress callback, or the test builds a step context. The move is
the better shape and is the same reason the calibration moved into the service.

The third test is the regression the review of #4855 found: the gather a
calibration reads before it fits must answer in words, because the handler
calls =calibrate()= and catches nothing.
