* Goal

A clickable prototype of the application navigation that the design can be judged against, on a throwaway page with mock data. It draws five structural models inside the shell the application actually has, each over the catalogue's real scale, so the choice is made against 21 journeys in 8 groups and the two people who see different parts of them. The question is the one nothing owns: how a person reaches a journey at all.

* Acceptance

- The prototype is published beside its description and the other prototypes, and it reads no service.
- Every journey a person may run is reachable from every model, and the journeys they may not run are absent rather than disabled.
- The catalogue's real scale is what the models are judged against: 21 journeys in 8 groups, of which a member sees 5 and a tenant administrator 19.
- What has been built and what has not is renderable without reading the code, as the =today= state.
- The verdict is recorded on the prototype page and the losing models stay as the record.

* Plan

1. Collect what the journey documents say about how a journey is reached, and
   what the catalogue actually holds: 8 groups and 21 journeys.
2. Read the shell as it stands, and the session the shell is given, because
   what the navigation can show depends on what the session carries.
3. Build the first model, the account menu the journey documents name, as a
   static page under =doc/prototypes/=.
4. Take it to the product owner, who rejected it: at the catalogue's scale a
   menu holds neither the administrator's work nor the groups.
5. Re-scope the task to the question itself, and rebuild the prototype as five
   structural models drawn over the catalogue's real scale, in the shell the
   application actually has, so a reviewer judges the navigation and not a page
   of its own.
6. Filter the journeys by actor rather than disabling them, and render what has
   been built as its own state, because a navigation that offers a screen
   nothing implements is a promise.
7. Publish it with the site build, beside the other prototypes; drive the whole
   matrix without a browser; then look at the models in Chrome, including at a
   laptop's width, which is where the navbar changes shape.
8. Record the verdict on the prototype page and distil the decisions into the
   story.

* Result

The shell is [[id:E6E32AB5-2F25-4230-AB93-E38E75DE23B6][Application shell prototype]], at
=doc/prototypes/navigation/= and published on the site. It renders the menu,
the cards and the avatar for the four sign-ins that decide the mode.

The verdict is recorded on that page, and so is why each of the five rejected
shapes went: the account menu, one axis of journey groups, a sidebar beside the
cards, "Administration" as an area, and a mode the person chooses.

The decisions it settles are distilled into the parent story's =* Decisions=.
What it does not settle is recorded too: how a person learns a journey exists
but is switched off, and where a party user with no areas of their own lands
once the party's data is real.
