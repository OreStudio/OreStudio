* Goal

A service that needs another service waits for it, and does not report
itself started until it answers. When a service it needs is lost later, it
keeps running in a degraded state, refuses only the work that needs the lost
service, and resumes that work when the service returns. Every service gets
this from the shared runner in =ores.service=, from its dependencies stated
as data, and no service writes its own wait.

** The problem

Start order decides today whether a service's startup work succeeds:

- On a fresh environment, the synthetic service's fixing writer asked the
  market data service for 7 series before that service answered, and never
  asked again. The fixings of GBP, USD, CHF and JPY LIBOR curves were lost
  until a restart. See the story
  [[id:0F936789-FEBE-4330-97B9-DFE0837E4466][Every index a generated trade floats on has a curve and fixings]].
- The market data service writes thousands of demand intervals before it
  sends =READY=, misses systemd's 90-second start timeout, and its official
  rates adapter fails on the dependency. See
  [[id:B3BEA3D5-1ABA-444E-8C51-13EBD8E6DF5F][Ready the market data service before its usage sync]].
- The CZK curve starts before the first CNB refresh lands and keeps its fixed
  rate until the synthetic service starts again.

** What exists

- systemd units with =Type=notify=, =After== and =Requires==, generated from
  the =:depends_on:= of =projects/modeling/service_registry.org=.
- =READY=1= in =systemd_notify.cpp=, sent from the shared runner.
- The =heartbeat_publisher=, and telemetry's =service_state= with =running=
  and =lost=.
- The client credentials provider, which already retries the start race with
  a backoff.
- The two bootstrap phases in [[id:1D02DB5B-1E50-4AA0-9780-A3F1CF237349][Service bootstrap phases]]: phase 1 subscribes,
  phase 2 calls other services once.

What is missing: =:depends_on:= holds one service, and 19 of the 21 services
in the registry name only =ores.iam.service=, although many call others; the
synthetic service calls market data and does not say so. =READY= does not
mean the dependencies answer. Nothing watches a dependency once a service
runs. And phase 2 runs once, so a failed call is never repeated.

** The pattern

This is the split between liveness and readiness that service platforms
make, with a reconciler loop for the work a service does on its own:

1. The dependencies are data: a list per service in the registry.
2. At start the runner waits, then reports ready: starting, waiting for the
   dependencies, ready.
3. At run time the runner watches the dependencies: ready, degraded when one
   is lost, ready again when it returns.
4. Startup work runs as reconcilers: each one reads what is there, writes
   what is missing, and is safe to repeat; the runner runs them on ready, on
   each return from degraded, and at an interval.

systemd's =BindsTo== would restart a service when its dependency stops. That
is not the answer: a short loss of market data would restart every service
that uses it, and the remote compute hosts run without systemd.

* Acceptance

- Every service in the registry lists each service it calls, and the systemd
  units follow the list.
- On a fresh environment, =compass services start= completes with every unit
  active on the first attempt, and no service logs a startup call to a
  service that does not answer.
- A stopped dependency leaves its dependents running and degraded; when it
  starts again, they return to ready and their reconcilers complete their
  work, with no restart of the dependents.
- The derived fixings of a fresh environment equal those after a restart.
- =service_bootstrap_phases.org= describes the states and the reconcilers.

* Decisions

- The service degrades and does not restart when a dependency is lost. A
  restart cascades, and it cannot be done where systemd does not run.
- The wait and the watch live in the shared runner, so a service gets them by
  declaring its dependencies and registering its reconcilers.
- The dependencies are data in the service registry, which already generates
  the systemd units, so the units and the runner cannot disagree.
