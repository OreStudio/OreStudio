#+title: Entity screen specification
#+description: The look and feel of a screen for an entity made of several kinds of record, such as a person: list, page, edit, history, people links, photos and flags, server paging, access and states. The Staff screens are the reference.
#+type: knowledge
#+level: cross
#+filetags: :web:ux:standards:entities:
#+created: 2026-10-10
#+updated: 2026-10-10

* Summary

The [[id:2BF7F03F-6BF4-45FF-8E26-4D3B4E2D943D][Record screen standard]] defines the screen of one kind of record: a currency, a
calendar, a role. Some things a person works with are not one record. A person
is an account, a contact record, the roles they hold, their sign-ins, the parties
they work in and who they report to, and each of those has its own table, its own
permission and its own history. This page defines the look and feel of the screen
for such an /entity/. The Staff screens are the reference.

The rules in short:

- One page per subject, with one tab per aspect. A tab the reader may not read is
  not drawn.
- Every list pages on the server, and the pager on the screen is the request. No
  screen reads an entity whole.
- Every mention of a person is a link to the person, with their photo where the
  place has room for one. An email is a =mailto:= link and a web address an
  =http= link.
- Every change is made in a dialog opened by an Edit button on the card it
  changes. The reason is asked in the dialog and nowhere else.
- History is a chart of events over time above a rail of one-line headers with
  the detail on demand. The author's photo stands at the left of each entry, and
  the note stands on its own line below the header.
- A combo box offers only values that exist, and a screen that shows one value at
  a time has no "all" choice.
- A screen learns that its data changed, turns Refresh gold, and waits for the
  reader. It never reloads by itself, and it never loses an edit.

* Detail

** Where this page applies

An /entity screen/ is a screen whose subject is one thing made of several kinds
of record, which a person reads and changes together. The Staff list and the page
of one person are the reference. The tenant, the party and the role are close to
it. Hierarchy, Requests and Where I work are screens about relationships between
entities and follow the rules on people, history and paging here.

A kind of record with one table and no aspects is still a record screen. Use the
[[id:2BF7F03F-6BF4-45FF-8E26-4D3B4E2D943D][Record screen standard]] for it. This page adds to that standard and never
replaces it. Where the two disagree on the same thing, the record standard rules
for a screen of one record kind, and this page rules for an entity.

** Words

| Word | Meaning |
|------+---------|
| /Entity/ | A subject made of several kinds of record, such as a person |
| /Aspect/ | One kind of record of an entity, or one view of it: details, contact, roles, sign-ins, history |
| /Subject/ | The one entity a page is about |
| /Card/ | A bordered panel holding one aspect's fields, with its title and its Edit button |
| /Directory/ | The people the reader may name, read from the widest read they hold |
| /Self read/ | A read of the signed-in person's own record, which needs no permission |
| /Gap/ | A part of a stream that the server cannot answer, stated on the screen |
| /Window/ | One page of rows, asked for by offset and limit |

** The shape of an entity screen

An entity has three screens, and each has one job.

| Screen | Job | Address |
|--------+-----+---------|
| The list | Find a subject, and see enough of each to choose | =/staff= |
| The page | Read and change one subject, one aspect per tab | =/people/:username= |
| The relationship views | Show how subjects relate: a tree, a chart, a requests queue | =/hierarchy=, =/requests= |

The list opens the page. The page links back to the list through its breadcrumb,
and to other subjects through people links. A relationship view opens the page of
any subject it draws.

A reader sees a screen only if the menu offers it, and the menu offers it by the
narrowest read the screen needs. [[id:339F0405-43AE-4049-B6B3-EB12E5A38CA4][Menu structure]] names the areas and the screen that
belongs to each. A member does not hold the read of accounts, so
Staff is a different list for them, built from the organisation read. See
[[*Who sees what][Who sees what]].

** The list

#+begin_src text
  Home › Organisation › Staff
  Staff                                                     [↻ Refresh]
  The people of the parties you work in, and everyone who reports to you.
  [ 🔍 Search by name, username or job title ]
  ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
   Person              Username        Kind     Job title      Parties
  ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
   (photo) Ada Lovelace  ada.lovelace  Person   Head of Desk   [Acme UK]
   …
  ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  1–15 of 58      [« First] [‹ Previous] [Next ›] [Last »]   Page size [15 ▾]
#+end_src

- The first column is the person: their photo, then their name. A name opens the
  page. The username, the kind, the job title and the parties follow.
- The parties are tags with the party's name, not its code. A person in several
  parties shows each.
- The search, the sort, the filter and the page all go to the server. The list
  never reads every row to filter them itself.
- The header, the table, the states and the pager are those of the record
  standard's list. The titles are in sentence case.
- A list for a member names what it holds in its lead sentence, because it is not
  the whole tenant.

** The page

  │ Adrian Vance     Group Chief Executive Officer                      │

  │ adrian.vance     adrian.vance@…         user                        │
  │ ─ Reporting line ───────────────────────────────────────────────── │
  │ (photo) Grace Hopper   Chief of Staff                  [↗ Propose]  │
  └─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
#+end_src

*** Header

- The breadcrumb is Home, the area, the list, then the subject. The areas are in
  [[id:339F0405-43AE-4049-B6B3-EB12E5A38CA4][Menu structure]].
- The photo stands large beside the title. The title is the person's name.
- Under the title, in small subdued type, is the subject's identifier. Nothing
  else. The version stands on each card, beside its title, because each card is a
  different record with its own version.
- The header carries no Edit button. Edit belongs to the card it changes.

*** Tabs

Tabs are held in the address (=?tab=roles=), so a link reopens the page on a tab.
They run in this order, and a tab appears only when the reader may read it.

| Tab | Holds | Shown when |
|-----+-------+------------|
| /Details/ | The identity card and the reporting line | Always |
| /Contact/ | The contact record | The reader may read contact records, or it is their own |
| /Roles/ | The roles held, then what they allow | The reader may read roles |
| /Sign-ins/ | The sign-in state and the sessions | The reader may read sessions |
| /History/ | The timeline | The reader may read the history of the subject. The tab is always drawn, and the timeline states what it cannot answer |

A tab that would answer with a refusal is not drawn. A refusal that arrives
anyway is stated on the tab in words, with the reason, and the page does not show
a banner for it: the read that can be refused is made quietly, and the tab
speaks for it.

*** Cards

Each aspect that a person can change is one card.

- The card's title is on the left. The version of the record it shows is small and
  subdued beside it. The Edit button is on the right.
- The fields are read as text, not as inputs. A label stands above each value in
  sentence case, with no colon. A value with nothing in it is a muted dash.
- A field that is fixed once the record exists, such as the username, says why in
  a line of small text under it: "Set when the account was made, and never
  changed here."
- A field that another person owns says who. The sign-in address says "An
  administrator owns it."
- A card the reader may not change shows the access mark, Read only, as the
  [[id:166B2D77-6D04-47D5-A651-5431E1F2E082][UX Language]] sets it.

** Changing a record

*** The Edit pattern

- Edit is one button with an icon and the word Edit, on the card it changes. It
  is drawn only for a person who may change that card.
- Edit opens a dialog with the current values. The dialog is titled for the card.
  It asks for the change reason and the commentary. The card itself asks for
  neither, and shows no reason field beside its values.
- Save stays disabled until a field differs from the record. The reasons offered
  follow the change: when a field changed, "No change in substance" is not
  offered, and when nothing changed only that reason is.
- The write claims the version that was read. A record that has changed since is
  refused with a conflict, and the dialog says so and offers to reload.
- Closing the dialog with changes asks "You have unsaved changes. Close anyway?"
- An optional field has a Clear control beside it, which writes the empty value.
  A Clear is not a Save of a blank by accident.

*** Small controls

- A change that is not a form, such as the reporting line, has a small icon
  button on the line it changes. It is drawn only when the reader may use it.
- The photo is its own control. Clicking the photo opens the chooser to replace
  it. There is no separate "Replace photo" button.
- A control that proposes and does not write, such as a change that another
  person must approve, says Propose and not Save. See
  [[*Proposals and four-eyes][Proposals and four-eyes]].

*** Changes the reader may not make

The control is not drawn. A disabled button asks a question that a missing one
does not. The server still decides every write; the screen hides only what the
server would refuse.

** People are links

A person is named in many places: a manager, a holder of a role, the author of a
history entry, the person who asked for something, the person who answered. The
rule is the same in all of them.

| What is on the screen | How it is drawn |
|-----------------------+-----------------|
| A person the reader can name | Their full name, then their username in brackets: "Ada Lovelace (ada.lovelace)" |
| A person the reader may open | The name above is a link to their page |
| A person the reader can name but not open | The same text, not a link |
| A name that is a service | Text, not a link: a service has no page |
| An identifier no read can resolve | "Someone outside your view". The identifier is never shown |
| An email address | A =mailto:= link |
| A web address | An =http= or =https= link that opens in a new tab with =rel="noopener noreferrer"= |
| A value that is neither | Plain text. It never becomes a link that goes nowhere |

- =PersonRef= draws a person from a username or an identifier. It reads the
  directory once, and the directory is the widest read the reader holds. A reader
  of accounts names anybody in the tenant. A reader of the organisation names the
  people in their parties and under them. Anybody else names nobody.
- =actorPathFor= decides whether a history author is linked.
- A sign-in address is not a mail address and is not a =mailto:= link. Only the
  contact record's email is.
- The email on the contact card says which address it is, and says that it is not
  the sign-in address.

** Photos, flags and images

- A person is drawn with a photo wherever there is room for one: the list, the
  page header, a manager line, a card in a chart, a person in a picker and the
  history rail. Where a person has no photo, their initials stand in the same
  circle, so a list does not jump.
- A photo is fetched by its image identifier, which the read already carries. A
  screen never asks for a person's picture once per row by username. The image is
  cached by its identifier for a year.
- The sizes are small (20 pixels) in rows, pickers and the rail, medium in
  cards, and large (56 pixels) in a page header.
- A country, a currency, a calendar or a business centre is drawn with its flag
  wherever its code appears. A country code on a contact card is the flag, then
  the code. Flags come from the one map in the record standard, and a screen never
  works out a flag itself.
- The photo of a person who wrote a history entry stands at the left of the
  entry, beside its header and changes. See
  [[*History and the timeline][History and the timeline]].

** Paging: the pager is the request

This is the rule that matters most, and the Staff work learned it the hard way.

#+begin_quote
The paging on the screen must match the paging of the data requests.
#+end_quote

- Every list of rows asks the server for one page: =offset=, =limit=, and the
  server's total. The pager, the page size and the "Showing 1–15 of 40" sentence
  are the request's own numbers. A screen that reads everything and slices it in
  the browser has a pager that is not a pager.
- The search, the filter, the area and the sort are fields of the same request.
  The server applies them. A new search or filter returns to the first page.
- A search is sent when the typing pauses, not on every key.
- The default page is 15 rows, and the sizes are those of the record standard. No
  screen sets its own.
- An entity is never read whole. That includes the lists a screen needs only to
  draw something else: the permissions of a role, the people who hold a role, the
  roles a person holds, the people a name is resolved against. Each of them is a
  page, or a lookup of the few rows the screen draws.
- A count that a screen needs, such as how many people hold a role, is part of
  the answer to the page, not a second read of everything.
- A thing that is not a list but looks like one to the reader, such as the
  catalogue of permissions, is paged by the unit the reader sees. A row on the
  screen is a resource with its actions, so a page is a whole number of resources
  and never half of one.

*** Combo boxes and filters

A combo box that filters a paged list obeys the same rule.

- It offers only the values that exist in the data, and the server says which.
  An area no role grants something in is not offered.
- A screen that shows one value at a time, such as one area of the permissions,
  has no "all" choice. One value is always chosen. When the reader has not
  chosen, the first value the server lists is chosen, and the server says which.
- A list that narrows rows, such as the roles list, may start with "All areas",
  because choosing none shows every row. The first choice there means no filter,
  and it is not a page of everything.
- A filter that matches nothing says so and offers to clear it.

*** Editing across pages

A change a person makes on one page must survive a move to another. The screen
keeps the changes as a set of differences from what the server holds, keyed by
the thing changed, and lays them over each page. A change that puts a value back
as the server holds it removes the difference. Save sends only the differences,
and the server applies them to what it holds. The screen never needs the whole set
to save.

** History and the timeline

An entity's history is one stream. It is drawn as a chart of events over time,
and below it a rail of entries, newest first.

#+begin_src text
  ▂ ▅ ▁   ▇ ▃          ■ Created  ■ Changed  ■ Granted  ■ Signed in
  10 Oct ─────────── 12 Oct

  Saturday, 10 October 2026
  03:51:36 ●  (photo) [Raised] account a452db76 v1  system.external_data_import
  ✓           ores_brave_hopper_iam_service  Published from organisation dataset
              Attached staff photo during provisioning
              ▸ Details
  03:40:12 ●  (photo) [Changed] account a452db76 v2  Job Title   Rectification
              Marco Craveiro
              Corrected the title
              ▸ Details
#+end_src

*** The event chart

- The chart is a histogram of the entries: a bar for each hour, or for each day
  once the history spans more than two days. Each bar is split by the kind of
  entry, and each kind has one colour that the legend repeats.
- Every bar has the same fixed width. A bar never stretches to fill the row, and
  a long history scrolls sideways.
- An hour or a day with no entries is empty space. The chart draws every bucket
  from the first entry to the last, so a gap in time reads as a gap.
- A bar shows its time and its count on hover, and each segment its kind and its
  count. The first and the last time stand under the bars. The legend names the
  kinds that appear, each with its count.
- A click on a bar scrolls to the entry nearest that hour or day, and marks it for
  a moment. A click on an empty bar goes to the closest entry in time.
- The chart reads the entries the list holds. It makes no read of its own.

*** One line per entry

- Each entry is a header on one line: the kind, the record and its version, the
  reason code, the author, what they did, and the time. The time and the
  current-version emblem stand in the left gutter.
- The note, such as "Attached staff photo during provisioning", is never on the
  header line. It stands on its own line below the header.
- The detail is collapsed. A field that changed is drawn as the difference, the
  old and the new, and a first version as the whole record. A reader opens the
  detail on demand. The first screen of a long history is a list of headers a
  person can scan.
- An entry that changed no field says "No changes". It has no detail to open.
- The newest version of a record carries a small emblem next to its time, with
  the words "Current version" as its tooltip. There is no sentence for it, and no
  Revert button on it, because there is nothing to revert to.
- An entry that cannot be reverted, such as a sign-in, simply offers no control.
  The rail does not explain what is missing: no "this entry records something
  that happened, so there is nothing to revert", and no "putting a field back is
  not offered here". A sentence that says what is not there adds nothing.

*** The author

- The author's photo stands at the left of the entry, beside the header and the
  changes, at medium size (2 rem) so that a face can be seen. Where the author has
  no photo, their initials stand in the same circle. A service is its initials.
- The author's name is a link to their page where the reader may open people.
  A service is text.
- The photo and the link come from the same directory as every other mention of
  a person.

*** One rail for several kinds of record

A person's history merges the versions of the account and the contact record,
the roles given, and the sign-ins. They share the rail, and each entry names its
kind and its record.

- Each kind of entry has its own tone: a change, a grant, an act, a decision.
- An act that is not a version, such as a sign-in, is drawn quietly and says that
  it cannot be reverted.
- A version of a record that may be reverted offers Revert to the person who may
  write it. The newest version says "Current version" and offers nothing.
- Entries are paired with the version before them to find what changed. The
  pairing is the screen's reading of the stream, not the server's claim.

*** Gaps

Some parts of a story cannot be answered, and the stream says so. A grant that was
taken away is closed and no read returns it. A junction has no versions. The
sign-in log keeps a window. Each such part is a gap on the answer, stated on the
screen in words. A gap is never rounded away, and it is never shown as an empty
list.

*** Narrowed histories

A history narrowed to one field, such as the reporting line, keeps every version in
the stream so each is read against the one before, and shows only the versions
where the field moved.

*** History and Audit

History is the versions of one record. A log of events that happened is Audit,
and has a Refresh and no revert. The sign-in record is Audit. In a person's
timeline it appears as acts.

*** Relationship histories

A history that spans many subjects, such as who changed their reporting line, is
two steps and not a merged rail.

1. A list of the subjects for whom the thing changed recently, newest change
   first, each with the old value and the new one.
2. The subject chosen from that list, drawn as its own narrowed timeline.

There is no card of the person beside it. The list is the way in.

** Several kinds of record in one entity

- One page per subject. An aspect is a tab, not a screen of its own.
- Each tab reads what it needs, and each read is its own permission. A reader who
  can read an account and not its roles sees the Details tab and not the Roles
  tab.
- A reader sees their own record whatever they hold. The signed-in person's own
  account, contact record, sign-in state, sessions and permissions are self reads
  on the allow-list of [[id:804C7048-DBBF-4B39-8737-BFB4949884C4][Authorised reads]]. A page that is the reader's own uses
  them and needs no permission. Any other person's page needs the read of that
  aspect.
- When the read of one aspect is refused, that aspect says so and the others
  carry on.
- The entity's relationships are shown where the reader needs them: a manager on
  the identity card, the people who report to a person in the hierarchy, the
  parties as tags in the list.
- Do not add a tab to say that something cannot be done. Say it where the
  control would be.

** Relationship views

A tree, a chart and a queue draw how subjects relate.

- A tree draws each subject once, under the subject it relates to. A subject can
  belong to several groups, such as a person in two parties, and is then drawn
  in each group, with the same person under the same manager in each.
- A group is a party, nested by its own parent, and its name is drawn, never its
  code.
- A filter by group appears only when the reader sees more than one, and its
  choices are the groups they see.
- A person with a manager the reader cannot see is marked, not drawn as a root.
- A chart zooms and fits, and scrolls sideways rather than squeezing its cards.
- A relationship view is paged or lazy in the same way as a list. It never reads
  every row to draw the part the reader sees.

** Who sees what

Visibility is decided by the narrowest read that does the job.

| Reader | Sees | Through |
|--------+------+---------|
| A tenant administrator | Every subject of the tenant | The read of accounts |
| A member | The people in the parties they work in, and everyone who reports to them | The organisation read, scoped by party and reporting line |
| Anyone | Their own record | Self reads |

- A member's list shows names, photos, titles and parties. It does not show
  email addresses, contact records, sign-ins or roles. Account data is not screen
  furniture.
- A party the reader does not work in, and does not manage someone in, is not
  named to them, even when a colleague in their own party also works in it.
- A person who is outside the reader's view is "someone outside your view", never
  an identifier.
- The menu offers an entry by the narrowest read that screen needs, so a member
  gets Staff and Hierarchy and an administrator gets the fuller list.

** Proposals and four-eyes

A change that another person must agree to is a proposal, not a write.

- The control says Propose, and the dialog says that nothing changes until the
  other person approves.
- The proposer sees the state of their proposal among their own requests and can
  withdraw it while it waits.
- The person who decides sees it in Requests, with who proposed it and what
  changes, and approves or declines it. A decline needs a comment.
- Requests is the one place for every kind of request. Every member sees their
  own there, and a person who can decide also sees the queue.
- The decision and the apply are recorded, so the history of the subject names the
  proposer, the decider and the time.

The reporting line becomes a proposal in the story "Approve a reporting line
change by the proposed manager". Until that story is done, the line is changed
by a direct edit for those who hold the update permission.

** The shell around a screen

- The footer states the tenant and the party the session acts for, as pills,
  because they decide what every screen shows. It also states the client, the
  server and the environment.
- The header names an administrator's mode and says nothing for an ordinary
  member, whose mode has no meaning to them.
- The notification bell and the account menu stand in the header. The account
  menu holds the person's own pages and signing out. It is not part of the menu
  of areas in [[id:339F0405-43AE-4049-B6B3-EB12E5A38CA4][Menu structure]].
- A person's own pages are My profile, My access and Where I work. They are
  reached from the account menu and from the profile's doors, each in one place.

** Feedback, states and words

- A screen states what it is waiting for. First load says "Loading…" in the place
  of the content. A reload keeps the rows, dimmed. An empty list says what is
  empty and what to do. An error is in place, in the server's words, with Retry.
- A refused read is a refusal and not a failure. The screen says the reader may
  not see this, in words, and never shows a permission code.
- A time is relative with the exact time on hover. "Never signed in" is a phrase
  and not 1 January 1970: a missing time is not a time.
- Words are in sentence case, and the verbs are Add, Edit, Save, Delete, Remove,
  Revert, Refresh, Cancel and Propose. An entity's own action is a verb and its
  object.
- Every string exists in English, French and Portuguese before the screen ships.
- A screen never shows an identifier where a name would do.
- A screen does not narrate what is absent. A control that is not offered is not
  drawn, and nothing says why unless the reader would otherwise be stuck. A sentence
  that only explains an empty space is removed.

** Updates and refresh

A screen learns that the data under it changed, says so in one place, and waits
for the reader. Nothing reloads by itself, and nothing that the person typed is
lost. The design is in [[id:F489BD14-CB2D-445F-9EDC-0CF4AC7F2A42][Change notification]]; this is what a screen must do.

*** How a screen hears

- The browser holds one stream of change news for the session. It opens when
  the first screen watches something and closes with the last, so a screen that
  watches nothing holds no connection.
- A screen names the entities it shows, as the services name their events: the
  component and the events collection, such as =iam= and =accounts=. The refdata
  registry states these, so no screen guesses a plural.
- The news is a time and not a list. A thousand changes in one import make a
  screen stale once.
- A screen is stale when a change reached the browser after the load that
  produced its data began. The comparison uses the browser's clock at both ends,
  because the server's clock differs. A change that arrives while a load is in
  flight counts.

*** Refresh

- Every list, relationship view and record page has a Refresh button in the
  header's actions, the same control and the same place (=RefreshButton=).
- When the screen is stale the button turns gold, pulses four times, and stays
  gold until the screen loads again. Its tooltip says the data changed and gives
  the time. A reader who asks for less motion gets the gold and no pulse.
- The gold (=--color-stale=) means "changed on the server" and nothing else.
- A click reads again whatever the screen shows. On a record page that is every
  card and the history.
- The page does not reload by itself. A list does not, a record page does not,
  and the history does not.

*** Rows and entries that changed

- After a reload, a row that was not on the page before, or whose content is
  not what it was, gets a gold left edge and pulses three times. The mark goes
  after four seconds. The list keeps its scroll position.
- The comparison is with the same page read before. It uses no clock, and moving
  to another page, searching or sorting marks nothing.
- A history entry that arrives with a read is marked the same way.

*** An edit in progress

- A dialog is never reloaded under the person. While it is open it listens for
  a change to its entity and reads the record again behind the form.
- When the record is newer than the one the edit started from, a warning above
  the form says who saved it and which fields moved, with the old and the new
  value. The person chooses Keep my edit or Take the new values.
- A save claims the version the edit started from. A save made against an old
  version is refused with a clear message, and the edit stays in the form.
- Keep my edit makes the newest version the base, so the save replaces it
  knowingly.

*** What is not heard yet

- The events of another tenant are not told to a session. An event names its
  tenant and, on a party-owned table, its party, in the envelope. A session hears
  its own tenant and the system tenant, and a member hears the parties they work in.
  The broker does not enforce this, so a client that listens to NATS directly still
  hears every tenant.
- A role granted or taken away is published inside the service only, so a person
  page cannot hear it.
- A reporting line has no event of its own. The staff list and the hierarchy
  hear it when the person changes too.
- A change to a record that a screen shows by reference, such as a country name
  on a book, is not heard.
- The classification lists, the staff list and the hierarchy mark no rows yet.
- The account profile write has no version claim in its protocol. The BFF checks
  the claim just before it writes, which leaves a very small gap.
- The notification bell still reads its count on a timer.

** What a screen or a form needs, and what the log keeps

A screen or a form that is drawn must work. So what it needs is known before it
is entered, and a screen the reader cannot use is not drawn.

- /Each screen and each form declares what it needs/, as typed permission
  constants (=access/permissions.ts=, =access/screens.ts=). A wrong name does not
  compile. The menu, the home tiles, the route guard and the button that opens a
  form read that one declaration.
- /A screen the reader cannot use is not offered./ A saved link to it does not show
  "not available". It names the screen, says in words what the reader would need to
  be able to do, and lists the roles they could ask for that allow it. Each has a
  link that opens the request for that role, already filled in. When no role can
  be asked for, it says to ask the tenant administrator and offers no link. The
  words for each permission are typed, so a new permission without words does not
  compile.
- /A screen that was drawn and then refused a read is our fault, not the reader's./
  It is replaced by a panel that says so, gives a reference that finds the request
  in the log, and offers Try again and Home. The roles are read again first. If they
  changed, the screen's own guard takes over and says what is missing.
- /Nothing is decided until the roles are known./ =Requires= and =FirstAvailable=
  draw nothing but "Loading" while the roles are read, so a screen never asks the
  server for what the reader may not read.
- /Two audiences are two screens./ The whole directory and the people of your own
  parties are two screens, each with its own declaration. A screen does not
  inspect permissions to decide which screen it is.
- /A form saves through one hook/ (=useRecordEdit=): save, close the form, then read
  the record again. The form never reloads under the person, and a change that
  arrives during a save is the save's own.
- /Every failed request names its operation/ (the method and the path, with no
  query), its status, the server's code, and the request id. The banner shows the
  operation and the first eight characters of the id. The BFF puts the same id on
  its own lines for that request.
- /The path a person took is in the log./ The browser sends a trail to the BFF:
  =screen entered=, =screen left=, =form opened=, =form closed=, =save requested=,
  =saved=, =save refused=, =save failed=, =request failed=. The BFF writes each in
  the line form of the services, under =ores.web.client.<area>=, with the
  session id: =time [LEVEL] [logger] message key=value=. A search on the session id
  follows the person through the browser, the BFF and the services. The trail
  holds no value a person typed and no secret.

** Building an entity screen

Work in this order. Each step ends in something to check.

1. Name the subject, its aspects, and for each aspect the table, the read and the
   permission. Say which aspects the reader sees whatever they hold.
2. Decide the list: its columns, its search, its filters and its default order.
   Say what the server searches and sorts.
3. For every read the page will make, write down its page: its offset, its limit,
   its total and its filters. If a read has none, it is not ready.
4. Add the self reads the signed-in person needs, and put each on the allow-list
   of Authorised reads in the same change.
5. Build the list from the shared parts, then the page: header, tabs in the
   address, one card per changeable aspect.
6. Put Edit on each card, with the reason in the dialog.
7. Draw every person with =PersonRef=, every email as =mailto:=, every web
   address as a link, every flagged code with its flag.
8. Build the timeline from the shared =Timeline=: headers, details on demand,
   author photo and link, gaps stated.
9. Add the strings in English, French and Portuguese.
10. Check it against data of the real size, as a member and as an administrator:
    a large tenant, a role that grants a whole area, a person with no photo, a
    person outside the reader's view, a refused read, an empty history.

** Verifying a rule

A rule is done when the screen shows what the rule says, tried against data of
the real size. A test that checks only the code's own reading of the rule is not
enough. The Staff work paged the areas of the permissions in the browser, passed
a test of that, and still showed a screen with every permission on it.
