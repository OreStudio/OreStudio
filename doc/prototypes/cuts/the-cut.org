* Summary

The screens a cut needs, as one plain page with seven surfaces: the cuts list, the
cut's record page, the take flow, the picker that consumes a cut, the run that
names one, and the named cut times of Reference data with the record of one.

The page draws the model
[[id:F6362451-D569-4609-97A3-F1C785AAAF02][The cut is the body a run prices
from]] states, and it does not restate the argument for it. The authorisation it
draws is the inbox's: a cut whose kind requires one raises an approval request,
and a cut whose kind requires none states the basis and is usable when taken. It takes the
[[id:2BF7F03F-6BF4-45FF-8E26-4D3B4E2D943D][Record screen standard]] for the
shape of the list and the record, the
[[id:E69C008D-7092-4855-98EC-47A75F422F61][Configuration screen standard]] for
the tab that holds the cut a report prices from, and the
[[id:FB8F701C-E56A-49A9-AA90-1B6BD0B02092][report binding decision]] for the
picker, the refusals and the run's card.

It is throwaway. It reads no service, it holds no session, and every value on it
is mock data. It exists because the journeys settle what a cut *is* and nothing
yet settles what a person *sees* while taking one, being refused one, or reading
what a run priced from.

* Detail

** The question

The decision note settles the record: the facts, the states, the permissions, the
identity and the second binding. It does not settle the screens, and the screens
are where the model lives or dies:

1. Where do the cuts live, so that a person who asks what a result was computed
   from finds them?
2. What does a body that cannot be assembled look like, and what may a person do
   with it?
3. What does a cut look like in every state, and how does a person tell a body
   that awaits a signature from one that holds none?
4. How is a cut chosen for a report, and what does the refusal say when the cut
   cannot be used?
5. What does a run say about the body it priced from, and can a person tell a
   reproduction from a movement?
6. What is a named cut time, where does a person read one, and what does the take
   read from it?

The prototype answers all six, and the notes below say what each answer costs.

The list is the home of the journey that takes a cut, and the record is the home
of the journey that audits one. The picker and the run are the second journey's.

** Why the cuts live in Reporting, and the cut times in Reference data

The [[id:339F0405-43AE-4049-B6B3-EB12E5A38CA4][Menu structure]] asks one
question of a screen: which topic would a person look in? A cut exists for one
purpose, the valuation a run computes, and a person who asks what a number was
computed from looks where the reports are. So the list, the record and the take
flow take the trail Home, Reporting, Cuts, and the run settings tab that names a
cut sits among the configuration's own tabs.

Two placements are rejected. Market data holds the feeds and curves that change by
the minute, and a cut changes once a day and is not a price. Trades holds what the
tenant holds and does with a trade, and a cut is not a trade. The reference data
the take reads is the opposite case: a named cut time is a list that changes by
the month, so it sits in Reference data among the currencies and calendars, and
the option-expiry sense of the word reads the same list.

The prototype adds no area and no card, because the menu page's rule is that a
screen is added to an area page as a card when the screen exists. The page states
the area and the trail, and the card follows the build.

** The list, and the day's states

The table is the standard's list with one column of the design's own: *State*
draws the derived state as a badge, and under it the fact that qualifies it — a
superseded cut says when it was replaced, a taken cut says which part it waits on,
and a cut in use says that its kind requires no authorisation. Two columns answer
the question the cluster exists for: *Taker* and *Authoriser*, and the count of
*Runs* that priced from the body. The *Authoriser* cell is a dash for a cut whose
kind requires none, and the state beside it carries the reason.

The default order is the timepoint, newest first, because the person reading the
list is asking which body the next run will price from. A superseded cut sits
under the one that replaced it and stays in the list: the runs that priced from it
still reproduce from it, so hiding it would hide the provenance of a result.

The state badges follow
[[id:166B2D77-6D04-47D5-A651-5431E1F2E082][UX Language]]. Grey is an inactive
record, so *Draft* and *Superseded* are grey. Blue is pending, so *Taken* is blue.
Green is the colour of a record that is active and in order, so *Authorised* and
*In use* are green: both are bodies a run may price from, and the word and the line
under it say which basis admitted each one. Red is a body that failed, so
*Incomplete* and *Refused* are red.

The indicative mark is drawn apart from the state, as a violet tag beside it,
because it is a fact of the kind and not a state of the cut: the flash kind fixes
indicative values, so its cut is *In use* and its results carry the mark, while the
start of day kind requires no authorisation either and carries no mark. A mark that
stood in the state column would say that the two cuts stand differently, and they
do not.

Two notices sit under the table. One names the cuts that are not consumable and
their states, so a person knows the reports of the scope are refused before trying
to run one. The other names the cuts in use with no signature and says why they are
admitted, so a body nobody signed is not read as a body somebody forgot.

** The record, and the four tabs**

*The body* is the design's own tab, and it draws the four inputs the
[[id:B5214F3A-A72E-421E-82BC-79338B2E95CB][Cuts]] note argues for: the deals
with their counts and states, the market data as a fixed snapshot with its sets,
values and fixings, the reference data by version, and the engine and the code. A sixth panel draws the identity: the derived label, the
kind, the timepoint with its timezone, the cut time it read, and the take ordinal.

The gap list is drawn where a body could not be assembled, as a table of part,
item, why it stands and what closes it. The shape is the one thing in this
prototype that no rule covers, and the note names it as the candidate for a
standard: a configuration's import lists what it could not read, and this lists
what an assembly could not fill, with the same three facts — the item, the reason
and the remedy.

*Authorisation* draws one of two things, and the kind decides which. A cut whose
kind requires an authorisation draws the acts — taken, and the decision with its
decider, its instant and its comment — a panel naming where the request lives (the
inbox's kind, the request, the permission that decides it and the queue), and the
four-eyes rule as a fact rather than a warning. A cut whose kind requires none draws
the one act and the sentence *No authorisation is required by its kind*, with the
reason in the kind's own words, and it says that the basis is a fact of the kind and
not an absence to be explained. *Runs* draws the runs that priced from the body,
which is the question a person asks before a cut is superseded. *History* is the
shared timeline, and it is short: the body is immutable once taken, so a signed cut
has two entries and a cut that required no signature has one.

** The take flow, and why it is four steps**

The take is the one place where a rail earns its place: it is four acts over one
body rather than one edit, and the review is the point of the exercise. The rail
reads: Kind and timepoint, The body, Review, Take the cut. The first step states
each kind's basis beside what the kind fixes, so the person who takes a cut reads
whether a signature will be needed before choosing one.

Step 2 is where the design has teeth. Every part is drawn with its state — closed
or open — and the gaps sit under the table in full. *Save as a draft* appears in
place of *Take the cut* while a gap stands, and the rule under the buttons says
why: a decider would be asked to sign a body that does not meet the definition.

Step 3 reviews the body against the previous cut of the same kind and scope,
because the movement between two cuts is what the day's reports state: the deals
that entered, the deals that left, the states that moved, the values that moved
and the versions that changed.

Step 4 draws what the press did, not a success message, and it has two shapes. A
cut whose kind requires an authorisation draws the body frozen at an instant, the
request raised of the kind =reporting.cut_authorisation= in the queue of the
permission that decides it, the cut in *Taken*, and the statement that the taker
cannot authorise it — with the button not drawn, which is the standard's rule for
an action a person may not take. A cut whose kind requires none draws the same
frozen body, the cut in *In use*, the sentence that no request was raised and why,
and the mark its results will carry where the kind is indicative.

** The picker, and the three refusals**

The picker is the record standard's =RecordPicker= over cuts, and it follows the
binding decision's findings: the shared picker reads its whole kind once and draws
no state, so the screen needs a =CutPicker= or the shared one needs a filtered
form and a state cell.

The footer is where a state becomes a sentence. A cut offers *Price from this cut*
when its kind admits it, and the footer states the basis: the authoriser where the
kind requires one, and *No authorisation required by its kind* where it does not. A
superseded cut is offered with the sentence that a rerun reproduces an earlier
result from it and a new run should normally name the cut in use, and the button
says *anyway* — the published-set pin is not repeated here, because a cut is a
record of an instant and not a value in use.

The three refusals are drawn in full. A taken cut is refused with the part it waits
on and the permission that decides it. An incomplete cut is refused with its gap
count and where the gaps are read. A refused cut is refused with its reason and the
statement that the remedy is a new take. Nothing else is refused: a cut of a kind
that requires no authorisation is offered when it is taken, and where the kind
fixes indicative values the footer says so and the button stands.

** The run, and the comparison**

The run's page carries the binding decision's card, read through the cut: the
label, the kind, the timepoint, the scope, the counts, the taker, the engine
version and the cut's basis — the authoriser and the instant where the kind
requires them, and the statement that the kind requires none where it does not —
and under it the market roles inside the body with their publish records. A run
that priced from a cut of an indicative kind carries the mark on the card, because
the figures are the kind's and not the state's.

Below the card sits the comparison, and it is one of three verdicts. *Reproduced*
draws the two runs' figures as equal and says they are one result computed twice.
*Differs* draws the figures that moved, and says the body and the engine are the
same, so the difference is a defect and not a movement of the market. *Cannot be
compared* names one of three reasons: the engine version differs, a part of the
body is no longer served, or the body was retaken. That third verdict is what an
auditor writes down, so it is stated as a sentence and not as a red mark.

The strip above the runs switches between all eight outcomes — three verdicts,
three reasons and two refusals — because a reviewer cannot judge the design from
the happy path.

** The named cut times**

The reference data surface is a standard list and a standard record. The list
draws the code and name, the time of day, the timezone, the centre, the kinds the
cut time serves and the counts of what uses it. The record carries the fields, the
daylight-saving rule, what uses the row, and its history, and it states the rule
that a cut time cannot be deleted while a book or a pair names it.

Three callers are drawn, and only one of them exists as a field: a book's centre,
a currency pair's planned =default_cut_code=, and the cuts that fixed an instant
at it. The page states that the times are our convention and no public source
fixes them, which is the position the cluster records.

** Driving it

The bottom bar switches the surface and the state, and the query string carries
the difference from the default, so any pair can be linked. A row of the cuts list
opens the cut. A row of the picker writes =?pick=. A row of the cut times opens
the record. The run's strip writes =?verdict=. A kind chosen at step 1 draws the
instant that kind fixes.

** What is fake

Everything on the page is mock data: the party, its three books, its seven cuts,
their labels, takers, authorisers and instants, the twelve hundred and eighty-four
deals, the three thousand nine hundred and twelve market data values, the seven
published sets, the eight runs and their figures, the six cut times and their
counts, and the four rows of the kind table that say which occasions need an
authorisation and which do not. There is no server, no session, no framework and no build step. A press
writes nothing; the query string is the only state, and a reload returns the page
as it was.

The mock tenant's clock is 07 October 2026, 09:00 UTC, which is the clock the
[[id:3E207A59-C1F8-429C-BA23-99DBAEB6EA5E][market bindings prototype]] uses. The
published sets the market data half names are that prototype's own fixtures moved
on a day, so the two pages agree about ACME London's USD-SOFR-3M curve: the set
published on 06 October at 16:00 and in use, publish record =8f2a4c19=.

The reviewer should know four assumptions. The scope is a book group of the
London centre, which the decision note takes as the convention and leaves open.
The end of day cut of 06 October is the one a report names, because the close of
07 October has not happened at the mock clock. The end of day and independent
verification kinds require an authorisation, decided by the permission the inbox
kind names, =inbox::approvals:decide_finance=; the start of day and flash kinds
require none. And the flash kind fixes indicative values while the start of day
kind does not, which is the pair that shows the two declarations are independent.

** The verdict

Open. It is offered for sign-off with the decision it draws.

The design to sign off is: cuts in Reporting with the list, the record and the
four-step take; the body drawn as its parts with a gap table that stops the take;
the seven states derived and drawn in the UX Language's colours, with *In use* as
the state of a body whose kind requires no authorisation; the basis drawn under
every state that has one, and apart from the indicative mark, which is the kind's;
the picker refusing a taken, an incomplete and a refused cut and offering a cut in
use; the run's card read through the cut and stating its basis, with the comparison
in three verdicts; and the named cut times as reference data in Reference data.

** What it does not settle

*The gap table as a standard.* The prototype defines a shape — part, item, why it
stands, what closes it — and no rule covers it. Whether it joins the record screen
standard as a shared panel, or stays the take's own, is open.

*The comparison's figures.* The verdict is drawn from two runs' figures, and no
operation reads a run's figures as rows: the run gives the location of an archive.
The prototype draws the comparison the design needs and states the gap on the
card.

*The scope.* A cut of one book group is drawn. Whether a group report prices from
one cut of the group, or from a composition of the cuts of its books, is the
decision note's open question and the screen does not answer it.

*One inbox kind, or one for each occasion.* The two authorising kinds raise the
same kind, =reporting.cut_authorisation=, so the queue groups both under one row
and one decide permission. A firm that wants the verification decided by another
function than the close needs a kind per occasion, and the request page would draw
the same detail either way. The screen does not answer it.

*Where an authorisation is withdrawn.* The inbox can reverse an approved decision
with a comment, and a cut's page draws no such action. Whether a firm reverses a
cut's authorisation, and what a result priced from it then means, is not settled
here, and the prototype draws no reversed cut.

*The cut time's callers.* Three callers are drawn: a book, a currency pair and the
cuts taken. Only the third would exist with the cut, and the pair's field is
planned and unbuilt. Whether the centre a book carries is a cut time or a separate
centre record is open, and the
[[id:DD828FB9-5901-4B9A-A767-71FD88472F0A][Business Centre]] page owns it.

*Two doors to the same refusal.* The picker refuses a cut and the run refuses it
again. Whether the configuration should warn before a run is scheduled — a cut
that is not consumable on the day a scheduled run fires — belongs to the schedule's
journey and is not settled here.

*The indicative badge.* The violet tag is a proposal. No badge definition holds
the word, so the build would draw the unmapped fallback until the seed gains it.
The tag stands beside the state rather than in it, which is what keeps *In use*
readable for a kind that carries no mark.
