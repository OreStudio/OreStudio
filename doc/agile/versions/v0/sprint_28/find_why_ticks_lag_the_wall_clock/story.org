* Decisions

- Closed at sprint 28 close with its 11 done tasks. The 20 open tasks and the original acceptance moved to [[id:42252DB8-F87A-4DAD-925D-655AA43FDD60][Find why market data ticks lag the wall clock — the remaining work]] in the product backlog inbox.
- A quote is perishable. A stale tick is worse than a lost tick, so the pipeline keeps the
  newest tick, drops what has aged out, and never lets a consumer wait for the store.
- Quotes travel on core NATS. The two JetStream tick streams stored every tick for seven days
  with no consumer, and each publish waited for an acknowledgement. A durable tier, for
  trades, fixings and closes, keeps a stream of its own.
- A time-series table sends no change notification. The signal serves an entity that people
  edit, and a transaction that notifies takes a database-wide lock at commit.
- Every per-class rule is keyed by the instrument type of the datum, with a default row, so a
  new feed needs no code in the pipeline.
- Ticks move to an append-only table. Step 0 measured about 187,000 rows per second for one
  connection, against about 8,300 for the observation table with its trigger.
- A tick has its own read, and the list of observations does not return ticks. A client
  makes two calls, one live subscription and one history call that returns every tick of a
  range in one reply, and it merges them by coordinate and time. The server never merges
  history with the live stream, and a reply has no pages. The design is in
  =doc/knowledge/architecture/a_chart_reads_a_series_in_two_calls.org=.
- The design is in =doc/knowledge/architecture/tick_ingest_pipeline.org=. Step 1 is built.
  Steps 2 and 3 are the tasks below.
