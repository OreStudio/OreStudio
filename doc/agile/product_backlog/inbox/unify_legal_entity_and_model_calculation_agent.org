#+title: Unify legal entity identity and model the calculation agent
#+description: Two related gaps found while documenting trade modelling: a trade role cannot name one of our own legal entities, so the common case of the booking party being calculation agent is unrepresentable; and the dispute consequence that follows from designating a calculation agent is recorded nowhere.
#+type: capture
#+level: cross
#+filetags: :trading:refdata:identity:
#+created: 2026-09-16
#+updated: 2026-09-16

This page is a [[id:671F18E4-E09C-4B3B-BD24-D33DF8AE38A6][capture]] in the *inbox* bucket of the product backlog — a pre-sprint idea, not yet pulled into a sprint as a story.

* What

Two related gaps in how a trade names the parties to it, found while
documenting trade modelling. They are captured together because the
second cannot be modelled cleanly until the first is decided.

*Legal entity identity is split in two.* =party= holds "an internal
legal entity participating in financial transactions" and
=counterparty= holds "an external trading partner". They are separate
tables with separate identity, joined by
=party_counterparty_junction=, which records only which counterparties
a party can see.

*The calculation agent cannot be one of our own entities.*
=trade_party_role= assigns a role on a trade as =(trade_id,
counterparty_id, role)=, and =party_role_type= already seeds
=CalculationAgent=, described as "determines fixings and payments".
But =counterparty_id= is non-nullable with a hard foreign key to
=ores_refdata_counterparties_tbl=, so only an external party can fill
a role. The common case, in which the booking entity is the
calculation agent, is unrepresentable.

* Why

Control of a trade has a legal consequence, and the notes state it
directly: "choosing the booking party means that in case of a
calculation dispute, the counterparty will not have the rights to
dispute the calculations done". The designation determines who may
challenge a valuation, so failing to record it correctly is not a
cosmetic gap.

The notes give three cases and a fourth arrangement.

- Control sits with the party owning the booking system.
- Control sits with the counterparty.
- Control sits with a third party.
- Control is joint between a pair of parties, or follows the ISDA
  Master Agreement, which supplies fallbacks where either party
  disputes the calculations.

Only the second and third are expressible today. The first requires a
role to name a =party=. Joint control is structurally possible,
because the indexes on =trade_party_role= are not unique and two
=CalculationAgent= rows would insert, but nothing declares whether two
rows mean joint control or a data error, so no consumer can rely on
it. The dispute fallback itself has no home at all.

The two gaps meet in the first case. Whether a role can name one of
our own entities depends on whether the split between =party= and
=counterparty= is kept, worked around, or removed.

** Why unification is worth analysing rather than assuming

A legal entity is a legal entity. Internal and external describe our
relationship to one, not a property of the entity itself, and the same
entity can be both: an inter-entity trade faces a legal entity of our
own group, which is external to the booking entity and internal to the
firm. [[id:03C12558-69AC-4925-AAE1-85FFF75B10BA][Trade classification]] makes that argument on the
trade side, where =inter_entity= is a first-class value precisely
because the distinction is a matter of degree.

Against that, the split is not arbitrary. The two tables carry
different attributes today, and unification touches
=party_counterparty_junction= and everything reading either table, so
the analysis has to establish that the shared identity earns its cost.

* Candidate approaches

Recorded to frame the analysis, not to pre-empt it.

| Approach | Effect | Cost |
|----------+--------+------|
| Exclusive-or reference | =trade_party_role= gains a nullable =party_id= beside a nullable =counterparty_id=, with a check that exactly one is set | Small; keeps both hard foreign keys, but every reader must handle the alternation |
| Party reference added | A role row may point either way, with no constraint | Smaller, and weaker: nothing prevents both or neither |
| Unified legal entity | One identity for any legal entity, with internal and external becoming a relationship rather than a table | Largest; touches the junction and every consumer of either table |

The dispute fallback is a separate decision in each case. It qualifies
the calculation agent role rather than the trade, so it belongs on the
role row rather than on the trade, in the same way that a framework's
qualifying flag belongs on a sensitivity rather than in the taxonomy.

* Acceptance sketch

- The three control cases from the notes, plus joint control, are each
  representable and distinguishable.
- Whether two =CalculationAgent= rows mean joint control is declared,
  and enforced rather than left to convention.
- The dispute fallback is recorded where a reader can find it from the
  trade.
- A decision on the =party= and =counterparty= split is recorded with
  its reasoning, whether or not it changes.
