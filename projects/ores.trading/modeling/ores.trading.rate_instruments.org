One row per interest-rates trade, the family's own header. It holds what
every product in the family states the same way: the trade it belongs to,
the trade type code, the party, the activity that wrote the version, the
instrument's start and maturity dates and its description. It holds no
product economics.

The header exists for two reasons. It is the family's *coverage*: the
=trade_type_code= check that the routed-instrument template generates from
=ores.trading.trade_type_catalogue= lists exactly the codes routed here, so
the family's codes sit in one table and no product table repeats the
identity columns. And it is the family's *owner*: the =* Delete cascade=
section below names every table the family writes, so the delete rule closes
the legs, leg children and facts with the header instead of leaving them
behind (finding R6 of the family's design).

The products' own fields stay in their fact tables, keyed by the same trade
and joined to this row by the trade id, exactly as =bond_instruments= joins
its facts. A fact table carries no =party_id= and no =trade_type_code=: the
header is the family's party boundary and its routing boundary, so a fact
row reaches its tenant and its code through the header (design decision D3).

The dates are the family's *common dates*. Each product used to state them
under its own name — =start_date=, =maturity_date=, =expiry_date=,
=end_date= — so nothing could read one date column across the family
(finding R9). The two columns here are the one set of names; a product that
means something else by a date keeps that date in its own fact table.

=InflationSwap= is a member until an inflation family is scaffolded. The
taxonomy already calls it an inflation product, and its columns move here
with the rest of the family's so that its rows are owned and routed like
every other rates row. It leaves with its own family's rework.

* Delete cascade

The rows a rates instrument owns. Each is keyed by the instrument's own
trade id, so the delete rule closes them all with one statement per table
and the family does not outlive its header.

The shared children the family writes — the strikes, the schedules and the
option block — are the shapes the bond pilot built, and the family writes to
them rather than widening them.

** Pinned keys

The header copies the anchor's party and trade type, so the insert trigger
checks the copies against the anchor's current row and refuses a row that
disagrees. The anchor is a versioned table, so a database foreign key cannot
name it and the pin is a trigger check, as the =trade_id= key is. The fact and
child tables carry neither column: they reach both through this row (design
decision D3), so the header is the only row of the family that can disagree.

The insert trigger stamps =party_id= from the session, and row-level
security hides another party's anchor, so in practice the pin refuses a
disagreeing =trade_type_code=; the party column is held to the anchor as
well, so a path that bypasses the stamp cannot drift from it.

The anchor's model states that its party and trade type are fixed for the
trade's life and that a change is a cancel and rebook. The anchor's write
path does not enforce it, so an amendment that changed the trade type would
leave the header unchecked. That gap belongs to the trade model, not to this
family, and it is open.

| name   | columns                           | table                  | target_columns            | error_message                                                          |
|--------+-----------------------------------+------------------------+---------------------------+------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| anchor | trade_id, party_id, trade_type_code | ores_trading_trades_tbl | id, party_id, trade_type | Invalid trade_id: %. The party and trade type must be the trade's own. |

The tenant isolation and the party isolation policies are hand-written in
=trading_rls_policies_create.sql=, as =bond_instruments=' are: the header
carries =party_id=, so it needs the restrictive party policy beside the
tenant policy, and the generated =:rls_tenant_isolation:= form states the
tenant policy alone.

* C++

The C++ domain class nests the scaffolding columns and the columns marked
=:group: identity= below into an =instrument_identity identity;= member
(see the ores.trading.instrument_identity field-group model), the implicit
audit columns fold into an =ores::dq::domain::audit_record audit;= member
(see the ores.dq.audit_record field-group model), and the dates and the
description stay flat in between. The SQL schema, DB entity and column
lists are unaffected. The entity templates consume these annotations;
domain and repository profiles regenerate correctly.
