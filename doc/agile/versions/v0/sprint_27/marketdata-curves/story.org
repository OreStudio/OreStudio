** Goal

Make a curve a thing ores.marketdata owns. Today a curve exists only as a
side effect of one service: a `market_series` row stamped
=derivation_kind='IR_CURVE_BOOTSTRAP'= with one =market_observation= per
pillar. Nothing gives it identity, nothing records what it was built from,
nothing tells a build apart from an observation, and nothing reads it back.

The outcome this story delivers: a curve can be created, identified, read
and asked about its provenance, without the reader having to know that it is
stored as a series.

** What exists today

The findings below came from a read-only investigation. Each is grounded in
code; the paths are the evidence.

**A curve is a derived series, and nothing else.** A curve is created by
=curve_republish_service::republish()= in
=projects/ores.marketdata/service/src/app/curve_republish_service.cpp= (note
=service/=, not =core/=). It stamps the pre-minted output series
=derivation_kind = "IR_CURVE_BOOTSTRAP"= (:182), calls
=quant::curve_bootstrap_engine::bootstrap()= (:224), and writes one
=market_observation= per pillar (:296) plus =observation_lineage= rows
(:299). Derivation kinds are seeded in
=projects/ores.sql/populate/refdata/refdata_derivation_kinds_populate.sql=:
=OBSERVED=, =IR_CURVE_BOOTSTRAP=, =CRM_DERIVATION=.

**There is no curve table, no curve entity and no curve type.** The
component's seven tables are series, the asset-class junction, observations,
fixings, observation lineages, feed bindings and classification rules.
=market_series= has no curve column: =marketdata_market_series_create.sql=
carries only the key columns, =series_subclass=, and the derivation markers.
=oresmd= declares =instrument_type::curve=, and no line of code references
it.

**A build is indistinguishable from an observation by shape.** There is no
=reference_date= and no =build_kind= anywhere. The only markers are
=derivation_kind= on the *series* and a =source= string of the form
="ir_curve_bootstrap:<config id>"= on the *observation*. The seeded example
=marketdata_market_series_fomc_populate.sql= shows the intended pairing: a
=RATES=/=YIELD= input grid and a =YieldCurve=/=DISCOUNT= output series for
the same qualifier.

**The recipe lives in refdata, not marketdata.**
=ir_curve_bootstrap_config= plus =ir_curve_bootstrap_pillar= hold the
interpolation method, day-count convention, split tenor, curve family role
(FUNDING or PROJECTION) and the ordered pillar list.
=source_series_id= and =output_series_id= are *soft* references into
marketdata's =market_series= — no foreign key, because the tables are in
different components. A database check requires
=source_series_id <> output_series_id=, so the first republish cannot
reclassify the raw feed's own series and overwrite live data.

**Nothing consumes a curve.** =curve_health_checker= has no production
callers at all. =interpolate_discount_factor= is called only from inside the
engine and its tests. The curve_snapshot reads
(=marketdata.v1.curve-snapshot.get= and =.buckets=) are thin wrappers over
=read_as_of= on one series' observations, so they work for any series and do
not know a curve when they read one. The bootstrapped output series is
write-only today.

**A curve is reachable only by hand-crafted NATS.** The two requests
=republish_curve_request= and =compute_curve_request= are registered at
=application.cpp:202,208=, with permissions
=marketdata::curve_bootstrap:republish= and =:compute=. There is no shell
verb, no HTTP route, no web client and no scheduler job that publishes on
either subject. The recipe table has no editing UI either: the model records
that its real editor, =CurveBuilderWorkbench=, lived in the removed desktop
client.

**The design for doing this properly exists and was abandoned.** Sprint 25's
=adopt-oresmd-native-market-data-identity= story proposed =curve_build= and
=curve_point= tables — a build is one observation of a curve entity, and its
points hang off it. That story was abandoned at sprint close on 2026-09-23
and nothing implements it. The classes survive in
=ores.marketdata.puml= under a "Target model" legend, marked =<<new>>=.

**Three vocabularies are called a role, and two of them are called
=curve_role=.** refdata's =curve_role= is DEPOSIT/FRA/SWAP/NONE and
classifies an *instrument code*, not a curve; oresmd's =curve_role= is
discount/projection/self_discounting; and
=ir_curve_bootstrap_config.curve_family_role= is FUNDING/PROJECTION, where
FUNDING does not mean discount. oresmd's =curve_role= is inert — parsed,
carried, re-serialised, and branched on nowhere. Its one behavioural
consumer in the whole tree is =feeds_conflict()= in
=ores.synthetic/service/src/feed_controller.hpp=.

**Nothing crosses the ORE boundary in either direction.** ORE builds a curve
in memory for the duration of a run from =curveconfig.xml= (which quote keys,
conventions, interpolation), =market.txt= (the values) and =todaysmarket.xml=
(which to build). None of that configuration is stored here: the only reader
of ORE's =curveconfiguration= model is a round-trip fidelity test. The
quote-to-curve wiring itself — ORE's =Simple=, =AverageOIS=, =TenorBasis=,
=CrossCurrency=, =ZeroSpread= segtypes — has no counterpart in this
repository. Going the other way, =report_package_handler::prepare_package=
describes itself as a stub that packs raw MsgPack rather than an ORE input
package, =collect_results= is a pass-through stub, and
=ores.ore/core/src/xml/document_kind.cpp= does not even classify a
=curveconfig.xml= dropped into an import.

under =external/ore/= ships examples, XSDs and prebuilt engine packages but

** Deeper analysis, 2026-10-06

A second read-only pass, at =d02032a18c=. It answers the open questions far
enough to choose a direction. It is a snapshot, not a description of the
system. Two facts the first investigation stated are corrected here, because
they change the shape of the answer.

*** What a curve is made of

Four things, and the tree already holds three of them under other names.

- *Identity*: which curve this is. =market_series= is already the identity of
  a term structure; the oresmd URI is the name. The seeded example is
  =oresmd://ir/USD?type=series&instrument=discount&quote=rate&curve_id=USD-SOFR-FOMC=
  (=(marketdata_market_series_fomc_populate.sql)=). There is no
  =instrument_type::curve= in code: the only =instrument_type= enum is ORE's
  own MarketDatum list (=api/include/ores.marketdata.api/datum/ore_types.hpp=),
  and the ={fixing, curve, quote, vol}= enum is a design-file fiction. A curve
  is named by =curve_id= in the URI today, not by an instrument type.
- *Points*: the pillar-to-value map. These are =market_observation= rows. The
  point identity is the =oresmd_uri=; **the observation table has no
  =point_id= column** (the design file draws one; the model does not). The
  pillar's tenor lives inside the URI.
- *Build*: when the curve was built against which source, and by which recipe.
  This is the part that is only half present. =market_series= carries
  =derivation_kind= and =derivation_config_id/version=; =observation_lineage=
  carries, per point, =derivation_config_id/version=, =source_as_of= and
  =source_series_ids=. What is missing is a *reference date* and a *build
  kind*, and a build identity that groups the points of one build.
- *Recipe*: how to build it. This lives in =ores.refdata= as
  =ir_curve_bootstrap_config= (family role, discount curve for a projection,
  interpolation, day count, tenor convention, split tenor) plus
  =ir_curve_bootstrap_pillar= rows. Its =output_series_id= and the pillars'
  tenor codes are *soft* references into other components.

*** The correction that matters: lineage is most of the build

The first investigation read the build as entirely absent. It is not:
=observation_lineage= is written by =republish()= beside every output
observation (=curve_republish_service.cpp:365-366=) and already answers "what
was this built from, by which recipe, and against what as-of". A build header
therefore does not have to invent provenance; it has to *group* the points of
one build and add the two facts lineage lacks, =reference_date= and
=build_kind=. That is a much smaller table than the sprint 25 design assumed.

*** The second correction: the point identity is the URI

The sprint 25 =curve_point= design draws a =point_id= column. The shipped
observation table has none; the point identity is the whole =oresmd_uri=. Any
curve design either reads the pillar identity out of the URI or adds a real
=point_id=/tenor column. The first is free; the second must be justified.

*** How each part maps onto the existing infrastructure

| Curve part | Existing home | State |
|------------+---------------+-------|
| Identity | =market_series= + oresmd URI =curve_id= | Exists; the curve is not *marked* as a curve beyond =derivation_kind= and =series_subclass= |
| Points | =market_observation= (=oresmd_uri=, =value=, =observation_datetime=) | Exists, bitemporal, versioned, RLS, evented |
| Point provenance | =observation_lineage= | Exists per point |
| Build identity and reference date | none | Missing |
| Build kind (bootstrap / derived / imported) | none | Missing (=IR_CURVE_BOOTSTRAP= sits on the series, not the build) |
| Recipe | =ir_curve_bootstrap_config= + =_pillar= in =ores.refdata= | Exists, cross-component soft refs |
| Recipe editing | refdata shell CRUD units | Exists for the recipe; no curve verb |
| Compute / publish | =marketdata.v1.curve_bootstrap.compute= / =.republish= | Exists server-side; no sender anywhere |
| Read back | =marketdata.v1.curve-snapshot.get= / =.buckets= | Exists; the handler checks no permission and no code is seeded |
| Consumer | none | Missing |

*** The modelling decision

Three shapes are available. The recommendation is the first, with the third
held open until a journey proves it.

1. *Keep the series as the identity, add a build header, keep the points as
   observations.* A =curve_build= row names the output series, the
   =reference_date=, the =build_kind=, the recipe (=config id + version=) and
   the =source_as_of=; the points stay =market_observation= rows whose
   =observation_datetime= is the reference date and whose =oresmd_uri= is the
   pillar. This reuses bitemporality, versioning, row-level security,
   eventing, shell units, the protocol and the TypeScript twin, and adds one
   small table. It matches what =republish()= already writes, so no data has
   to move.
2. *A =curve_point= table beside =curve_build=.* The sprint 25 design. It buys
   an explicit point-to-build link and a place to hang point-level
   trustworthiness fields, and it pays for it with a second bitemporal,
   versioned, evented, shell-served table that duplicates the observation
   stack. Justified only if a consumer needs point sets that are independent
   of the series' own history, such as two builds compared point by point.
3. *A =curve= entity distinct from =market_series=.* Rejected as the starting
   point: it duplicates the identity, versioning and history the series
   already owns. The series is the term structure; a curve is a series whose
   points are pillars.

The deciding input is *what a consumer does with a curve*, which is what the
journeys are for. The design deliberately stops before =curve_point= and lets
the journey work decide whether it is earned.

*** Curves and tenors

The relation is real but undeclared today, in three places.

- *The recipe side.* =ir_curve_bootstrap_pillar.start_tenor_code= and
  =.end_tenor_code= are undeclared soft references to =refdata_tenors=: no
  foreign key, and the only database check validates =curve_role_code=, not
  the tenors. The engine resolves a pillar by
  =end_tenor_code -> tenors_by_code -> resolution(convention) -> anchor +
  offset [+ schedule steps] -> date= (=tenor_resolution.cpp:151-222=), and the
  end tenor doubles as the =point_id= the resolver reads the quote under
  (=curve_republish_resolver.cpp:90-92=). A typo in a tenor code is a
  runtime throw, not a save-time refusal.
- *The point side.* The observation's =oresmd_uri= carries the term
  coordinate (=datum::term=: period, date, fx_tenor or continuation), and the
  oresmd =term= field is the canonical datum-side name for a tenor. The two
  vocabularies join only by string at the pillar bridge; nothing checks that
  the URI's term is the pillar's tenor.
- *The gaps.* Tenor resolution never rolls for a business day (the
  conventions carry no calendar column), so a pillar that lands on a holiday
  is not adjusted; =split_tenor_code= is stored but read by no consumer logic;
  and =dataset_family_role=FUNDING/PROJECTION= is the one role vocabulary that
  is live, while refdata's =curve_role= is hard-coded in the engine and
  oresmd's =curve_role= is inert outside the synthetic feed's conflict check.

The recommendation is to make the pillar's tenor a checked reference to
=refdata_tenors=, and to carry that same tenor code on the curve's points so a
reader can group by tenor without parsing the URI. The business-day gap and
the split tenor are separate defects, not part of the representation.

*** The user journeys are the input that decides this

The tree has no market-data journey group and no journey that owns a curve;
the closest analogues are =journey_keep_a_holiday_calendar_right= (rules, then
a derived artefact consumers read) and =journey_add_or_correct_a_currency=
(entity with owned child panels and history). Curve creation, review and
consumption have no owner, and the journey-extraction task for marketdata is
still =BACKLOG=. The candidates a journey pass should settle, one person and
one goal each, are:

| Candidate journey | Person | First consumer it creates |
|-------------------+--------+---------------------------|
| Build a curve for a currency and a date | Rates quant or market data engineer | The =republish= sender the server has never had |
| Keep a curve recipe right | Market data engineer | The recipe editor the desktop client took away |
| Read a curve and trust it | Rates quant | The curve read-back: points, shape, provenance |
| Correct a curve point | Market data engineer | Point-level override and re-publish |
| See how a curve changed | Risk reviewer or auditor | Build history and build-to-build comparison |
| Take a curve to ORE, and bring one back | Platform | The lossless =curveconfig= / =market= round trip |

They settle the model because each names an operation the server must serve:
a build journey needs a build, a comparison journey is what earns
=curve_point=, and a provenance journey decides which of
=curve_point_provenance='s fields are worth storing. They also force the
vocabulary question, because the actor classes in the journeys are
=TenantAdmin= and =Operations= today and neither is a rates quant.

** Curve data model, 2026-10-06

A design pass against the existing tables and the
[[id:BBD43BF2-9FEE-42B0-A667-9468AF759DC2][data-oriented design]] criteria, at
=d02032a18c=. It answers one question: which curve tables does the model need,
and how do they join to the ones already there? The joins below were run
against the recreated database.

*** The joins a curve needs already exist

A curve is a =market_series= whose oresmd URI names it. Its points are
=market_observation= rows of that series. Its inputs are per-point
=observation_lineage= rows. Its recipe is the refdata config and its pillars.
None of this needs a new key.

| Hop | From | To | Key | Note |
|-----+------+----+-----+------|
| Recipe to curve | =ir_curve_bootstrap_configs.output_series_id= | =market_series.id= | soft, no FK | cross-component; verified, one row |
| Curve to points | =market_series.id= | =market_observations.series_id= | hard FK | indexed; points are the curve |
| Points to inputs | =(series_id, observation_datetime, oresmd_uri)= | =observation_lineage= same triple | soft, natural key | per-point recipe and sources |
| Pillars to tenors | =ir_curve_bootstrap_pillars.end_tenor_code= | =refdata_tenors.code= | soft, no FK | verified for the seeded pillars |
| Point to tenor | =market_observations.oresmd_uri= coordinate | =refdata_tenors.code= | by code, via the URI codec | the coordinate *is* the pillar's end tenor |

The point-to-tenor hop is the one that has no column. That is deliberate: the
component removed its decomposed key columns and made the URI the single
identity, and the model says a reader that wants the key's parts reads them
through =oresmd_uri_codec= rather than off a row
(=ores.marketdata.market_series.org=). Adding a =tenor= column to
=market_observation= would reverse that decision, and it would pollute a
generic time-series table with one facet's coordinate. The relation is instead
carried where it is authoritative: by the recipe's pillar, whose tenor must be
validated (see below), with the point's coordinate equal to it by construction
(=curve_republish_service.cpp= passes =point.point_id=, the end tenor, to
=datum_at_point=).

*** The decision: the values add no table; the shape and the provenance do

The facts a build needs are already stored, or are derivable:

| Fact | Where it is today |
|------+-------------------|
| Which curve | =market_series.oresmd_uri= (=curve_id=) |
| When the curve answers | =market_observations.observation_datetime= for its points |
| When the build ran, and its versions | the observation rows' bitemporal =valid_from= |
| Which recipe, and its version | =market_series.derivation_config_id/version=, and =observation_lineage= per point |
| What it was built from | =observation_lineage.source_series_ids= and =source_as_of= |
| Which values are quoted, interpolated or overridden | not stored; the =curve_point_provenance= proposal |

A build is therefore the =(series, observation_datetime)= slice of a curve
series' observations, and two builds are told apart by that slice, by the
bitemporal version within a slice, and by the recipe version. No table is
needed to represent that, and adding one would denormalise a fact the
observation hypertable already holds: the table is append-only, partitioned
on =observation_datetime=, carries no audit payload, and gives identity by
key. That is the shape a data-oriented model wants for the points.

The cold tables the requirements do imply are two, and neither is a second
representation of the points:

- the *shape* tables, which state the object's axes and their ordered values
  so a hole is a hole (see *Where is the shape?* below), and
- the *per-point provenance* annex, keyed by the point and holding no value
  (see *Manually keyed points* below).

Both are cold data split away from the hot row, which is what the
data-oriented criteria ask for.

*** If a first-class build is needed, it is a cold header

One consumer type would earn a table: a journey that treats a build as an
object in its own right, with a state, a reviewer or a sign-off, rather than
as a slice of a series. The prior Qt surface had exactly that shape, *Build &
Diagnostics* then *Publish*, and the review/sign-off task is still a recorded
intention. If that journey is agreed, the table is small and cold, and it
joins without touching the points:

#+begin_example
ores_marketdata_curve_builds_tbl
  id               uuid      -- surrogate key
  tenant_id        uuid      -- row-level security
  party_id         uuid      -- ownership, natural key part
  version          integer   -- bitemporal version
  valid_from       timestamptz
  valid_to         timestamptz
  series_id        uuid      -- FK -> ores_marketdata_market_series_tbl(id)
  reference_date   timestamptz  -- the instant the curve answers at; the join key
  build_kind       text      -- BOOTSTRAP | DERIVED | IMPORTED, validation fn
  source_as_of     timestamptz  -- when the inputs were observed
  -- the standard record provenance: modified_by, performed_by,
  -- change_reason_code, change_commentary
natural key:  (party_id, series_id, reference_date, build_kind)
unique index: (tenant_id, party_id, series_id, reference_date, build_kind) current only
index:        (tenant_id, party_id, series_id, reference_date desc)
FK:           series_id -> market_series(id), list_by, order reference_date desc
#+end_example

Its one join is =market_observations.series_id = builds.series_id AND
market_observations.observation_datetime = builds.reference_date=, which the
existing =observations_series_datetime= index already serves. The recipe and
the inputs stay where they are: =market_series.derivation_config_id= for the
build, =observation_lineage= per point. The build table carries only what
neither holds, and it stays off the hot path: the kernel reads the points, not
the header.

The recommendation is to *not* create it until that journey is agreed, and to
write the journey first if it is. This is the one place the curve model may
add a table, and the journeys decide it.

*** The data-oriented layout

The three regimes get three shapes, and the storage shape is already the
right one.

- *Storage (Regime 2).* The points are rows, not a blob: identity by key
  (=series_id=, =observation_datetime=, =oresmd_uri=), table-shaped children,
  core state split from the cold =observation_lineage= annex, batched access
  through one repository call. The one decision to keep is that a curve must
  not be stored as a serialised snapshot on a single row: that would break
  bitemporality, the as-of read and the eventing that every other series has.
- *Kernel (Regime 1, the bootstrap and any valuation).* The existing
  =read_as_of= returns an array of observation objects, each holding its value
  as text and its coordinate inside a URI string. A kernel should not walk
  that. Add one read that projects the same rows into arrays:

  #+begin_example
  struct curve {
      boost::uuids::uuid series_id;
      std::chrono::system_clock::time_point as_of;
      std::vector<std::chrono::sys_days> pillar_dates;   // resolved tenors
      std::vector<double>                discount_factors;
  };
  curve read_curve(context, series_id, as_of);           // one query, one parse
  #+end_example

  The value text is parsed once at the boundary, the tenors are resolved once,
  and the kernel sees stride-1 spans. No per-point object, no variant, no
  parse in the loop. =read_as_of= stays for the generic reader; =read_curve= is
  the kernel projection of the same rows, not a second source of truth.
- *Wire (Regime 3).* The curve read is one batched message carrying parallel
  arrays, not one message per point, and the existing
  =marketdata.v1.curve-snapshot.get= is reshaped to it rather than joined by a
  second subject.

*** Does the storage hold up for the evolution read?

It does. The composite object is a series; its nodes are the series' point
observations at each instant; the hypertable already stores them that way, and
=read_as_of_buckets= already reconstructs snapshots. Nothing about the
evolution requirement needs a new table:

- Discovery by typed attributes — currency pair, object kind, party — is a
  *wire* and *service* concern: the request carries typed fields and the
  server resolves them to the series URI. If that lookup needs an index rather
  than a scan, the index goes on a derived projection of the URI's parts, not
  on a new table.
- Coordinate typing is a *read* concern: the server parses each node's
  =oresmd_uri= once through the codec to produce the coordinate labels and to
  align the matrix. The identity stays the URI, as the component decided.
- Alignment, shape change and the instant range are the evolution read's
  design, not the schema's.

What the evolution requirement does change is the priority: the aligned
matrix read is the first thing to build, and the single-curve build journey is
not.

** The composite object is the requirement, 2026-10-06

A person does not ask for a series or a point. They ask for a *term structure*
or a *surface* — a composite object with named axes — and then they ask how it
*changed*. "Show me the FX vol surface for EURUSD over the last quarter" is
the canonical question, and the acceptance below did not ask it: it asked for
a curve's identity, one build and one consumer. Evolution, surfaces and axes
were absent, and the word *surface* did not appear.

*** Today's answer to the canonical question, and why it is not easy

The storage supports the question. An FX vol surface is one =market_series=
whose points carry =(expiry, strike_label)= coordinates — the =fx_option= row
in =datum/schema.hpp= — and the observation hypertable is append-only and
partitioned on time. The *retrieval* does not:

- *Discovery needs the URI string.* Both reads are keyed by =oresmd_uri=
  (=get_curve_snapshot_buckets_request.oresmd_uri=). "The FX vol surface for
  EURUSD" must be turned into the exact oresmd string by the caller; nothing
  indexes the identity's parts or lists the surfaces of a currency pair.
- *Evolution is a fixed window ending now.* The one evolution read takes
  =bucket_seconds= and =bucket_count= (=5= buckets of =1800s= by default) and
  ends at the present. There is no time range and no set of instants, so "over
  the last quarter" is not expressible.
- *The result is not aligned.* =get_curve_snapshot_buckets_response.buckets=
  is a =vector<vector<market_observation>>=: the client matches nodes across
  buckets itself, and each value is text. Nothing returns the surface as a
  matrix of instants by nodes.
- *Shape change is undefined.* When the surface gains an expiry or loses a
  strike between two instants, the buckets simply hold different point sets.
  Nothing reports an added, dropped or persistent node.
- *The object has no type.* A curve, a surface and a grid are all "a series"
  with different coordinate shapes; nothing states which series are surfaces,
  or what their axes are. A generic evolution view cannot offer "all the vol
  surfaces for EURUSD" because it cannot tell one composite object from
  another.

So the data model does *not* make the FX vol surface evolution easy to
retrieve, and the reason is the requirements, not the storage. The storage
already holds the right rows; the reads and the acceptance never ask the
question in the person's terms.

*** Where is the shape? There is no shape table, and there must be

The question is the right one, and the answer today is: nowhere. The seven
marketdata tables hold the series identity, the points, the fixings, the
asset-class junction, the lineage, the feed bindings and the classification
rules. None of them says which tenors, expiries or strikes make up a term
structure. The shape is inferred from the points and nothing else:

- *For an observed structure* the shape is whatever arrived. The set of tenors
  is =SELECT DISTINCT= over the point URIs, so a tenor missing because the feed
  dropped it is indistinguishable from a tenor the structure does not have.
- *For a bootstrapped curve* the shape is the refdata recipe's pillars:
  =ir_curve_bootstrap_pillar= carries =start_tenor_code=, =end_tenor_code= and
  =sequence_index=. That is close, but it is a *build recipe in another
  component*, it exists only for bootstrapped curves, and it describes the
  recipe's inputs rather than the term structure's nodes.
- *The oresmd schema* declares the coordinate *fields* an instrument type
  carries — the axes — but no per-series values, so it cannot say which tenors
  a particular curve comprises.

Without a shape, several of the requirements below cannot be met. A missing
node cannot be drawn as a hole. Completeness cannot be checked. "Which nodes
were added or dropped between two instants" cannot be answered against an
expected axis. And a surface's components cannot be enumerated without
scanning history.

*** The shape is a first-class, cold declaration

The object's shape is its axes and their ordered values:

| Object | Axes | Axis values |
|--------+------+-------------|
| A yield curve | =term= | 1M, 3M, 6M, 1Y, ... |
| An FX vol surface | =expiry=, =strike_label= | the expiries; ATM, 25RR, 25BF |
| A swaption cube | =expiry=, =term=, =strike_level= | ... |

A *node* is one value per axis; the grid is the product of the axes; an
observation is the value at a node; and a node with no observation is a hole,
which is exactly what the rendering, completeness, alignment and component
requirements need. The tables are small and cold:

#+begin_example
ores_marketdata_series_axes_tbl
  series_id   uuid      -- FK -> market_series(id)
  axis_field  text      -- an oresmd field code: term, expiry, strike_label, ...
  sequence    integer
  unique current: (tenant_id, party_id, series_id, axis_field)

ores_marketdata_series_axis_values_tbl
  series_id   uuid
  axis_field  text
  value       text      -- the code or level, validated where a vocabulary exists
  sequence    integer
  unique current: (tenant_id, party_id, series_id, axis_field, value)
#+end_example

Both are cold: tens of rows per object, read once when an axis is drawn or a
build is validated, never on the point path. They join to the series by
=series_id=, and to the points by =(series, axis attribute)= against the
coordinate the read extracts from the point's URI. A point whose coordinate is
not a declared axis value is a defect, and the write-time check that refuses
it is what keeps the shape and the points from drifting.

For a bootstrapped curve the shape and the recipe's pillars are the same tenors
seen twice, so the rule is: the *shape* is the marketdata-side statement of
what the object comprises, and the *recipe* states how each node is built. A
build writes the shape from its pillars when it publishes, so the two cannot
drift; an observed or imported structure writes its shape from what it
declares, with no recipe at all. This is also where the tenor validation
lands: an axis value that is a term must be a =refdata_tenors= code.

*** The components: ATM, RR and BF are the surface's own axis

The vol surface's quoting convention is not a detail beside the surface; it
is one of its axes. The FX option key is
=FX_OPTION/<quote>/unitCcy/ccy/expiry/strike=, and ORE keeps the strike as a
*label*: =ATM=, =25RR=, =25BF= (=ore_key_fx.cpp:56-64=). So ATM, risk
reversal and butterfly are =strike_label= values, one per node, and each one
is a term structure over =expiry= — exactly the three the question names. That
decomposition is already in the data; the model exposes none of it:

- There is no read that returns a component. "The ATM ladder" is every node of
  the surface whose URI carries =ATM=, and the client finds them by parsing
  and filtering.
- The labels are free text with no vocabulary and no validation, so =BF=,
  =FLY= and =25BF= are three spellings the model cannot reconcile, and the
  delta convention they imply is not stored at all.
- There is no "what are the components of this surface" read, so a screen
  cannot offer ATM, 25RR and 25BF without scanning history.
- The evolution read is URI-keyed and windowed, so "the ATM ladder over the
  last quarter" is not expressible either.

This is the same gap as the surface, one axis down, and it argues for the axis
work rather than for a table: the components are slices of the surface's grid,
not independent objects.

*** One series per surface, or one per component?

The question this raises is whether ATM, RR and BF should each be a series of
their own, grouped into a surface, rather than a coordinate of one surface
series. The answer turns on what changes and what is consumed:

- A market quote is a =(pair, expiry, strike label)= point. The grid is one
  object quoted by one convention, and a change to its shape is a point
  change. One series per surface makes that an ordinary bitemporal point
  write.
- One series per component would make a shape change a change of *group
  membership* with its own history, which is a junction with validity rather
  than a point, and it would spread one object's identity across N series.
- The components are almost always read together with the surface, and the
  [[id:71E4EB7A-20EA-46E1-8832-05D103C330EF][curve set]] storage grouping is
  for grouping *independent* series — a funding curve and its projections —
  not for slicing one grid.

So the recommendation stays: the surface is one series; ATM, RR and BF are
values of its strike-label axis, and what they need is a typed, named,
validated axis and a read that returns a slice, not a series and not a table.

*** Manually keyed points

Users key points by hand: a trader's IR level, a broker's vol, a correction
after a bad feed. That is a first-class market-data operation, not an anomaly,
and it needs three facts the model does not store at a point today: that the
value is manual, who keyed it, and when.

Two facts decide the design:

- *The hot row must not carry them.* =market_observation= is
  =no_audit_columns=, append-only, and the hot path. Adding actor and reason
  columns would put cold metadata on every one of its rows and undo the
  hot/cold split the model already has.
- *A manual point is an ordinary bitemporal write.* The value has the same
  shape as any other point, and the table's =soft_update_delete= trigger
  already closes the prior row and inserts the new one. The override therefore
  needs no value storage of its own: the manual write is a normal write, and
  the history keeps both the fed value and the manual one.

What is missing is the point's *provenance*, which is cold and belongs in the
existing per-point annex. =observation_lineage= is keyed by the same triple
and already carries =modified_by=, =performed_by=, =change_reason_code= and
=change_commentary=. What it lacks is the two things a manual point needs in
order to be told from a fed or derived one:

- a *point source kind* — quoted, interpolated, extrapolated and manual (the
  [[id:573F9165-C20B-463C-B0B3-F324AFE4E81F][curve point provenance]]
  proposal's derivation kind, extended with manual), and
- the *actor and time at point granularity*, which the annex has and the
  observation does not.

The clean shape is one per-point cold annex, not a second table keyed the same
way: either broaden =observation_lineage= into the point-provenance annex that
document already proposes, or rename it to say what it now is. Two cold tables
keyed by =(series_id, observation_datetime, oresmd_uri)= would be a smell.

*** Precedence: a feed must not silently undo a manual point

Because the manual write is bitemporal, a later feed tick would close the
manual row and take over, so the precedence is a policy the model must state
rather than a consequence of write order. Either the point is marked locked in
its provenance and the ingest skips a locked point, or the read prefers the
latest manual point over the latest automatic one until the manual point is
cleared. Either way the rule is explicit and testable, and the evolution view
can show the manual segment with its actor and its reason.

*** The requirements the use case implies

1. A composite object is first-class: identifiable and of a kind (term
   structure, surface, grid), with its axes named, before any point is read.
2. Its *shape* is declared: the axes and their ordered values are stored as a
   cold, per-object declaration, so a node with no value is a hole and not an
   absent node, and completeness can be checked.
3. Its nodes have typed coordinates -- terms, expiries, strikes or deltas,
   dimensions -- that can be queried, joined and aligned, not only text inside
   an identity string, and every node coordinate is a value of the declared
   shape.
4. Every axis value is a typed, validated code with a vocabulary, so =ATM=,
   =25RR= and =25BF= are canonical and a component can be named.
5. The components of an object along an axis are retrievable as term
   structures in their own right, over time: "the ATM ladder of the EURUSD
   surface, over the last quarter" is one request.
6. Evolution is a first-class read: one bounded request returns the aligned
   matrix of instants by nodes, with the coordinate labels and the values.
7. Shape change is explicit: the read reports nodes added, dropped and
   persistent against the declared shape.
8. The single-instant read, the slice read and the evolution read share one
   definition of the object, so they cannot disagree.
9. Every node's value carries its provenance per instant, so the view can
   explain a change.
10. A point's source kind is stored per point -- quoted, derived or manual --
    and a manual point names the actor, the time and the reason.
11. A manual point is not silently overwritten by a later automatic write; the
    precedence between the two is explicit.
12. The evolution view distinguishes manual points and shows their actor and
    reason, so a person can see what was keyed by hand.

*** What to add, in order

1. The composite-object identity at the wire boundary: a typed request (asset
   class, scope such as a currency pair, object kind, party) that resolves to
   the series, so "the FX vol surface for EURUSD" needs no hand-built URI.
2. The shape tables: the per-object axes and their ordered values, written
   from the recipe's pillars by a build and from the declaration by an import,
   with every term value validated against =refdata_tenors= (which also closes
   capture =A6472918= on the recipe side) and a write-time check that refuses a
   point whose coordinate is not a declared axis value.
3. The axis values as typed, validated codes: the strike-label vocabulary and
   the quoting convention, so ATM, 25RR and 25BF are canonical and the
   components of a surface are enumerable.
4. The slice read: a component along one axis -- the ATM ladder -- returned as
   a term structure over time, from the same object definition.
5. The aligned evolution read: the object plus a range or a set of instants,
   returning instants by nodes with the coordinate labels and numeric values,
   and the added, dropped and persistent nodes against the declared shape. It
   replaces the fixed five-bucket window and returns a matrix, not arrays of
   objects.
6. The single-instant read reshaped to the same object definition, so the
   three cannot disagree.
7. The per-point provenance annex: the source kind (quoted, derived, manual),
   and for a manual point the actor, the time and the reason, in one cold
   annex keyed by the point's natural key. Name it once -- broaden
   =observation_lineage= or rename it -- rather than adding a second cold
   table beside it.
8. The manual precedence rule: what a later automatic write does to a manual
   point, stated as a policy and covered by a test.
9. Guard the curve-snapshot reads (capture =D9258D1F=).
10. Only then decide the =curve_builds= table, from the agreed journeys.

*** Open questions, answered

1. *How does a curve read its points?* Today =read_as_of= returns an array of
   =market_observation= objects whose value is text and whose coordinate is
   inside a URI (=market_observation_repository.cpp:418-451=). *Decision:* add
   the =read_curve= projection returning parallel arrays, as the story's
   data-oriented layout asks (:445-463=). It is a read over the same rows, not
   a table, and it matches the Regime 1 criteria for SoA and stride-1 access
   (=data_oriented_design.org:162-167=). The engine already consumes a
   =vector<bootstrapped_point>=, so the kernel is one parse away from the
   projection.
2. *An entity or a shaped series?* *Decision:* a shaped series plus the cold
   point annex, not a =curve_point= table. The points must be bitemporal,
   versioned, evented and hypertable-backed exactly as observations already are
   (=create.sql:118-138=); a second point table duplicates that stack, and the
   story already priced it (:233-238=).
3. *Does a curve need its own table when it is one series plus points?* *No.*
   Identity is =market_series.oresmd_uri= (=marketdata_market_series_create.sql:61=),
   and the series already carries a validated taxonomy in =series_subclass=
   (18 seeded codes at =refdata_series_subclass_codes_populate.sql:43-96=,
   including =yield= and =volatility=) and a derivation marker
   (=create.sql:64-66=). A curve-vs-surface kind is the coordinate shape, which
   the read derives from the URI's declared coordinate fields
   (=schema.hpp:304-386=). Reviving a =curve= enum is not needed.
4. *What consumes a curve?* *Nothing in production.* The only caller of
   =curve_republish_service= is its own wire handler
   (=curve_republish_handler.hpp:67-145=, registered at =application.cpp:199-210=),
   and no web route, HTTP route or shell verb sends either subject. The client
   package has no curve call and the shell has no curve command. The engine's
   other callers are its tests, and =curve_health_checker= has no production
   caller at all. *Decision:* record that there is no consumer and keep the
   representation minimal and reversible; the first consumer is the compute or
   the evolution read, built before any table is added.
5. *Does the oresmd =curve= vocabulary become load-bearing?* *Moot.*
   =instrument_type::curve= and =to_curve_key()= were deleted with the
   generated grammar; the URI is the identity. No enum is revived.
6. *Is =IR_CURVE_BOOTSTRAP= the whole story?* *No.* =CRM_DERIVATION= is seeded
   and unused (=refdata_derivation_kinds_populate.sql:44=). *Decision:* put the
   point source kind on the generic annex so CRM and any later derived kind
   reuse it unchanged, which is what that table's own doc says it is for
   (=ores.marketdata.observation_lineage.org:25-30=).
7. *Where does the recipe belong?* *Leave it in =ores.refdata=.* Its config
   already owns CRUD, history and row-level security there
   (=refdata_ir_curve_bootstrap_configs_create.sql:29-33=), and
   =market_series.derivation_config_id= is the established soft reference. A
   move would duplicate refdata and break the soft-reference principle.
   *Decision:* keep it, and close the tenor-validation gap on the recipe side.
8. *How does a curve relate to =feed_binding= and =observation_lineage=?*
   =feed_binding= maps a producer channel name to a party
   (=feed_binding.hpp:65,93=); lineage records the *series* a derivation read
   (=observation_lineage.org:166-177=). *Decision:* a derived curve has no feed
   binding; its inputs are series ids, and =republish()= already writes those
   into =source_series_ids= (=curve_republish_service.cpp:311,354=). No change.

*** The smallest representation that satisfies the acceptance

The acceptance demands four things the model lacks: a point's source kind, a
manual point's author/time/reason, a precedence rule, and one read that returns
a component's ladder over time. Three are provenance and one is a read. None
needs a new table.

*Where the point's provenance lives.* On the existing cold per-point annex,
=ores_marketdata_observation_lineages_tbl=. A point with no annex row is
*quoted*: absence is the default, and quoted ticks keep writing one row to the
hypertable and nothing else, which preserves the hot/cold split the model
already has (=marketdata_observations_create.sql:37-38=). A point with an annex
row is *derived* or *manual*, named by one new column:

#+begin_example
point_source_kind   text   not null   -- 'derived' | 'manual', validated
#+end_example

Everything else the manual point must name is already on that annex: =modified_by=
is who keyed it, =change_reason_code= with =change_commentary= is why, and the
annex's bitemporal =valid_from= is when (=marketdata_observation_lineages_create.sql:66-71=).
The annex already registers a history provider
(=observation_lineage_history_provider_registrar.cpp=, fields at
=observation_lineage_history_field_mapper.cpp:41-62=), so the auditor trail and
the per-point provenance read exist for free once the row is written. Adding a
second cold table keyed by the same triple would be a smell, exactly as the
story says (:682-685=).

Two constraints have to move so a manual row can exist: =derivation_config_id=
and =source_as_of= must become nullable, and the
=jsonb_array_length(source_series_ids) > 0= check must allow an empty array,
because a manual point has no derivation config and no source series
(=create.sql:62-65,80=). These are edits to the entity model
(=ores.marketdata.observation_lineage.org:52-204=) followed by a regenerate,
never a hand edit of the generated SQL or header.

This is the smallest representation that works. It is also the most
data-oriented: cold provenance stays in a cold annex keyed by the point, the
hot observation row is untouched, and no value is duplicated.

*How the precedence rule is enforced and tested.* *Decision: read-time
precedence.* A current annex row with =point_source_kind='manual'= owns its
=(series_id, oresmd_uri)= coordinate for reading until it is cleared.

What an as-of instant means here, stated because it is easy to get backwards.
An as-of timepoint is a *market* instant, and the read for that instant must
take manually keyed points into account. A manual point whose own instant the
snapshot covers belongs in that snapshot whenever it was keyed, this morning or
five years ago: over-keying is an operator correcting what the curve is for
that instant, not a statement about when they said so. So applicability is
decided by the point's instant alone, and the annex's =valid_from= does not
decide it. A manual point whose instant falls after the snapshot's instant
belongs to a later instant and is not part of this snapshot.

The rule is one pure predicate over the kind of the latest automatic value and
the active manual value, applied by =read_as_of= and =read_as_of_buckets=
(=market_observation_repository.cpp:418-516=) as a small overlay over the cold
annex, not a join in the hot loop. Read-time is chosen over a write-time guard
because the manual write is an ordinary bitemporal write
(=create.sql:77-99= closes the prior row and inserts the new one), so the
history keeps the fed value *and* the later feed values, which the acceptance
requires; a write-time skip would discard them. The overlay is over tens of
manual points, not over every tick, so the aligned read stays contiguous.

The test lives in =projects/ores.marketdata/core/tests/repository_market_observation_repository_tests.cpp=,
beside the existing =read_as_of= cases (=:135-226=). It writes a feed point,
writes a manual point for the same coordinate, writes a later feed point, and
asserts =read_as_of= returns the manual value and =read_as_of_buckets= shows it
in every bucket whose boundary is at or after the point's instant, which is the
bucket where it starts to apply. A second case flips the
predicate's preference and asserts the feed value wins, so the test fails when
the rule is reversed, which is the acceptance's own wording.

*What is deliberately not added.* No =curve_build=, no =curve_point=, no
=curve= entity, and no shape table in this task. The build stays the slice
=republish()= already writes (=:324-333=), and identity stays the series. The
declared shape and the aligned read are the first consumer's work; the shape
tables are a named gap with a decision (journey 13) and can be added when the
evolution read's added/dropped/persistent requirement is implemented.

*** The first production consumer

*None exists today, and the acceptance allows the record to say so.* The
bootstrap engine is called by the republish service and by its own tests; the
health checker by its own tests; the snapshot reads by their own handler; and
no sender, page, route or verb reaches the handler. The consequence for the
representation is direct: because no consumer exists, the representation must
stay minimal, reversible and cold, and the first unit of work is the consumer,
not a table. The cheapest real consumer is journey 13's aligned evolution read
over one series, because it needs only the rows and the provenance overlay
above, and it proves the object definition that the single-instant and slice
reads will then share. The compute preview of journey 4 is the second, because
it needs no storage at all.

*** Decisions taken here

1. Keep the series as the identity; add no curve, build or point table.
2. Put the point's source kind on the existing cold annex; quoted is the
   absence of a row.
3. Reuse the annex's record provenance for a manual point's author, time and
   reason; add no fields for them.
4. Enforce the manual precedence at read time with one pure predicate, and test
   it in the existing repository test file so a reversed rule fails.
5. Build the aligned evolution read first; it is the first consumer and the
   first user of the point source kind.
6. Correct the joins record: the curve-to-points hop is soft, not a hard FK.
7. Treat the snapshot-permission defect as closed and the oresmd curve
   vocabulary as deleted; strike both from the open questions.

*** What this pass could not determine

- Whether a consumer wants the *raw* feed value beside an active manual value
  at the same instant. Read-time precedence can return both in one response; a
  journey has to say whether it should. Its answer decides whether the overlay
  is a filter or a second field, not whether a table is needed.
- Whether =curve_build= is earned. That turns on the approval and review
  journey (9-12), which has no owner and no prototype beyond the wireframe. The
  decision to defer it is recorded, not derived from a consumer.
- The rename question: whether the annex should be renamed to say it now
  carries a point's provenance and not only a derivation's lineage. It is a
  mechanical codegen rename with no behavioural effect, and it is left to the
  task that adds the column.
- The exact vocabulary for =point_source_kind=. Settled: the column carries
  =derived= or =manual=, and *quoted is the absence of an annex row*. The
  acceptance's three kinds are all answerable, because a reader classifies a
  point in one step: no annex row means quoted, and a row names the other two.
  Absence carries quoted rather than a third stored value because of the
  hot/cold split. A quoted tick is the common case and writes one row to the
  hot hypertable and nothing else; storing 'quoted' explicitly would write a
  cold annex row for every tick in the system, which is the cost the split
  exists to avoid. The invariant this rests on is that a point a derivation
  created always has an annex row, so absence cannot mean derived. The wider
  set the provenance proposal carries -- interpolated, extrapolated, flattened,
  back-filled -- stays open, and adding it later changes no representation,
  only the check on this one column.

  a feed or an import, and nobody signs it off. It is the evidence.

** Acceptance

Read every bullet in the person's terms: the composite object first, its
evolution second, the representation third.

- *A composite object is first-class.* A term structure or a surface is
  identifiable, and of a kind, with its axes named, without knowing which
  strings to combine. "Is this a curve or a surface" and "which one is this"
  are answerable from the data.
- *Its shape is declared.* The axes and their ordered values are stored, so a
  node with no value is a hole and not an absent node, completeness can be
  checked, and every node's coordinate is a value of the declared shape.
- *Its nodes are typed coordinates.* A node's coordinate tuple — terms,
  expiries, strikes or deltas, dimensions — is queryable and joinable, not
  only text inside an identity string.
- *Its axis values are typed codes.* ATM, 25RR and 25BF are validated
  vocabulary, so a component can be named and reconciled, and the quoting
  convention they imply is recorded.
- *A component is a term structure in its own right.* The ladders along one
  axis — the ATM, RR and BF term structures of a vol surface — are retrievable
  over time in one request, as term structures, not filtered points.
- *Evolution is one bounded read.* Given the object and a time range or a set
  of instants, the server returns the aligned matrix of instants by nodes,
  with the coordinate labels and the values. "The FX vol surface for EURUSD
  over the last quarter" is one request, not a client-side join.
- *Shape change is explicit.* Nodes added, dropped and persistent between
  instants are reported, never silently different point sets under the same
  object.
- *A node's provenance is readable per instant*: what it was built from, by
  which recipe, and when.
- *Staleness is visible whenever a term structure is drawn.* A curve's points
  rarely share one observation instant, so a plot can mix horizons without
  saying so. Every point carries how old it is, and the view shows it, so a
  reader never mistakes a composite for a single market state.
- *A point can be keyed by hand.* A manual point or override is written as an
  ordinary point, and the history keeps the value it replaced.
- *A manual point is attributable.* A point says whether it is quoted, derived
  or manual; a manual one names who keyed it, when and why, so it is never
  indistinguishable from a fed or derived point.
- *A manual point is respected.* A later automatic write does not silently
  undo it; the precedence rule is explicit and tested.
- *The single-instant, slice and evolution reads agree*, because they share
  one definition of the object at an instant.
- At least one production consumer reads the object, or the story says in the
  record why none does.
- The three role vocabularies are either reconciled or documented where a
  reader meets them, because two of them share a name.
- No new name collision between this work and the oresmd layer's existing
  =curve= vocabulary.
- Every market data journey has a plain-HTML prototype, the prototypes are
  listed in one inventory, and the inventory check is green.

** Open questions for the deeper analysis

Answered by the =Deeper analysis, 2026-10-06= section above; the questions
are kept as the record of what was asked.

- **How does a curve read its points?** The grammar story keeps the 45-way
  variation in the datum identity, which is cold, and leaves the hot side
  here: a series' points as parallel arrays per coordinate shape (about six:
  none, term, contract, expiry by strike or delta, maturity by strike, and the
  shape-profile slot), batched, with each point referring to its series by
  index. Curve and surface building should read that shape, per the
  [[id:BBD43BF2-9FEE-42B0-A667-9468AF759DC2][data-oriented design]] criteria
  for kernels.
- **An entity or a shaped series?** Sprint 25 proposed =curve_build= and
  =curve_point= tables. The shipped implementation reused the observation
  tables instead. Which is right, given that a curve's points must be
  bitemporal and versioned the way observations already are?
- **Does a curve need its own table when it is one series plus points?** If
  not, what is the minimal thing that gives it identity — a marker column, a
  reserved =series_type=, a view, or the oresmd URI?
- **What consumes a curve?** Nothing does today. Until something does, every
  representation decision is speculative, so the analysis should establish the
  first real consumer before choosing a shape.
- **Does the oresmd =curve= vocabulary become load-bearing here?**
  =instrument_type::curve= is dead and =to_curve_key()= is gated on
  =type=fixing=. If a curve gains identity, is the URI its identity, and does
  =to_curve_key() then mean what its name says?
- **Is =IR_CURVE_BOOTSTRAP= the whole story?** =CRM_DERIVATION= is seeded and
  unused, so there is at least one other derived kind waiting. Does the shape
  chosen generalise to it?
- **Where does the recipe belong?** It is in refdata today behind soft
  references. Should a curve and its recipe live in one component?
- **How does a curve relate to =feed_binding= and =observation_lineage=?=
  Lineage is documented as deliberately generic, and feed bindings map a raw
  producer channel to a series. A curve's inputs are a series, not a channel.

** Staleness, 2026-10-07

A term structure is drawn from points that rarely share one observation
instant. One pillar may have ticked a minute ago and the next three days ago,
and the plot looks like a single market read when it is a composite of several.
Worse, the mix is usually invisible: a stale pillar's value is a real value, in
the right place, at the wrong time.

The rule: *whenever a term structure is drawn, every point carries how old it
is, and the view shows it.* The reader must be able to tell a curve built from
one market state from a curve stitched across several, without asking.

What each point needs, and where it already is:

| Fact | Source |
|------|--------|
| The instant the point represents | =market_observation.observation_datetime= |
| When that value was recorded | the row's bitemporal =valid_from= |
| Whether it is fed, derived or manual | =point_source_kind= on the annex, or the absence of a row |

Two ages are readable and they answer different questions. *Market staleness*,
the snapshot instant minus the point's instant, says the point belongs to an
older market state than the snapshot claims. *Record staleness*, now minus the
point's =valid_from=, says how long ago the value arrived. A curve read as of
one instant has the first for its spread; a curve read as of now shows the
second. Both are derivable from rows already selected, so no column is added
and no new table is needed.

Proposed encoding, for the prototype to settle:

- Per point: an age band, so a stale pillar is visible without reading a number.
- Per curve: the oldest point's age, and the spread between the oldest and the
  newest, because the spread is what measures the mixing.
- A warning when the spread crosses a threshold the journey states, rather than
  a silent plot.

This is a requirement on the read as well as the drawing: a consumer that
returns points without their instants cannot draw it. The aligned evolution
read is the first place it lands, since that read already returns instants by
nodes, so the age comes with the matrix rather than from a second call.
