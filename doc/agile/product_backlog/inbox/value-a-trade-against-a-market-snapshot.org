* What

The Price tab of the structure page is a valuation of one deal: what the deal is
worth against a market as it stands, read on demand rather than streamed.

The signed-off prototype asks it for four things. A figure for the deal, read
against a market snapshot, with the snapshot's own time stated so a person knows
how fresh it is. The shifts applied to that snapshot named, so the figure is
traceable. No ticking: the number is indicative and Refresh is what reads a newer
snapshot. And a structure valued as a whole while a package is valued leg by leg
and summed, because that is how each is held.

None of that exists. The product has no valuation: nothing values a trade, a leg,
a structure or a portfolio. The reporting side runs ORE over a report, which
answers a different question — a batch of deals, a run, a set of result rows —
and it is not a figure for the deal in front of a person.

* Why

The tab exists, is signed off, and says in its own words that it reads nothing.
That is the honest state and it is also a dead end: no later task in the arc makes
a number appear, so the tab stays a notice for as long as valuation is absent.

It is also the first question a trader asks about a deal they are looking at. The
blotter, the structure page and the trade page all state what a deal is; none of
them says what it is worth.

* Shape of the work

Not designed here, and it is bigger than a tab. It needs a valuation capability
before a screen can show one:

- A market snapshot with an identity and a time, so a figure can name the market
  it was struck against. Published curves have a snapshot read; a deal needs the
  curves, surfaces and fixings its own instruments take, which is a selection
  rather than a lookup.
- A valuation of one deal against that snapshot, with the shifts applied to it.
- A decision about what is valued: the deal as a whole, or its legs and their sum,
  which the prototype already distinguishes by kind.
- Then the tab: the figure, the snapshot and its time, and Refresh reading a newer
  one.

* What done looks like

The Price tab states a figure, the market it was struck against and when that
market was read. A package states one figure per leg and the sum the customer is
quoted. Refresh reads a newer snapshot; nothing ticks.
