* Goal

Three versioned ores.synthetic records hold the whole audit trail: the input snapshot (pair, item, provider, policy and its version, window, inversion, counts, who and when), one point per fixing date (fixing, return, whether it was used and why not), and the calibration (the input it read, method and version, alpha, gamma, beta, persistence, long-run variance, starting variance, likelihood, convergence, admissibility, and a review state of proposed, approved, rejected or superseded with its reason). The calibration policy gains the variance window.

* Acceptance

- The three entities are generated across SQL, domain, repository, protocol, shell and TypeScript, with history for each.
- Checks on write: a calibration's shape is admissible (alpha, beta and alpha plus gamma not negative, persistence below one); its state is one of the four; an approved or rejected state carries a reason; a point that is not used carries a reason.
- At most one approved calibration per pair is current, by a unique index, so approving one supersedes the pair's earlier approval whatever its method; an authored calibration is a record of method authored with no input.

* Plan

Three =ores.synthetic= entities, system-tenant rows under the system party as the
FX spot feeds are (profile =uuid-identified-lookup=, visible to every tenant), and
one column on the calibration policy.

- =variance_calibration_input=: one capture. Natural key: party, fixing URI,
  window end and window length, so a repeat over the same window is a new
  version, not a second row; the item names the pair, so the pair is not in the
  key. Columns: the pair; =fixing_uri= and =provider_code=
  of the item read; =inverted=; =policy_id= and =policy_version= of the policy
  it was read under; =window_days= and =window_end=; =point_count= and
  =used_count=. Who and when are the temporal columns every row carries.
- =variance_calibration_input_point=: one fixing date of an input, foreign key
  to the input. Natural key: party, input, date. Columns: =fixing_date=;
  =fixing_text= as stored; =fixing_value= and =log_return= (in market
  convention, nullable: the first date has no return and an unreadable value
  has neither); =used=; =exclusion_reason=, which a point not used must carry.
- =variance_calibration=: one fit or authored shape. Columns: the pair, so the
  approval index needs no join; =input_id= (nil for an authored shape, a check
  ties the two); =method= (=gjr_garch_qmle= or =authored=) and
  =method_version=; =alpha=, =gamma=, =beta=, =persistence=,
  =long_run_variance=, =starting_variance_ratio=; =log_likelihood=,
  =returns_used=, =starts=, =starts_agreed= (fit evidence, nullable or zero for
  an authored shape); =state= (=proposed=, =approved=, =rejected=, =superseded=)
  and =state_reason=. A rejected fit may be inadmissible, so the admissibility
  check applies to the other three states only.
- =calibration_policy.variance_window_days=, default 1826, between 30 and 3660,
  the window the feed's own =garch_window_days= held.

The capture, fit, review and application operations are later tasks; this task
adds the records, their checks and their generated stack.

* Result

Three =ores.synthetic= records hold the audit trail of a variance calibration, each
generated across SQL, domain, repository, protocol, shell and TypeScript with its
history: =variance_calibration_input=, keyed by party, fixing URI and window;
=variance_calibration_input_point=, one fixing date of an input, used or left out
with its reason; and =variance_calibration=, a fitted or authored shape with its
evidence and its review state. The calibration policy gains
=variance_window_days=, five years by default. The records are system-tenant rows
that every tenant reads, as the feeds are; people get read access only, so the
capture, fit and review operations of the next tasks are the only writers.

Each bound is written so that a missing value or NaN fails it, because a check
passes on null and PostgreSQL orders NaN above every number. One approved
calibration of a pair is current, by a partial unique index.

Measured on a recreated database at this branch: =ores.synthetic.api.tests= (186
calibration cases), =ores.shell.synthetic.tests= (102) and
=ores.synthetic.core.tests=, with the three eventing round trips, pass;
=synthetic_variance_calibration_checks_test.sql= passes 29 of 29, and the six
other synthetic pgTAP files pass, 69 tests in all. A first run showed the
generator could not satisfy a natural key that held the validated currency codes,
so the pair left the input's key; the fixing URI already names the pair.
