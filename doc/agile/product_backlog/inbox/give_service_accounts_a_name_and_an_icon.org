* What

A service account carries data that says what the service is, as a person's
account does:

- *A human readable name*, such as "Analytics Service", in the account's
  =full_name=, rather than only the username =ores_<env>_analytics_service=.
- *A picture*, which for a service is an icon specific to that service. The
  icon comes from the project's own icon sets rather than a new drawing, so a
  service looks like the rest of the interface.

The service status screens will use both, so a person reads "Analytics
Service" with its icon, not a database role name.

The super administrator and tenant administrator pictures are revisited in the
same pass, so the seeded accounts look consistent with each other and with
the services. They are photographs today, vendored as
=external/avatars/super_admin_avatar.png= and =tenant_admin_avatar.png=.

To work out:

- Where the name and the icon are declared. The service registry already
  states each service once, and generates the service accounts and roles from
  it, so it is the natural home for both.
- How an icon reaches an account's picture: the administrators' pictures are
  an =assets.system_avatars= dataset published to the system tenant, and an
  account names its picture by =image_id=.
- Which icon set, and which icon per service.

* Why

A service account is a username and a role today. Anything that shows a
service to a person, such as service status, sign-in audit or a role's
holders, has nothing better to show than a database role name, and no picture
at all. A name and an icon make a service as legible as a person.
