#+title: A volatility surface moves through its SSVI parameters
#+description: How a synthetic volatility surface evolves: SSVI as the surface, its ATM variance, skew and curvature as mean-reverting state processes held inside the no-arbitrage region, the shocks correlated with spot, and the MDN route this replaces.
#+type: knowledge
#+level: cross
#+filetags: :marketdata:synthetic:volatility:ssvi:knowledge:
#+created: 2026-11-03
#+updated: 2026-11-03

* Summary

A synthetic volatility surface is an SSVI surface, and it moves because its
parameters move. SSVI (Gatheral and Jacquier, 2014) describes a whole surface
with an ATM total variance per tenor, one skew parameter and one curvature
parameter, and it states in closed form when that surface is free of static
arbitrage. Each parameter is a mean-reverting state process that stays inside
the region where those conditions hold, so every surface the generator emits is
free of static arbitrage by construction. The shocks of the surface are
correlated with the shocks of spot but are not driven by them, so a surface can
move when spot is flat, and it reacts to a spot crash through the correlation.
The generator prices ATM, RR and BF quotes from the state, as the IR curve
producer prices pillar quotes from its short rate. This replaces the
GJR-GARCH MDN route of the approach document, which is abandoned.

* Detail

** The decision, and what it replaces

[[id:BB2B7C4C-FD4E-4DCA-BF1D-00F929622B87][Synthetic market data generation: approach]]
chose the GJR-GARCH mixture density network (MDN) of
[[id:3F849D36-022B-49CC-AD71-791481F36581][the MDN paper summary]] as the Q-measure
generator for FX, equity and commodity option surfaces. The MDN maps the GARCH
state of spot to a terminal density, and the surface follows from that density.
That design has three problems, and the third one decides it:

1. The network is pretrained, and we do not have it. Training one is a project
   of its own, with its own certification against Monte Carlo.
2. It does not cover the IR vol surfaces, so a second method is needed for them
   in any case.
3. The surface is a function of the spot variance only. It cannot move unless
   spot moves. A 3M volatility spike before a central bank meeting, with spot
   flat, is not possible. Neither is a skew that turns while the ATM level
   holds.

The decision, taken on 2026-11-03: the MDN route is abandoned. A surface is
an autonomous system with state of its own. Its state is the parameter set of
a parametric surface, and the parameters follow stochastic processes with
their own shocks. The alternatives for that parametric surface are compared in
the next section.

** Why SSVI, and not SABR, raw SVI or the frozen pool

| Surface | Parameters | Static arbitrage | Across tenors | Verdict |
|---------+------------+------------------+---------------+---------|
| SABR per tenor | 4 per tenor ($\alpha, \beta, \rho, \nu$) | The Hagan expansion can give a negative density at low strikes | No condition between tenors; calendar arbitrage is possible | Rejected for the generator. Kept as a smile model a blueprint may fit. |
| Raw SVI per tenor | 5 per tenor ($a, b, \rho, m, \sigma$) | A slice may admit butterfly arbitrage; the check is numerical | Calendar arbitrage between slices is possible | Rejected: too many parameters to evolve, and no closed-form bounds |
| Frozen-pool mixture | Mixture weights per pillar | Free by construction | Interpolates between calibrated pillars | Not a dynamic: it needs pillars from somewhere. Kept for IR vol cubes. |
| SSVI | $\theta_T$ per tenor, $\rho$, $\eta$ | Closed-form conditions (below) | Closed-form calendar condition | Chosen |

SSVI wins on the two properties a generator needs. It has few parameters, so
there are few processes to evolve and to calibrate. And its no-arbitrage
conditions are inequalities on those parameters, so a process can be bounded to
satisfy them. Gatheral and Jacquier note this themselves: the ATM variance curve
is a parameter of the surface, directly observable, and a natural place to
impose dynamics (Remark 4.1).

The other surfaces stay in the knowledge base for what they are good at. SABR
and the five-point spline are smile models in
[[id:5B513D53-5DF7-4540-99C0-60D909678873][FX Volatility Surface]], which a
blueprint may fit to quotes. The frozen pool, in
[[id:EC220F01-1844-4EED-A61D-BCF9F01EE00C][the frozen-pool paper summary]],
remains the candidate for IR vol cube interpolation, behind open decisions OD-2
and OD-3 of the approach document.

** The SSVI surface

Write $k = \ln(K / F_T)$ for the log-moneyness against the forward to tenor
$T$, and $w(k, T) = \sigma_{BS}^2(k, T)\, T$ for the total implied variance.
SSVI is Definition 4.1 of Gatheral and Jacquier:

$$w(k, \theta_T) = \frac{\theta_T}{2}\left(1 + \rho\,\varphi(\theta_T)\,k + \sqrt{\left(\varphi(\theta_T)\,k + \rho\right)^2 + 1 - \rho^2}\right)$$

- $\theta_T = \sigma_{ATM}^2(T)\, T$ is the ATM total variance at tenor $T$. It
  is the level of the surface, and it is the only parameter that varies by
  tenor.
- $\rho \in (-1, 1)$ is the skew. The smile is symmetric if and only if
  $\rho = 0$. A negative $\rho$ makes puts dearer than calls, a negative risk
  reversal.
- $\varphi(\theta)$ is the curvature function. It sets how fast the wings rise.

The ATM volatility skew is $\partial_k \sigma_{BS}(k, T)|_{k=0} = \rho
\sqrt{\theta_T}\, \varphi(\theta_T) / (2\sqrt{T})$ (eq. 4.2).

*** The curvature function

Gatheral and Jacquier give two forms:

- *Power law* (Example 4.2): $\varphi(\theta) = \eta\, \theta^{-\gamma}$ with
  $\eta > 0$ and $0 < \gamma < 1$. Its short-expiry smile is V-shaped, which is
  what observed surfaces show and what jumps produce.
- *Heston-like* (Example 4.1): $\varphi(\theta) = \frac{1}{\lambda\theta}\left(1
  - \frac{1 - e^{-\lambda\theta}}{\lambda\theta}\right)$. It is free of static
  arbitrage for every expiry when $\lambda \ge (1 + |\rho|)/4$ (Remark 4.5), but
  its smile flattens at short expiries.

We use the power law with $\gamma = 1/2$ fixed, so $\varphi(\theta) = \eta /
\sqrt{\theta}$. Three reasons:

1. The FX short end has a pronounced smile, which the power law gives and the
   Heston-like form does not.
2. With $\gamma = 1/2$ the ATM skew is $\rho\eta / (2\sqrt{T})$ (Example 4.2),
   so the skew falls as $1/\sqrt{T}$. That is the same scaling the derived
   source gives the risk reversal from the spot history (see below), so the
   starting state and the dynamics agree on the term structure.
3. With $\gamma = 1/2$ the butterfly conditions become simple bounds, as the
   next section shows. The state processes need simple bounds.

This leaves three kinds of state: $\theta_T$ for each quoted tenor, one $\rho$
and one $\eta$.

** The no-arbitrage region

Corollary 4.1 of Gatheral and Jacquier: an SSVI surface is free of static
arbitrage if, for all $\theta > 0$,

1. $\partial_T \theta_T \ge 0$ (no calendar spread arbitrage, Theorem 4.1);
2. $0 \le \partial_\theta(\theta\varphi(\theta)) \le \frac{1}{\rho^2}\left(1 +
   \sqrt{1 - \rho^2}\right)\varphi(\theta)$ (Theorem 4.1);
3. $\theta\varphi(\theta)(1 + |\rho|) < 4$ (no butterfly arbitrage, Theorem
   4.2);
4. $\theta\varphi(\theta)^2(1 + |\rho|) \le 4$ (Theorem 4.2).

Lemma 4.2 of the paper shows condition 3 is necessary and condition 4 is tight.
With $\varphi(\theta) = \eta / \sqrt{\theta}$ the four conditions reduce to:

| Condition | With $\gamma = 1/2$ | Holds when |
|-----------+---------------------+------------|
| 1 | $\theta_T$ rises with $T$ | The forward variances are positive (below) |
| 2 | $\partial_\theta(\theta\varphi)/\varphi = 1/2$ | Always: $1/2 \le (1 + \sqrt{1-\rho^2})/\rho^2$ for every $\rho \in [-1, 1]$ |
| 3 | $\eta\sqrt{\theta}\,(1 + \lvert\rho\rvert) < 4$ | $\theta < \left(4 / (\eta(1 + \lvert\rho\rvert))\right)^2$ |
| 4 | $\eta^2(1 + \lvert\rho\rvert) \le 4$ | $\eta \le 2 / \sqrt{1 + \lvert\rho\rvert}$, and so whenever $\eta \le \sqrt{2}$ |

So the region is a box with one corner cut. If $\eta \le \sqrt{2}$, condition 4
holds for any $\rho$. Condition 3 then holds whenever $\theta < 2$, that is
whenever $\sigma_{ATM}\sqrt{T} < \sqrt{2} \approx 1.41$. An FX surface at 10%
volatility reaches $\theta = 0.1$ at ten years, a twentieth of that bound. A
generator that keeps $\eta \in (0, \sqrt{2}]$ and $\theta_T$ rising is
therefore free of static arbitrage for every FX surface it can plausibly emit.
The blueprint still checks all four conditions on what it fits (see below),
because a check costs nothing and a bound in a config can be wrong.

** The state processes

Each parameter follows a mean-reverting process from
[[id:A8C46639-0639-4587-B07D-B8B006C46618][Stochastic Processes]], chosen so
that its bound holds for every path, not just on average.

*** The ATM variance curve

The curve $\theta_T$ must rise with $T$. A process per tenor would not keep that
order, because two independent paths cross. So the state is the *forward
variance* of each segment between quoted tenors:

$$\theta_{T_i} = \sum_{j \le i} v_j\,(T_j - T_{j-1}), \qquad T_0 = 0$$

Each forward variance $v_j$ follows a
[[id:2350DE1D-00F7-4909-8308-D91831F4C0EA][Cox-Ingersoll-Ross process]]:

$$dv_j = \kappa_j(\bar{v}_j - v_j)\,dt + \xi_j\sqrt{v_j}\,dW^{v_j}$$

A CIR process stays non-negative, and strictly positive when the Feller
condition $2\kappa_j\bar{v}_j \ge \xi_j^2$ holds. Every $v_j > 0$ gives a
$\theta_T$ that rises strictly, so condition 1 holds on every path. The shocks
$dW^{v_j}$ of neighbouring segments are strongly correlated, so the curve
mostly moves as one, and the correlation is below one, so a single segment can
spike. That is the 3M spike before a central bank meeting.

The long-run level $\bar{v}_j$ is the forward variance of the starting state,
so the curve reverts to where it was calibrated.

*** The skew

$\rho$ must stay inside $(-1, 1)$. The state is an unbounded
[[id:4F7C0348-3C53-44E2-8C95-B3B26852FB94][Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process]] $y$,
and the skew is its hyperbolic tangent:

$$dy = \kappa_\rho(\bar{y} - y)\,dt + \sigma_\rho\,dW^\rho, \qquad \rho = \tanh(y)$$

$\tanh$ maps the real line onto $(-1, 1)$, so no path leaves the bound. Near
zero $\tanh(y) \approx y$, so for the small skews of the major pairs $\rho$
behaves almost as the OU process itself. $\bar{y} = \operatorname{artanh}(\rho_0)$
reverts the skew to its starting value.

*** The curvature

$\eta$ must stay inside $(0, \sqrt{2}]$. The state is a second OU process $u$,
and the curvature is a scaled logistic function of it:

$$du = \kappa_\eta(\bar{u} - u)\,dt + \sigma_\eta\,dW^\eta, \qquad \eta = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{1 + e^{-u}}$$

*** The shocks

The shocks $(dW^S, dW^{v_1}, \dots, dW^{v_n}, dW^\rho, dW^\eta)$ of spot and of
the surface are joined by one correlation matrix. The generator takes its
square root with =psd_matrix_square_root= in
=ores.analytics.quant/math/psd_matrix.hpp=, which tries a Cholesky
factorisation first and falls back to an eigen decomposition when the matrix is
only semi-definite. Two of its entries carry the economics:

- $\operatorname{corr}(dW^S, dW^{v})$, spot against the ATM level. In equity
  this is strongly negative: a crash raises volatility. In FX it has no
  universal sign. A crisis lifts the vol of a pair in whichever direction the
  safe-haven currency moves, so the sign depends on the pair and how it is
  quoted. It is a per-pair parameter.
- $\operatorname{corr}(dW^S, dW^\rho)$, spot against the skew. When spot falls
  and the market buys protection, the risk reversal moves further into puts.

Every other shock is independent of spot, which is what lets the surface move
on its own.

*** Jumps

The analysis this design came from adds a jump term to the ATM level, driven by
a Hawkes process, for volatility shocks that arrive in bursts. That is not in
the first design. It belongs with activity and volatility exciting each other,
which [[id:9D778F72-8FF6-4F01-AF4B-696DC7BA855A][Market volatility channel]]
leaves out of scope and captures as
[[id:4A00CBE5-E42B-46A8-A09B-FD698A09E4BB][Make activity and volatility excite each other]].
A jump of the CIR level keeps it positive, so the bound survives the extension.

** From state to quotes

The surface ticks as quotes, never as parameters. This is the IR curve's rule:
[[id:FB57F761-0FC3-4283-AD32-CCF2FE68AD2E][The IR curve producer]] advances one
short-rate state and prices one swap-rate quote per pillar. The surface
generator advances its SSVI state and prices one lognormal-vol quote per
pillar. Every quote is named by its oresmd URI
([[id:C3E053CA-0D4B-480B-9119-E11530160EC1][oresmd: ORE Studio Market Data URI]]);
an ORE key exists only at the ORE XML boundary, as
[[id:7AE57098-08D4-4D4A-83D6-918FE520B68E][A pillar names the market series it reads]]
states. All the quotes of one EUR/USD surface are points of one series:

: oresmd://fx/EUR?type=series&instrument=fx_option&quote=rate_lnvol&ccy=USD

and one point adds the two axes of the surface, the expiry and the strike
label:

: oresmd://fx/EUR?type=quote&instrument=fx_option&quote=rate_lnvol&ccy=USD&expiry=1Y&strike_label=25RR

The pillar ladder is a template of expiries times strike labels (=ATM=,
=25RR=, =25BF=, =10RR=, =10BF=), as the IR template is a ladder of tenors
([[id:F15697C8-091E-4FA7-804E-C49021B9BDEF][The IR curve template and its parameters]]).

For each tenor the generator:

1. Builds the slice $\sigma(k) = \sqrt{w(k, \theta_T)/T}$.
2. Finds the ATM strike by the pair's ATM convention, usually delta-neutral
   straddle, and reads $\sigma_{ATM}$. SSVI's $k = 0$ is ATM forward; the two
   differ by $\pm\sigma^2 T/2$ in $k$, so the generator solves for the
   convention rather than reading $\theta_T$ directly.
3. Solves the 25-delta and 10-delta call and put strikes under the pair's delta
   convention (spot or forward, premium-adjusted or not, see
   [[id:5B513D53-5DF7-4540-99C0-60D909678873][FX Volatility Surface]]), and
   reads their volatilities from the slice.
4. Emits $RR = \sigma_C - \sigma_P$ and the smile butterfly
   $BF = (\sigma_C + \sigma_P)/2 - \sigma_{ATM}$.

Step 4 gives the *smile* butterfly. Brokers quote the *market* strangle: one
volatility $\sigma_{ATM} + \tilde{\sigma}_{BF}$ applied at strikes of its own,
and finding it needs a numerical solve against the smile (Healy, 2025,
equations 5 to 12). The two conventions give different numbers for the same
surface, and the quote URI does not say which one a number is. The convention
is the pair's: the refdata entity =fx_option_convention= states its
=atm_type=, =delta_type=, =butterfly_style= (=Broker= or =Smile=) and
=risk_reversal_in_favor_of=, and the surface's =fx_volatility_config= refers
to it. So the generator quotes in the pair's convention, solving the market
strangle when the style is =Broker=. A synthetic quote and a vendor quote of
the same point then mean the same thing, which is what lets a vendor replace
the synthetic source without changing any consumer.

*** The quotes are observations, not the surface

The quotes above are the surface itself, sampled cleanly. A real broker feed
is not like that: several contributors quote a fuller delta ladder, with bid
and ask, noise, stale and missing points, and they disagree. A desk marks its
own surface from those inputs. So the published quotes come from an
*observation layer* inside the simulator adapter: the SSVI state is the latent
true market, and synthetic contributors, each a provider, publish noisy
observations of it. The noise never touches the state. A contributor whose
noise profile is =none= publishes the clean quotes, which is what the tests
use. The layer is designed in the note on the surface pipeline.

*** What oresmd already names, and what it does not

The identity covers the quotes. The FX row of the schema
(=ores.marketdata.api/datum/schema.hpp=) has the identity fields =unit_ccy= and
=ccy= and the axes =expiry= and =strike_label=. A =strike_label= is a canonical
code with the forms =ATM=, a delta call (=25C=), a delta put (=25P=), a risk
reversal (=25RR=), a butterfly (=25BF=) and a level, so a generator may also
quote calls and puts outright. The other vol families have rows of their own:
the swaption cube (=expiry=, =term=, =dimension=, =strike_level=), the
cap/floor surface, and the equity and commodity surfaces, whose =strike=
takes ORE's strike grammar (absolute, ATM by type, delta, moneyness). Reading
one slice, such as the ATM ladder, fixes the other axes, as the oresmd note
describes.

Two things the identity does not name, by design or not yet:

- *The quoting convention.* By design. A convention is how a consumer reads a
  number, and it is reference data of the pair, as the curve role is data of the
  consumer and not of the curve.
- *A fitted surface.* Not yet. A bootstrapped curve has an identity of its own:
  its points are a =discount= series with a =curve_id=, distinct from the swap
  quotes it inverted. A surface built from quotes has no such identity, so a
  fitted surface cannot yet be stored as derived data with its lineage. Which
  object the build of a surface writes, and how it is named, is decided in the
  note on the surface pipeline.

** From quotes to a published surface

The consumer side follows
[[id:446F28DF-155A-47EC-8D66-FDB90A8FBFA7][Bootstrapping a curve: from quotes to a published set]].
A curve blueprint names its pillars, its interpolation and its conventions, and
a bootstrap inverts the quotes into discount factors, checks them, and writes a
candidate that publication makes authoritative. A surface blueprint names its
tenors, its strike labels, its delta and ATM conventions and its smile model
(SSVI, SABR, a spline or Vanna-Volga: the tenant chooses, and SSVI in the
generator does not constrain the choice), as
[[id:F439271D-F8CD-4A6E-A9FE-E523A4419846][User Journey: Author a surface's framework]]
describes. Its build fits the model to the quotes, checks the four conditions
above and the checks of
[[id:3F7A2C91-84BE-4E12-B5D0-1D6E5F08A432][Vol surface no-arbitrage conditions]],
and writes a candidate. A pillar reads its quote series by oresmd identity, as
[[id:7AE57098-08D4-4D4A-83D6-918FE520B68E][A pillar names the market series it reads]]
requires. Nothing in this chain knows whether the quotes are synthetic.

** The starting state

The processes need a starting surface and long-run levels. No free public
source publishes FX implied volatilities: the DBnomics, BIS and ECB checks of
2026-11-03 found none, and the daily surfaces on offer are commercial. The
starting quotes therefore come from a *derived source*: a publisher of its own
whose engine reads the stored spot fixings and backs out plausible ATM, RR and
BF values. Its full treatment is its own note; the mapping it uses is the
Gram-Charlier smile of Backus, Foresi, Li and Wu (1997), Proposition 2:

$$\sigma(d) \approx \sigma\left(1 - \frac{\gamma_1}{3!}\,d - \frac{\gamma_2}{4!}\,(1 - d^2)\right)$$

where $\sigma$ is the volatility of the log spot over the tenor, $\gamma_1$ its
skewness, $\gamma_2$ its excess kurtosis, and $d$ the moneyness of their
equation 9. A 25-delta call sits near $d = -0.674$ and a 25-delta put near
$d = +0.674$, the points where the normal distribution leaves a quarter in the
tail. That gives:

| Quote | From the spot history |
|-------+-----------------------|
| ATM | $\sigma\,(1 - \gamma_2/24)$, then raised by an authored premium of implied over realised volatility |
| 25RR | $\approx 0.225\,\sigma\,\gamma_1$ |
| 25BF | $\approx 0.019\,\sigma\,\gamma_2$ |

For independent daily returns, the skewness of an $n$-day return is
$\gamma_1/\sqrt{n}$ and its excess kurtosis $\gamma_2/n$, so the risk reversal
falls as $1/\sqrt{T}$, the same scaling the SSVI skew has with $\gamma = 1/2$.
The paper notes the approximation is close for $|d| < 1.5$, which covers the
10-delta points at $d \approx \pm 1.28$. The values are plausible, not real: a
spot path does not contain the risk premium the option market charges. Because
the derived source is a provider like any other, a real vol source, such as an
LSEG surface, ranks above it and replaces it with a change of route, as in
[[id:FA280E9B-7E00-4747-8D29-42B3D4258873][A source switch changes one route and nothing else]].

The generator fits SSVI to those starting quotes, and the fitted
$(\theta_{T_i}, \rho, \eta)$ become the starting state and the long-run levels.

** What this does not do

- It rules out *static* arbitrage only: each day's surface, on its own, is
  consistent. It does not make the sequence of surfaces free of dynamic
  arbitrage, which would need the surface dynamics to be consistent with the
  spot dynamics under one risk-neutral measure. ORE prices from one surface at a
  time, so static consistency is what it needs; see
  [[id:A3F7C2E1-84B9-4D56-9E2A-1F6D05B38C47][Probability measures: P and Q]].
- One $\rho$ for every tenor cannot give every term structure of skew. The
  extended SSVI of Hendriks and Martini (2019) makes $\rho$ depend on the tenor
  and keeps closed-form calendar conditions. It is the extension to take if the
  risk reversals of the starting state do not fall as $1/\sqrt{T}$.
- The CIR process does not yet take a variable step in =ores.analytics.quant=:
  [[id:F135B9AF-902F-46C5-847C-0820D8C92D28][Tick arrival]] lists only the two
  mixtures and the OU process. A surface feed is therefore periodic at first,
  which fits the approach document's rule that a surface updates less often
  than its underlying.
- The surface of other asset classes follows the same pattern with its own
  quoting: an equity or commodity surface quotes by strike or moneyness rather
  than delta, and an IR cube adds the swap tenor. Each is its own step.
