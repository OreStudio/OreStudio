#+title: An Acme approval is decided by a permitted person who did not raise it
#+description: The four approval kinds and four parts the platform seeds, the decide permission each names, the Acme persona that holds it, and the requester-not-decider rule that makes the decision four eyes.
#+type: knowledge
#+level: cross
#+filetags: :knowledge:approval:four-eyes:acme:demo:

* Summary

An approval request names a kind, and a kind names one permission that its
decider must hold. A request may also name parts — Controller, Finance, Market
Risk, Operations — and each part names a permission of its own, with an answer
order. The demonstration populates each of those five permissions with at least
one Acme persona, and separates the two people the control exists for: the desks
raise, and Operations, Market Risk, the Controller or Finance decides. A person
who raised a request can never decide it, whatever they hold. That single rule is
what turns a permission into four eyes.

* Detail

** The four kinds

#+ATTR_HTML: :class hug-leading
| Kind | Decides what | =decide_permission_code= | Holder in Acme | Approvals | Hold | Comment on approve | Expires |
|------+--------------+--------------------------+----------------+-----------+------+--------------------+---------|
| =trading.operational_authorisation= | a booked trade waiting to be released | =inbox::approvals:decide_operations= | Operations | 1 | yes | yes | never |
| =refdata.book_change= | a proposed change to books | =inbox::approvals:decide_operations= | Operations | 1 | no | no | 14 days |
| =refdata.counterparty_change= | a proposed change to counterparties, their identifiers and the parties that may trade with them | =inbox::approvals:decide_operations= | Operations | 1 | no | no | 14 days |
| =iam.role_grant= | a person asking to be given a role | =iam::roles:assign= | TenantAdmin | 1 | no | no | 14 days |

An operational authorisation does not expire and requires a comment on
approval, because an unreleased trade is a risk a timeout must not hide and the
decision has to be readable afterwards. A role request carries a review window,
because an administrator who never answers asks the member to wait forever.

** The four parts

A part is what a kind asks for when one function's sign-off does not imply
another's. A request that names parts is decided by part instead of by count.

#+ATTR_HTML: :class hug-leading
| Part | Answers | =decide_permission_code= | Answer order | Holder in Acme |
|------+---------+--------------------------+--------------+----------------|
| =controller= | the first independent check on a new book | =inbox::approvals:decide_controller= | 1, before the rest | Controller: 4 personas |
| =finance= | the accounting identity, the ledger feed, closing a book | =inbox::approvals:decide_finance= | 2, in parallel | Finance: 4 personas |
| =market_risk= | classification, capital treatment, agreements, conventions | =inbox::approvals:decide_market_risk= | 2, in parallel | Operations: 16 personas, of whom the heads of market risk |
| =operations= | access, memberships, links, allowed currencies and products | =inbox::approvals:decide_operations= | 2, in parallel | Operations: 16 personas |

The controller answers first because a desk must not be able to authorise its own
new book; the other three answer together, because each approves its own
contribution and no one function's sign-off implies another's.
[[id:C5846977-529F-4E36-A9DB-EA48A7476E6F][Book types and the controls on
each]] is where those four answers come from. The platform seeds the routing as
policies keyed by the field a change touches, and the policies follow the same
division: a book's functional currency, GL account reference, cost centre, owner
unit and ledger feed go to Finance, its regulatory type, purpose, rates centre and
sweepable flag to Market Risk, and its status, portfolios and memberships to
Operations.

** The requester-not-decider rule

#+caption: The Acme demonstration: a desk raises through the owning component's operation, the decider reads a queue filtered by the kind's permission, and the decision is refused when the person who asked is the person answering.
#+attr_html: :width 100%
[[proj:doc/knowledge/demo/acme_approval_request_and_decide.png]]

Source: [[./acme_approval_request_and_decide.puml][=acme_approval_request_and_decide.puml=]]. Regenerate with
=plantuml -tpng doc/knowledge/demo/acme_approval_request_and_decide.puml=.

The rule is enforced three times, at three levels, and each is worth knowing.

1. *The queue.* The list of requests a person may decide is filtered by the
   permission each kind names, and the person's own requests are cut from the page
   before it is paged. A person does not see their own request in their queue at
   all.
2. *The decision.* The decisions table refuses a decision whose decider is the
   request's raiser, and the service answers =four_eyes= with the words "You asked
   for this request, so someone else decides it."
3. *One decision per person.* A request that needs several approvals, or several
   parts, takes one decision from each person, so a person holding two of the
   deciding roles still answers once.

What the demonstration showed, on a database recreated and provisioned from the
seed alone:

#+ATTR_HTML: :class hug-leading
| Who | What they tried | What happened |
|-----+-----------------+---------------|
| a junior trader, who raised four authorisations | read the queue | 0 requests: the role holds no decide code |
| the same trader | approve a request of their own | refused, =not_a_decider= |
| the head of middle office | read the queue | 60 waiting authorisations |
| the head of middle office | approve an authorisation raised by the group treasurer | approved; the request moved to =approved= and the kind's apply ran |
| the head of middle office | approve a book change the same person had raised | refused, =four_eyes=: "You asked for this request, so someone else decides it." |
| the head of financial control | open a book change from the notice, then approve the controller part | approved; the request stays waiting for the other parts |
| the group finance director | approve the finance part | approved |
| the head of market risk | approve the market risk part | approved |
| the tenant administrator | read the queue | 63 waiting requests, including the role request a member raised |

** What a person still cannot do, and what that leaves undecided

The journeys and the seeds disagree in five places, and this page records the
disagreement rather than resolving it.

- *A part decider reads an empty queue.* The queue filters by the kind's decide
  code, and a Controller or Finance persona holds no kind's code, only a part's.
  Such a person therefore learns of a request from the notice the raise sends and
  must open it by identifier; the queue shows nothing. Whether the queue should
  also list the requests whose parts a person may answer is an open question.
- *No policy names the controller part.* The approval policies route a changed
  field to Finance, Market Risk or Operations, and the controller answers only an
  opening. No seeded policy row produces a controller part, so today no request
  the platform raises asks a controller to answer. A Controller persona can decide
  the part, as the run above shows, but only a request raised with that part asks
  for it.
- *No market-data approval kind exists.* *Decide a publish* describes an approver
  waiting on a curve publish, and the kind it would filter the queue by is not
  seeded, so the market data part of the demonstration has no approval to decide.
  [[id:A85B6C4E-0F93-4410-BF2E-7167722849ED][Decide a publish]] carries the gap.
- *Which market risk decides a tenant-wide change.* *Decide a convention change*
  names Market Risk as the decider of a tenant kind and does not say which
  party's Market Risk answers for the whole tenant. This demonstration answers
  the question by accident rather than by design: the queue is tenant-wide, so
  any office's head of market risk sees the request. That the queue is not
  party-scoped is a property the journeys do not state.
- *No kind carries a hold reason list.* An operational authorisation allows a
  hold and has no reason lookup, so a hold takes the free-text comment alone.
