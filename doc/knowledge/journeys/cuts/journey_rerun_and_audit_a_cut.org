This page documents a [[id:F44766FA-1E9C-416F-849A-E131101B2ECA][user journey]]: An auditor reads the history of a cut, who took and authorised it, the runs that priced from it, and reproduces a result from the same body and the same engine, or reads why it cannot.

A user journey describes what one person is trying to do, before anything is
built. It is a design document that outlives any sprint, so it names the
screens, the entity fields each screen shows, and the operations and messages
each step needs.

The decision behind it is
[[id:F6362451-D569-4609-97A3-F1C785AAAF02][The cut is the body a run prices
from]]. This journey is the one the whole cluster exists for: the
[[id:B5214F3A-A72E-421E-82BC-79338B2E95CB][Cuts]] note states that the purpose of
a cut is a valuation that is fully reproducible, and a purpose is not met until
somebody reproduces one.

* Goal

"A number in a report was signed off months ago and I am asked to stand behind it.
I want to read which body it came from, who fixed it and who signed it, every run
that rested on it, and then produce the same number again from the same body and
the same engine — or be told, in words I can put in a file, why the same number
cannot be produced."

The journey is done when the person has either reproduced a figure from the cut, or
holds a statement of the reason reproduction is not possible and what would be
needed.

* Actor and trigger

An auditor runs this journey, and so does a market-risk owner answering a challenge
to a number, and an engineer chasing a difference two runs of one body show. All
three read the same surfaces; only the engineer takes the fourth step, which
compares the figures rather than restating the verdict.

Reading needs =reporting::cuts:read= and =reporting::report_instances:read=.
Re-running a report needs =reporting::report_instances:write=, which is the
permission a person already needs to press *Run now*. Reading the engine version is
part of the run's own record. A person who may not re-run a report reads the cut and
its runs and is drawn no *Run it again*.

The trigger is a request from outside the party: a regulator's question, an internal
audit's sample, or a challenge from the desk. The person starts from the report the
number appears in, or from a cut whose label they were given, and the journey ends
with a comparison on the screen.

This journey reads and re-runs. It does not authorise a cut, which is
[[id:E840111E-AA2F-4746-8D49-A1D73742925E][Take a cut]], and it does not choose
which cut a report prices from, which is
[[id:9123193E-352D-4F46-8174-7763FA0A44CD][Price a report from a cut]].

* Steps

** Step 1: open the cut, and read what it is

1. *Reach the cut.* The person reaches it from the run's card, from the *Runs* of a
   report, or by opening Cuts and searching the label. The record page's heading is
   the derived label, and the lead line reads the kind, the timepoint, the scope and
   the state.
2. *Read the body.* The *The body* tab draws the parts, each with the counts and the
   state it held when the body was fixed.

   | Part | What the tab draws |
   |------+--------------------|
   | The deals | The population by count and by state, the books, the corrections inside it, and the read that produced it |
   | The market data | The publish record of every set the body fixed, its curve, party, as-of and publisher, the fixings in force, and the value count |
   | The reference data | The version in force of every configuration the run reads, each a link to that version |
   | The engine and the code | The engine version, the wrapper version and the service build |
   | The taking identity | The account, the party and the instant |

3. *Read the gap list, where there is one.* An incomplete body draws its gaps and
   says that no run ever priced from it. That is the answer to a challenge about a
   number the cut does not hold, and it is stated rather than implied.

** Step 2: read who took and who authorised it

4. *Read the authorisation.* The *Authorisation* tab draws the acts and the
   sign-off: the taker and the instant, the request, the account the request waited
   on, the decider, the instant and the comment, or the refusal and its reason.
   Each names its account and links the request in Approvals, which is the record
   the inbox keeps for every kind.
5. *Read the four-eyes fact.* The tab states, in one line, that the taker and the
   decider are two people, and it says what the schema enforces: a decision by the
   person who asked is refused, and one person approves a request once. The rule is
   the inbox's for every kind, and
   [[id:0FE6BDD9-EB24-4B07-8B0C-D816AAF1BEBF][Approval requests]] states it. A
   signature a reader can check is worth more than one a reader must trust.
6. *Read the basis where the kind requires no signature.* A cut of a kind that
   requires no authorisation draws one act and the sentence that names the basis:
   that the kind requires none, and, where the kind says so, that every result
   priced from it is marked indicative. The tab draws it in the place the decision
   would stand, so an auditor reading the page does not take a signed body and an
   unsigned one for the same thing.
7. *Read what the label does not say.* The tab states that the label names the kind,
   the scope and the date, and that it says nothing about who signed it, so the
   state and the basis are read as facts and not inferred from the label.

** Step 3: read the runs that priced from it

8. *Read the runs.* The *Runs* tab lists every run that priced from this cut, newest
   first: the report, the definition, the run's instant, its state, its engine
   version and its result's location. The count of those runs is the column the cuts
   list draws, so the two screens agree.
9. *Open one run.* A row opens the run's page, and the run's card names the cut it
   priced from, as
   [[id:9123193E-352D-4F46-8174-7763FA0A44CD][Price a report from a cut]] draws
   it. The two pages are the same fact read from each side.
10. *Read a run that did not price.* A run refused before the engine appears in the
   list with its refusal and does not count as a run that priced from the cut. The
   distinction matters to an auditor: a refusal is not a result.

** Step 4: reproduce a result

11. *Press Run it again.* The press is offered on a run that completed, and on the
    cut itself for the report that consumed it. It re-runs the same report against
    the same cut, and the dialog states what it will do: the same body, the same
    engine version if the version is still served, and a result to compare with the
    original.
12. *Read the verdict.* The comparison is one of three, in the words the card uses.

    | Verdict | What the screen draws |
    |---------+-----------------------|
    | Reproduced | The two runs side by side, the figures equal, and the sentence that they are one result computed twice. The cut and the engine version are stated once and shared |
    | Differs | The first figures that differ, each with both values and the difference, and the statement that the body and the engine are the same. The screen says this is a defect in the engine or in the code and not a movement of the market |
    | Cannot be compared | The reason, in one of three forms, and what would be needed to compare |

13. *Read why reproduction is not possible.* The third verdict is the one an
    auditor writes down, so it is stated precisely.

    | Reason | What the screen says |
    |--------+----------------------|
    | The engine version differs | The original run used one version and today serves another. The body is the same and the software is not, which is the condition the cluster's quoted definition of reproduction excludes |
    | A part of the body is no longer served | The body records the part and the store no longer serves it: today, a fixed market data snapshot that was never stored, or a configuration version that was withdrawn. The screen names the part and the series |
    | The body was retaken | The run priced from an earlier cut of the same day, and the comparison names both labels. A retaken body is a different body, and its results are comparable only as a movement |

14. *Read what the rerun keeps.* The new run records the cut it priced from, its own
    instant and its engine version, so a third person can repeat the exercise without
    asking the person who ran it. The screen states that no figure is overwritten: the
    two runs are two records.

** The audit across the day**

15. *Read the day's sequence.* From the cuts list, a person reads one day's cuts in
    order: the cut taken at the close, the retake that superseded it, and the runs
    that rested on each. The sequence is the audit trail a challenge asks for, and it
    is why a superseded cut is kept and drawn rather than hidden.
16. *Read who rests on a superseded cut.* The *Runs* tab of a superseded cut names
    the runs that priced from it and the cut that replaced it. That is the question a
    person asks before a retake is authorised — *what already rests on the body I am
    about to replace?* — and the answer is a list rather than a guess.

* Screens and wireframes

Two surfaces carry this journey: the cut's record page, read through its four tabs,
and the run's page with its card and its comparison. The cuts list is the third,
because the day's sequence is read there.

* Prototype

The [[file:../../prototypes/cuts/the-cut/][the cut prototype]] draws this
journey on two of its surfaces: the cut's record with its *Runs* tab, and the run
that names the cut with its three verdicts. Its
[[file:../../prototypes/cuts/the-cut.org][design note]] records the states
and what is fake.

It must draw a cut whose runs all reproduced, a rerun that reproduced, a rerun that
differs, a rerun that cannot be compared for each of the three reasons, and a
superseded cut with the runs that still rest on it. Every value is a fixture.

a cut that has been taken is immutable, and an audit that could change the object it
audits would be worth nothing.

The comparison reads the two runs' results as rows. The output of a run is an
archive, and the last journey of the reporting group states the same gap from its
own side: nothing parses a run's archive into figures. Until something does, the
verdict this journey draws can be stated only from the facts a run records, which
are its cut, its engine version and its state.

* What is missing

2. *Nothing compares two runs.* The reproduction promise is the purpose of the whole
   cluster and no operation answers it. A comparison needs the two runs' figures as
   rows, which needs the archive parsed, which is a gap the reporting journeys already
   record.
3. *The run does not record its engine version.* A run records the date it priced at
   and the note that explains a moved date, and nothing names the version of the
   engine that computed its result. A reproduction verdict that cannot state the
   software is not a verdict.
4. *The cut records the service build, and nothing provides it.* The body's engine and
   code part needs a revision for the services that assembled it, and the platform
   records no build for a service. The
   [[id:E840111E-AA2F-4746-8D49-A1D73742925E][Take a cut]] journey records the
   same gap from the side of the take.
5. *A fixed snapshot is recorded and not served.* A cut whose market data half is a
   snapshot nothing materialises can be read today only if the store still holds the
   values. That is the second reason a comparison fails, and it is a property of the
   gap rather than of the audit.
6. *No list of runs by cut.* Runs are read by definition, by health and by instance.
   Nothing reads them by cut, so the *Runs* tab and the cuts list's count have no
   source.
7. *No route and no screen for reporting.* The cut's record, the run's card and the
   comparison are all new, as the two journeys beside this one record.
8. *The day's sequence is not a read.* The list ordered by timepoint answers it for
   one scope, and no read groups the cuts of a day by kind and ordinal. The first
   slice reads the list and states the order it uses.
