FX Volatility Surface Blueprint Methodology
===========================================

A blueprint is the recipe a calibration fits one FX volatility surface by: the
pair, the quoted points it reads, the smile model, how it joins its expiries,
and the grid it writes. The system tenant holds one blueprint for each pair the
derived source quotes. Provisioning gives each party its own copy, and the
party's copy reads the party's own quotes.

This dataset holds recipes only. It holds no volatility. The levels a surface
shows come from the quotes its pillars read.

1. PAIRS

   One blueprint for each of the ten pairs the derived source of FX
   volatility quotes publishes: EUR/USD, GBP/USD, USD/JPY, USD/CHF, AUD/USD,
   USD/CAD, NZD/USD, EUR/GBP, EUR/JPY and EUR/CHF. The source's inventory is
   in projects/ores.sql/populate/marketdata/marketdata_derived_catalogue_populate.sql.
   These are the G10 pairs that carry most FX option turnover (BIS Triennial
   Central Bank Survey of foreign exchange and OTC derivatives markets, 2022).
   A pair keeps market quoting order: EUR, GBP, AUD and NZD quote against USD,
   USD quotes against CAD, CHF and JPY, and EUR quotes against GBP, JPY and CHF.

2. PILLARS

   Each blueprint reads 35 points: the expiries 1W, 1M, 2M, 3M, 6M, 9M and 1Y,
   each quoted as ATM, the 25 and 10 delta risk reversals (25RR, 10RR) and the
   25 and 10 delta butterflies (25BF, 10BF). This is the quote set an FX option
   broker shows for a pair (I. Clark, Foreign Exchange Option Pricing: A
   Practitioner's Guide, Wiley 2011, chapter 3; D. Reiswich and U. Wystup, FX
   Volatility Smile Construction, Wilmott 2012).

   The 2Y expiry is left out. The derived source quotes 2Y only from two years
   of fixings, and the seeded history does not reach that far. A blueprint
   whose pillar has no quote cannot be calibrated.

3. SOURCES

   Each pillar has one source: the pair's fx_option series of the derived
   source, with contributor "derived" and weight 1. The series holds every
   quote of the pair, so the pillar's expiry and label find its quote inside
   it. The staged rows carry no series id. The publish finds the series by its
   oresmd URI, or creates it with the derived source's own derivation stamp, so
   the feed writes into it and does not create a second one.

4. MODEL

   Every blueprint fits SSVI (J. Gatheral and A. Jacquier, Arbitrage-free SVI
   volatility surfaces, Quantitative Finance 14(1), 2014), and joins its
   expiries linearly in total variance, which keeps the surface free of
   calendar arbitrage between pillars.

5. OUTPUT GRID

   The grid is the pillar expiries by five strikes: the 10 and 25 delta puts,
   ATM, and the 25 and 10 delta calls. This is the grid an ORE FXVolatility
   entry of dimension Smile and smile type Delta reads (deltas 10P, 25P, ATM,
   25C, 10C), so the export writes a published surface as quotes ORE reads
   without a second interpolation.

   A strike's delta type is the short-dated delta type of the pair's FX option
   convention in the ore.conventions dataset, which is extracted from the ORE
   example corpus (external/ore/examples). Five conventions state a premium
   adjusted spot delta (PaSpot): USD/JPY, USD/CHF, USD/CAD, EUR/JPY and
   EUR/CHF. The other five state a spot delta (Spot). The test
   refdata_fx_vol_surface_blueprints_publish_test.sql refuses a blueprint whose
   delta type differs from its convention.

6. OUTPUT SERIES

   Each blueprint writes its calibrated grid to its own fx_vol_surface series,
   oresmd://fx/<base>?type=series&instrument=fx_vol_surface&quote=rate_lnvol
   &ccy=<quote>&surface_id=<name>. The publish creates it OBSERVED, and the
   calibration claims it on its first run.

7. NAMES

   A blueprint is named for its pair and model, such as EURUSD_SSVI.

GENERATION

   File: generate_sql.py (this directory).
   Output: projects/ores.sql/populate/refdata/refdata_fx_vol_surface_blueprints_artefact_populate.sql,
   which registers this methodology and the refdata.fx_vol_surface_blueprints
   dataset and stages its rows. The staged ids are fixed by hashing, so a
   regeneration reproduces them.

   The publish, ores_refdata_publish_fx_vol_surface_blueprints_from_dq_fn,
   writes into the system tenant under its system party, as part of the
   system_core bundle. It only inserts. A change to a blueprint here reaches a
   database when the database is recreated, and a party's copy when the party
   is provisioned again.

REGENERATE

   python3 projects/ores.seeder/datasets/fx_vol_surfaces/generate_sql.py
