#+description: The secrets an installation keeps for its own use, the consumers that may receive them, and the record of who asked for what.

The vault is the one place a value ORE must present to somebody else is kept, and
almost nothing a person does with it is routine. A deployment puts the values
there; a person looks when a rotation is due, when a consumer has to be given a
key, or when something asked for a secret it should not have.

That shapes the area: it is small, it is read-mostly, and the one act that writes
a value is rare and heavy. The screens follow the
[[id:CF052805-2B7A-46B9-B14C-EF8BCF810C44][record screen standard]] because what
they list are records — a secret, a consumer, a use — and the standard already
settles how a record is listed, opened, written and read through its versions.

* The area

The screens take an area of their own rather than joining *Operations*.
*Operations* gathers the installation's own life — the services running, the
grid, the bus, the logs — and every screen there answers a question about the
process. The vault answers a question about a tenant's data, and its one
sensitive act is a read of a value, which belongs beside the data and not beside
the runtime. The
[[id:339F0405-43AE-4049-B6B3-EB12E5A38CA4][menu structure]] page carries the
area once the owner has signed the journey off.
