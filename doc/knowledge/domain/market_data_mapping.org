#+title: The mapping belongs to the provider and not to the source
#+description: How a vendor instrument becomes an internal item, why the mapping is on the provider, and what that allows.
#+type: knowledge
#+level: cross
#+filetags: :marketdata:mapping:catalogue:
#+created: 2026-10-22
#+updated: 2026-10-22

* Summary

The mapping links a vendor instrument (provider, id scheme, external id, field)
to an internal item, which is an oresmd datum. It is on the provider. The live
and the simulated sources of one provider share the mapping. That is what lets a
party change its source and keep its series.

* Detail

** The row

| Column | Meaning |
|--------+---------|
| =provider=, =id_scheme=, =external_id=, =field= | The vendor instrument |
| =oresmd_uri= | The internal item |
| =valid_from=, =valid_to= | The period in which the mapping holds |

The mapping is many-to-one. Several vendor instruments, of different providers,
can map to one item. That is how two providers quote one EUR/USD spot.

** Why provider and not source

If the mapping were on the source, =bloomberg-live= and =bloomberg-sim= would
need two mappings that must stay equal. A drift between them would make the
simulated run a worse test of the live run. One mapping rules this out.

** What the mapping replaces

An adapter never publishes an oresmd URI. The service applies the mapping at
ingest. See [[id:00D63182-C7FF-4AC9-8A83-9A5D984B2876][The ingest loop and the one pipeline]]. This is the same job that a Calypso feed address does:
it links an internal quote name to a vendor instrument and field.

** Changing a mapping

A mapping change is an effective-dated change with a reason. The service
refreshes its ingest cache when a mapping changes. The history of a mapping is
readable.
