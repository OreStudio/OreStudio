* Summary

The credit family is eight trade type codes held in one table,
=credit_instruments=, of 25 columns. The ORE importer writes six of the
codes. Two codes, =CreditDefaultSwapOption= and =CBO=, have no importer, so
the table holds nothing for them.

The table is wide because it is the union of what the six products share
and what each keeps for itself. Six columns are never written by any
mapper. Two are written and never read. Four more belong to one product: =option_strike=, =option_expiry_date=,
=tranche_attachment= and =tranche_detachment=. The mapper drops most of the elements the ORE schema
gives these products: the upfront fee, the accrual flags, the payment time,
the basket, the knock-out flag, the strike type and, for the risk
participation agreement, the participation rate, the protection fee and the
whole underlying trade. The two required elements of the agreement are
written as nothing at all, so its export is not a valid ORE document.

The proposal follows the bond and rates reworks:

- one *family header*, =credit_instruments=, holding what the eight codes
  share: the trade, the party, the trade type, the reference entity and the
  two common dates;
- six *product fact tables*, each keyed by the trade, holding what one
  product keeps for itself;
- *shared children* that already exist, reused: =swap_legs= with its amounts
  and rates for the premium and payment legs, =instrument_options= and
  =instrument_strikes= for the option on an index;
- one new child, =credit_basket_constituents=, for the names of a basket;
- one new nullable column, =leg_group= on =swap_legs=, to say which payment
  group of a credit linked swap or which side of an agreement a leg belongs
  to.

The money columns become exact decimals, and the ORE binding keeps the
digits for the credit money members (the upfront fee and the option strike),
as it now does for the FRA. A continuous quantity stays a binary float: the
recovery rate, the tranche points and the participation rate, as the
data-oriented design rule says. The rework is
staged into eight units; the last proves the eight credit documents
(eight trades) over the round trip.

* Detail

** Context: the bond pilot, the rates rework and the instrument table rule

Three bodies of prior work bound this one, and their conclusions are
assumed.

- Section 14 of
  [[id:FDC35F65-CBF0-40A0-96D0-EA180F2237EE][Investigation: the trade shape]]
  states the instrument table rule: a family header names the rows it owns,
  every instrument is keyed by its trade, and the family's trade types route
  through the catalogue.
- The bond pilot, and then the rates rework, settled the shape: a header, product
  facts keyed by the trade, trade-owned legs, and the shared =instrument_*=
  children for the list shapes. The rates story added three things credit
  reuses: the common-date view, the generated eventing tests, and the
  digits the ORE binding keeps.
- The rates analysis left credit one job on purpose. =RiskParticipationAgreement=
  is a credit product. The rates repair routed it to the credit table and
  stopped there; the table that was meant to hold its facts is now written
  by nothing.

The question this page asks is the one the rates page asked: *what does a
family header hold?* For credit the answer is short. The six products share
a reference entity and little else.

** What counts as the credit family

The trade type catalogue routes eight codes to =credit_instrument=.

| Code | ORE data element | Importer | In the corpus |
|------+------------------+----------+---------------|
| CreditDefaultSwap | =CreditDefaultSwapData= | yes | 1 document |
| IndexCreditDefaultSwap | =IndexCreditDefaultSwapData= | yes | 2 documents |
| IndexCreditDefaultSwapOption | =IndexCreditDefaultSwapOptionData= | yes | 1 document |
| SyntheticCDO | =CdoData= | yes | 1 document |
| CreditLinkedSwap | =CreditLinkedSwapData= | yes | 1 document |
| RiskParticipationAgreement | =RiskParticipationAgreementData= | yes | 2 documents |
| CreditDefaultSwapOption | none | no | none |
| CBO | =CboData= | no | none |

The =Example_Trades= corpus holds eight credit documents with one trade
each. The two index credit default swap documents are the plain one and the
one with a bespoke basket. The other example directories carry 24 more
documents with credit trades; the corpus proof walks =Example_Trades= only,
as the rates proof did.

Two codes are in the catalogue and in the table's check but nothing imports
them. =CreditDefaultSwapOption= has no ORE data element at all in the binding.
=CBO= has one, =CboData=, with investments and a structure, and no mapper.

** The current model, surface by surface

*** The instrument table

=ores_trading_credit_instruments_tbl= keyed by =(tenant_id, trade_id)=, 25
columns. The columns by use:

| Columns | Written by |
|---------+------------|
| =reference_entity=, =currency=, =notional=, =spread=, =recovery_rate=, =day_count_fraction_code=, =payment_frequency_code=, =start_date=, =maturity_date= | every product through the one leg, where it has a leg |
| =index_name= | index credit default swap and its option; never read back |
| =linked_asset_code= | credit linked swap; never read back |
| =option_strike=, =option_expiry_date= | the option |
| =tranche_attachment=, =tranche_detachment= | the CDO |
| =tenor=, =index_series=, =seniority=, =restructuring=, =description=, =option_type= | no mapper |

The recovery rate is a continuous quantity, so =double precision= is its
right type. The tranche points are the disagreement: their columns are
=numeric(38, 12)= and their domain type is =double=. A column and its domain
type must agree.

*** The legs

The credit table holds one leg's worth of terms in its own columns:
currency, notional, spread, day counter and payment frequency. A credit
default swap has a premium leg, and a credit linked swap has four groups of
payment legs (independent, contingent, default and recovery). The mapper
reads the first contingent leg of a credit linked swap and drops the rest.
The reverse writes one fixed leg, always as the payer.

*** The ORE shape the table has to hold

The elements below are in the ORE schema and in no column of the table.

| Product | Elements the mapper drops |
|---------+---------------------------|
| CreditDefaultSwap | =ReferenceObligation=, =SettlesAccrual=, =RebatesAccrual=, =PaysAtDefaultTime=, =ProtectionPaymentTime=, =ProtectionStart=, =UpfrontDate=, =UpfrontFee=, =TradeDate=, =CashSettlementDays= |
| IndexCreditDefaultSwap | the same accrual and payment elements, =UpfrontDate=, =UpfrontFee=, =CashSettlementDays=, =BasketData= |
| IndexCreditDefaultSwapOption | =KnockOut=, =IndexTerm=, =StrikeType=, =FrontEndProtectionStartDate=, the exercise rules beyond the first date |
| SyntheticCDO | the accrual and payment elements, =UpfrontDate=, =UpfrontFee=, =BasketData= |
| CreditLinkedSwap | =SettlesAccrual=, =DefaultPaymentTime=, three of the four payment groups |
| RiskParticipationAgreement | =ParticipationRate= (required), =ProtectionFee= (required), =Underlying= (required), =SettlesAccrual=; =IssuerId= replaces the credit curve id when both are present |

The agreement is the worst case. Its schema requires =ParticipationRate=,
=ProtectionFee= and =Underlying=. The reverse mapper writes none of them, so
the exported document is not valid. The underlying holds legs, an optional
option and an optional treasury lock: the two corpus documents carry a
vanilla swap and a callable swap there.

*** The ORE binding

The credit members that carry a money value are bare floats in the binding:
=UpfrontFee= (in two structs) and the index option's =Strike=. A bare float
keeps only the nearest binary value, so the mapper reads the strike through a
double and writes it back as a nine-digit float. The FRA rework put a lexical
leaf in the binding through the reapply script. The credit money members need
the same.

The other numeric members are continuous quantities: =FixedRecoveryRate=
(four structs), =AttachmentPoint=, =DetachmentPoint= and =ParticipationRate=.
They stay bare floats, because the exact-money check keeps a recovery rate or
a tranche fraction as a binary float. =ProtectionFee= and the upfront fee of
the CDO have their own typed leaves.

*** The programming surface

The table has a full generated surface: repository, service, messaging
handlers, eventing, the shell commands, the history mapper and the
TypeScript wire protocol. 50 tracked files are named for it. The
hand-written code is the ORE mapper, =credit_instrument_mapper.cpp= (391 lines), and the
dispatch in the trade mapper and the exporter.

*** Test coverage

The credit surface has 33 test cases: seven mapper round-trip cases, eight
golden documents, one generated eventing test and 17 shell command cases.
The simulation suites concern another part of ORE and are not counted. No
repository or service test is specific to credit beyond the generated ones.

** Findings for the family

| # | Finding | Evidence |
|---+---------+----------|
| C1 | One 25-column table holds eight codes; most columns belong to one or two products | the column use table above |
| C2 | Six columns are written by no mapper: =tenor=, =index_series=, =seniority=, =restructuring=, =description=, =option_type= | no assignment in =credit_instrument_mapper.cpp= |
| C3 | =index_name= and =linked_asset_code= are written and never read back | the forward writes them; the reverse reads =reference_entity= only |
| C4 | =CreditDefaultSwapOption= and =CBO= route to the table and no importer writes them | the catalogue rows; no =forward_= function for either |
| C5 | The agreement's required elements are not exported, so the export is invalid ORE | =reverse_rpa= writes the curve id, the dates and the recovery rate only |
| C6 | The agreement's underlying trade is dropped on import, and an issuer id replaces the credit curve id | no member of =rpaData= is read beyond the curve id or issuer id, the dates and the recovery rate |
| C7 | The mapper drops most credit elements of the schema | the dropped elements table |

** The proposed entity-relationship model

The diagram is in =design_er.puml= and =design_er.png= beside this page.

*** The family header

=credit_instruments= (the table keeps its name and loses its product
columns). It holds:

| Column | Why it is on the header |
|--------+-------------------------|
| =tenant_id=, =trade_id= | the key; the trade is the instrument |
| =trade_type_code= | the check lists the codes the importer writes |
| =party_id=, =trade_activity_id= | the identity every instrument carries |
| =reference_entity= | all six products state the entity the risk is on |
| =start_date=, =maturity_date= | the common dates |
| =description= | the family's free text |

The check lists six codes. =CreditDefaultSwapOption= and =CBO= leave the
catalogue's routing until a mapper writes them: the catalogue rule is that an
empty =instrument= means no table holds the type yet.

*** The product facts

Six tables, one row per trade, keyed by =(tenant_id, trade_id)= and pinned to
the header by the generated delete cascade.

| Table | Holds |
|-------+-------|
| =credit_default_swap_instruments= | credit curve id, issuer id, reference obligation, the three accrual and payment flags, payment time, protection start, upfront date and fee, recovery rate, cash settlement days |
| =index_credit_default_swap_instruments= | credit curve id, issuer id, the flags, payment time, protection start, upfront date and fee, cash settlement days |
| =index_credit_default_swap_option_instruments= | knock-out flag, index term, strike type, front-end protection start |
| =synthetic_cdo_instruments= | qualifier, protection start, attachment and detachment points, the flags, upfront date and fee, recovery rate |
| =credit_linked_swap_instruments= | credit curve id, settles accrual, default payment time, recovery rate |
| =risk_participation_agreement_instruments= | participation rate, protection fee, credit curve id, issuer id, settles accrual, recovery rate |

The option on an index wraps =IndexCreditDefaultSwapData=, so its one fact row
carries that swap's credit curve id and the trade has no row in
=index_credit_default_swap_instruments=. The carrier holds one fact row, and a
second row would need a second carrier slot for no gain. The strike and the
exercise dates are the shared =instrument_strikes= and schedule rows, as for a
swaption. A column lands in a fact table only when a mapper writes it, so the
tables hold the columns above that the mapper reads today, and unit 6 adds the
rest with their writers. Every money column is =numeric(38, 12)=. A continuous
quantity (the recovery rate, the tranche points, the participation rate) is
=double precision=.

*** The shared children

Reused as they are, because every one is already keyed by the trade:

- =swap_legs=, =swap_leg_amounts=, =swap_leg_rates=: the premium leg of a
  swap and the payment groups of a credit linked swap. The leg's currency,
  day counter and payment frequency are columns of the leg, its notional an
  amount row and its spread or coupon a rate row.
- =instrument_options=, =instrument_strikes=, =instrument_schedules=: the
  index option's exercise rules and strike.

One column is new. =swap_legs.leg_group= is nullable text, =NULL= for a swap
and one of =premium=, =independent=, =contingent=, =default=, =recovery=,
=underlying= for a credit leg. It says which group of a credit linked swap
or which side of an agreement a leg belongs to.

One child table is new. =credit_basket_constituents= is keyed by the trade and
a sequence number and holds the name of a basket member and, where the
document states one, its weight. It serves the index swap and the CDO.

*** Why not a table per leg group

The groups of a credit linked swap have the same columns as a swap leg. A
second leg table would repeat =swap_legs=, which the rates story made
trade-keyed for exactly this reuse. A group column costs one nullable column
and no table.

** The blotter's common-date view

The header maps =start_date= and =maturity_date= to the common names with
=:common_date:=. The agreement's =ProtectionStart= and =ProtectionEnd= are
those dates. No credit product has an expiry; the option's first exercise
date is the =expiry_date= and lives on =instrument_options=, which the view
does not read yet, so the option's expiry stays out of the view until that
child maps its date.

** Mapping the current model onto the target

| Today | Becomes |
|-------+---------|
| =reference_entity= | the header |
| =start_date=, =maturity_date= | the header |
| =description= | the header |
| =currency=, =notional=, =spread=, =day_count_fraction_code=, =payment_frequency_code= | a =swap_legs= row with its amount and rate rows |
| =recovery_rate= | the fixed recovery rate of the product's fact table, as a decimal |
| =option_strike=, =option_expiry_date= | =instrument_strikes= and =instrument_options= |
| =tranche_attachment=, =tranche_detachment= | =synthetic_cdo_instruments= |
| =index_name=, =linked_asset_code= | dropped: the credit curve id of the product's table holds the name |
| =tenor=, =index_series=, =seniority=, =restructuring=, =option_type= | dropped: no mapper writes them |
| =rpa_instruments= and its surface | retired; the facts move to =risk_participation_agreement_instruments= |

No migration is written. The project recreates the database.

** What each surface must change

- *Models.* One header model loses its product columns and gains the delete
  cascade; six fact models and one child model are new; =swap_legs= gains
  =leg_group=; the =rpa_instrument= model and its files are removed.
- *Catalogue.* The two importer-less codes leave the routing; the header's
  common dates are mapped.
- *Mapper.* The six forward and reverse mappings write the header, the fact
  row, the legs and the basket. The agreement writes its required elements
  and its underlying.
- *ORE binding.* The credit money members become lexical leaves through the
  reapply script.
- *Generated surface.* The repository, service, messaging, eventing, shell
  commands, history mapper and TypeScript protocol of the seven new entities
  are generated.
- *Tests.* The mapper suite, the goldens and the corpus proof hold the
  family.

** Staging

The units are ordered so each ends in a check and none depends on a later one.

| Unit | Task | Check |
|------+------+-------|
| 1 | Analyse the credit family and propose its relational data model | this page |
| 2 | Carry the credit digits: lexical leaves for the credit money members, and the tranche columns agree with their domain type | a mapper test per money member; the goldens that change are numeric text |
| 3 | Retire the unproduced =rpa_instruments= table | the tree builds and passes without its 47 files |
| 4 | Write the credit legs and the basket | the corpus documents' legs come back as rows |
| 5 | Author the credit family header and its fact tables, with the header's common dates | codegen drift clean; the generated eventing tests pass; the corpus documents come back through the carrier |
| 6 | Close the credit round trip: the agreement's required elements and underlying, the dropped elements | the mapper round trip per product |
| 7 | Prove the credit dates in the common-date view (unit 5 declared them) | a probe across a swap and a credit trade |
| 8 | Prove the credit round trip over the corpus | eight documents, eight trades, equal family rows |

Retiring the agreement table is its own unit because it touches 47 files and
a rates wire variant, and it can land before the new tables exist.

The legs land before the header. The leg and basket tables are keyed by the
trade, not by the header, so they stand without it. The header unit removes the
currency, notional, spread, day count and payment frequency columns from the
credit table, and the legs are where those values go, so the legs must exist
first or the header unit would drop data.

** Open questions

- *Two sets of option rows on one trade.* The agreement's underlying can be a
  callable swap, which has its own =instrument_options= rows. The index option
  writes an option row on the same trade, which the shared table keys by the
  trade. The design does not yet say how an underlying's option is told apart
  from the trade's own. Unit 6 settles it: either a group or role column on
  =instrument_options=, or a rule that a trade holds one of the two. The
  corpus holds an agreement on a callable swap and an index option, never both
  on one trade, so the rule is a safe default.

* Decisions

- *The family header holds the reference entity and the two dates.* The
  six products share nothing else. Reason: a header column must hold for
  every code, and these three do.
- *Credit reuses the swap leg tables.* A =leg_group= column says which group a
  leg is in. Reason: the legs are keyed by the trade, so a second leg table
  repeats the first.
- *The two codes with no importer leave the routing.* Reason: the catalogue
  rule says a type with no writer has no table.
- *The agreement's facts get a table of their own.* Reason: its
  participation rate and protection fee are required by the schema and belong
  to no other product.
- *Every money column is an exact decimal, and a continuous quantity is a
  binary float.* Reason: the rates rework showed a float column stores an
  artifact of a money value, and the exact-money check keeps a recovery rate
  or a tranche fraction as a float.

* Out of scope

- The =CBO= product and a =CreditDefaultSwapOption= importer. They need a
  mapper and a corpus document first.
- The agreement's treasury lock underlying (=TreasuryLockData=).
- The inflation family and the rates children the agreement's underlying
  borrows beyond legs and options.
- The credit simulation suites, which concern the simulation part of ORE.
