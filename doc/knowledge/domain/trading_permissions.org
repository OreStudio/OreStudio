#+title: Trading Permissions
#+description: Sketch: the standing constraints on what may be booked, where, and by whom — distinct from the per-trade decision that operational authorisation makes.
#+type: knowledge

* Summary

#+begin_note
*This is a sketch.* It names the theme and its dimensions so that
neighbouring documents have somewhere to point, and records what
already exists. It does not propose a model.
#+end_note

A *trading permission* is a standing rule about what may be booked. A
cable book takes GBP/USD and nothing else; a desk is mandated for
vanilla options and not exotics; the firm holds a licence covering
some instruments and not others.

The rule is evaluated when a trade is booked, and a booking that
breaches it does not become a trade. That is what separates permissions
from [[id:A9E9D1EF-5879-4488-87C8-BDD914C3B502][operational authorisation]], which is a decision a person takes
about a trade that already exists.

* How it differs from authorisation

The two are easily conflated, because both answer "is this allowed",
and both are operational concerns. They differ in every other respect.

| | Trading permission | Operational authorisation |
|-+--------------------+---------------------------|
| *What it is* | A standing rule | A decision about one trade |
| *Set by* | Risk, compliance, the desk's mandate | Operations, case by case |
| *Evaluated* | When the trade is booked | After the trade exists |
| *Scope* | A class of activity | A single trade |
| *On failure* | The booking is refused | The trade sits unauthorised |
| *Changes* | Rarely, by a deliberate act | Per trade, continuously |

A permission that is absent stops a trade coming into existence. An
authorisation that is absent leaves a trade in the book contributing to
nothing, which [[id:B8D4F2A1-9E37-4C6B-A8D5-2F1E0C9B7A3D][the trade lifecycle]] covers.

* The dimensions

Three questions, and a permission constrains some combination of them.

** What may be traded

- A *product type*, from the catalogue in [[id:C5C95B29-2EF9-4DFC-9E04-428C41C1925D][ORE Products by Asset Class]].
- A *product family* or an *asset class*, where the mandate is coarser
  than a single product: vanilla options but not exotics, rates but
  not credit. [[id:936ACDC4-830B-434E-82E7-A92276869869][Product family]] and [[id:7F5EBFBD-FB8C-4E79-B386-7098104D3EDB][asset classes]] supply those
  classifications.
- A specific *underlying* or *currency pair*: GBP/USD on a cable book.
- A *counterparty*, where the firm is not permitted to face one at all.

** Where it may be booked

- A *book*, which is the finest and most common grain.
- A *desk* or *business unit*, inherited by the books beneath it.
- A *legal entity*, where a licence or a local regulation binds one
  entity of the group and not another.
- The *firm*, where the constraint is universal.

The levels compose, and the narrowest applies. A book cannot widen
what its entity permits.

** Who is driving it

A permission is usually not Operations' own rule, even though
Operations enforces it.

| Source | Typical constraint |
|--------+--------------------|
| Risk | Exposure to an asset class or underlying |
| Compliance | Restricted lists, Chinese walls between desks |
| Licensing | Instruments the entity is authorised to deal in |
| The desk's mandate | What this desk exists to trade |

Recording which of these a permission comes from matters, because it
determines who may change it and what evidence a change requires.

* What already exists

Two pieces of this are modelled.

[[id:C5561A84-D060-4AB4-AB40-4ABC08875040][Book currencies]] holds the allowed-currency set that constrains what
may be booked into a book, which is the currency dimension at book
grain.

[[id:C84899BD-5EC8-4AA7-88D7-CE7ED19BC573][Book access and permissions]] holds the fine-grained model by which
books are permissioned and users grouped, including Chinese walls
between desks. That is access control — who may see and act on a book —
rather than a constraint on what the book may hold, and the two are
worth distinguishing even though they share the word.

Neither covers product type, product family, asset class or underlying,
and nothing holds a firm-wide or entity-wide constraint.

* Open questions

- Whether a permission is a rule evaluated at booking or a
  pre-computed set a book carries. The second answers "what may I
  book here" without evaluation, at the cost of recomputing on every
  change.
- Whether a breach is always a refusal, or whether some permissions
  produce a warning and a trade requiring escalation instead.
- How a permission is evidenced when a regulator asks, given the
  source matters as much as the rule.
- Whether the meta-authorisations recorded in [[id:A9E9D1EF-5879-4488-87C8-BDD914C3B502][operational
  authorisation]] belong here instead. A standing permission to trade FX
  vanillas is a permission by the definition above, whatever the notes
  call it.

* Sources

The theme is drawn from the internal analysis notes on authorisation,
which record standing permissions alongside per-trade decisions
without separating them. No external standard is cited.
