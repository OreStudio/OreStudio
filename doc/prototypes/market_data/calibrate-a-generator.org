* Summary

A synthetic feed takes its first value from a public fixing, and the size of
its moves from the spread of that fixing's returns. The prototype argues how
that works with no configuration: the defaults calibrate every feed, and a
class policy, a pin on one feed or an explicit list are optional refinements.
It exists because the first draft gave every feed its own ranked list, and
that does not scale: with a hundred feeds, a person would author a hundred
lists and still not see the market's coverage. The page opens in automatic
mode and offers three variants of the same screen. Vintage is retired and the
screen says so.

* The question

The runner resolves a feed at start: it walks the candidates, skips one whose
provider's terms are not accepted or whose fixing is missing or after the
clock, takes the first usable one, reads it as =1/x= when the item quotes the
pair the other way round, and scales the mixture by the measured spread. The
question is where the ranking comes from. A list on every feed is exact and
unmaintainable. A policy on the class is one decision, but it must still let a
person fix an exception and see what the policy does to every feed.

* The variants

** A — Policy first

The card opens in automatic mode with the defaults a person can accept without
touching anything, and a *Customise this class* action reveals the window, the
inverse rule, the provider order and the fallback. Under it, the coverage
table resolves every feed and counts what came from a fixing, what kept a
level and what is blocked. This variant argues that the market's calibration
can be one decision when wanted, and needs no decision at all.

** B — Feed inherits

The feed page opens on automatic calibration, the resolved start and the
derived candidate list, each candidate with its reason. Two selects pin an
item or a window for this feed only, and the banner turns from automatic to
override. This variant argues that adding a feed is reading what the defaults
did, and the exception is the rare act.

** C — Flow

The policy, the candidates for one pair, the resolver and the feed as one
path, over the recalculation record and the class coverage. This variant
argues that the policy is a pipeline a person can trace, and every
recalculation is the proof that it ran.

Switch with the bottom bar, or with =?variant= (=a=, =b= or =c=; the default is
=a=).

* What it settles

- **Calibration is automatic.** With no policy, the provider catalogue order
  and a default window calibrate every feed; a person configures nothing.
- **A policy is optional.** A class may state its window, inverse rule, order
  and fallback; returning to automatic is one action.
- **Candidates are derived, not authored.** The pair and the provider order
  produce the list; direct quotes come before inverses, and a provider whose
  terms are not accepted never starts a feed.
- **An exception is optional and reversible.** A feed pins one item or one
  window; the banner says it overrides, and clearing the pin returns it to the
  policy or the defaults.
- **Coverage is the point of the policy screen.** How many feeds resolve, keep
  a level or are blocked is the number a person governs, and a recalculation
  records every start.
- **The fallback is a policy choice.** Keep the fixed level or refuse to start;
  the screen states which, and no feed invents a second source.
- **The volatility is measured.** The daily spread, the hourly it implies, the
  window and the factor are shown with the start.
- **Vintage is absent.** No vintage control, field or wording offers one.

* What it does not settle

- Who may edit the policy, and whether a change needs approval. The prototype
  edits it in place.
- When a recalculation runs: on a config save, on every start, daily, or only
  when a person asks. The prototype offers the button and shows the record.
- Where the policy lives in the product: a market data settings page, the feed
  class page, or the feed screen itself. The prototype draws it as a class
  screen.
- The exception's granularity beyond one pinned item and one window.
- Whether a provider's rejection removes its items from the list or greys them.
  The prototype keeps them visible with their reason, so a person sees why a
  pair lost its best source.

* The mock data

Six FX feeds (EUR/USD, GBP/USD, USD/JPY, EUR/GBP, EUR/CHF, AUD/USD) and eleven
items across the ECB, the IMF, the Bank of Canada and the TCMB, at a market
clock of 2026-10-31. The policy orders ECB, IMF, BoC and allows inverses.
EUR/USD resolves from the ECB, GBP/USD only as an inverse of the IMF's USD/GBP,
and AUD/USD has no covering source, so it keeps its level. Turning the inverse
off sends GBP/USD to its fixed level; setting the fallback to refuse blocks
AUD/USD and counts it. The state panel prints the policy and each feed's start
after every action.
