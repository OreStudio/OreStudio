* Goal

Our FX spot conventions disagree with the market in four places, and one of them is visible on screen: a GBP/USD series shows the same number for several ticks, because the rate is printed to the pip and not to the pipette, and USD/CAD settles two days out where the market settles it in one. The investigation [[id:65FC9650-5C05-4670-B95E-B4F2EF022B99][The FX spot conventions against the market]] records the evidence and its sources. This story corrects the model, the seed and the display so a rate we hold reads the way the market quotes it.

* Acceptance

- A pair's spot lag is stated, per pair, instead of derived as the max of its two legs, so USD/CAD, USD/TRY, USD/PHP and USD/RUB settle T+1 and every other pair T+2.
- A rate's quote precision is the market's and not the pip's: five decimals for a non-JPY pair and three for a JPY pair, so a movement of a fraction of a pip shows on the series page.
- The smallest spot increment is a fraction of a pip, set per venue, and not one whole pip, so the tick size agrees with the model default.
- End-of-month forward dates follow the market, and the choice stays configurable.
- The volatility a synthetic spot feed realises matches the volatility it states, and the annualisation clock it uses is written down.
- The domain page that states the old precision, and the pages that cite it, are corrected to the market convention.
