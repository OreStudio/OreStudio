#+title: The regulatory treatment of an FX forward turns on settlement and purpose
#+description: The US Treasury exemption for deliverable FX swaps and forwards, and the EU spot and means-of-payment exclusions.
#+type: knowledge
#+level: cross
#+filetags: :domain:finance:fx:fx_forward:
#+created: 2026-11-09

* Summary

Regulation depends on how the contract settles and what it is for. A physically settled FX swap or forward has a US exemption, and an EU contract can fall outside MiFID II by its delivery window or its purpose.

* Detail

** United States

The 2012 Treasury determination exempts FX swaps and forwards from the Commodity Exchange Act definition of a swap. They avoid mandatory clearing, mandatory trade execution and margin. They remain subject to trade reporting and to anti-manipulation rules. The exemption does not cover FX options, currency swaps or non-deliverable forwards.

** European Union

Article 10 of Delegated Regulation 2017/565 excludes a spot contract, delivered within two trading days for major pairs. It also excludes a contract that is a means of payment, such as one used for identifiable goods, services or direct investment, if it meets all the conditions. The EMIR definition of a derivative refers to MiFID financial instruments, so an FX contract outside this exclusion can be an EMIR derivative.
