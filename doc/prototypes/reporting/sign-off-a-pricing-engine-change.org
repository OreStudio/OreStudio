* Summary

This prototype draws the control around the pricing engines: how a change to an official configuration is signed off. A party holds few official configurations, one for each cycle, and model risk controls their settings. A change to one is therefore a proposal, and the version in force stays in force until every role that model risk names has approved it.

The page is a record list and a record page, as the [[id:2BF7F03F-6BF4-45FF-8E26-4D3B4E2D943D][Record screen standard]] draws them. The queue lists the proposals. A proposal's page has four tabs: Sign-off, Changes, Reach and History. The Sign-off tab lists each role with its decision, the person, the time and the comment, and states the four-eyes rule: the person who proposed a change cannot sign it off, even when they hold one of the roles.

The page answers to the journey [[id:41C7A1F2-8E5B-4D36-9A0C-7B2E5D1F6A93][Sign off a pricing engine change]]. It is throwaway: it reads no service, writes nothing to one, and states which parts of the interaction no operation supports. It follows the rules in [[file:../index.org][Prototypes]]: plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript, no framework, no build and no backend, with the design tokens of =projects/ores.web/packages/web/src/styles.css=.

* Detail

** The question the screen answers

"I must decide if this change to the way we price is safe, and I must leave a record that I decided." Each role reads the same change, the same reach and the same evidence, and decides for its own role.

** States and switches

- =?screen=queue= and =?screen=proposal&id=pr1|pr2|pr3&tab=signoff|changes|reach|history=. =pr1= is a proposal for version 5 of End of day pricing. =pr2= is a proposal for Intraday pricing. =pr3= is an earlier proposal that was made official.
- =?proposal=pending|partly|ready|rejected|superseded= sets the state of =pr1=: no approval, one of three, all three, rejected by market risk, or out of date because another version was approved first.
- =?actor=proposer|quant|risk|validator|viewer= sets the person. The proposer holds a market-risk role, so the refusal is reachable: the row shows no button and says another person in the role must sign it off. A viewer sees no button.
- =?state=all|empty|error= for the queue.
- Approve asks the signer to confirm that they read the changes and the reach. Reject needs a reason. Making a proposal official names the reports it reaches. A rebase moves an out-of-date proposal onto the version in force and clears the approvals. A withdrawal closes an open proposal.

** Rules the page follows

- *Four eyes.* The proposer cannot sign off. The screen shows the refusal and states that the server must also enforce it, because no operation does.
- *The version in force stays in force.* Every state of a proposal says so, and the Reach tab says that a run made after the change is not comparable with an earlier run for the changed products.
- *The roles are assumed.* The quant team, market risk and model validation are a fixture. Model risk confirms the roles, their order and the number of people each needs.
- *A rejection needs a reason and deletes nothing.* The proposal stays in the history.

** What is fake

Every value. No operation holds a proposal, a decision, a status, a role or a policy. The evidence files are names. The reports, their portfolios and their product types are fixtures. The roles are the page's assumption.

** Where the code lives

=doc/prototypes/reporting/sign-off-a-pricing-engine-change/index.html=. One file, plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript, with the style and the script inline, no modules, no fetch and no build step.

** What it argues

- A change to an official configuration is a proposal, and the sign-off is its own screen, because the control is the screen's job.
- The person who proposes cannot sign off, and the page draws the refusal as a state that a reader can reach.
- A proposal that every role approved is not yet in force. Someone makes it official, and the page names what that reaches.
- A proposal that sits on an old version must be rebased, and the approvals do not carry over.

** The verdict

None yet. This page is on the branch that proposes it and waits for review.

** What it does not settle

- The roles, their order and the number of people each needs. Model risk decides.
- Whether making a proposal official needs a role of its own, or an effective date other than the next run.
- Whether a candidate that replaces the official configuration is the same flow. It also rebinds the reports of a cycle, and the page does not draw that step.
- Whether a rejection by one role ends the proposal, or the other roles may still decide.
