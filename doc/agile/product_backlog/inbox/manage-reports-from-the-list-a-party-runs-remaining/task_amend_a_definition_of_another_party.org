* Goal

Let the page that opened a definition save it, whichever party owns it.

The reporting list spans the parties the session sees: for the tenant
administrator of ACME that is the login party and the three below it, 78
definitions across four parties. Opening one of them works, because the row is
read by its id. Saving one that belongs to a party other than the acting party
does not: the insert trigger refuses the write.

The fault is not the definition's. It is the write's row-level scope. The
trigger supersedes the current row by looking it up under the write's scope, and
a row of another party is not in that scope, so the trigger finds nothing, treats
the write as a new row, and inserts it against a table whose unique index on
(tenant, name, party) still holds the old current row. The person sees a
database fault about a constraint, which says nothing about what they did.

* Acceptance

- An amend of a definition of any party the person reads either lands, or is
  refused in words that say a definition of another party is not amended from
  here.
- The refusal is a refusal and not a fault: no constraint name reaches the
  screen.
- A write to the acting party's own definition is unchanged.
- The write still claims the version it was read at, and a stale version is still
  a conflict.
