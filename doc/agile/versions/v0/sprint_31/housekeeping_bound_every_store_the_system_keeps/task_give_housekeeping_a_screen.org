* Acceptance

- The screen lists each policy with its target, period, state and the size of its target
- An enabled policy that has freed nothing is visible as such
- A person can enable, disable and change a period, and the change is recorded

* Plan

The screen reads one answer and writes one small change, so the work went
bottom-up in four layers, each provable before the next.

1. *The read serves the screen.* The bounds read carried what bounds a store and
   how large it is, and not the policy's cadence, its version or its words, nor
   the store's time column and measure. A second read of the policy would have
   been a second answer to the same question, and the form that changes a bound
   has to send the row back whole, so the group and the row grew instead:
   =store_bound_action= gained =cadence_hours=, =policy_version= and
   =policy_description=, and =store_bound= gained =time_column= and
   =measure_kind=. The view answers them, and the write composes from them.
2. *Two facts the view had wrong.* The bounds view could not be read at all: its
   select list omitted the description the repository asked for, so the previous
   task's read would have failed on its first call. And it answered a store as
   bounded when the last sweep had failed. Both are corrected in the view, with
   the failure stated as unbounded because the mechanism exists and did nothing.
3. *The wire and the BFF.* One wire module reads the bounds, reads a series and
   writes a bound; the write takes the store's contract from the row the route
   just read, so a client can change a period and never a target. Three routes
   serve them, and none composes a bound.
4. *The screens.* The list states the counts, the verdict in red and one row per
   store, ordered with the unbounded first; a row opens the store's page, which
   states the record, the size the sampler last found, the plot of a week, the
   bound with its last sweep, and the form. The prototype's editor moved into the
   store's page, as the review decided, and is offered only to a role that holds
   the policy write.

The journeys were updated in the same change, because they are the design of
record and three of the gaps they stated are now closed.

* Result

Housekeeping has a screen, and the read behind it answers on a live installation.

- *The list* states every store with its verdict, what bounds it, the size it
  holds and its last sweep. Unbounded is red, the unbounded stores sort first,
  and the filter narrows the table to them; the summary states the count that has
  to reach zero and the ground the stores occupy.
- *The store's page* states the catalogue record, the size now with the plot of a
  week of measurements, and the bound with its period, its cadence, its policy
  and its last run. The form that changes whether the bound applies, for how long
  and how often sits there, offered to a role that holds the policy write and to
  no other. A store the catalogue says no period can bound states that instead of
  offering a form.
- *The screen composes nothing.* =scheduler.v1.store_bounds.list= answers the
  verdict the database states, and =scheduler.v1.store_samples.list= answers the
  series. The write sends three numbers and the reason; the server composes the
  policy from the catalogue row it reads, so a client can change a period and
  never a target.
- *The read grew what a form needs,* and lost a defect that made it unreadable:
  the view's select list omitted the description the read asked for, so the
  previous task's read would have failed on its first call. The view also states
  a store unbounded when its last sweep failed, because the mechanism exists and
  did nothing.
- *The two journeys* are updated to what exists, and the gaps that remain — the
  runs ledger, the object store's own numbers, a dry run — are named there.

Proved by the scheduler's three suites, including four cases that state the
view's columns as the database sends them; 8 cases on the wire reads and the
write; 8 on the three routes; 24 on the two screens; the non-heavy checks; and
the read run against a recreated database, which answered the ten stores with
their bounds and, after one sampler pass, the calendar dates' 283 MiB.
