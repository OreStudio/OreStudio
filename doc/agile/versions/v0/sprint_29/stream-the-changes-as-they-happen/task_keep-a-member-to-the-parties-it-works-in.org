* Plan

- A subscription states a =stream_scope=: its tenant, the party the caller works
  in, and whether the caller sees every party. The hub filters on it when it
  queues a frame and when it replays one, so a resume cannot widen the scope.
- A change that names no party concerns the whole tenant and reaches every party.
  A change that names one reaches that party and the administrator.
- The route reads the party and roles from the caller's claims: =TenantAdmin= and
  =SuperAdmin= see every party. The gateway decides with what the caller already
  proved rather than asking IAM, which is what a stream needs: a per-frame call
  to IAM would be a round trip per change.

* Result

A stream now hears its own party and the tenant-wide changes, and an
administrator hears every party of the tenant. The hub filters on a
=stream_scope= when it queues a frame and when it replays one, so a reconnecting
client cannot widen what it sees by asking for a resume. A change that names no
party concerns the whole tenant and reaches every party; one that names a party
reaches that party and the administrator.

The route takes the party and the roles from the caller's claims, so the
decision costs nothing per frame: =TenantAdmin= and =SuperAdmin= see every party.

Three cases prove it: a member hears their party and the tenant-wide changes and
not another party's; an administrator hears every party of the tenant and no
other tenant; and a replay applies the same rule.
