* Goal

The calendar dates exist once per installation, in the system tenant, and every
tenant reads them there, so adding a tenant adds no copy of a calendar it shares
with every other tenant. A party keeps its own holidays: its exceptions stay its
own rows and are applied over the shared days when it reads them.

This task follows the party scope, not the other way round: the overlay is per
party, so the exception must name its party first
([[id:BB0570CB-7C89-4079-AE37-39EB300F2252][Scope the calendar exceptions to a
party]]).

* Acceptance

- The dates are materialised into the system tenant, once, and no tenant holds a
  copy
- A tenant reads them through the shared-reference policy, as it reads the
  calendars and the exceptions
- A party's own exceptions still change the days that party reads, in both
  directions: an extra holiday closes a day the rules open, and a business-day
  override opens a day they close
- Two parties in one tenant read different days on the same date when their
  exceptions differ
- A party with no exception of its own reads the shared days unchanged
- The recompute path stays, and rebuilding the system tenant's copy is a command
  rather than a migration
- A test covers a tenant reading the installation's dates

* Analysis

The decision is recorded on
[[id:A54B0989-BFCB-45C6-80B7-279BF9843E78][Decide whether calendar dates are
stored or computed]]. In short: a calendar's days follow from its rule and its
exceptions, both of which the platform authors and publishes once, so the days
are the same in every tenant. A per-tenant copy is therefore the same bytes
written twice, and the treatment the classification names for derived reference
data -- recompute, or share one copy -- applies.

What this task does not do: it does not stop materialising. The dates sit on the
pricing path, and moving the computation there would charge every run for work
the installation can do once. It moves the materialisation's target from the
caller's tenant to the system tenant, and makes the copy readable across
tenants.

The read is the part that needs care, and it has two halves. The first is the
policy: =ores_refdata_calendar_dates_tbl= carries the tenant in its key and its
reads filter on the caller's tenant, so a shared-reference read policy must admit
the system tenant's rows, as the exceptions' own read already does with
=where (tenant_id = caller or tenant_id = system)=.

The second is the overlay, and it is what keeps a party's own holidays. The
shared copy is the baseline the rules state; the party's exceptions are applied
over it at read time, with the semantics the materialiser already uses:
=is_business_day = false= closes a day the rules open and =true= opens a day they
close. The read is therefore a merge keyed by date and party rather than a plain
select, and it is cheap: a party that adds no exception carries no rows to merge,
and a party that does carries a handful. A test that publishes one party's
exception and reads the dates must show the day changed for that party and
unchanged for another.

* Notes

** Where the work stands

Started, not finished. The dates table now admits the installation's rows beside
the caller's (the shared-reference read policy), and nothing else has changed.

The next step, and its blocker: the days move to the system tenant, and every
reader then has to read them there. That means one read per reader, of the shape
=`(tenant_id = caller or tenant_id = system)=`, and the generated finder reads
only the caller's tenant. The obvious home is a custom repository method on
=ores.refdata.calendar_date=, as the exceptions now have, but that model states
=:client_read_only: true= and the generator emits no repository for it, so the
custom method is silently dropped. Two ways forward, and the choice is the next
decision: drop =:client_read_only:= and take the generated repository with a
custom read on it, or write the read by hand beside the generated service and
leave the model as it is.

The work was carried as far as a build that compiles and a suite that passes, and
then reverted to that state rather than left half-applied: the materialisation
writing into the system tenant without the matching read leaves every reader
seeing nothing, which the refdata suite showed as seventeen failures.
