* Goal

One loop schedules every feed. Feed i fires when tau reaches its next mark over its sensitivity times its base rate, so a market change moves no heap entry and takes effect at once. tick_clock, refresh and re_rate are gone, and every feed takes a scheduler.

* Acceptance

- A tick costs O(log n) in the number of feeds, with a benchmark at 100,000 feeds.
- On the manual clock with a fixed seed, a test asserts the exact sequence of feeds and event times across a pinned regime change.
- The per-series checks still hold: mean rate, Fano factor and time rescaling.
- The service-test fixtures and the adapter test run on a scheduler, the tests that assert times use the manual clock, and tick_clock is deleted.
- Control operations reach the loop as commands applied at the clock's now.
- set_baseline_multiplier and i_arrival_process are gone: the market reaches a law only through the clock.
- The Arrivals panel help says a burst fades faster in a busy market, because a Hawkes law runs in operational time.
- A tick's observation time comes from the run clock, not from system_clock::now().

* Plan

This task and [[id:6207909E-FC52-4EC4-86DE-DA76CEDB8D98][Let one loop own the market]]
land in one change. A scheduler keyed on tau has to own the operational
clock, and owning it is the other task's change. Done apart, this task
would need a bridge from the old market_regime to the scheduler, and the
next change would delete it.

- =tick_scheduler= is rewritten in place, so the feeds and handlers keep the
  name. It owns the =operational_clock=, borrows an =i_run_clock=, and keeps
  a min-heap of series keyed on the tau mark at which each fires.
- A series with sensitivity s whose law draws a gap g fires when tau has
  moved by g / s. The mark is fixed when the gap is drawn, so a change in
  the market moves no heap entry. A removed series leaves its mark behind,
  and the mark is dropped when it reaches the top.
- The loop fires every tick due by now in order, then moves the market to
  now and applies the queued commands, then waits for the next tick or the
  next heat sample. A command therefore never makes a due tick late.
- =remove= and =pin= wait until the loop has applied them; before the loop
  runs, and on its own thread, a command applies at once.
- =step()= takes the next tick without waiting, for tests on the manual
  clock.
- A feed takes the scheduler by reference, registers its body at start, and
  is told the wall seconds since its last tick and the wall time of the
  tick. The tick stamp comes from the run clock.
- =running_scheduler= runs a scheduler on the steady clock for as long as it
  lives, for the service tests that wait for ticks over NATS.
