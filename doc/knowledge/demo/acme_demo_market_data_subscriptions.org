#+title: A subscription names the provider and the route names the source
#+description: The two rows that entitle an Acme party to market data, what the provisioning step writes for each party, and the flow a demand follows from a report to a tick the party receives.
#+type: knowledge
#+level: cross
#+filetags: :knowledge:marketdata:acme:demo:subscriptions:

* Summary

Two rows tie an Acme party to market data, and they answer two different
questions. The subscription says which providers the party may take and with
which rights; it never names a source. The route says which source serves each
of those providers for this party, and there is exactly one. The demonstration
writes both for every party of the tenant in one provisioning step, so a party
that lands in the tenant later is subscribed the same way. Rights are display,
non-display and derived and all three are true, which is what lets a curve be
bootstrapped from other series and used in a report as well as drawn on a screen.

* Detail

** The two rows

#+ATTR_HTML: :class hug-leading
| The row | Its key | Answers |
|---------+---------+---------|
| =subscription= | party, provider | may this party take this provider, and for which rights |
| =route= | party, provider | which of the provider's sources serves this party |

The separation is the design, not a normalisation accident. A subscription names
a provider and never a source, because the licence is with the provider and
because the same instrument must map the same way whichever source serves it;
[[id:31BCA3F8-31AD-49C2-9B6B-F2312EE7DECB][A party takes a provider's feeds
through a subscription]] states it. A route names one source per provider for the
party, because two sources for one provider would deliver the same series twice;
[[id:37B27CA8-9F44-4A61-9B02-3EAABD8C3818][A party routes to exactly one source
for each provider]] states it. The rights are
[[id:B746D4F2-F1B5-4563-A6E4-D36BDF8E6BBE][display, non-display and
derived]], modelled on the subscription.

** What the demonstration writes

#+ATTR_HTML: :class hug-leading
| Party | Subscription (provider) | display | non-display | derived | Route (source) |
|-------+-------------------------+---------+-------------+---------+----------------|
| Acme Corporation Plc | =bloomberg= | yes | yes | yes | =bloomberg-a= |
| ACME Corporation UK plc | =demo= | yes | yes | yes | =demo-a= |
| ACME Corporation US Inc | =lseg= | yes | yes | yes | =lseg-a= |
| ACME Corporation HK Ltd | =bloomberg= | yes | yes | yes | =bloomberg-a= |
| System Party | =bloomberg= | yes | yes | yes | =bloomberg-a= |

The step is =subscribe_market_data=, the eighth step of the profile, and its own
arguments name one provider and one source as the default and then the parties
that differ from it. In the shipped profile the default is =bloomberg= through
=bloomberg-a=, which is exactly what the group, Hong Kong and the system party
take, and London and New York are the two rows that differ. A party the step does
not name takes the default, so a fifth entity added to the tenant is subscribed
without the profile changing. The step's arguments are
[[id:6014CFE1-D978-4ABA-BBA9-D87040FE44BA][its own]].

** The step runs for every party, not only for the named ones

The step pages the tenant's parties, matches each against the rows it names by id
or by full name, and writes the two rows for every party on the page. Three
consequences follow.

- A party the step names twice, or names by a name the tenant does not hold, is a
  failure: the step refuses rather than subscribing three of four parties.
- The rows are written idempotently. Re-running the profile looks for a
  subscription of that party and provider, and a route of that party and provider,
  and writes only what is missing, so a second run changes nothing.
- A subscription the party administrator later narrows is not widened by a second
  run of the profile, because the read finds the row. Whether the whole profile
  should overwrite a party's choices is a question the profile and the journey
  answer differently; see /What the journeys leave undecided/.

** The flow a demand follows

#+caption: The Acme demonstration: the provisioning step writes the two rows, a run registers demand, the service resolves the party's route, and the routed source's ticks are stored for that party.
#+attr_html: :width 100%
[[proj:doc/knowledge/demo/acme_market_data_demand_and_subscription.png]]

Source: [[./acme_market_data_demand_and_subscription.puml][=acme_market_data_demand_and_subscription.puml=]]. Regenerate with
=plantuml -tpng doc/knowledge/demo/acme_market_data_demand_and_subscription.puml=.

A run does not ask a party for its data. It names an item and an as-of, and the
service resolves the rest: the provider that carries the item, the party's route
to a source of that provider, the vendor id the source announces, and then the
generator that produces it. The tick comes back on the party's own stream, stored
under the party, and the delivery is counted against the party, the provider and
the item so that the bill follows the use. A simulated source carries a rate card
of the same shape as a live one, so the demonstration shows the bill the same use
would cost on a live source;
[[id:1B8A3DB7-006A-4E14-AC11-57FA3741222D][A rate card on the source turns
usage into a bill the platform shares]] states the general case.

** Who may change them

#+ATTR_HTML: :class hug-leading
| Act | Permission | Role holding it in Acme |
|-----+------------+-------------------------|
| Read the party's subscriptions and routes | =marketdata::subscriptions:read= and =marketdata::routes:read= | Trading, Sales, Support, Viewer and Operations |
| Add or change a subscription or a route | =marketdata::subscriptions:write= | Operations |
| Remove one | =marketdata::subscriptions:delete= | Operations |
| Read the sources the routes point at | =marketdata::market_data_sources:read= | the same five roles |

The journey names the party administrator as the actor who changes a
subscription, and no seeded role is called that: the write sits with Operations,
which is the function that maintains the sources and the routes in this
deployment. [[id:D46E4DEF-7431-419B-BA88-476AA7B98D24][Manage market data
subscriptions]] is the journey, and
[[id:3F19F33A-161C-4E61-BD92-CB32953DA8F9][the permission matrix]] holds the
whole table by role.
