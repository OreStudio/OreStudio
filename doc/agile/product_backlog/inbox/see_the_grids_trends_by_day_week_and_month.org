* What

The grid screen shows the last hour, day or week. A person also needs the shape over longer spans, so that an outlier stands out. Show three views of the grid's load, throughput and outcomes:

- *Daily averages.* One value for each day, over a range of weeks.
- *Monthly averages.* One value for each month.
- *Averages by day of the week.* The mean of every Monday, every Tuesday and so on, so that a quiet Tuesday or a busy Friday can be seen against its own kind.

An outlier is a day, a month or a weekday whose figure sits far from the norm of its kind. The screen should mark it, for example a day more than two standard deviations from the mean of the same weekday.

* Why

The charts of the grid screen read the stored samples in buckets of one minute to thirty minutes, over at most a week. The samples are kept for thirty days on a TimescaleDB licence that sets a retention policy, and without bound otherwise, so they are too short or too fine to show a trend by week or by month. Three points need a decision:

- *Source.* A read over the raw samples with a coarser bucket is enough for days and weeks while the samples last. Months and weekday averages need either a longer retention or a daily roll-up table that keeps one row for each day, tenant and party.
- *Scope.* The averages follow the session's scope as the series does: the whole grid for the operator, the tenant for a tenant administrator, the party for a party member. A roll-up row therefore has the same tenant and party as a sample row.
- *Outliers.* The marking needs a rule that the owner chooses, and a minimum number of days before it is shown.
