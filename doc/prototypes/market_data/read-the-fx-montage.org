* Summary

A montage is for a base currency, not for a pair: =AUD= brings =AUD/USD= and
=GBP/AUD= together because both are AUD crosses, even though the second spells
AUD on the quote side. The prototype argues that one wall of quotes plus one
opened pair is the right amount of screen, and that the change, the day's high
and low, and the instant they are measured from are the server's facts.

It is ported from hedgr's =prototypes/fx_spot_montage.html=. hedgr's Forwards tab
is not ported: ORE Studio reads a pair's forwards on its own page and journey,
and a second forward view here would be a second definition.

* Detail

** The question

Is a montage a wall of quotes, or a board of moves? A dealer checks a level per
provider; a salesperson wants the move and the range. The variant decides.

** The variants

Switch with the bar at the foot, or =?variant=.

| Variant | Name              | The structural argument |
|---------+-------------------+-------------------------|
| =a=     | Providers as columns | Each provider's spot, the consensus, the age and a sparkline. The full picture. |
| =b=     | A board of moves  | One row per pair: last, change, low, high, age and the day's shape. No provider columns. |

** The states

- =?state=full= — six crosses, most quoted by two providers.
- =?state=thin= — two pairs lose a provider and one keeps only a derived value.
  The cell is a dash, and a consensus over one value is that value.
- =?state=stale= — every quote is over the threshold.
- =?state=none= — no provider quotes a cross of the base. The page says so and
  does not carry yesterday's price.

** What is fake

Every price, age and move is mock, and the intraday series behind the sparkline
and the chart is a made-up walk. The page computes the consensus and the change
so its own numbers agree; the server will state them, with the window, because a
client must not decide what "today" means.

** Where the code lives

=doc/prototypes/market_data/read-the-fx-montage/index.html=, self-contained.

** The verdict

Take it, minus the Forwards tab, plus the stated change window. The window is
the point of the screen: hedgr showed high, low and change without saying what
they were measured from, and a person reading "+0.09%" needs the instant it
started at.

** What changed from hedgr

| Item | hedgr | Here | Why |
|------+-------+------+-----|
| Tabs | Spot, Forwards, Chart | Spot and the opened pair on one page | Forwards has its own page and journey; the chart is the row's detail |
| Flags | Emoji flags | Emoji flags, kept | Nothing argued differently |
| Change window | Implied | Stated as a fact under the table | A client must not decide what today means |
| Chrome | A dialog | A page with a facts row | Prototypes read as the product |
| Pair list | Six AUD crosses | The same six, including GBP/AUD | The base-on-the-quote-side case is the argument |
