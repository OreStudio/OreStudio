* Goal

The fourth Access journey runs in =ores.web=: the member asks for a role
beyond the one registration gave them, and the tenant administrator answers.

The journey was designed around a =workflow_instance= that waits on a person.
It was landed a different way, by
[[id:7CCED02B-8B95-4FF1-94F8-B67BE86C4256][Complete the ores.inbox approvals
and notifications]]: an approval request is its own record with a state and an
answer, and the role grant is its first kind. That story built the request, the
four-eyes guard, the grant, the two notifications and both screens. Four of the
journey's seven gaps closed there, and a fifth was answered by the change of
design.

What remained was what no screen could yet say: which roles a member may ask
for, and what becomes of a request that nobody answers. Both are built. A role
now says whether it may be asked for, and a request carries a deadline, closes
as expired when it passes, and tells the person who asked. The journey document
has been rewritten around the design all of this landed, and it marks every one
of its seven gaps.

[[id:D5A29471-B0E3-4C69-B7CE-3477752C8ED7][Implement the access journeys in
ores.web]] when that story was reworded to the three journeys it delivered
and closed DONE at the sprint 26 close on 2026-10-05; the task Land the role
request moved here.

* Decisions

- *A request is a record, not a waiting workflow.* The journey put the request
  in =ores.workflow= as an instance that suspends and resumes. The workflow
  engine cannot wait on a person, and extending it to do so would serve this one
  journey. The request is instead a row of its own with a state, a raiser and an
  answer, and the states are =waiting=, =held=, =approved=, =refused=,
  =withdrawn= and =expired=. Recorded in
  [[id:0FE6BDD9-EB24-4B07-8B0C-D816AAF1BEBF][Approval requests]].
- *The journey's five request subjects were not built.* They named IAM as the
  holder of the queue and the decision. The queue is generic across kinds, so it
  belongs to =ores.inbox=, and IAM keeps only the detail of a role grant. See
  [[id:2BA72C77-99DC-4538-8937-52A572CF6F53][Role requests]].
- *Four eyes is a rule of the store, not of the screen.* The decisions table
  refuses a decision by the person who asked, so a screen cannot be the only
  guard.
- *An approval is not a grant.* IAM reconciles approved requests into role
  assignments exactly once, rather than granting at the moment of the decision.

* Out of scope

- The notification that tells an administrator a request is waiting, and that
  tells the member the answer. Built by the inbox story, not here.

* Result

The journey is done, and the document that describes it says what was built
rather than what was intended.

Four tasks. The request, the four-eyes guard, the grant and both screens landed
under the inbox story. This story added the two things no screen could yet say:
a role states whether it may be asked for (PR #2754), and a request carries a
deadline it takes from its kind, closes as expired when it passes, and tells the
person who asked (PR #2784). The fourth task rewrote
[[id:DF0FBED2-59C1-43DE-945F-D8839884C38D][Ask for more access]] around the
design all of that landed (PR #2785).

Six of the journey's seven gaps are closed and the seventh is half closed. Two
of the six were closed by changing the design rather than by building what the
gap asked for: the request became a record of its own, so the workflow engine
was never the holder and needed no waiting state, and the queue became a read of
a table rather than a projection over an instance's payload. That is recorded in
the document itself, in a table with a state per gap, because it is the part a
future reader would otherwise have to reconstruct.

What is left of the seventh gap — a reminder before a request lapses, rather
than the close after it does — is captured as "nobody reminds a decider that a
request is about to lapse". It is a different behaviour from the deadline, with
its own timing and its own audience, and it was deliberately not built here.

Four decisions this story made are in the =* Decisions= above. The one worth
repeating: *work that recurs is the component's to declare and the
installation's to schedule*. The sweep is the first thing to take that shape,
and the pattern is written up on
[[id:F06175E8-CA73-4A21-BEC5-898D0A388E17][Approval request expiry]].
