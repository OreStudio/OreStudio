* Goal

SWESTR is stored as fixings in the system tenant, with its history, read from the Riksbank through the SWESTR API, =api.riksbank.se/swestr/v1=, in JSON. The adapter is specialised: it reads the Riksbank alone, and it supplies only its fetcher to the shared half of the official adapter.

* Acceptance

- The adapter reads the Riksbank alone, and it does not copy the announce, the demand, the backfill or the publish code.
- The Riksbank is a supplier in the catalogue, its series maps to the SEK overnight item that this task catalogues, and a session and a feed scoped to that item are seeded in the system tenant.
- A fresh environment stores the history as fixings within minutes of provisioning, and the log names the source and the count it stored.
- Each fixing is the decimal of the published percent, with the credit Source: Sveriges Riksbank.
- The depth of the history is measured and written into the calibration sources note.
- Unit tests parse a recorded response, and a malformed response gives an error, not a fixing.

* Plan

- =riksbank_fetcher= implements =fetcher=: adapter =riksbank_swestr=, scheme =riksbank_swestr=, field =rate=. A source's key is a series name, =SWESTR=. A read is one GET of =/swestr/v1/all/<key>= over the window, in JSON; the rates are numbers, read as their shortest text, as the New York Fed and Bank of Japan fetchers do.
- The API answers both a window with no observation and an unknown series with a 404, so the fetcher uses =read_window= with =/swestr/v1/latest/<key>=, as the Norges Bank fetcher does with its latest observation.
- The SEK item: the built-in SEK indices are SIOR and STINA, and the =SEK-SWESTR= convention, which the SEK overnight curve names, stated no fixing item. The convention now states =oresmd://ir/SEK?type=fixing&index=ibor&name=SWESTR=, and the series maps to it.
- The same gap held for EUR and JPY. Their curves name the alias conventions =EUR-ESTR= and =JPY-TONA=, which stated no item, while the stored fixings sit under the items of =EUR-ESTER= and =JPY-TONAR=. Each alias now states the item of its built-in twin, so the calibration finds the fixings through the convention a curve names. The seed keeps an item already set when it gives none.
- Option =--riksbank-url=. The seeds add the =riksbank= supplier and provider, with the conditions page and the credit "Source: Sveriges Riksbank", the =riksbank_swestr= adapter, the =riksbank-api= session, the =riksbank-swestr= feed scoped to the SWESTR item, and the system party's subscription and route.

* Result

SWESTR is stored as fixings in the system tenant, read from the Riksbank's SWESTR API by a specialised fetcher that the official adapter hosts beside the others. A fresh environment accepted the Riksbank's terms at setup and stored 274 fixings, the last 400 days, with the log line "Sent 274 fixing(s) of 'riksbank-swestr'". The values are the decimals of the published percents, such as =0.01656= for 1.656, and the provider =riksbank= carries the credit "Source: Sveriges Riksbank".

The SEK item is the one the =SEK-SWESTR= convention now states, the convention the SEK overnight curve names. The work found the same gap for EUR and JPY: their curves name =EUR-ESTR= and =JPY-TONA=, which stated no item, while the fixings were stored under the items of =EUR-ESTER= and =JPY-TONAR=. Each alias now states the item of its index, so the calibration can find the fixings of EUR, JPY and SEK through the conventions their curves name. The catalogue's pgTAP test checks each series against both conventions.

The calibration sources note records the 1,305 rates the route serves from 2021-09-01, and how the alias conventions state their items.

Unit tests parse a recorded answer and a null rate, refuse six malformed answers, check the URL of a read, an empty window of a known series, an unknown series, and names that are not series names.
