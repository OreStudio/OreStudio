* Goal

ACME's books hold equity trades: total return swaps, vanilla, barrier, digital, Asian and variance options, forwards, positions and accumulators, across the nineteen routed types. The family carries 12% of the population. Two of its codes route to composite_instrument rather than an equity table, which the planner must state rather than assume.

* Acceptance

- Every equity trade type the catalogue routes books a trade, including the two that route to composite_instrument.
- The thirteen unrouted types are routed or named out of scope. This is the largest hole of any class.
- The class share of the population matches its weight within tolerance, and every routed type appears at least once.
- The family exports as the ORE portfolio the engine reads, and reverse-maps without loss.
- The report that prices the family runs against spot prices, dividend yields and the volatility surfaces it needs, or the task states which pricing it cannot prove yet.

- *The routing.* Eighteen types reach nine tables: =equity_option_instrument=

  =equity_asian_option_instrument= (EquityAsianOption),
  =equity_barrier_option_instrument= (EquityBarrierOption,

  =equity_digital_option_instrument= (EquityTouchOption, EquityDigitalOption),
  =equity_forward_instrument= (EquityForward),
  =equity_swap_instrument= (EquitySwap, EquityWorstOfBasketSwap),
  =equity_variance_swap_instrument= (EquityVarianceSwap),
  =equity_accumulator_instrument= (EquityAccumulator, EquityTaRF) and
  =equity_position_instrument= (EquityPosition, EquityOptionPosition).
- *The writer, service side.* =write_instrument_batch= writes every one of those
  arrays.
- *The pair and index precedents.* Commodities and currency pairs are drawn from
  tenant reference data, so the shape of a catalogue and its scope load is
  settled.

** The gap this family has that the others did not

An equity instrument states =underlying_name=, documented as the ORE equity
Name identifier, and **nothing in the tree holds one**. The instrument code
catalogue is not it: =ores_refdata_instrument_codes_tbl= holds product codes
(=EQOPT=, =EQFWD=, =EQSWP=), which is what maps an ORE product to a trade type,
not what an equity trade is written on.

So the family needs reference data it does not have, and it belongs in the data
quality pipeline in the way the commodity catalogue did: an entity, an artefact
table, a publish function, a dataset and a catalogue, seeded from a source that
can be checked.

*The source is ORE's own examples.* The trade corpus names the underlyings it
prices, and they are real: =.SPX=, =.NDX=, =.STOXX50E=, =.STOXX50=, plus the
ISINs and RIC forms the same files state. A catalogue seeded from those is not
invented, and a reader can check each row against the file it came from.

** Decisions

- *D1 — the catalogue is its own task, as the commodity one was.* It carries a
  schema, a DQ pipeline and a publish, and mixing it with the planner would make
  one task that cannot be verified in two steps.
- *D2 — the payload states one idiom or two.* The nine tables are less varied
  than FX's seven were: an option shape, a forward, a swap, a variance swap, an
  accumulator and a position. =equity_terms= carries the union as =fx_terms=
  does, and the writer dispatches per table.
- *D3 — basket columns are named out of scope unless a source exists.*
  =equity_swap_instrument= carries four =*_json= columns for baskets, knockout
  levels and trigger levels. A population that filled them with invented JSON
  would be worse than one that leaves them null, so the task states which it
  does and why.

** Steps

1. *The catalogue.* Seed the equity underlyings from the ORE examples through
   the DQ pipeline, as its own task.
2. *The payload.* =equity_terms= and its variant entry, plus the underlying list
   on the scope.
3. *The planner.* =equity_trade_planner= registered under =equity=, cycling the
   eighteen routed types.
4. *The writer.* One arm per routed table.
5. *The profile.* A config for an entity whose books trade equities.
6. *The proof.* Nine export cases, one per table, then a population run
   measuring the type coverage and the share.
7. *The record.* Measurements, Result, and the gaps this task cannot close.

** Analysis of the catalogue

*What the corpus actually holds.* Restricting to trades whose type is an Equity
one gives 121 trades across 40 files, sixteen of the eighteen routed types, and
**ten distinct underlying strings**. Two of those are RIC-prefixed forms of
names that also appear bare, so the catalogue the corpus supports is about eight
underlyings:

| Name | Form | Note |
|------+------+------|
| =.SPX= | index | the one the corpus uses most, eleven trades |
| =RIC:.SPX= | index | the same index under its RIC name |
| =.STOXX50E=, =RIC:.STOXX50E=, =.STOXX50= | index | the Euro Stoxx family |
| =.NDX=, =SP5E= | index | Nasdaq 100 and an S&P variant |
| =GB0005405286= | ISIN | a single name |
| =TR20H-EUR-SEK=, =TR20H-USD-CAD= | basket constituent | only in worst-of basket swaps |

That count is *Equity-typed trades only*, and it undercounts the shares. The
corpus also prices single names in its total return swaps, which are not Equity
typed: =AMZN.O=, =CR.N= and =MSFT.O=, three exchange-listed shares. Adding them
gives about eleven instruments, of which four are shares (=GB0005405286= as an
ISIN, and those three tickers) and the rest indices. So the catalogue is not
indices only, and the question is the width of the set rather than its kind.

Two further things follow. The set is **small**: a population of eighteen types
over eleven underlyings means each is traded many times, which is realistic for
indices and less so for a book that claims breadth. And the set carries
**aliases**: =.SPX= and =RIC:.SPX= are one instrument under two names.

*What the catalogue must hold.* The instrument states =underlying_name= and the
document's =Name= is what the engine resolves, so the catalogue's key is the
string a trade states. The question the schema has to answer is whether an alias
is a row of its own or a second name on one row. The two options:

- *One row per string.* Simple, and every document value resolves. It also lets
  the same instrument enter a portfolio twice under two names, which a pricing
  run would treat as two underlyings.
- *One row per instrument, with aliases.* The key stays the string a trade
  states, and a second column names the instrument the alias resolves to. More
  schema and more seed rows, and a population cannot then double-count.

*What the pipeline needs*, taking the commodity catalogue as the template — 62
files, of which the hand-written ones are the shape to copy:

1. An entity model under =projects/ores.refdata/modeling/=, which generates the
   C++, the TypeScript, the create and drop scripts, the protocol, the
   repository, the handlers and the shell verbs.
2. A data quality artefact table, generated by the same entity flag the
   commodity catalogue used.
3. A publish function in =refdata_publish_from_dq_create.sql=, hand written.
4. A seed populate carrying the catalogue, the dataset and the artefact rows.
5. Wiring into the create, drop and populate indexes — hand maintained, and the
   three times this work has forgotten it the failure was a database recreate.

*Open decisions.*

- *Aliases.* One row per string, or one row per instrument with aliases.
- *Scope.* A commodity catalogue is tenant scoped and published per tenant. An
  equity underlying is a market fact every tenant needs, so the same choice has
  to be made deliberately rather than copied.
- *Width.* Eight underlyings from a checkable source, or a wider set from a
  source not yet identified. Inventing tickers is not an option, so a wider set
  needs a corpus or a published list to point at.

** Progress after resuming, 2026-10-27

Steps 2, 3 and 4 are done: the payload, the planner and the writer.

- =equity_terms= states the union of the nine routed tables, with the basket
  constituents and the periods as lists of their own row types.
- =equity_underlying_reference= on the scope, loaded from the tenant's
  catalogue in =trading_trade_population_writer=.
- =equity_trade_planner= cycles the seventeen routed codes and refuses an empty
  catalogue.
- The writer fills one arm per table: the option table serves three codes, the
  barrier table three, the digital table two, the accumulator two, the swap two
  and the rest one each. The worst-of swap also states its constituents and its
  periods.

Four cases prove it: seventeen types appear in a population of seventeen, every
slot names a catalogue underlying, a worst-of states two constituents and four
periods, and an empty catalogue is refused. The synthetic suite passes 1,733
assertions in 78 cases.

**What step 5 needs and does not have**

The profile config needs a book chartered to trade equities, and no ACME book
is. The seeded books are intercompany loans, FX hedges, vanilla swaps, rates
options, CDS and forwards; none is an equity book. Adding one means a new row in
=acme_uk_books_artefact_populate.sql= with its catalogue and dataset, through
the same pipeline the equity underlying catalogue took.

Until then the family is proven in code and not in the books, and the export
cases and the population run wait on it.

** The population run, and the two things it found

The run is the proof that matters, and it found two gaps in what came before.

*1. The catalogue's handlers were never registered.* =equity_underlying_registrar=
exists and =registrar.cpp= declares its function, and nothing called it, so the
service answered no request for the list:

  generate_trades: failed — no service is handling subject
  'refdata.v1.equity_underlyings.list'

=registrar.cpp= is hand maintained, which is why the aggregate drifted from the
entity it was meant to serve. With the call in place the error moves on to the
next gap, which is how a wiring fix should behave.

*2. The catalogue is seeded but never published.* The run now fails on the
planner's own refusal:

  generate_trades: failed — The tenant holds no equity underlying, so no
  equity trade can name one. Publish the equity underlying catalogue first.

The seed fills the data quality artefact table and the publish function works
when called by hand; the =publish_bundle= step of the =acme_demo= profile does
not reach it. Adding the dataset to the =base= bundle made no difference, so the
publish path is not bundle-driven for this dataset and the mechanism is still
unknown. Commodities are in the same position in this database -- their
catalogue is empty too -- so the commodity population likely never ran through
this profile either.

Until publishing is wired, a population cannot depend on a catalogue, and that
is the next thing to understand rather than to guess at.

** How a catalogue publishes, and where the run now stops

The question was answered by following the path rather than the bundle table.
A dataset publishes through three things that must all exist:

1. An *artefact type* row naming the data quality artefact table, the live
   table and the publish subject.
2. A *publish operation*: the refdata service derives the SQL function from the
   subject's last segment, so =refdata.v1.ops.publish_equity_underlyings_from_dq=
   calls =ores_refdata_publish_equity_underlyings_from_dq_fn=.
3. Membership of the bundle the profile publishes, which is =base= for the
   reference data a tenant works from.

The catalogue had the SQL function and none of the three. All three are in
place and the catalogue publishes during provisioning; the population run now
books equity trades.

Booking then found five refusals, each a value the schema does not accept:

- the accumulator's =payoff_type= and =target_type=, which are a fixed set, and
  the split between an accumulator, which states no target, and a TaRF, which
  must state one;
- the digital and touch split, where a digital states an option type and a
  strike and a touch states a barrier and neither;
- the forward's =quantity=, which the schema requires above zero and the writer
  never set;
- the swap's =return_type=, which is =Total= or =Price= and not a phrase I
  invented;
- the constituent's kind, which is the *vocabulary* -- =equity= -- and not the
  catalogue's =index= or =share= classification.

=EquityOutperformanceOption= is excluded: it routes to the option table, and
that table's check admits =EquityOption= and =EquityCliquetOption= only, so no
row of that type can satisfy it. That is a defect in the check, not in the
planner, and it is why the family books sixteen of the seventeen routed codes.

** Where it stops, and why that is not another value to patch

The run now fails inside the basket constituent's check:

  ores_trading_basket_constituent_resolves_fn(text,text) line 7

The resolver works when called by hand, and the catalogue's eleven rows belong
to the ACME tenant. The write that triggers it runs in the trading service's
session, whose tenant is not the catalogue's. So the refusal is correct and the
question is who the population writes as: either the catalogue must be visible
to that session, or the resolve must read the catalogue of the tenant the trade
belongs to rather than of the session. That is a design question about service
origin writes, and it is the next thing to settle.
