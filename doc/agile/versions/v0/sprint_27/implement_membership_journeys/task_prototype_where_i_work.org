* Goal

Answer one question with a throwaway prototype: what should the member's
*Where I work* screen look like in =ores.web=?

[[id:0B3A2F4E-F4F0-4F19-8D07-EED38EF65B61][Choose where I work]] carries
one screen. It shows who the member is signed in as, the party the session is
acting for, and the parties the account works in with the default called out,
because the member thinks of them as one question: where am I, and where can I
be. The prototype decides the layout, the order of the panels, the copy of the
unavailable and empty states, and how the screen presents the operations the
server does not have.

* Notes

The journey's gaps, from the =* What is missing= section of
[[id:0B3A2F4E-F4F0-4F19-8D07-EED38EF65B61][Choose where I work]]. The screen
states each one, and the prototype decides how.

1. *No readable party list outside sign-in.* =account_parties= carries
   =party_id= alone, and =iam.v1.ops.login= is the only read that returns a
   party name. The candidate is a party lookup the member may call, scoped to
   the parties the account works in.
2. *Nothing in the web client switches party.* =selectParty= and =switchParty=
   exist in the wire protocol and =SessionProvider= exposes =chooseParty=, but
   the only caller is the sign-in picker. There is no account menu entry and no
   *Where I work* screen.
3. *No default-party call at all in the web client.* =set_my_default_party=
   exists on the server and is mapped to no method, no BFF route and no
   control.
4. *Clearing the default is not expressible.* =set_my_default_party_request=
   carries only =party_id=, so "no default" cannot be stated. The candidate is a
   nullable body or a =clear-default-party= subject.
5. *No client-side wrapper for the association read.* The generated protocol has
   =account_parties.list_by_account_id=; =operations.ts= and the BFF do not
   speak it.

* Result

Built and accepted 2026-10-09. Variant A.

*Accepted by the product owner on 2026-10-09: variant A, one place each.*

The recommendation was A. What it settles: the two things a member does to a
party — act for it, or make it the default — sit on the card they change, so
nothing depends on a selection made elsewhere. B's action bar and C's repeated
acting party are not carried, and the design accepts that A is the tallest of
the three when a member works in many parties.

- Source :: =doc/prototypes/membership/where-i-work/index.html=
- Accepted commit :: =15a9f9f5dc=, the commit the accepted page was signed off at
- Variants :: =?variant=a|b|c=, switched from the bar at the foot of the page
- Cases :: =?case=three|one=

*The three variants.*

- *A · One place each.* A card per party, each carrying *Switch to this party*
  and *Set as default*. Accepted.
- *B · One list, one action bar.* A table of parties and a bar of actions under
  it, acting on the selected row.
- *C · Two columns.* "Where you are" beside "Where you can be".

*Decisions the prototype settles.*

- *The acting party and the default party are two states, and every party shows
  both.* The acting party decides what the screens read now; the default decides
  what quick sign-in picks next time. They are easy to run together, so the
  screen draws them apart rather than explaining the difference.
- *"Clear default" is drawn and unavailable.* The request cannot state "none",
  so the build draws no control; the page keeps it to show what is missing.
- *The one-party case says there is nothing to switch.* It is the journey's
  empty state, and it states the rule it obeys: an account with no party is
  refused at sign-in, so the list is never empty.

*Document claims the tree contradicts.*

- The journey document says "nothing in the web client switches party".
  =POST /api/session/switch-party=, =api/client.ts= and
  =SessionProvider.switchParty= are wired end to end, and
  =NewPartyJourney= is their only caller. What is missing is the member's
  screen and the account-menu entry point.
- The journey document names =TopBar.tsx= as where party switching is left
  with the account. No =TopBar.tsx= is in the tree. The corrections belong to
  the extraction pass, which owns the document.
