#+title: The identity an adapter runs as
#+description: Why an adapter runs as a system service with no tenant and no party, how the service identifies it, and how a restart restores its work.
#+type: knowledge
#+level: cross
#+filetags: :marketdata:adapters:identity:iam:
#+created: 2026-10-19
#+updated: 2026-10-22

* Summary

An adapter runs as a system service. It has a service identity, and that
identity holds no tenant and no party. The market data service authenticates
the adapter by its service token, and the adapter learns nothing about the
parties it serves. A person never starts an adapter on behalf of a party.

* Detail

** Three questions, three owners

| Question | Owner | When it is asked |
|----------+-------+------------------|
| May this process be an adapter? | IAM, from the service identity | At announce |
| Which ids must it publish? | The market data service | At each demand request |
| Which party gets a value? | The market data service | At ingest, from the routes |

The adapter answers none of the last two. This is what keeps a tick free of
tenant and party.

** What a restart restores

The state of an adapter is its connection and its demand set. Neither is stored
in the adapter. After a restart the adapter announces again. The service then
sends the current demand set, and the adapter serves it. A restart needs no
person and no stored consent.

** The simulator

The simulator reads vintage prices from the market data service to anchor its
generators. It reads them as the system tenant, with a service identity. No
per-collection grant exists. Authoring a generator is a separate act of a person
with the permission of the system tenant. See [[id:3EF67CE4-6AF2-45FC-AA9B-D543550EF8C2][Generators belong to the system tenant and the system party]].

** Why no run grant

A run grant records that a person agreed that something acts in a tenant and
party. An adapter acts in none, so there is nothing to agree.
