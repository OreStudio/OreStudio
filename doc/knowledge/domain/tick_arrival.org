#+title: A tick clock draws its gaps from an arrival process
#+description: How a synthetic feed decides when its next tick comes — the tick clock takes its gaps from an arrival process that is periodic, Poisson or Hawkes — and how the elapsed time reaches the price, so that bursts change when prices arrive and not the law of the price.
#+type: knowledge
#+level: cross
#+filetags: :synthetic:producers:arrival:point_process:design:
#+created: 2026-10-23
#+updated: 2026-10-24

* Summary

A synthetic feed has two separate jobs. One job decides /what/ the next price
is. The other decides /when/ it arrives. The
[[id:E1C64302-FA31-4C25-8E92-38265013F644][generator library]] holds the first
job: a value process whose =next()= returns a price. The *tick clock* holds the
second. Both live in =ores.analytics.quant=.

1. *The tick clock takes its gaps from an arrival process.* The arrival process
   is periodic, Poisson or Hawkes. A periodic one gives equal gaps, so a feed
   can be fully regular. The other two give random and bursty gaps, as live
   ticks have.
2. *The value process is told how much time has passed.* A bursty clock then
   changes when prices are seen, and does not change the law of the price in
   real time.

* Detail

** The tick clock

The tick clock is the one clock every feed runs on. It takes an arrival
process. Before each tick it asks the process for the next gap in seconds, and
sleeps for the gap in slices of a hundred milliseconds, so that =stop()= is seen
quickly. It then runs one tick body,
counts the tick and passes it to a hook. A throw from the body goes to a failure
hook and the tick is skipped, because a tick-loop thread has no caller to
receive an exception. The clock is described from the feed's side in
[[id:46FF8EF4-3874-4C9A-9222-69DA691E793F][the FX spot producer]].

The clock holds no database, messaging or logging code. It needs only
=<chrono>=, =<thread>=, =<atomic>= and =<functional>=, so it keeps the rule of
=ores.analytics.quant=: =service/tick_clock.hpp=, beside the processes it is
driven by. The synthetic side holds only the feeds. A feed reads its mode and
numbers from the generation config, asks the factory for an arrival process,
builds a clock around it and gives the clock a tick body. Any
other consumer, such as a calibration tool or =ores.marketdata=, can pace itself
the same way without linking the synthetic service.

The note
[[id:BB2B7C4C-FD4E-4DCA-BF1D-00F929622B87][Synthetic market data generation: approach]]
lists the modes a user may choose and the control panel that sets them. This
page says where the choice lives in the code.

** The arrival processes

=i_arrival_process= is the interface, beside =i_stochastic_process=. It has one
method, =next_gap()=, that returns the time in seconds from the last tick to the
next. A process owns its state and its random engine and is not thread-safe,
like every value process.

- =periodic_arrival_process= returns the same gap every time. It is the default.
  A feed that wants no randomness uses it.
- =poisson_arrival_process= returns an exponential gap
  ([[id:A296C7C0-780D-46DD-A8B4-8B7998C17B8C][Poisson Process]]).
- =hawkes_arrival_process= returns a gap that depends on the earlier ones
  ([[id:D72B9899-C7EC-4F27-BE17-89AE6FC18A7A][Hawkes Process]]).

=process_factory= has three entries: =make_process= for the value processes,
=make_yield_curve_process= for the short-rate processes, and
=make_arrival_process= for the arrival processes. The second and the third throw
on a name they do not know. A silent fallback to a periodic timer would hide a
mistyped mode as a feed that quietly stays regular.

*** Why "periodic"

The obvious word, "steady", is taken. =std::chrono::steady_clock= and the asio
=steady_timer= mean a clock that never goes backwards, which says nothing about
how far apart the ticks are. "Fixed" is vague about what is fixed. A point
process with equal gaps is called *periodic* in the literature, and a reader of
the code meets the same word. The "Fixed interval" label in the approach note
is the periodic arrival process.

** The parameters

A feed names its mode and one set of numbers.

| Mode | Parameters | Meaning |
|------+------------+---------|
| Periodic | ticks per hour | Equal gaps. |
| Poisson | ticks per hour | The mean rate. Gaps are exponential. |
| Hawkes | ticks per hour, branching ratio, decay | The long-run mean rate, how clustered the stream is, and how fast a burst fades. |

The Hawkes row uses the parametrisation in
[[id:D72B9899-C7EC-4F27-BE17-89AE6FC18A7A][Hawkes Process]]: the mean rate
$\bar\lambda$, the branching ratio $n$ and the decay $\beta$. The factory turns
them into the baseline $\mu = \bar\lambda(1-n)$ and the jump $\alpha = n\beta$.
=ticks_per_hour= keeps its meaning in every mode. A person who switches from
Periodic to Hawkes keeps the same load on the pipeline, and changes only how
that load is spread in time. The approach note's $(\mu, \alpha, \beta)$ is the
same process in other coordinates.

The unit of the arrival process is the second, and =ticks_per_hour= is the
unit of the config. The factory converts once. The second is the unit because
the clock sleeps in wall time. The process has no notion of a unit, and a
template parameter would add noise for no gain.

** How the elapsed time reaches the price

A gap can be half a tick or three ticks, so a value process cannot assume a
step of one fixed length. The step takes the elapsed time:

- =i_stochastic_process= has =next(double elapsed)=. =elapsed= is the time since
  the last step, counted in *nominal ticks*, where one nominal tick is the tick
  length the process is configured for: the length of one period at the feed's
  =ticks_per_hour=. It has no unit and it needs no calendar. =next()= means
  =next(1.0)=.
- The clock turns a gap in seconds into nominal ticks by dividing by the
  nominal period, and passes the result to the tick body. A periodic clock
  passes exactly one. A Poisson or Hawkes clock at the same =ticks_per_hour=
  passes numbers whose long-run mean is one.

A process applies =elapsed= by the rule that is exact for its law:

| Process | Step for elapsed $s$ |
|---------+----------------------|
| Geometric mixture | The component's log-return is $N(m_k s,\ \sigma_k^2 s)$. Each component is a Brownian motion seen for $s$ ticks. |
| Arithmetic mixture | The same, on the increment. |
| Ornstein-Uhlenbeck | The exact transition with $\Delta t = s \cdot dt$. The formula holds for any step. |

The mixture rule is a modelling choice. Each regime is a Brownian motion, so
means scale with $s$ and variances scale with $s$, and the weights stay as set.
It is exact if the regime holds for the whole step. At $s = 1$ each rule is one
nominal tick, so a periodic feed with a given seed always steps the same
sequence.

*** What the rule gives

The price process, in real time, does not depend on the tick times. A feed that
ticks twice as often sees half-size steps, and the sum of those steps over an
hour has the same law. Volatility per unit of real time is constant, so a burst
of ticks shows many small moves and a lull shows few large ones. A Hawkes clock
changes when the price is observed and leaves the price alone. This is also the
limit of the model. In a live market bursts and large moves come together, and
here they are independent. Linking the two is a different model.

*** Processes that do not take it

The short-rate processes of the yield curve path precompute their step from
=dt=. A lattice such as Black-Derman-Toy has steps fixed by its construction,
and the matrix processes (Heath-Jarrow-Morton, Libor market, the affine and
quadratic Gaussian models) precompute a covariance for one step. Only the three
engines above take =next(elapsed)=. The others throw when called with an elapsed
other than one, and the validation refuses a non-periodic mode for a process
type that does not support it. The IR curve feed is periodic, and its
=discount_factor(ticks_ahead)= counts nominal ticks.

** What validation guards

=validate_process_parameters= is the one rule set that the service and the Qt
client share. It holds the arrival rules:

- the rate is positive and finite;
- the decay is positive and finite;
- the branching ratio is at least zero and strictly below one;
- a non-periodic mode is allowed only for a process type that takes a variable
  step.

A branching ratio of one or more gives a stream that does not settle. A ratio
very close to one gives gaps so short that the pipeline cannot keep up. The
Hawkes process also stops after a fixed number of events in one request and
reports that it did so, which guards the tick loop against a parameter set that
the rules let through.

** What the pipeline sees

Nothing changes after the feed. A tick carries its time, the marketdata ingest
loop plans and stores it, and the rule in
[[id:F64DB34E-084D-4437-B536-392AA26D8B77][No code path may behave differently because a source is simulated]]
holds: the arrival process is a property of the generator and no later stage
reads it.
