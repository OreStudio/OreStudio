#+title: Trade Population Weights
#+description: A weighted distribution across the ORE product set, for generating realistic synthetic trade populations.
#+type: knowledge
#+level: cross
#+filetags: :domain:finance:synthetic:population:
#+created: 2026-09-08
#+updated: 2026-09-08

A synthetic trade population is realistic when its mix of products
resembles a real book. This note records a weighted distribution across
the ORE product set, so a generator can size each product rather than
sample uniformly.

* Summary

ORE's product catalogue defines 88 products, of which 86 are distinct
instruments. A population that samples them uniformly is not a book: it
holds as many rainbow options as vanilla swaps, which no institution's
book resembles.

The distribution below assigns a weight to every one of the 86, grouped
by asset class. Interest rate products carry 42% of the trades, foreign
exchange 25%, equity 12%, credit 6%, commodity 5%, bonds 4%, and
inflation and scripted exotics 3% each.

Two rules shape it. The weights follow trade count rather than notional,
because a population is sized in trades. Every product carries a
non-zero weight, so a generated population exercises every pricing path
at least once.

The weights are a modelling assumption, not a measurement. They are
stated so they can be argued with and replaced.

* Detail

** Why notional cannot set these weights

The public sources report notional outstanding by asset class, and
notional is the wrong measure here.

A single interest rate swap carries a notional that hundreds of FX
forwards do not reach. Weighting by notional would therefore give a
population far too few FX trades and far too many interest rate trades,
because it measures the size of exposures rather than the number of
contracts. The Bank for International Settlements puts interest rate
derivatives at 79% of notional outstanding; that figure is correct and
it does not answer the question a population asks.

The weights below are trade counts. They are informed by the notional
shares, by which products a dealing institution transacts most often,
and by judgement. No public source publishes a trade-count distribution
at this granularity.

** Why no product carries zero

A weight of zero removes a product from the population, and with it the
only exercise its pricing path gets.

The smallest weights below are therefore floors rather than estimates. A
weight of 0.01% is a statement that the product is rare and must still
appear. A generator honouring these weights should round up to at least
one instance of every product, whatever the population size, so that a
tiny preset still covers the catalogue.

This is the one respect in which the distribution is deliberately not
realistic. A real book of a thousand trades holds no ascots at all.

* Provenance and status

The product list is derived from ORE's own catalogue, the =tradedata=
inputs of =Docs/UserGuide/products.tex=, which is the definition of what
ORE prices. Every row links to this corpus's page for that product.

The weights are this project's assumption. They were informed by an
analysis of a dealer's product mix, adjusted in two respects: entries
that are not ORE trade types were removed, and the remainder were
re-based onto the catalogue's actual contents. Several products named in
that analysis, among them Bermudan swaptions, cross-currency swaps and
inflation swaps, are not separate ORE trade types. They are configurations
of =Swap= and =Swaption=, or leg types within them, so they carry no
weight of their own here.

The distribution should be replaced by measurement or by a
desk's stated mix when either is available. Until then it is a starting
point that is explicit about being one.
