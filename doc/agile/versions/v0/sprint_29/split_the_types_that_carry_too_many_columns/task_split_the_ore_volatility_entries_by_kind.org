* Goal

Each of the five volatility entries holds one kind of entry, and the block that only exists for one kind is a type of its own. Cap floor and swaption share one proxy part, so the same split is made once and used twice.

* Acceptance

- Every one of the five types ends with at most one block per entry, and no column is meaningful only for a value another column selects.
- The proxy block of each entry is one part of its own, and no part carries a column another entry's kind cannot use.
- The ORE export and import of a volatility entry writes one typed array per part, and the bytes the reader sees stay what the ORE file states.
- No compiler flag, unity exclusion or field-group reshuffle is added.
- The split is argued by the invariant each part holds, not by the entry's member count, and the argument is in the task's Result.

* What the split is, measured

The cap and floor entry's =ProxyConfig= was seven nullable columns. It is now a
row of =cap_floor_proxy_config=, and the presence of the row is the flag the
flat shape spelled =has_proxy_config=.

The task was accepted as "the proxy block is one part type that cap floor and
swaption both carry". Reading the two blocks in the ORE schema shows that is
false, and the acceptance is corrected:

| Entry | ProxyConfig source | ProxyConfig target |
|-------+--------------------+--------------------|
| cap floor | =CurveId=, =Index=, =RateComputationPeriod= | =Index=, =RateComputationPeriod=, =ONCapSettlementDays= |
| swaption | =CurveId=, =ShortSwapIndexBase=, =SwapIndexBase= | =ShortSwapIndexBase=, =SwapIndexBase= |

The two share a shape, not a type. One part carrying both blocks would be a
union with nullable columns again, which is the shape this story exists to
remove. Each entry gets its own part.

* What a split costs beyond the model

Recorded because the next four entries pay the same price:

- the document model gains a vector, so the protocol and its TypeScript twin
  change;
- the document service writes, reads and removes the part beside its entry,
  and the read predicate is over the entries' ids because the part is keyed by
  its entry and not by the curve definition;
- the ORE projection builds the part on import and looks it up by the entry's
  id on export;
- a new entity generates its whole family -- repository, service, protocol,
  history provider, shell recipe, ER diagram -- and three of those are
  hand-wired: the two =component_files.cmake= lists, and the create and drop
  aggregators.

* What the ORE schema says, and where we improve on it

Read from =external/ore/xsd/curveconfig.xsd= before moving another column. It is
the first evidence for what a document holds, and it decides one of the three
remaining entries outright and improves the model for the other two. Where its
answer is a limit of the XSD rather than a statement about the data, the task
says so and models the condition instead.

** =curve_volatility_config=: one entry, several kinds, each with a priority

A volatility entry writes either one bare kind element, or a =VolatilityConfig=
element holding up to one of each of seven kinds in an =xs:all=. Each kind is its
own complex type with its own fields and a =priority= attribute:

| Kind | Its own fields |
|------+----------------|
| =Constant= | =Quote= |
| =Curve= | =Quotes=, =Interpolation=, =Extrapolation=, =EnforceMontoneVariance= |
| =StrikeSurface= | =Strikes=, =Expiries=, the interpolation block, =Extrapolation=, =TimeExtrapolation=, =TimeExtrapolationVariance=, =StrikeExtrapolation=, =ParametricSmileConfiguration= |
| =DeltaSurface= | =DeltaType=, =AtmType=, =AtmDeltaType=, =PutDeltas=, =CallDeltas=, =Expiries=, the interpolation block, =FuturePriceCorrection= |
| =MoneynessSurface= | =MoneynessType=, =MoneynessLevels=, otherwise the delta surface's shape |
| =ApoFutureSurface= | not modelled yet |
| =ProxySurface= | =ProxyVolatilityCurve=, =FXVolatilityCurve=, =CorrelationCurve=, =CDSVolatilityCurve= |

Every one of the seven repeats the same four fields -- =QuoteType=,
=VolatilityType=, =ExerciseType= and =Calendar= -- because the XSD has no way to
factor them out. That repetition is a limit of the schema, not a fact about the
data, so the model factors it:

- =curve_volatility_config= is the entry: =id=, =party_id=,
  =curve_definition_id=, =position=, =is_wrapped=;
- =curve_volatility_kind= is the kind: the entry's key, =kind_code=, =priority=
  and one =volatility_quote_terms= field group carrying the four fields every
  kind shares. This is the central row of the atomised shape, and it is where
  "the same entry holds several alternatives in priority order" becomes explicit
  instead of implied by an =xs:all=;
- one part per kind -- =curve_volatility_strike_surface=,
  =curve_volatility_delta_surface=, =curve_volatility_curve=,
  =curve_volatility_constant=, =curve_volatility_proxy_surface=,
  =curve_volatility_moneyness_surface= -- each keyed by the kind, carrying the
  kind's own fields and nothing else.

That is one level more than the schema states, and it is justified by what the
schema cannot say: an =xs:all= of seven optional kinds is a list of alternatives
with an order, which is a row and its parts, not a row with 30 nullable columns.
The two kinds not modelled yet -- =ApoFutureSurface= and =MoneynessSurface= for
the surfaces the projection refuses -- arrive as parts when they are modelled,
without touching the entry.

** =fx_volatility_config=: the schema cannot state the smile's condition, so we do

=fxVolatility= is one =xs:all=: a mandatory =Dimension=, then optional
=SmileType=, =SmileInterpolation=, =Deltas=, =SmileDelta=, =SmileExtrapolation=,
=ButterflyErrorTolerance=, =TimeInterpolation= and =TimeWeighting=. There is no
element per dimension, so the schema says "these fields are optional", and only a
reader knows that the smile ones are meaningless in one dimension.

That is the deficiency worth improving on: the smile block exists for a two
dimensional surface and not for a one dimensional one. The model states it:

- =fx_volatility_config= keeps the entry: the curves, the spot, =dimension=,
  =deltas=, =expiries=, the conventions and the calendar;
- =fx_volatility_smile= is a part holding =smile_type=, =smile_interpolation=,
  =smile_delta=, =smile_extrapolation=, =butterfly_error_tolerance=,
  =time_interpolation= and =time_weighting=, and a row exists only for a two
  dimensional entry.

=dimension= stays on the entry because it does not only gate the smile: it
decides how =deltas= is read. That is a value of the component, and the smile's
presence is the conditional.

** =inflation_cap_floor_volatility_config=: the strike axis is a group, and =Type= needs reading

=inflationCapFloorVolatility= is one =xs:all= with a mandatory =Type=,
=QuoteType=, =Tenors=, =Calendar=, =DayCounter=, =BusinessDayConvention=,
=Index=, =IndexCurve=, =ObservationLag= and =YieldTermStructure=, and with
=CapStrikes=, =FloorStrikes= and =Strikes= as three optional lists. The three are
alternatives -- a cap quote is struck on caps, a floor quote on floors, and a
surface on both -- so they are one =strike_axis= field group carrying the mode and
the lists, not three columns of a union.

Whether =Type= is a discriminator is not settled here: the schema makes it
mandatory and the ORE conventions behind each inflation type differ, so the
reading of =Type='s conventions is what decides between =keep= and a split into
parts. That reading is the next step, not a guess.

* Plan

** The rules a split obeys

The data-oriented model decides the shape, not the column count. Its rules, and
the reasons behind them, are in
[[id:BBD43BF2-9FEE-42B0-A667-9468AF759DC2][Data-Oriented Design in ORE Studio]] and
[[id:56AD6F46-99E0-482E-B7B5-C9FDEC8A7641][The data classification checklist]]; this task applies them and
does not restate them. In short:

- a type holds one component: fields that share one invariant, hold at one
  instant, and are keyed by the same key;
- a part is joined by key and never nested, so the wire carries one typed array
  per component and no part's fields appear inside another's object;
- a field read on a different cadence, or by a different screen, job or service,
  goes with its readers and not with the row it happens to sit in;
- the classification comes first. A compile error never reclassifies data: if a
  shape has to be denormalised for a compiler, the classification stays and the
  denormalisation is recorded;
- the centre keeps the identity and whatever the parts must agree on. A part one
  reader needs does not stay on the centre.

** This grouping

Five refdata volatility entries, and their shapes come from
[[id:E6C92BB1-8AB4-4580-A318-A5EB2A10BBB1][the inventory]]:

| Type | Members | Columns | The block that should not be a column |
|------+---------+---------+---------------------------------------|
| =cap_floor_volatility_config= | 39 | 39 | =has_proxy_config= and seven proxy columns |
| =curve_volatility_config= | 32 | 32 | =kind= and one curve column per surface |
| =swaption_volatility_config= | 27 | 27 | =has_proxy_config= and five proxy columns |
| =fx_volatility_config= | 22 | 22 | =dimension= and the smile columns it selects |
| =inflation_cap_floor_volatility_config= | 22 | 22 | =quote_type= and one column per quote structure |

The proxy block is the same component in the cap floor and swaption entries: it
names a source curve, an index, a rate computation period, a target and a
scaling factor, and it is present or absent as a unit. It is one part type, not
two copies, and its own key is the entry's key.

The kind columns are the entry's kind stated as a column. =kind=,
=dimension= and =quote_type= select a construction, and a column that exists for
one construction is that construction's part.

** What moves in one change

Per entry: the model, every generated address, the ORE projection and the
fixtures, and the screens that read the volatility section.

** What proves it

- No row carries a column its own kind cannot use.
- The proxy part is one type, and both entries use it.
- A round trip of the ORE fixture states the same values, written as arrays per
  part rather than one row of nulls.

* Result

Three of the five entries are modelled and the third one's shape is in place.

Cap and floor and swaption are done: their proxy blocks are parts, the entries
lost the columns, and the document, service and projection carry the parts.

=curve_volatility_config= is modelled but not yet wired. Two entities exist:

| Entity | Members | What it is |
|--------+---------+------------|
| =curve_volatility_kind= | 9 | the entry's key, =kind_code=, =priority= and the four terms every kind states |
| =curve_volatility_strike_surface= | 11 | the strike and expiry grid of one =StrikeSurface= kind |

The entry itself still carries every kind's columns -- 32 members, unchanged --
because nothing reads the new entities yet. Wiring the kind through the document
and the ORE projection is what lets each kind's columns leave the entry, one kind
at a time, and that is the next step rather than a part of this one: a half-wired
kind would give the same fact two homes.
