This page documents a [[id:699A8D45-B67E-4D3E-9206-24FA17C51ADA][story]] in [[id:C863DD3D-740F-4234-B5AB-BD9F3950E09D][Sprint 31]]. It captures the goal, current status, acceptance criteria, and the tasks that compose it.

* Goal

ORE already needs one secret it cannot do without: the client secret that a provider's token exchange presents. Today it lives nowhere, because the rule was that a secret is named and never stored, and naming it was the only way to keep it out of the tree.

This story gives the secrets ORE must use a home. A secret is written once, encrypted with AES-256-GCM under a key that lives outside the database, the repository and the environment file. Only the service that owns the secret reads it back, in memory, for a caller the bus already trusts. A rotation replaces the key without re-entering anyone's secret, as one audited act.

The story settles what that home is before it builds one: a small service of ORE's own, inspired on a vault and not a copy of one, or a capability inside IAM, which already holds the rows the secrets belong to. A spike proves the cipher with one test before either choice is built on.

Then the store is modelled, served, wired into the provider registry and proven by the gates, so that the first secret ORE needs never reaches a file again.

* Acceptance

- A secret is written once, encrypted, and read only by the service that keeps it: no wire surface carries a ciphertext, and no caller sees another tenant's plaintext.
- The key that protects the secrets is never in the database, the repository or an environment file, and its home is recorded.
- A ciphertext is bound to the row it belongs to, so a copy moved to another name, tenant or version does not open.
- A rotation rewrites every secret under a new key as one audited act, and leaves each plaintext unchanged.
- Every use of a secret is recorded against the caller that asked for it.
- The provider registry takes its client secret from this store, so no provider secret is in a file.
- The cipher is proven by a test before anything is built on it, and the gates cover the new code.

* Decisions

- *The decision to store a secret belongs to this story* (2026-11-26, product
  owner). The rule that a secret is named and never stored was replaced, while
  the identity story was in flight, by the rule that a secret is encrypted in
  the database under a key that lives outside it; see [[id:0D2294A4-873A-47FE-908A-60BC21F7AAA9][Sign a person up and in
  through an external identity provider]]. That rule needs a store, a place to
  put the key and an audit of every use, which is a capability of its own and
  not a step in a sign-in. It is therefore built here, and the identity story
  asks this store for its provider secret rather than growing one.

* Out of scope

- A secret ORE only proves and never recovers. A password hash is one-way by
  design and stays in IAM.
- The certificates a service presents to NATS, and the keys ORE signs its own
  tokens with, until one of them must be rotated without a restart.
- A general-purpose secret manager for the fleet: no dynamic credentials, no
  leases, no PKI engine, and no storage engine of its own.
- The test double's and the seeder's fixtures. They move here only once the
  store exists and the gate can run without a file.
