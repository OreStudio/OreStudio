* Goal

A person who designs a service interaction can say "use pattern X" and point at
one page. That page names the established pattern, cites its authoritative
sources, and states its forces, its failure handling, its pitfalls and its use in
ORE Studio. The patterns form one pattern language with one hub, so a design
inherits the known failure modes instead of finding them again.

The design for scheduled reporting is the trigger. It needed a grant, a token
exchange and a token cache, and none of them is specific to reporting: any work
that starts from a schedule and acts as a tenant and a party needs them. Today
the platform's pattern knowledge is spread across the IAM pages, two plans, the
workflow engine pages and that design, and no page names an established
pattern.

* Acceptance

- A =pattern= document type exists, with a contract, a template, a glossary
  entry and a =compass add pattern= scaffold. Every pattern page states its
  established name and its sources.
- A pattern hub orders the patterns by group, and the knowledge index links it.
- The identity patterns exist: access token, client credentials, token relay,
  token exchange, grant management, and the composite for scheduled work that
  acts as a tenant.
- The IAM pages name these patterns and do not restate them. Nothing they hold
  is lost.
- The reporting design links the patterns and keeps only what is specific to
  reporting.

* Decisions

- *One hub for the whole pattern language, not a catalogue under security.*
  About half the patterns are about communication and resilience, and the
  identity patterns depend on them, for example token exchange on timeout and
  retry. One language keeps one set of names. The Identity and Access hub links
  the identity group of the pattern hub.
- *They are service architecture patterns.* The type, the folder and the hub are
  named =service_architecture_pattern=, not a bare "pattern". Every page is about
  how services work together across a process boundary. "Architectural pattern"
  alone also means a system's overall style in Buschmann's sense, and the codegen
  already calls its local SQL, HTTP and shell conventions "patterns". The type
  was first added as =pattern= and renamed before the branch was pushed.
- *The established name comes first.* A page is named for the pattern in its
  authoritative source. Local names are aliases.
- *A pattern page owns the pattern; an architecture page owns a use of it.*
  The reporting design states the role, the audience and the moments it grants
  and revokes. The pattern page states the mechanism and its failure handling.

* Out of scope

- Building the run grant, the token exchange and the token cache. This story
  writes the patterns; the reporting story builds them.
- Patterns the platform does not use. The hub lists a pattern only when ORE
  Studio uses it or a design has chosen it.
