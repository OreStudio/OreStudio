This page is a [[id:671F18E4-E09C-4B3B-BD24-D33DF8AE38A6][capture]] in the *inbox* bucket of the product backlog — a pre-sprint idea, not yet pulled into a sprint as a story.

* What

Four parts of the signed-off queue are not on the screen, and the reason for
each is the same: the read behind the screen does not carry the fact. The
screen states the deal, its type, its book, its counterparty, its trade date,
its status and the version it is at, and it decides the request. It cannot yet
state:

- *The expiry a deal is pinned by.* The design pins a deal that is not
  exercised and is close to expiry at the top of the queue, in red, with a
  banner. Nothing in the trade summary states an expiry date: the option
  instruments carry one, per family, and the queue would have to join the
  family of the type before it can read it.
- *The operational error flag.* An amendment carries a reason code, a
  department and a flag that separates a deal booked wrongly from one that
  moved for a business reason. The authorisation request holds the trade, the
  version it was raised against and its audit columns; the flag and the
  department are not modelled.
- *The fields an amendment changed, with the previous values.* A trade's
  request is a fact about a version, not a field-level proposal, so there is
  nothing to diff: the changes read answers role grants, engine settings and
  book changes, and no kind for trades. The trade's own history holds the
  versions, and the request names one of them.
- *The legs of a package.* A structure authorises as a whole and its legs
  belong inside the open deal. The queue read joins one trade per request and
  does not read the structure it belongs to.
- *The person and the time the row last moved.* The design puts "updated 2 min
  ago by …" on the row. The trade summary states neither, and the request's
  audit columns state who raised it rather than who last touched the deal.

* Why

Each is a gap between a screen drawn and signed off and the store under it. None
is a defect in the screen: the screen states what it can and says so where the
design promised more. The point of recording them together is that they are one
question — what a queue row must state about the deal it names — and answering
them one at a time would leave four separate joins into the trading component,
each reading a different table for the same row.

* Shape of the work

Not designed here. The pieces that appear necessary:

- One read of the queue's deals that states the facts a row needs: the expiry
  where the type has one, the structure the deal belongs to, and when and by
  whom it last moved. It is one join into trading, widened, rather than five.
- A decision about where the amendment's flag, reason code and department live
  once the request stops being a bare pointer at a version.
- The changed values, which need either a field-level proposal for a trade or a
  read of the version's difference, and the trade history already holds the
  versions to compare.

* What done looks like

A deal close to expiry is at the top of the queue with its banner, an amendment
says which fields moved and what they were, a package lists its legs, the flag
is visible where the design puts it, and the row says who last touched the deal
and when.
