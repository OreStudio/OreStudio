* Acceptance

- The feeds screen shows the market state in force: calm, active or forced.
- A user sets the regime there: auto, pinned calm or pinned active. The
  transition rate and the shared intensity's jump and decay need verbs the
  service does not have yet, so they are the
  [[id:4A5C7202-6D31-43BE-8E06-42BE74FD1A5C][chain and heat task]].
- The same screen edits the per-feed sensitivity, on the feed's Arrivals tab,
  which reads the market and cannot change it.
- A manual switch forces the state at runtime, from the screen and from a shell
  command, and takes effect on the next tick without a restart.
- The switch reports what it forced, so a test can assert it.
- Forcing is reversible: returning to auto hands control back to the state
  machine.

* Plan

1. Read the screen's boundary first: the factor belongs to the run, so the
   control is a tab of the synthetic screen and not a field of a feed. A feed's
   Arrivals tab reads it and shows it, and changes nothing.
2. Give the browser one route for the state and one switch, both over the two
   subjects the service already handles, and one route for the raster.
3. Draw the raster from the ticks the browser can already see: the BFF owns a
   per-source tick buffer, so the read is the running feeds the service names,
   each with the times of its ticks in the window.
4. Prove it where a person would: a harness test opens the tab, forces the
   active state, and hands it back.

* Notes

- The tab is =?tab=regime= on the synthetic screen, beside Dashboard and
  Provider.
- The raster draws every feed the service lists, the ones that run first and the
  rest behind a line the reader opens, and stops at 120 rows. A feed's own label
  is the axis, the dots take their colour from the asset class, and the count at
  the end of the row says how many arrivals the window held: times, not prices,
  because what a shared factor changes is when ticks arrive.
- Above the rows sit the two halves of the factor: the shared heat the service
  sampled, which is the internal state jump and decay act on, and the rate each
  asset class ticked at, in ticks a second per feed. The state is the band the
  arrivals sit on, a line marks the moment a switch landed, the legend names all
  of them, and the numbers that move the heat are stated beside it.
- The heat is served by the service, not inferred by the browser: the owner of
  the heat takes a reading every 100ms as it fades it and keeps the last 2048,
  so the window is the service's own record rather than what one tab happened to
  see. The jump and the decay travel with it.
- The state band begins when the tab opens, because the service reports the state
  in force and not its history; the rate curve fills as the BFF's buffers fill.
- The tree order the prototype asked for is not available: the service lists
  sources, not tree paths. The feeds that run come first and the label order
  holds inside each group.
- A row is empty for three different reasons: a feed too slow to tick inside the
  window, a curve feed that serves an adapter and published no display tick,
  which was a defect and is fixed on this branch, and a tab that has just
  opened, whose rows fill as each feed ticks. The third is stated on the screen,
  and the second is now the expected-rate column's job: a row that asks for
  0.02 a second says so.
- The state names and the multiplier are the screen's only claim about the
  market; who pinned it and why is in the change record, not in this reply.
- =compass smoke ticks= fails on this environment with 20 of 86 demanded ids
  rejected. It is not this task's doing, and it is captured: the demo profile
  asks for series no feed in this build makes.

* Result

The backend is in: one verb reads the regime (state, multiplier, pinned), one pins
calm, active or back to automatic. A read needs a session so it is attributable; a
pin needs what starting a feed needs, because it changes what every feed publishes.
Both sit on the synthetic service's own subjects, so nothing touched the scheduler
service or the market data tree.

The clock is also proven at the feed level: two feeds register with one scheduler,
twenty steps are taken, and their own counts add up to the steps.

The screen is in and proven in a browser.

The Market regime tab reads the factor and forces it: the state, the multiplier,
whether a person or the chain holds it, and three switches. Beside them the
raster gives every feed a row, read from the ticks the browser already tails, the
feeds that run first and the rest behind a line, each row a label, its class's
colour on every arrival, and the count the window held. Above the rows sits the
multiplier the tab saw, with a line where a switch landed, so a reader sees one
market rather than one feed's copy of it.

The BFF holds the two routes and the raster, and a web test proves each. The
harness opens the tab as the tenant administrator, presses Active, sees the
multiplier and the pinned wording, presses Auto, and sees the chain take it back.

What the switch does not set is the three numbers the story also asks for, the
chain's two transition rates and the shared heat's jump and decay: the service
has no verb to set them, so they are the chain and heat task rather than a
control this screen pretends to have.
