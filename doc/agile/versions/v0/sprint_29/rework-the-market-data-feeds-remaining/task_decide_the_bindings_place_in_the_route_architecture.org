* Result

The binding retires. The route is the one record that says which source serves
a party for a provider, and so which party consumes which source. The binding
is not kept as a projection of the routes: a projection keeps two records for
one fact and needs a writer to hold them in step, where the route already
answers the question.

** The evidence

- The ingest reads two consumer paths. =refresh()= reads the feed bindings,
  then reads the routes and turns each route into a binding-shaped consumer, so
  both arrive in one snapshot: =projects/ores.marketdata/service/src/app/feed_ingest_loop.cpp=,
  =consumer_of= at line 163, the two reads at lines 179 and 194, the merge at
  line 201.
- Nothing derives one path from the other. Only =feed_binding_service= writes
  a binding, and neither =subscription_service= nor =route_service= writes one.
- No seed and no provisioning step inserts a binding. A fresh database has an
  empty binding table, and the routes supply every consumer.
- The architecture pages describe routes and subscriptions only. No page of
  the market data cluster names a feed binding.
- The newer screens task retires the bindings screen and replaces it with the
  subscriptions screen: =task_build_the_market_data_screens.org=, line 38.
- The simulator move is done. Its result says the old control plane still
  publishes =market_tick= on its binding subject, and the screens task retires
  it: =task_move_the_simulator_to_ores_marketdata_synthetic.org=.
- The activity row is keyed (tenant, party, source_name), not by a binding id,
  so it needs no binding to survive:
  =projects/ores.sql/create/marketdata/marketdata_feed_binding_activity_create.sql=,
  line 39.

This reverses the Sprint 27 decision "consumption is a binding, not a copy".
When the routes did not exist, the binding was the only consumption record.
The route now holds the same fact with a provider, a source, a validity window
and an audit trail. The binding duplicates it, and its =enabled= flag repeats
what the absence of a route already says.

** The consequence

- *Ingest:* read the routes only. Remove the binding read, the =consumer_of=
  copy and the binding change-event subscription.
- *Web:* retire the bindings list and record page, the BFF read and the menu
  card. The subscriptions screen and the source status screen take their place.
- *SQL:* drop =ores_marketdata_feed_bindings_tbl= and
  =ores_marketdata_feed_binding_activity_tbl= with their triggers, event
  mappings and two policies. Remove =feed_bindings_tbl_tenant_isolation_policy=
  from the widening guard when the table drops. The audit story records the
  decision for that policy as "retired", not "role-scoped". No migration and no
  data are kept: the database is recreated.
- *Stories:* the bindings screen story =AE947749= closes as superseded by the
  service screens task. The subscribe story =3BAE4AAA= is reworded to
  subscriptions and routes.

** The tasks this decision unblocks

1. Retire the feed binding. The ingest reads the routes only, and the binding
   model, code, tables, policies, screen, permissions and generated artefacts
   are deleted in the same change. The database is trashed and recreated, so
   there is no migration and no legacy row to keep.
2. Reconcile the binding stories and the market data docs with the retirement.

** No migration

The database is recreated from the create scripts. The drop keeps no data and
needs no row count, and no compatibility view, dual write or deprecation window
is added. A database that still holds binding rows is discarded.
