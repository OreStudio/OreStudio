#+title: Intraday valuation of options expiring today
#+description: Pricing same-day options up to their cut with an intraday ORE build, and the open questions for its reports.
#+type: knowledge
#+audience: user
#+level: cross
#+filetags: :architecture:ore:expiry:reporting:intraday:
#+created: 2026-11-22
#+updated: 2026-11-22

* Summary

An option that expires today carries most of its gamma, vega and theta in the
last hours before its cut, and ORE values at day resolution: on the expiry date
an option is either fully alive or already expired. ORE Studio's response is a
second build of ORE in which QuantLib dates carry a time of day, so that
options expiring today price and produce sensitivities up to their cut. The
engine side exists: it is proposed to ORE as a draft pull request and packaged
in ORE Studio. The reports that would use it do not exist, and the work on them
has not started. Many questions about how those reports will work remain open,
beginning with how a book's headline position is stitched together from two
engine builds.

* Detail

** Status

This note records a design in progress. The engine work is done and under
review; the report work is not started. The open questions below are genuine:
they are not settled by this note, and the answers may change the approach.

** The problem

Near the cut, the gamma of a vanilla option grows as the inverse square root of
the time left, and a digital's delta and gamma near the strike are larger
still. A desk that runs a same-day options book needs its position and
sensitivities through the day, which is where the decision of
[[id:970D334D-2576-41BF-8664-1AA4CB76D31B][Pin risk in the expiry of near-the-money options]] is made. At day
resolution these figures do not exist: the engine sees no hours left to expiry.
Volatility within a day is also not uniform; Andersen and Bollerslev (1997,
1998) document its intraday periodicity, and Wright (2020) the pricing of
options around scheduled events, which is why the remaining hours must be
weighted rather than counted.

** The engine

QuantLib has long had a build option, =QL_HIGH_RESOLUTION_DATE=, under which a
date carries a time of day (Spanderen, 2015). The pull request proposes to make
ORE usable in that build:

- dates accept ISO 8601 datetimes, converted to UTC;
- a trade states its expiry cut in =Envelope/AdditionalFields/ExpiryCutTime=,
  which the regular build ignores, so one trade XML serves both builds;
- FX option quotes may carry an expiry date as well as a tenor, so a volatility
  surface can have a pillar at today's cut;
- a new optional FX volatility curve setting, =EnforceMonotoneVariance=, makes
  a decreasing ATM total variance an error rather than a warning.

The design, its examples and its limitations are set out in the accompanying
paper (Craveiro, 2026). ORE Studio packages the intraday build beside the
regular one, as =ore-1.8.17.0-intraday= in
[[proj:external/ore/packages/manifest.json][the engine package manifest]], built
from the branch =feature/intraday-dates= of the ORE Studio fork. The pull
request is a draft and asks the ORE maintainers whether a second build is an
acceptable route, whether the envelope is the right place for the cut, and
whether the FX market data changes fit ORE.

** Scope

The intraday build values only the options that expire on the valuation date:
European vanilla, digital, barrier and touch, and American options, on equity,
FX and commodity underlyings. Every other trade is valued by the regular build.
Linear and rates products have no exposure to the remaining hours; variance and
volatility swaps maturing today have one return left; an option past its cut
carries no volatility risk and awaits only its settlement fixing.

** Open questions

The report work raises questions that no part of the design answers yet.

- *Stitching the headline position.* A desk reads one headline for a book, but
  the book's options expiring today are valued by the intraday build at a time
  of day and everything else by the regular build at day resolution. How the
  two results become one total is undecided: whether both runs read one
  snapshot at one valuation time, how the regular run is kept from valuing
  today's expiries as alive for the whole day, and which run owns an option
  past its cut that awaits settlement. The same question returns for every
  report that totals a book.
- *Which runs, when.* Whether a report run selects the engine build by its
  asof, by its trades or by configuration, and how the compute grid carries
  two engine packages for one report; see [[id:98B9FE2A-57FB-464F-A4E5-198461FE6987][Compute Engine]].
- *Several cuts in one day.* A day can hold the Tokyo, ECB and New York cuts.
  Whether a same-day report runs once per cut, once per snapshot, or once with
  every cut, and how it groups the results.
- *The cut on the trade.* FpML names a cut through its cut name coding
  scheme, but ORE Studio's trades carry no expiry cut today, so
  =ExpiryCutTime= has no source in the trade data; see
  [[id:3B39B2E2-6E05-4DF8-B68B-6ACA12F2FCE9][The option-expiry cut code]].
- *Settlement fixings at a cut.* An option past its cut settles on the fixing
  of its cut's source, which the catalogue of
  [[id:5AF9CEEF-A878-443A-8C32-C6E9F6C26FC9][Fixing sources for option cuts and non-deliverable currencies]]
  must hold before such an option can be valued.
- *Time-weighted volatility.* The weights for the remaining hours come from a
  process outside ORE (Clark, 2011, gives sample day and intraday weights);
  where that process lives in ORE Studio is undecided.
- *Vega and theta within a day.* With the surface not simulated, the
  sensitivity analysis reports no vega to today's pillar, and a theta period
  of one day steps past the cut.
- *Value at risk.* Parametric VaR on deltas and gammas does not suit options
  minutes from expiry; full revaluation over scenarios is untested at an
  intraday asof.
- *Times in the output.* ORE's reports print dates without the time of day, and
  serialization drops it.
