#+title: The feed and its lifecycle
#+description: What an IFeed is, the identity triple it carries, the blocking start and stop contract, and the publish counter it exposes.

* Summary

A feed is a market data producer that ORE Studio runs as a live source.
Every producer, whatever its asset class, implements the one interface
=IFeed=, declared in =ores.marketdata.api/domain/i_feed.hpp=. The
interface lives in =ores.marketdata.api= rather than in the generation
service, so =ores.synthetic= and any future calibration service see the
same type without creating a dependency cycle.

* Detail

** The identity triple

A feed carries three names and each answers a different question.

- =source_name()= is the producer's unique identity within a tenant, for
  example "eur_usd_gbm_1". The feed controller keys its map of running
  feeds by this name.
- =series_uris()= lists the oresmd series that the feed publishes into: the
  series URIs that consumers look up. See
  [[id:C3E053CA-0D4B-480B-9119-E11530160EC1][oresmd: ORE Studio Market Data URI]],
  where a curve is a series. An FX spot feed reports the one series of its
  quote, for example
  =oresmd://fx/EUR?type=series&instrument=fx_spot&quote=rate&ccy=USD=. An IR
  curve feed reports each series its pillars land in.
- =role()= is the use the feed's curve is built for, such as discount or
  projection. The oresmd grammar does not hold a role, so the feed states it
  in its own data and the role plays no part in what the feed owns. It is
  empty for a feed without roles, such as FX spot.

Two feeds must not run while their series overlap, because both would write
points into one series. =feeds_conflict()= compares the two lists and finds
whether they share a URI. A feed that names no series has an empty list and
conflicts with nothing. Such a feed has no published market data series to
protect.

** The lifecycle contract

=start()= blocks the calling thread until =stop()= is called from a
different thread, so a caller always runs a feed on a thread it owns.
=stop()= is thread safe, may be called from any thread, returns
immediately, and lets the tick loop exit asynchronously. Every other method
is single threaded. =publish_count()= returns the number of ticks published
since start.

A feed publishes its own ticks to NATS and the loop invokes no handler on
it, so the publishing path sits inside the producer rather than in the
framework around it.

** The remaining two members

=nats_subject()= is the subject the feed publishes on, derived from its
source name and its binding mode. =kind()= is the factory kind string the
producer registers under, and it is fixed for the producer's lifetime.
[[id:97208391-AE4B-4032-9744-8F8B228FD567][The producer subject]] states the subject grammar and
[[id:D67C930B-261F-480B-B5EF-020C6D617333][The feed factory and the kind seam]] states how the kind selects a builder.
