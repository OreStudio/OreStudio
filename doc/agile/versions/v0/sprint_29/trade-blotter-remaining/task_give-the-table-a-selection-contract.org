* Acceptance

- RecordTable takes an opt-in selection: which rows are chosen, how a row is toggled, and whether the whole set may be chosen at once.
- A screen that does not ask for selection draws exactly as it does today.
- The grouping screen draws its picker with RecordTable and loses its hand-rolled table.
- The chosen count and the mixed-counterparty guard stay the screen's, since they are its rule and not the table's.

* Plan

The contract is one optional prop. Three fields and no more: what is chosen,
how a row is toggled, and, only where the screen offers it, whether the whole set
may be chosen at once. A table that states no selection draws exactly what it drew
before, which is what makes this safe to add to a component every list uses.

What stays outside the table is the part that is not drawing: the count the screen
shows, the rule that refuses a mixed counterparty, and the act the choice is for.
The table reports keys and draws boxes; it decides nothing.

* What stayed the screen's

The count it shows, the rule that refuses a mixed counterparty, and the fact that
a grouping needs two trades at least. The table reports a key when a row is
toggled and draws the box that is in the state the screen states; it holds no
opinion about what may be chosen.

* What a screen that does not choose sees

Nothing different. The prop is optional, and every other table in the product
passes none.
