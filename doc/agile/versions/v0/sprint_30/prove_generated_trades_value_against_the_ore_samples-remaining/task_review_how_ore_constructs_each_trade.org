* Acceptance

- A table holds one row for each of the 85 generated trade types: its ORE data class, the terms its =fromXML= reads as mandatory, the terms its conventions supply, and its engine builder with the market data that engine asks for.
- It says which types =TradeGenerator= builds (11 products) and what it defaults for each, and which types have no ORE builder to follow.
- It states how the approach of =TradeGenerator=, economic terms in and the rest from conventions, extends to each trade family it does not cover, including the shared pieces such as legs, option blocks and barriers.

- The table is checked in where the builder task reads it, and states the ORE commit it was read at.

* Result
[[id:10F9A53C-B978-428F-A0B1-48C97AC5828C][What ORE requires of each trade]] holds a row per generated trade type, generated by =build/scripts/generate_ore_trade_requirements.py= at ORE commit =3d75a6908=, and states how =TradeGenerator='s approach extends to every family.

Of the 85 types, 84 are registered ORE trades, 82 have a data element in ORE's XSDs, and 11 are built by =TradeGenerator=. The recorded trades lack 145 required terms and 5 types are refused, which is the work the builders must do.

The table lists required terms against what the generator writes, by element name. A term that no recorded trade writes is the term a builder must add, and the generate task clears it. 15 types have no engine the scan can name, among them positions that use no engine, so the market data and engines task reads those by hand.

Findings handed on to the generate task: =CommodityPairwiseVarianceSwap= is not an ORE trade (ORE has equity and FX only), and =BondPosition= and =CommodityWindowBarrierOption= have no XSD data element.
