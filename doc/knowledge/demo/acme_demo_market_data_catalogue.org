#+title: The Acme catalogue is installed by the bundle, and the feeds generate the series
#+description: What the market data catalogue holds in a provisioned Acme tenant, which component writes each part of it, and the series the simulated feeds then generate for the parties.
#+type: knowledge
#+level: cross
#+filetags: :knowledge:marketdata:catalogue:acme:demo:
#+created: 2026-10-26
#+updated: 2026-10-26
#+startup: inlineimages

* Summary

A provisioned Acme tenant holds a market data catalogue it never wrote. The
system bundle installs the providers, the sources, the vendor instruments, their
mappings to internal items and the rate cards, all in the system tenant, and the
simulator installs the generators and its own universe beside them. The tenant's
own setup writes no catalogue row. What the tenant gains is the other half: the
feeds the catalogue describes run, and the series they produce land in the party
that subscribed to them. A fresh Acme tenant therefore holds 7 providers, 6
sources, 447 vendor instruments, 447 mappings and 24 rate card components before
any person signs in, and 341 series and 1651 observations once the feeds have
run. The first four counts are fixed by the bundle; the last two were measured on
a freshly provisioned tenant and grow as the demonstration is used.

* Detail

** What the catalogue is, and who writes it

#+ATTR_HTML: :class hug-leading
| Part of the catalogue | Rows in a fresh Acme tenant | Installed by |
|-----------------------+-----------------------------+--------------|
| Providers | 7 | the system bundle |
| Sources | 6 | the system bundle |
| Vendor instruments | 447 | the system bundle |
| Mappings to internal items | 447 | the system bundle |
| Rate card components | 24 | the system bundle |
| Generators and the simulator universe | per feed collection | the synthetic feeds bundle |
| Series and observations | 341 and 1651 after the feeds run | the running feeds, in the party |

The rule that produces this split is
[[id:922C155B-6733-4D2B-A675-A7361A5BDC63][A system bundle installs the
catalogue and the simulator installs its own universe]]: a definition has one
home, and a tenant's setup never writes the catalogue. It follows that a
demonstration showing the wrong instrument count is a bundle fault and not a
tenant fault, and that a person cannot break the catalogue by provisioning a
second tenant.

** The vendor instrument and its mapping

A vendor instrument is an id scheme, an id and a field, in the vendor's own
notation, and a mapping is the link from it to the internal item a consumer names.
The two tables hold the same count because the bundle installs them together and
a vendor instrument with no mapping is a row the service can announce but nothing
can demand. [[id:5297997A-5175-43E9-861B-2A11D003CD35][A vendor instrument is an
id scheme, an id and a field]] states the row, and
[[id:BAE0F052-CCA6-43FB-A139-03518CE2D929][The mapping belongs to the provider and
not to the source]] states why the link hangs off the provider: two sources of one
provider must map an instrument the same way.

** The feeds that generate the series

The series a party holds come from a feed collection, and each collection quotes
what the ACME reports need.

#+ATTR_HTML: :class hug-leading
| Collection | Provider it stands for | What it quotes |
|------------+------------------------+----------------|
| =synthetic.feeds.bloomberg= | =bloomberg= | a USD spot and an interest rate curve for every currency of an ACME book, and the USD FOMC-dated short end |
| =synthetic.feeds.uniform_demo= | =demo= | eight major and three emerging pairs at one deliberately exaggerated volatility, so movement is visible on a chart |
| =synthetic.feeds.lseg= | =lseg= | fourteen cross pairs that Bloomberg leaves to the cross-rates matrix |
| =synthetic.feeds.ice= | =ice= | the LIBOR curves the Bloomberg feeds leave out |
| =synthetic.feeds.tradeweb= | =tradeweb= | the EURIBOR and LIBOR dealer composites Bloomberg does not quote |

Two properties of the collections matter to a demonstration. Coverage is closed:
every currency of an ACME book has a USD spot and a curve, and a cross comes from
the cross-rates matrix rather than from a feed of its own, so a run that resolves
a curve for any ACME book finds one. And one series has one running publisher:
the five collections name disjoint series, so the four offices can read four
different providers and none of them fights another for a series. The levels are
sourced where a free source exists: the thirteen pairs the Federal Reserve H.10
release quotes start at the 2026-05-05 vintage imported by
=marketdata.reference_vintage_2026_05_05=, and the rest start at a fixed,
illustrative level near the 2026 market.

** What a party ends up holding

#+ATTR_HTML: :class hug-leading
| Party | Series from its subscribed provider | Series from =ore= |
|-------+-------------------------------------+-------------------|
| Acme Corporation Plc | 54 | 17 |
| ACME Corporation UK plc | 26 | 30 |
| ACME Corporation US Inc | 14 | 20 |
| ACME Corporation HK Ltd | 54 | 20 |
| System Party | 54 | 27 |

The =ore= rows are the third source of the two parties' market: the values of
the ORE example market data files, imported by the reference vintage the profile
publishes, so a report that reads a value ORE itself states finds it. The
per-party counts differ because a party holds the series its own provider quotes
plus the ones the reference vintage carries, and the two collections quote
different markets. [[id:E4736D88-03B2-4A40-B7F4-01B06B9B9FD7][A quote series is
keyed by item, provider and party]] is why the same item is a row per party
rather than a shared one, and
[[id:D71F9446-C62E-48A6-A87B-136EF598A59E][One shared tick or one copy for
each party]] is the cost decision behind it.

** A demand is what makes a generator run

A series exists for a party because something demanded it. The reports and the
curve screens register demand, the service resolves the party's route to a
source, and the synthetic runner starts the generator for the item.
[[id:083D4B54-549B-4D56-AE89-430A8A4277FF][Demand drives what an adapter
publishes]] is the general rule, and
[[id:54C9E31E-26FF-4530-A868-0850FCE1FE30][The simulator is one adapter plus
the tools that author its generators]] says where the generators live. The count
above is therefore a count of what the demonstration's runs and screens asked
for, not a fixed size.
