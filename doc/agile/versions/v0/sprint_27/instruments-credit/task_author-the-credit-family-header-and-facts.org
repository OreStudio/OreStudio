* Acceptance

- The header's trade_type check lists the six codes the importer writes, and the catalogue routes only those.
- Six fact tables are keyed by the trade and pinned to the header by the generated delete cascade.
- Codegen drift is clean and the generated eventing tests pass.

* Plan

1. *The header.* =credit_instrument= loses its product columns and keeps the
   trade key, the party, the type, the reference entity, the two common dates and
   the description. It is cloned from the rates header: the delete cascade names
   every table the family writes, and a pinned-key trigger holds the party and
   type to the anchor trade. The check lists the six codes the importer writes.
2. *Six fact tables.* One model per product, cloned from the rates facts: keyed
   by the trade, tenant isolated, party scoped for writes, with generated
   eventing tests. A column lands only when a mapper writes it today, so unit 6
   adds the rest with their writers.
3. *The routing.* The two codes with no importer leave the catalogue's routing.
   The six facts join the catalogue's children and the header's digest.
4. *Generate and wire.* Regenerate the trading component, then wire the six
   entities by hand as the basket child was: the create and drop includes, the
   permission seeds, the registrars, the history providers and the event
   mappings.
5. *The carrier.* A =credit_fact_variant= holds the product's fact row. The
   carrier gains it, with the shared strikes and schedules, and the batch, the
   export service, the import handler and the mapper read and write it.
6. *Prove it.* Update the credit mapper tests, recreate the database, and run
   the suites and every check.

* Notes

- The ctest runs went straight to ctest, because the host build lock was held
  by other worktrees. The user allowed it.
- The index option's fact row holds the wrapped index swap's credit curve id,
  and the trade has no row in the index swap's table. The design said two rows;
  the carrier holds one fact row, so the design now says one.
- The header's dates carry =:common_date:= and the common-date view lists the
  credit header. Unit 7 keeps the probe that proves it.
- The generated header eventing test seeds an anchor trade of the header's own
  type, because the pinned-key trigger refuses a header that disagrees with its
  anchor. The rates header passes by coincidence: both default to Swap.
- An index option states one strike and its exercise dates in the shared strike
  and schedule rows. The rest of its option block (long or short, style,
  premium) waits for unit 6.
- Fact columns without a writer are not in the tables. Unit 6 adds the flags,
  the upfront fee and date, the participation rate and the protection fee with
  the code that writes them.

* Result

The credit family has a header and one fact table per product, generated from
models. =credit_instrument= is the slim header with the common dates and the
delete cascade, and six fact tables hold the credit curve, the issuer, the
recovery rate, the CDO tranche and the wrapped index curve. The catalogue routes
the six importer codes and leaves the two codes with no importer out. The
carrier, the batch, the export service, the import handler and the mapper read
and write the header with its fact row, and the corpus documents come back
through them.

Acceptance met: the header lists the six codes; six fact tables are keyed by the
trade and pinned by the generated delete cascade; and codegen drift is clean
with the generated eventing tests passing. Unit 6 closes the round trip.
